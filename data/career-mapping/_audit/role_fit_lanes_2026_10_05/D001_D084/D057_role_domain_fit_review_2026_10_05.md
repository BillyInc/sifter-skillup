## Evidence and limits

[WHO digital health overview](https://www.who.int/europe/health-topics/digital-health) defines digital health as development/use of digital technologies to improve health and includes health information systems and telemedicine. [WHO CDISAH classification](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240081949) catalogs digital-health interventions and services, including electronic records and telehealth systems. [WHO telemedicine implementation guide](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240059184) supports telemedicine as a digital-health service area.
[O*NET Health Informatics Specialists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1211.01), [O*NET Health Information Technologists and Medical Registrars](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/29-9021.00), [O*NET Medical Records Specialists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/29-2072.00), and [AHIMA health-information overview](https://www.ahima.org/certification-careers/certifications-overview/career-tools/career-pages/health-information-101/) corroborate the records, coding, computerized-system, and health-information-management task families.

WHO domain classifications do not validate an individual job title. The six conditional records therefore remain non-confirmed student paths until the exact role identity/task profile is evidenced or the role remit is explicitly limited to digital health. General hospital work is not automatically digital health.
