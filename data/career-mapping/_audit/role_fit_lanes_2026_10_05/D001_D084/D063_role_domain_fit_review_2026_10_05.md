## Evidence and limits

[OECD competition policy](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/policy-issues/competition.html) links competition policy to economic policy, consumer welfare and growth. The [ILO employment-policy overview](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/formulation-employment-policies) describes employment policy as connected to macroeconomic and development policy. The [UNFCCC climate-policy impact-assessment database](https://unfccc.int/response-measures/impact-assessment-tools-database) explicitly includes economic and socioeconomic impact analysis, but does not make all climate-policy roles economics roles. [O*NET Economists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3011.00), [postsecondary economics teachers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/25-1063.00), [Environmental Economists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3011.01) and [Political Scientists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3094.00) provide occupation/task boundaries.

Conditional is not confirmed: it requires the stated task assignment. The combined secondary teaching role is conditional on economics being taught as a substantive subject; climate policy is conditional on economic/socioeconomic analysis. Political-scientist tasks remain outside D063 absent a specific economic-analysis assignment.
