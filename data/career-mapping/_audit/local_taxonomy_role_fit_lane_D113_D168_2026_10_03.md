# Local subgroup and role-fit lane: D113–D168

Date: 2026-10-03  
Status: in progress; entries are appended after source review. This file is not external academic sign-off.

## Decision rules

- Review each specialization against the parent domain independently from the roles attached to it.
- Review every role’s fit to its parent domain independently from its fit to a specialization. ISCO/ESCO family membership is identity/classification evidence, not proof of either fit.
- A role can be a direct domain role only if it fits that domain and no specialization membership is supported. A role that does not fit the domain must be removed from that mapping or reassigned only when another domain is supported.
- Specializations are student-facing areas of specialization within a domain. Counts are justified by distinct tasks, knowledge, machinery/materials and outputs, not by current ISCO group counts.
- Domain foundations should contain shared learning; specializations should add their distinct learning; roles should add role-specific learning. Do not duplicate inherited items or use role aggregates as specialization-owned content.
- Global scope is the default. A national regulator or national curriculum may support a clearly labelled country-specific variant, not define the global taxonomy.

The current parent description names construction, ports and plant hire but omits mining, manufacturing/warehouse handling, and seasonal road maintenance even though the attached role set spans these contexts; the domain scope/name must be reconciled before final student copy is adopted.

### Specialization-set disposition

The current eight visible subgroup records are **not eight reviewed student specializations**: they are inherited ISCO sub-major groups, including broad families such as teaching professionals, assemblers, and mining/construction/manufacturing/transport labourers. Current membership follows the code family; that does not show that the category is a coherent rail-specific area of specialization. The current student scope paragraph also focuses on operating services and yards and omits infrastructure engineering and rolling-stock design/manufacture/maintenance, although these areas appear in the catalogue’s 20 role mappings.

For audit and correction, the evidence supports an internal candidate set of **four** student-facing areas, pending another independent curriculum or international rail-sector framework review before external academic submission:

1. **Rail Operations, Traffic Control and Safety** — train movement, driving, dispatch, signalling/switching, shunting, train preparation, passenger/freight train crew, and operational safety.
2. **Rail Infrastructure, Track and Construction** — track and rail infrastructure design, construction, inspection, maintenance, and renewal.
3. **Rolling-Stock Engineering, Manufacturing and Maintenance** — rail-vehicle engineering, assembly, electrical/mechanical systems, testing, inspection, maintenance, and asset readiness.
4. **Rail Freight and Intermodal Operations** — freight-terminal and intermodal loading/transfer tasks that are specifically performed as part of rail-freight operations.

The four areas are an explicit internal synthesis of the two curricula and role-task scope; neither curriculum prescribes exactly four. The broad parent should be described as a **rail-system** domain that covers operations, infrastructure, rolling stock, and freight interfaces. If the product intends “Railway” to mean train service operations only, infrastructure/manufacturing roles need separate domain destinations rather than silently broadening the name. The four-area recommendation therefore remains provisional on the final parent-domain boundary and additional multiregional evidence.

## D119 — Livestock, Poultry and Dairy Production

### Evidence and subgroup-set correction

The current six cards are ISCO-derived occupation families, not six validated student specializations. The previous three-area proposal was also incomplete: it did not account for the catalogue's equine and bee-breeding roles and grouped species into a catch-all non-ruminant area without a task-level boundary. FAO describes livestock as a global production system and its Farmer Field School platform names poultry, dairying, pig, rabbit, beekeeping, beef, camelid and small-ruminant systems; WOAH independently treats beef, dairy, broiler and pig production as distinct standards contexts. ILO task definitions distinguish animal raising/breeding from processing animal products. These are global scope references, not a fixed worldwide academic taxonomy.

Candidate student-facing areas, pending the catalogue-wide root/scope and independent subgroup-identity implementation:

1. **Poultry Production** — bird breeding, hatchery, flock, meat and egg production systems.
2. **Ruminant Livestock and Dairy Production** — cattle, sheep, goats and other ruminants; meat and dairy enterprises.
3. **Pig and Rabbit Production** — species-specific swine and rabbit husbandry, with shared foundations shown once and non-shared techniques kept distinct.
4. **Equine Production and Management** — horse breeding and farm/yard husbandry; only production contexts belong here, not every sport, boarding or companion-animal service.
5. **Apiculture and Insect Production** — managed bee/insect production and animal products; do not equate general crop pollination work with animal production.

Fur-animal breeding is left on a direct domain→role candidate path rather than forced into a broad or unrelated specialization. Its global ethical, legal and welfare context needs separate treatment. The root label names poultry and dairy but omits equine and apiculture, while its description includes horses and bees; the root naming/scope audit must reconcile this before student publication. Five is a defensible candidate set from global comparative sources and the actual role mix, not a mandated count.
