## Sources

- **ILO_ISCO08_ADMIN_SOCIAL — International Labour Organization (ILO), ISCO-08 Volume I** ([source](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf)). ISCO-08 3344 defines medical secretaries using medical terminology and healthcare procedures; 2635 describes professional social workers; 3412 describes social-work associate professionals who administer/implement social assistance and community services and assist people with personal/social problems. Limitation: Supports task-defined global occupational families; it does not decide whether current specialized role paths already cover these tasks, nor establish a separate global youth-worker title or product-domain placement.
- **ILO_ISCO08_EARLY_CHILDHOOD_ARMED_FORCES — International Labour Organization (ILO), ISCO-08 Volume I** ([source](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf)). ISCO-08 includes early-childhood educators (2342) and an armed-forces occupations major group, supporting task-family and classification boundaries for those role areas. Limitation: The classification does not establish national service ranks, licensing, nor whether each domain's active title list adequately represents a locally specific institutional structure.
- **WHO_CHIROPRACTIC_TRAINING_GUIDELINE — World Health Organization (WHO)** ([source](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/43352/9241593717_eng.pdf?sequence=1)). A distinct chiropractic practitioner pathway and training/safety model; the guideline explicitly notes variation in practitioner training and national regulation. Limitation: Does not establish uniform global licensure, identical legal scope or evidence that every chiropractic technique is effective.
- **WHO_GLOBAL_TCIM_STRATEGY — World Health Organization (WHO)** ([source](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240113176)). WHO recognizes traditional, complementary and integrative medicine as a global policy/workforce area while prioritizing evidence, safety, regulation and context-appropriate integration; it does not assert that every modality is equally evidence-supported or regulated everywhere. Limitation: This is a global policy framework, not proof of universal acceptance, licensure, efficacy or identical scope for individual modalities.
- **WHO_NATUROPATHY_TRAINING_BENCHMARK — World Health Organization (WHO)** ([source](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241599658)). Defines training models, competencies and practice responsibilities for naturopathic practitioners, demonstrating an established modality-specific practitioner pathway while acknowledging need for training/examination/licensing systems. Limitation: A training benchmark does not mean naturopathy is accepted, regulated or licensed identically in all countries.
