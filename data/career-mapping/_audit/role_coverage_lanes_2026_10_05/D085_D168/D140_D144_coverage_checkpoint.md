## Sources

- **EMBL_EBI_BIOINFORMATICS_ROLE_PROFILE — European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI), European Molecular Biology Laboratory** ([source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/bioinformatics-for-principal-investigators-collection/what-is-a-bioinformatician/)). Bioinformatician is described as a broad term involving biological-domain knowledge plus informatics, programming and statistics, including data management, analysis, visualization and pipeline development. Limitation: The source describes a broad role family and task patterns, not a distinct title boundary between bioinformatician and computational biologist.
- **ENFSI_FORENSIC_PRACTITIONER_ROLES — European Network of Forensic Science Institutes (ENFSI), Quality and Competence Committee** ([source](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/QCC-CAP-006-001.pdf)). Distinguishes scene-of-crime examiner, analyst/assistant, reporting analyst and reporting scientist functions across the forensic process; scene investigators focus on initial scene assessment and collection, while reporting scientists examine items, interpret findings and report evidence. Limitation: ENFSI states that organizations define recognized roles; these are functional role families, not necessarily identical job titles across jurisdictions.
- **WMO_METEOROLOGICAL_OBSERVER_FRAMEWORK — World Meteorological Organization (WMO)** ([source](https://etrp.wmo.int/pluginfile.php/69555/mod_resource/content/1/1209_en.pdf)). WMO defines observer functions distinct from forecaster functions, including observing and recording weather parameters, monitoring conditions, quality control and communicating observations; meteorological technician pathways can also maintain observing instruments. Limitation: Frameworks define competency functions, but exact job titles, service assignments and qualification routes vary by national meteorological service and job.
