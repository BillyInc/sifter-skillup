# Catalogue-wide role/domain and computational-method review — 2026-10-03

## Scope and status

## Defensible review method

Each specialization decision must have a traceable record: stable domain ID; prior and proposed structure; criteria applied; primary-source title, publisher, URL, access date and exact page/section/course locator; the specific claim supported and its limits; alternatives considered; uncertainty; and a separate status for internal review versus external academic sign-off. Prefer two independent primary sources for a proposed specialization set. A count is justified by distinct, non-overlapping curriculum/practice areas and documented coverage—not role counts, ISCO buckets, keyword similarity, or an arbitrary threshold. ISCO/ESCO can support occupational identity, but never substitute for evidence of domain fit or specialization fit.

This review records an internal evidence assessment only. It must not be represented as accredited, endorsed, or formally approved by a university or professional body unless that review is separately obtained and documented. The current decision register and exact source locators are in `domain_specialization_review_2026_10_03.json`.

The rule is now explicit across the full catalogue: an ISCO group/subgroup match establishes only occupational-family consistency. It does **not** establish that the occupation belongs in the selected career domain or in a particular specialization. Each role mapping must be checked against its tasks, the domain's scope, and the specialization's scope.

**Terminology and learner model:** a subgroup is an **area of specialization within a domain**, not a separate learner path. Content should accumulate as domain foundation → specialization-specific additions → role-specific requirements. For every domain, audit each specialization's fit to the parent and its roles, then decide whether the specialization set has the right coverage/count, needs additions/merges/removals, or should be intentionally unsplit.

**Independent fit rule:** the role→domain relationship must be assessed before and independently of role→specialization membership. A role may appear to fit an existing specialization yet still be outside the parent domain; in that case, fix or remove the domain link rather than treating the subgroup as justification. The inventory now records separate review statuses for both relationships and marks current subgroup membership as unverified.

**Confirmed modelling limitation:** the current source and seed identify a subgroup by `(domain_id, isco_submajor)` and attach roles by the first two digits of `role.isco_group`. This forces specialization boundaries to follow an occupational classification. It cannot express two different domain specializations among roles sharing one ISCO submajor, nor a coherent specialization combining roles from different ISCO submajors. Therefore the current 400 visible groups and 72 zero-group domains are inventory facts—not a validated specialization taxonomy. Correcting the mappings requires explicit domain-specialization definitions and explicit role→specialization assignments, with ISCO retained as role provenance rather than the parent key.

The deterministic inventory is `domain_subgroup_role_taxonomy_inventory_2026_10_03.json` with a readable table in `DOMAIN_SUBGROUP_ROLE_TAXONOMY_INVENTORY_2026-10-03.md`. It merged canonical role and overlay sources using seed precedence and scanned every domain and role mapping. It does not automatically decide semantic fit.

### The learning layers must add, not echo

The specified student progression is **domain foundation → specialization-specific additions → role-specific additions**; where a domain is intentionally unsplit, it is **domain → role**. The audit found 27 active specialization records with no recorded specialization-specific skill additions. Separately, the existing `domain_skill_provenance.json` audit reports **38,845 overlapping skill occurrences across 544 source-provenance subgroup bundles**. Inspection shows these bundles are marked `ownership: role_aggregated` and `student_visibility: review_only`; they are not approved specialization foundations and must not be used to fill the 27 gaps or surfaced as student requirements. The full audit must compare student-visible domain/specialization/role payloads, deduplicate inherited items, and record only verified additions at each level.

## Social Sciences / Sociology (D078)

The current evidence supports keeping D078 as **Social Sciences**, not renaming the root domain “Computational.” Computational social science is an established methods-oriented area within social science. Cornell describes social network analysis, social sequence analysis, topic modelling, and other large/digital-data approaches as methods; the American Sociological Association separately describes computational models of social dynamics, computational data-analysis methods, and research into human behaviour online or in computer-mediated systems. These sources support a computational-methods specialization and optional learning content, not a claim that all Sociology students or every Sociology occupation must code.

The two distinct D078 routes are appropriate as learning directions—(1) research into social, cultural, and economic life, and (2) quantitative/computational methods for social questions—but that decision is supported by the Sociology worked example and external field evidence, not by “the JSON currently has two rows.” They are now visible, with role counts recalculated. A full source-based label/role review is still required across the other 167 domains.

The role **Biometrician** is not justified in the D078 student route just because it shares ISCO 2120 with statistics. ESCO describes biometric work around identifying people from measurements such as fingerprints, retinas, and body features, mainly in medical or industrial applications; O*NET places the title among biostatistics/life-science statistics examples. That does not substantiate a Social Sciences occupational path. The D078 mapping is therefore tagged excluded for students and retained internally with its reason; it is not silently reassigned to another domain. The remaining mathematician/statistician/demographer rows still need role-task and domain-context dispositions; ISCO alone is not their sign-off.

Four additional source-backed **method/knowledge** rows were added under the D078 quantitative/computational subgroup: social sequence analysis; topic modelling and computational text analysis; computational models of social dynamics; and analysis of online/computer-mediated social behaviour. They are explicitly optional, question-dependent methods, not universal role requirements. No new occupation title was fabricated. Four existing role→method links remain separate and must not imply that a method is required for every person in those roles.

## Subgroup-name audit interpretation

The 283 exact ISCO-label matches across 106 domains show why a structurally valid taxonomy may still read poorly to learners. ISCO is useful for identifying broad occupational families, but its labels are not automatically good names for domain-specific specializations. Evaluate each candidate against member role tasks, sibling specializations, root-domain scope, and authoritative professional or curriculum sources. Some broad labels may remain understandable where the specialization is genuinely broad; others need a meaningful specialization name or the domain may appropriately remain unsplit.
