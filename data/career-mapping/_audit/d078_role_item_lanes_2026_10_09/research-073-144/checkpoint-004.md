### 103 — demography [essential], Demographer

Research task hypothesis: compile or interpret population composition/change, yielding a dated, sourced demographic table/profile or indicator note with geography, definitions and caveats. UNFPA's [State of World Population 2023](https://nigeria.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/swop2023-english-230329-web_1_0.pdf) supplies global-comparable demographic framing and a Nigeria country publication; see its indicator/data tables. Nigeria's [NBS Demographic Statistics Bulletin 2022](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/DEMOGRAPHIC_BULLETIN_2022_FINAL.pdf), Preface and 2019–22 tables, directly evidences country indicators including population, fertility and mortality. Neither is a role-task standard; dated indicators must not be called current. Prerequisites: denominators, time/geography definitions, basic statistics and data quality. Complements: inspect data, data quality assessment, GIS. Population statistics/population studies are nearby labels, not assumed synonyms. No demographer task manual or verified hands-on free course found. Exact ESCO URI/type/version, exact role task, current indicator source and task-specific learning remain open.

### 104 — demography [optional], Criminologist

Keep this separate from key 103 despite identical label: optional, criminologist occurrence. A bounded plausible output—only if task evidence supports it—is a descriptive comparison of population/crime-related social patterns with denominators, time/place and confounders; never an individual risk label. NBS Bulletin 2022 establishes Nigerian demographic data availability, not crime-demography practice or a validated causal relationship. Prerequisites include rates/denominators, descriptive statistics, ecological-fallacy awareness and privacy/small-cell safeguards. Complements: inspect data, data quality assessment, criminology. Population statistics may support description but cannot substitute for criminological theory, legal classification or causal design. Nigeria-specific role evidence, exact concept identity and learning source are unresolved; group rates cannot be projected onto individuals.

### 105 — develop professional identity in social work [optional], Social Work Researcher

This reads as a learning outcome, not necessarily a discrete skill. [IFSW/IASSW Global Standards](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards-for-social-work-education-and-training/) (2020) provide an international, context-sensitive education/identity framework. Nigeria-specific context comes from the [NASoW Constitution](https://www.ifsw.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/body-of-Nasow-constitution.pdf), clauses 5.1–5.5 and 5.10–5.12, on standards, ethics, interdisciplinary study, training and social-problem work. This is an association document, not current statute or proof of national consensus. Free learning: OpenLearn, [§2.3 “Developing a professional social work identity”](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=19003&section=2.3), which names empathy, ethics and accountability; its participation statement is not an accredited qualification. A reflective development plan is a possible observable output, not yet proven required for this role. Prerequisites: ethical/role boundaries, reflection, local institutional context. Complements: ethics, qualitative research, culturally diverse services. Exact vocabulary identity, research-role task linkage and current Nigerian framework remain unresolved; do not imply licensure/registration.

### 106 — development economics [optional], Economist

The item appears to be a field of applied inquiry, so concept type needs resolution rather than relabeling as a skill. The World Bank's open comparative paper, [“What Is Considered Development Economics?”](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/612241568129628879/pdf/What-is-Considered-Development-Economics-Commonalities-and-Differences-in-University-Courses-around-the-Developing-World.pdf), compares courses and supports heterogeneity, not a universal syllabus. Nigeria evidence: [NISER Economic Intelligence Report 2026](https://niser.gov.ng/v2/niser-economic-intelligence-report-2026/) describes macro, meso, micro and valuation analysis; it is one institutional example, not national occupational breadth or independent validation of recommendations. Output hypothesis: policy/evaluation note stating question, data, method, distributional result and caveats. Prerequisites: economic foundations, empirical methods, measurement and context. Complements: forecasting, government policy, demography and data quality. Development studies overlaps but is not equivalent; descriptive monitoring is not causal impact evaluation. Exact concept and role-task evidence and hands-on free learning remain open.

### 107 — digital communication and collaboration [optional], Communication Researcher

[DigComp 3.0](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/scientific-activities/key-competences-lifelong-learning/digital-competence-framework-digcomp/digcomp-30_en), fifth edition (2025), Area 2, especially competencies 2.1–2.5, defines appropriate digital interaction, responsible sharing and collaborative co-creation, with proficiency levels and access prerequisites. It supports the competence semantics but is European, not a Nigerian job requirement. [NUC Communication and Media Studies CCMAS](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Communication-and-Media-Studies-CCMAS-2023-FINAL.pdf) is Nigerian curriculum context; the exact relevant course outcome must be located before claiming this task. A plausible observable artifact is a versioned collaborative brief/data note with attribution, source trail and access controls; role linkage remains unproven. Prerequisites: literacy/access, channel selection, privacy/copyright and verification. Complements: communication, information sourcing, data ethics. Asynchronous or in-person work may substitute under access/security constraints; platform use alone does not demonstrate collaboration. Framework plus UNESCO MIL catalogue are candidate learning resources; a verified practical module is still needed.

### 108 — digital data processing [optional], Geographer

OpenLearn's free [Data and processes in computing](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/digital-computing/data-and-processes-computing/content-section-0?trk=public_profile_see-credential) course overview and §§1, 4 explain data forms and simple transformations with explicit input/output; the course page states a free participation statement. It uses a supermarket-till example, not spatial data. DigComp 3.0, competencies 1.2–1.3, offers broader data management context. A plausible output is a documented transformation into a clean/map-ready table with source, rules and checks, but this is not proof of the Geographer task. Nigerian curriculum context from NUC CCMAS is broad, not task evidence. Prerequisites: data formats, tabular literacy, reproducible transformation and coordinate awareness if spatial. Complements: GIS, inspect data, quality assessment. Manual processing is only a constrained fallback; GIS analysis requires more than the introductory computing course. Exact vocabulary and role/Nigeria task evidence and GIS-specific practice resource unresolved.

### 111 — economic anthropology [essential], Anthropologist

The open [AAA Perspectives “Economics” chapter](https://perspectives.americananthro.org/Chapters/Economics.pdf), p. 6 onward, treats livelihoods, production, exchange and consumption as culturally embedded, distinguishing this lens from a narrow market-only account. This is a global explanatory primer, not Nigerian evidence. No Nigeria-specific economic-anthropology study was adequately verified in this batch, so no national-context claim is made. Task hypothesis: ethnographic interpretation of economic life, with an output that separates participant meanings, observed practice and analyst inference. Prerequisites: ethnographic method, economic concepts, local history/language and reflexivity. Complements: qualitative research and interviews, demography. Development economics/economic sociology overlap but are not substitutes when lived meanings are the object. Exact ESCO type/URI, role task and Nigeria case/learning exercise remain open.

### 112 — editorial standards [optional], Communication Researcher

The [Nigerian Union of Journalists Code of Ethics](https://nuj.org.ng/code-of-ethics-for-nigerian-journalists/) offers Nigeria-specific professional context on accuracy, fairness and public interest, but is an association code—not automatically binding on all communication researchers or editors. Reuters [Journalistic Standards](https://www.reuters.com/standards/) is an independent explanatory comparison, but reflects one newsroom and is not Nigerian law. Task hypothesis: standards-based review producing a corrected copy or documented editorial audit; task linkage to this role is unproved. Prerequisites: audience/genre, fact-checking, attribution, copyright and workflow. Complements: journalist ethics, copyright, communication. Institutional policy may operationalize standards but is not necessarily equivalent to a professional code. Exact source concept, role task, code authority/current scope and verified free practical course remain unresolved.
