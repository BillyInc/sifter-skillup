Access date: 2026-10-09. This checkpoint is item-level source research, not role-fit adjudication, legal advice, or approval. See `checkpoint-005.json` for the full source registry, separate global/Nigeria claims, prerequisites, complements, alternatives, limits and unresolved gates.

### 113 — election law [optional], Political Scientist

The operative Nigerian primary source is the [Electoral Act 2026](https://nass.gov.ng/documents/download/11248), Act No. 1, from the National Assembly. It repeals the 2022 Act. For accessible contextual learning, UNESCO's [Media and Elections](https://www.unesco.org/en/freedom-expression-rule-law/media-elections?hub=370) page lists a free course, “Information and Elections in the Digital Era,” but that is information integrity—not comprehensive statutory analysis. PLAC's [2022 Electoral Act app](https://electoralact2022.placbillstrack.org/guide) is a strong plain-language explainer, but now historical and must not be taught as current law. Task hypothesis: a cited legal-policy brief that names jurisdiction, effective date, provision, and electoral consequence. Prerequisites: public/constitutional law, statutory interpretation and electoral institutions. Complements: policy, governance, foreign affairs. Comparative systems/election administration do not substitute for local legal interpretation. Exact ESCO identity, role task, current provision-to-task mapping and free legal-analysis module remain open.

### 114 — environmental legislation [optional], Geographer

Nigeria's [Environmental Impact Assessment Act](https://nesrea.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/EIA-ACT.pdf), including §61 definitions and assessment provisions, is primary federal context; [NESREA's law/regulation inventory](https://nesrea.gov.ng/laws-regulations/) lists federal rules. It is not a complete state/local/international inventory. Global law remains jurisdiction-specific rather than one universal code. A plausible geography-linked output is a dated, jurisdiction-scoped map/note of project constraints and permit questions—not legal advice. Prerequisites: law hierarchy, environmental effects and spatial context. Complements: GIS, policy implementation and geology. Environmental policy/standards are not binding legislation; an EIA is not the entirety of environmental law. OpenLearn Environmental Management is a candidate free introduction, but exact active module/task locator is unverified. Role task, precise ESCO identity and a state-level example remain unresolved.

### 115 — ethical code of conduct of journalists [essential], Media Researcher

The [NUJ Code of Ethics](https://nuj.org.ng/code-of-ethics-for-nigerian-journalists/) provides Nigeria-specific professional text: preamble and principles 1–6 cover independence, accuracy/fairness, privacy, confidentiality and discrimination. [PLAC's Nigerian Press Council Act archive](https://lawsofnigeria.placng.org/view2.php?sn=384), §9(3)–(4), refers to approval/publication of a professional code. Verify the Act's current consolidated text, code revision and legal effect before asserting enforceability. UNESCO's [media/elections context](https://www.unesco.org/en/freedom-expression-rule-law/media-elections?hub=370) is global but not a universal code. Candidate task output: a documented review of media/research artifact against principles with evidence and correction. A Media Researcher is not necessarily a journalist, so task link is unresolved. Complements: editorial standards, ethics, copyright. NUJ code is primary reading, not practical training; exact ESCO and free course remain open.

### 116 — ethics [essential], Philosopher

“Ethics” is a broad philosophical field, not a single rule set. The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's Virtue Ethics entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/) is a free, clear account of one normative tradition; it does not represent all ethics or a Nigerian consensus. A bounded output hypothesis is a reasoned analysis that states a normative question, principles/reasons, counterarguments and distinction between factual and value claims. Prerequisites: argument analysis and familiarity with multiple traditions. Complement: history of philosophy. Moral/applied/normative ethics overlap but differ in scope; no school settles all cases mechanically. No Nigerian philosophical source or exact Philosopher task has been established, nor a free practice course. Exact vocabulary identity unresolved.

### 117 — ethics [optional], Behavioural Scientist

Preserve this as a distinct optional occurrence from key 116. For D078, ethics can govern research on social/group/institution/community behaviour. The [BPS Code of Human Research Ethics (2021)](https://explore.bps.org.uk/content/report-guideline/bpsrep.2021.inf180) §§2, 4 and 5 specify autonomy/dignity, integrity, harm minimization, consent and confidentiality. This is UK professional guidance, not Nigerian law. No Nigeria-specific REC rule or assigned task was verified; local institutional review requirements must be checked for the actual study. A reasoned ethics-review record is a plausible output. Complements: data ethics, psychological research and qualitative research. Institutional policy/REC approval is not replaced by general moral reasoning. Hard boundary: social behaviour only; no D064 clinical/counselling/diagnostic/treatment practice. Exact ESCO, role task and locally applicable evidence remain open.

### 119 — excavation techniques [essential], Archaeologist

The [UNESCO excavation principles](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-international-principles-applicable-archaeological-excavations) are international normative guidance. The [2001 Convention Annex](https://www.unesco.org/en/underwater-heritage/annex), 36 Rules, addresses project design, qualifications, documentation and conservation, but specifically underwater heritage. Nigeria context: [NCMM Act scan](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/nig67463.pdf), §§19–20, addresses permits and notice of discoveries; confirm current consolidated official law and NCMM permit process. A field output would include contextual/provenience records, plans/maps, finds register and report. Excavation is destructive; non-invasive survey can precede or sometimes substitute, but not yield equivalent evidence. Prerequisites: stratigraphy, recording, safety, conservation and heritage permissions. Complements: conservation, history, mapping. No free terrestrial hands-on learning module was verified; exact ESCO and task/site method remain open.

### 120 — execute analytical mathematical calculations [essential], Demographer

This phrasing is an action competence, but exact ESCO record is unresolved. A defensible output is a reproducible calculation with formula, units, denominator, data period, check and interpretation (e.g. a defined demographic rate); software output without assumptions is not enough. NBS demographic tables can provide Nigerian examples but do not define the calculation or prove a role requirement. OpenStax [Introductory Statistics](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics/pages/1-introduction) is a free structured learning resource; an exact demographic-rate chapter still needs selection. Prerequisites: numeracy/algebra, rates, units and assumptions. Complements: demography, data inspection/quality. Manual work can cross-check software; neither replaces validating denominators and population estimates. Role task, Nigeria standard and exact module remain unresolved.

### 121 — experimental psychology [optional], Behavioural Scientist

Treat as a research method/field, not clinical practice. A possible D078 task is an ethically reviewed experiment on social behaviour, with operational definitions, comparison condition and transparent results/limitations; role evidence is still absent. The [BPS Human Research Ethics Code](https://explore.bps.org.uk/content/report-guideline/bpsrep.2021.inf180), §§2, 4, 5, supports participant-protection requirements, but is UK guidance, not an experimental-method text or Nigerian REC rule. Prerequisites: measurement, design, statistics/bias and consent. Complements: conduct psychological research, ethics and data quality. Surveys/observations/qualitative approaches answer different questions; experiments do not guarantee external validity. Strictly bounded to social/group/institution/community behaviour—exclude D064 clinical, counselling, diagnosis, assessment and treatment. Exact concept, Nigerian review context and free social-behaviour methods course unresolved.

### 122 — family law [optional], Economic Adviser

Nigeria-specific introductory context: [NOU LAW343 Family Law I](https://nou.edu.ng/coursewarecontent/LAW%20343%20Family%20law%20I.pdf) discusses the Matrimonial Causes Act and customary law; [Action4Justice's MCA guide](https://nigeria.action4justice.org/resource-bank/the-matrimonial-causes-act/) explains statutory marriage matters. Both need checking against current primary statute/case law and do not exhaust religious/customary regimes. Global evidence is that family law is jurisdiction-dependent, not governed by a universal code. A possible output, only if task evidence supports it, is a jurisdiction-specific policy note on family-law effects with legal review; not legal advice. Prerequisites: legal source/jurisdiction literacy and policy framing. Complements: civil law, government policy and ethics. Social policy analysis may overlap but cannot substitute for legal analysis. Exact ESCO identity, Economic Adviser task, updated primary-source set and task-specific learning remain open.
