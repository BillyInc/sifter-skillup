### 011 — `religious studies` / optional (3 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Knowledge/disciplinary study, not belief, religious practice, or counselling. ESCO exact skill-pillar URI/type not resolved. NERDC distinguishes religious education curricula and specific subjects; that distinction cautions against treating a generic label as one homogeneous topic.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** Open University OpenLearn explains the “lived religion” approach: study beliefs/practices in situated social life and avoid assuming all adherents share one position ([Why not ‘World Religions’?, section 3](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/why-not-world-religions/content-section-3), full section). This supports a method of contextual study, not any D078 role requirement. Independent scholarly education resource: [Religious diversity: rethinking religion](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/religious-studies/religious-diversity-rethinking-religion/altformat-printable), introductory materials on religious classification and diversity.
- **Nigeria context:** Nigeria's NERDC curriculum includes Christian Religious Studies and Islamic Studies as named school subjects ([NERDC curriculum foreword](https://www.nerdc.gov.ng/content_manager/primary/pri1-3_islamicf.pdf), foreword and curriculum placement); NUC Arts CCMAS includes a course in social anthropology of religion and explicitly distinguishes religious methods from social anthropology ([Arts CCMAS](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Arts-CCMAS-2023-FINAL.pdf), REL 104, printed p. 575). These are curriculum facts, not evidence of a general D078 role requirement or a single Nigerian religious experience.
- **Task/output rationale:** When a social-science question concerns institutions, practices, identities or public life, compare situated accounts and documentary evidence without adjudicating theological truth. Output: a context-rich analytic note that identifies whose practices/claims, setting, period and evidence are represented. Religious studies is distinct from theology, sociology of religion, anthropology and religious leadership.
- **Prerequisites, complements, alternatives, limits:** Contextual literacy, source/participant ethics, and careful distinction between description and normative judgment; interviews, observation, text analysis and history can complement, depending on the question. A secular or non-religious analysis is an alternative when religion is not relevant. Never generalise one faith, sect or region to Nigeria; no clinical or counselling practice is included.
- **Free learning resource:** OpenLearn, “Religious diversity: rethinking religion,” direct course sections on how religion is classified and studied in varied contexts; the free course may require sign-in for some activities ([course](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/religious-studies/religious-diversity-rethinking-religion/altformat-printable)). Introductory, UK-authored framing; use Nigerian sources for local cases. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity/type/URI; which listed role task, if any, needs this knowledge; Nigeria-focused free research-method resource.

### 012 — `statistics` / optional (3 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Could mean foundational quantitative knowledge or a toolkit of methods; exact ESCO concept/type/URI for this role-list label not confirmed. ESCO separates knowledge from skill/competence concepts and publishes descriptions/URIs ([ESCO skills pillar](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/classification/skill-main), taxonomy structure; current v1.2.1).
- **Global evidence/explanation:** The American Statistical Association's GAISE college report frames statistics learning around statistical thinking, data/context, variability, distributions, inference and communication ([ASA GAISE College Report](https://www.amstat.org/education/guidelines-for-assessment-and-instruction-in-statistics-education-(gaise)-reports), recommendations). This is education guidance, not proof every role performs formal statistical analysis. OpenIntro provides an independent, free introductory text with data, probability, inference and regression chapters ([OpenIntro Statistics](https://www.openintro.org/book/stat/), book and chapter index).
- **Nigeria context:** NBS publishes official Nigerian statistical data and metadata, including GDP and labour-force series ([NBS GDP catalogue](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/147), scope/reference reports; [NBS NLFS methodology](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/download/1241365), official production/method description). These provide local materials for statistical exercises and make methods/source quality concrete; they do not establish a role credential or guarantee datasets are suitable for every analysis.
- **Task/output rationale:** In a task using numeric observations, statistics helps describe patterns and uncertainty and evaluate whether an inference is supported. Output: a reproducible table/plot and a short interpretation that states population, variable definitions, method and uncertainty. Three optional occurrences do not establish a shared foundation.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Numeracy, data definitions, study design and measurement; coding/software may support computation but does not replace statistical reasoning. Descriptive statistics, estimation, hypothesis tests and models are different tools, not an automatic bundle. Statistics alone does not establish causation, representativeness, data quality or policy effect.
- **Free learning resource:** OpenIntro Statistics, chapters 1–2 for data, study design and exploratory analysis, then inference only when needed ([web textbook](https://www.openintro.org/book/stat/), chapter index and free access; license page confirms most textbooks are CC BY-SA 3.0: [license](https://www.openintro.org/license/)). Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept/type/URI; role-specific task evidence; identify whether source label means statistics knowledge, analysis method, or professional statistical practice; local introductory statistics resource.

### 014 — `anthropology` / optional (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Disciplinary knowledge. ESCO exact knowledge concept URI/type is not resolved. American Anthropological Association describes anthropology as a broad study of human experience, with cultural, archaeological, biological and linguistic branches ([AAA: What is Anthropology?](https://americananthro.org/practice-teach/what-is-anthropology/), scope and branch explanation); this US association's four-field model is not imposed as a universal taxonomy.
- **Independent/global task evidence:** O*NET's Anthropologists and Archeologists profile lists observation, interviews, document review, material evidence, research and reporting among tasks ([O*NET 19-3091.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3091.00), occupation description, tasks and detailed work activities). The profile combines anthropology and archaeology and includes biological/forensic activities, so only its social/cultural work examples are relevant here. It does not establish role mapping for the queued behavioural/communication-scientist labels.
- **Nigeria context:** University of Ibadan's official course listing includes cultural anthropology, Nigeria cultures/societies, ethnography, communication in societies and fieldwork ([UI Departmental Courses](https://sci.ui.edu.ng/archcourse), undergraduate course table). This shows one Nigerian programme's disciplinary/fieldwork context, not national curriculum coverage or a D078 job requirement.
- **Task/output rationale:** When a task asks how people organise social life, make meaning or vary culturally, use context-sensitive evidence and report the group's own categories and the researcher's interpretive limits. Output: a situated ethnographic/comparative account or documented analysis. Anthropology is not identical to sociology, biological science, archaeology or cultural stereotyping.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Question/context, field or document method, positionality, consent and community protocols; interviews, observation, material/document analysis may complement one another. Choose methods conditionally. Do not generalise from one group, treat cultural claims as timeless, or include forensic/biological/clinical practice without its own scope. No universal anthropology certification established.
- **Free learning resource:** AAA's open textbook *Perspectives: An Open Introduction to Cultural Anthropology*, opening chapter “Introduction to Anthropology,” scope/subfields and culture-as-context discussion ([PDF](https://perspectives.americananthro.org/second-edition/Chapters/Introduction_to_Anthropology.pdf)). Introductory, US-authored, free; its examples are not Nigerian evidence. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** ESCO URI/type; task-specific role evidence; Nigeria-authored free resource; distinguish the two occurrences' intended knowledge from a method or occupation label.

### 020 — `company policies` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** The label refers to knowledge of an organisation's internal policies, not a general policy-analysis method or one universal policy set. Exact ESCO concept/type/URI unverified. Since policies vary by employer, sector and jurisdiction, no stable “company policies” curriculum can be inferred from the label.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** ISO 37301 describes organisational compliance-management systems as processes for establishing, developing, implementing, evaluating, maintaining and improving compliance obligations ([ISO 37301 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html), scope). This is a management-system standard, not a definition of every company policy and not proof the listed roles own compliance. OpenStax explains policies as organisational rules within planning/control examples ([Principles of Management §17.1](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/17-1-is-planning-important), policies/planning example), but the example is not a cross-industry rule.
- **Nigeria context:** Nigerian SEC states that listed/public companies must follow the Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance and SEC guidance, including specified governance policies ([SEC Codes of Conduct](https://sec.gov.ng/our-mandate/regulation/codes-of-conduct/), public-company scope; [SEC Guidelines](https://www.sec.gov.ng/our-mandate/regulation/guidelines/), applicability). This is a bounded regulated-company context; it must not be generalized to all Nigerian companies or to the queued roles. Current statutes and employer policies need case-specific checking.
- **Task/output rationale:** For a task that interprets or evaluates an organisation's own rules, identify the actual policy version, owner, applicability and decision affected; output: a scoped compliance/implications note with source clauses and exceptions. This is conditional contextual knowledge, not assumed universal D078 competence.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Identify employer, sector, date and jurisdiction; policy reading complements legal/regulatory research and operational analysis. Internal policies, codes and law are different authority levels. Do not give legal advice, assume a policy is lawful/current, or infer employee/board authority. No credential or eligibility claim.
- **Free learning resource:** ISO's public explainer gives the compliance-management-system scope and terminology ([ISO 37301 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html)); it is free explanatory reading but not the paid standard or a practical company-policy course. Accessed 2026-10-09. Keep non-displayable until a direct task-specific policy-reading lesson is verified.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity/type; source task context in the two role profiles; current Nigerian policy/legal examples beyond public-company governance; verified free instructional resource.
