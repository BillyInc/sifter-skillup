### 021 — `consider economic criteria in decision making` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Action/competency phrase; exact ESCO type and URI not verified. It likely concerns applying economic evidence to choices, but it is not itself a named method or credential.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** World Bank's cost-benefit analysis introduction describes CBA as comparing economic costs and benefits to inform development-project/programme choices and discusses limitations ([World Bank CBA introduction](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/447571492971511594), purpose and method). The EPA's 2025 analytical guidelines explain baseline, costs, benefits, distribution and uncertainty in policy appraisal ([EPA Guidelines, 3rd ed.](https://www.epa.gov/environmental-economics/guidelines-preparing-economic-analyses-3rd-edition), chapters 3, 5, 7–11). These do not establish which economic criteria the role source means.
- **Nigeria context:** Nigeria Agenda 2050 identifies national priorities and trade-offs across economic, social, infrastructure and environmental domains ([FMBEP Nigeria Agenda 2050](https://www.nationalplanning.gov.ng/), plan parts/objectives). This establishes a local policy-planning context, not a rule for these roles or a single decision metric.
- **Task/output rationale:** When a role compares options with economic consequences, state the decision, affected groups, time horizon and evidence; output: an options table or appraisal note with costs/benefits, assumptions, distributional effects and non-monetised impacts. “Economic criteria” does not mean price or GDP alone.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Defined objective/options, relevant economic concepts and comparable data; cost-benefit, cost-effectiveness, distributional and qualitative criteria can complement or serve different questions. No one criterion settles ethical/legal/political trade-offs. No implementation authority or credential follows from the phrase.
- **Free learning resource:** World Bank CBA introduction, sections on CBA features, uses and limitations ([document page](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/447571492971511594)); free public document and directly teaches appraisal reasoning. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO identity/type/URI; role-level task wording and intended criteria; Nigeria-specific appraisal lesson and current method authority.

### 022 — `develop economic policies` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Policy-design activity/competency; no exact ESCO skill URI/type confirmed. Distinguish policy development from economic research, advice, political authorization and implementation.
- **Global authoritative evidence:** OECD's framework says sound policy begins by defining a problem, checking evidence and jurisdiction, engaging stakeholders, assessing and drafting options, and planning implementation/monitoring ([OECD Policy Framework, “Toward sound problem identification, policy formulation and design”](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/policy-framework-on-sound-public-governance_c03e01b3-en/full-report/component-9.html), problem-identification and formulation sections). OECD's public-policy overview emphasises evidence-informed evaluation and implementation ([OECD public policymaking](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/public-policymaking.html), systemic approach).
- **Nigeria context:** Federal Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning describes its responsibility to advise on national priorities and formulate/coordinate long-, medium- and short-term plans ([FMBEP](https://www.nationalplanning.gov.ng/), ministry functions and Nigeria Agenda 2050). This supports an institutional Nigerian policy-development setting; it does not establish that all D078 roles have that mandate.
- **Task/output rationale:** Conditional task when a role develops or assesses economic policy options: translate a defined problem into objectives, evidence, alternative instruments, distributional effects and implementable recommendations. Output: a policy-options brief/draft with assumptions, affected groups, responsibilities and monitoring indicators.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Economic theory/data, legal mandate, political/stakeholder context and implementation feasibility; policy analysis and consultation complement drafting. Advice is distinct from elected/authorized approval and agencies' execution. No role or policy impact is inferred from the label.
- **Free learning resource:** OECD Policy Framework section above, especially problem identification/formulation/design, provides a structured free reading exercise; learner output can be a one-page options matrix. Accessed 2026-10-09. It is general governance guidance, not a Nigeria-specific policy-writing course.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity; exact policy type/sector and role owner; locally grounded economic-policy drafting lesson; no evidence yet for a professional credential.

### 023 — `develop organisational policies` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Organisation-level policy-design competency; exact ESCO URI/type not resolved. It is distinct from `company policies` (knowledge of existing rules, index 020) and from government economic-policy development (index 022).
- **Global evidence/explanation:** OECD's policy-formulation framework describes defining a problem, selecting instruments, allocating resources, planning enforcement/implementation and designing monitoring/evaluation ([OECD framework](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/policy-framework-on-sound-public-governance_c03e01b3-en/full-report/component-9.html), policy formulation/design). ISO 37301's public overview describes establishing and improving organisational compliance systems ([ISO 37301 overview](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html), scope); this is a standard outline, not a mandate for every organisation.
- **Nigeria context:** SEC describes the Nigerian Code of Corporate Governance and its mandatory public-company scope, with recommended broader adoption ([SEC Codes of Conduct](https://sec.gov.ng/our-mandate/regulation/codes-of-conduct/), Corporate Governance for Public Companies). This is one regulated Nigerian context, not proof that an economic adviser or tax policy analyst writes company policies.
- **Task/output rationale:** Where an authorised organisational task involves policy design, identify the risk/problem, accountable owner, applicable law, affected staff, operational controls, consultation and review cycle. Output: a draft policy plus rationale, version/owner, implementation and review plan.
- **Prerequisites, complements, limits:** Organisational mandate, legal/sector knowledge, affected-user input and implementation capacity; compliance review and evaluation complement drafting. Policies are context- and authority-specific. A draft is not law or evidence of approval; do not imply legal advice or authority.
- **Free learning resource:** OECD policy design/formulation section ([OECD framework](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/policy-framework-on-sound-public-governance_c03e01b3-en/full-report/component-9.html)) teaches problem/options/implementation framing; pair with the relevant employer/regulator's current template. Free resource accessed 2026-10-09; generic government guidance, not company-policy drafting instruction.
- **Open gaps:** ESCO identity/type/URI; evidence tying specific role tasks to drafting; Nigeria-specific organisational-policy guide beyond public-company governance; free step-by-step policy drafting lesson.

### 024 — `environmental economics` / optional (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Disciplinary knowledge integrating economics and environmental/natural-resource questions; exact ESCO URI/type not confirmed. EPA defines environmental/natural-resource economics as applying economic principles to development and management of environmental/natural resources ([EPA Environmental Economics](https://www.epa.gov/environmental-economics), overview).
- **Global evidence/explanation:** EPA's current 2025 guidelines describe policy analysis of environmental benefits/costs, baselines, distribution, uncertainty and non-monetised impacts ([EPA Guidelines 3rd ed.](https://www.epa.gov/environmental-economics/guidelines-preparing-economic-analyses-3rd-edition), chapters 3, 5–11). This is a US regulatory framework; it illustrates a method, not a universal occupational requirement. The overview independently explains the discipline's scope.
- **Nigeria context:** NBS' pilot natural-capital accounts for Kaduna and Nasarawa states describe ecosystem accounts and how environmental depletion/benefits can inform economic decisions ([Nigeria Natural Capital Accounting pilot](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/ecosystem%20services%20accounting%20report%20for%20Nasarawa%20and%20Kaduna%20states.pdf), foreword and report scope). This is bounded pilot work, not nationwide coverage or a role standard. World Bank documents the 2025 Nigerian effort to institutionalise natural-capital accounting ([World Bank Nigeria NCA](https://www.worldbank.org/en/brief/2025/08/04/nigeria-moves-toward-institutionalizing-natural-capital-accounting), conference/agency account).
- **Task/output rationale:** Conditional when a question weighs environmental/resource effects alongside economic choices. Output: a comparison of baseline/options, affected communities, valued and unvalued effects, uncertainty and distribution; no environmental outcome should be collapsed into monetary value alone.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Economic concepts, environmental evidence and baseline; ecological assessment, valuation and policy analysis complement but are distinct disciplines. Monetary valuation is one tool, not intrinsic worth or the only decision criterion. No credential established.
- **Free learning resource:** EPA Guidelines Chapter 3 (policy options) and Chapter 5 (baseline/scope) are downloadable, free, peer-reviewed public guidance ([chapter downloads](https://www.epa.gov/environmental-economics/guidelines-preparing-economic-analyses-3rd-edition)); exact learner locator, not the full text: Chapters 3 and 5 headings. US-specific; use Nigerian accounts for local application. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** ESCO identity/type; role-task support and intended environmental question; Nigeria-focused free instruction beyond reference accounts; do not generalise two occurrences.

### 025 — `european integration` / optional (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Regional-history/institutional knowledge, not generic international relations or a Nigerian integration policy. Exact ESCO knowledge URI/type not verified.
- **Global evidence:** EU official chronology records the post-war integration steps, including the ECSC, EEC and development of EU institutions ([EU key dates](https://europa.eu/abc/12lessons/key_dates_en.htm), dates 1950–1967 onward). European Commission's House of European History provides free HistoriCall learning content and an integration-history video/module ([HistoriCall](https://historia.europa.eu/en/learn/historicall), “EU Pioneers: Meet the Founders” and six-minute integration milestones). These are EU institutional/historical sources and do not define a universal integration model.
- **Nigeria context:** ECOWAS states the Treaty was established in Lagos in 1975 and identifies regional trade facilitation activities involving Nigeria ([ECOWAS Nigeria country page](https://ecotis.ecowas.int/ecotis/page3?langid=1&pcid=67&pid=24), member-country entry and 2026 committee item). Nigeria's ECOWAS role is regional context, not European integration, and must not be substituted for the exact label. No Nigerian task requirement for European integration established.
- **Task/output rationale:** If a comparative/international task examines European institutions or integration, output a dated institutional/treaty chronology or bounded comparison that names participating states and scope. Two optional occurrences do not establish the concept as relevant to all role tasks.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** European post-war history, institutions and treaty chronology; comparative regionalism can complement but does not make EU and ECOWAS institutions equivalent. Distinguish integration from cooperation, enlargement and EU law. No legal/credential claim.
- **Free learning resource:** HistoriCall “EU Pioneers” module and its six-minute milestones video ([House of European History learning page](https://historia.europa.eu/en/learn/historicall)); free, secondary-school level, directly on European integration history, not an EU-law course. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept/type/URI; exact role-task use; EU source version/date for the chronology; no Nigeria-specific European integration learning resource or occupational requirement.

### 026 — `gather data` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Generic research action/skill; exact ESCO concept URI/type not confirmed. Data collection is a stage, not a complete method or guarantee of quality.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** United Nations Statistics Division's *Handbook of Surveys on Households and Individuals* covers survey design, collection, processing, analysis and dissemination under statistical principles ([UNSD Handbook, 2026 PDF](https://unstats.un.org/UNSDWebsite/statcom/session_57/documents/BG-3k-Handbook_on_Surveys_of_Household_and_Individuals-E.pdf), chapters on survey design/data collection/processing). O*NET's sociologist profile gives concrete sources—observation, interviews and document review—for social data collection ([O*NET sociologists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3041.00), tasks). These support multiple modes, not one required method.
- **Nigeria context:** NBS' Nigeria Labour Force Survey documentation states the survey's purpose and methodological updates ([NBS NLFS introduction](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/download/1241365), survey purpose/methodology); NBS microdata terms specify access, confidentiality and restricted-data conditions ([NBS data privacy](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/page/data-privacy-policy), licensed/enclave access). This establishes Nigerian official-statistics practice and access limits, not that every dataset is open or that the queued roles collect survey data.
- **Task/output rationale:** For a defined research question, identify needed observations, data source/instrument, sampling, permissions and quality controls. Output: a documented dataset/source log and collection record, with missingness and provenance noted.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Question, construct/measure, sampling frame, instrument, consent/legal basis and data-management plan; cleaning, analysis and interpretation follow collection. Existing data, interviews, observation, surveys and documents are alternatives or complements. Do not assume self-reported data are accurate, samples representative, or data collection ethically cleared.
- **Free learning resource:** UNSD Handbook, direct chapters on survey design and data collection/processing ([PDF](https://unstats.un.org/UNSDWebsite/statcom/session_57/documents/BG-3k-Handbook_on_Surveys_of_Household_and_Individuals-E.pdf)); free official methods reference. It is advanced and survey-focused, not a beginner exercise for all data forms. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO type/URI; role-specific collection task; accessible beginner lesson and local ethics/consent path; identify whether data are primary, secondary, administrative or relational.

### 027 — `international business` / optional (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Knowledge area involving cross-border business conditions, trade rules, markets and organisations; exact ESCO URI/type not verified. It is broader than international trade and narrower than all international relations.
- **Global evidence:** WTO explains its multilateral trading system, agreements and market-access topics; its e-learning programme offers free training on WTO rules and trade ([WTO e-Learning](https://www.learning.wto.org/?lang=en), topic catalogue and access; [WTO FAQ](https://www.learning.wto.org/local/faqs/), public self-study access). These cover trade-regulatory elements, not all international business management. Independent global explanation: OECD's trade topics place trade in wider economic policy and global value-chain context ([OECD trade](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/trade.html), overview).
- **Nigeria context:** Nigeria is represented in ECOWAS regional trade facilitation and the ECOWAS page records the Treaty signed in Lagos ([ECOWAS Nigeria page](https://ecotis.ecowas.int/ecotis/page3?langid=1&pcid=67&pid=24), country entry and 2026 reform meeting). This is one regional trade context; current customs, tax, exchange-control and product rules require separate Nigerian sources. It is not evidence that the two roles conduct cross-border business.
- **Task/output rationale:** If a task evaluates a firm's/sector's cross-border exposure, identify product/service, markets, value chain, rules, currency/logistics and risk. Output: a sourced market/regulatory comparison or scenario note. Do not assume every economist or policy officer works in international business.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Basic economics/business, jurisdiction-specific law and current trade data; international trade, foreign exchange, logistics and market analysis are complementary specialisms, not a mandatory package. WTO rules do not replace national law or firm-specific facts.
- **Free learning resource:** WTO e-Learning catalogue, self-study courses under “The Multilateral Trading System,” “Market Access,” and “Trade and Development”; page confirms free public learning access ([WTO course catalogue](https://www.learning.wto.org/course/index.php?categoryid=1), topic filters; FAQ above). Accessed 2026-10-09. Official, task-specific to trade dimensions, not full business management.
- **Open gaps:** ESCO identity/type/URI; exact role-task context; Nigerian trade compliance lesson; course enrolment and language/level check for a particular module.

### 028 — `maintain relations with local representatives` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Stakeholder/liaison activity, exact ESCO URI/type not resolved. “Relations” should mean transparent, role-relevant communication and coordination, not political influence or privileged access.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** IFC/World Bank's *Stakeholder Engagement: A Good Practice Handbook* says consultation is about initiating and sustaining constructive external relationships and details identification, engagement, feedback and disclosure ([handbook](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/579261468162552212/pdf/399160IFC1StakeholderEngagement01PUBLIC1.pdf), opening definition and process chapters). World Bank consultation guidance requires clear purpose, stakeholder accessibility, representation and feedback loops ([consultation guidelines PDF](https://pubdocs.worldbank.org/en/248301574182372360/World-Bank-consultations-guidelines.pdf), implementation components and stakeholder engagement).
- **Nigeria context:** Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Special Duties and Inter-Governmental Affairs says its department links federal, state and local governments and supports policy coordination and local-government administration ([IGA department](https://fmsdiga.gov.ng/iga/), listed functions). This supports a specific public-sector coordination context, not a mandate for an economist or private adviser to liaise with elected officials.
- **Task/output rationale:** When a defined policy/development task requires local-representative input, map affected parties and formal roles, explain purpose, record input and follow up transparently. Output: a contact/consultation log, issues summary and documented response path.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Identify authority, representation, language/access needs, consent and conflict-of-interest safeguards; stakeholder analysis and communication complement. Written consultation, public meeting and official correspondence are task-dependent methods. A contact list does not prove representation or consent; avoid patronage, promises, or conflating “local representative” with a specific office.
- **Free learning resource:** IFC/World Bank handbook, Chapters 2–3 on stakeholder identification/analysis and engagement planning ([PDF](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/579261468162552212/pdf/399160IFC1StakeholderEngagement01PUBLIC1.pdf)); free and instructional. It is project/investment guidance and should not be presented as universal public-service law. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept/type; identify the “local representative” and role task; Nigerian protocol/ethics source; task-specific beginner lesson for public officials.

### 029 — `maintain relationships with government agencies` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Inter-organisational coordination/liaison skill; exact ESCO URI/type unverified. Distinct from maintaining personal relationships, lobbying, regulatory compliance and government implementation.
- **Global evidence:** World Bank stakeholder-relations material identifies policy dialogue, consultation, information sharing and partnership as distinct engagement purposes and emphasises transparency/respect ([World Bank stakeholder relations](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/partners/stakeholder-relations), purposes/principles). OECD's open-government recommendation distinguishes information, consultation and engagement across the policy cycle ([OECD Recommendation on Open Government](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/359/359.en.pdf), policy-cycle and stakeholder participation definitions). These support process concepts, not D078-specific need.
- **Nigeria context:** The federal Inter-Governmental Affairs department explicitly serves as a link among federal, state and local governments and advises on policy coordination ([FMSDIGA IGA](https://fmsdiga.gov.ng/iga/), functions). It demonstrates an institutional Nigerian example, not universal authority or private-sector access.
- **Task/output rationale:** If coordination requires agency input/approval/data, identify the responsible agency and formal channel, exchange accurate information, record commitments and track issues. Output: a dated coordination record/action tracker tied to a legitimate project or policy objective.
- **Prerequisites, complements, boundaries:** Agency remit, project purpose, information-sharing/privacy rules and conflict controls; stakeholder mapping and clear documentation complement. Formal letters, consultation, data agreements and intergovernmental mechanisms are alternatives by need. Do not imply influence, approval, procurement access or authority from a relationship.
- **Free learning resource:** OECD Recommendation on Open Government, sections defining information/consultation/engagement and the policy cycle ([legal instrument PDF](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/359/359.en.pdf)); free authoritative guide. It is public-governance learning, not a relationship-management course. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO identity; role-level task link; Nigerian agency-by-agency protocols and current legal basis; specific free practical liaison lesson.

### 030 — `mathematical economics` / essential (2 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Disciplinary/method knowledge applying mathematical structures to economic concepts/models; exact ESCO knowledge URI/type not confirmed. Distinct from general mathematics, statistics, econometrics and quantitative analysis.
- **Global evidence/explanation:** MIT's undergraduate course catalog frames mathematical economics as formal economic analysis; MIT OCW provides free mathematical methods linked to finance/economic applications ([MIT OCW 18.642](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/), course purpose/topics). A mathematical economics course can use functions, derivatives, optimisation and matrices to express and solve models; the mathematical solution remains conditional on model assumptions.
- **Nigeria context:** National Open University of Nigeria's economics curriculum lists Mathematics for Economists and Advanced Mathematical Economics; the course guide covers derivatives, optimisation, multiple-variable functions, matrices and applications ([NOUN Economics courses](https://fss.nou.edu.ng/economics-courses/), curriculum table; [ECO 256 guide](https://www.nou.edu.ng/coursewarecontent/ECO%20256%20MATHEMATICS%20FOR%20ECONOMISTS%20II.pdf), course aims/content). This is a Nigerian programme example, not proof of a job-entry requirement for the two role records.
- **Task/output rationale:** When an economic question is formalised as a model, translate assumptions/relationships into equations, solve comparative or optimisation questions and interpret results economically. Output: a worked model with assumptions, solution, sensitivity/checks and plain-language meaning.
- **Prerequisites, complements, limits:** Algebra/functions and calculus as the selected model requires; economic theory and data/econometrics complement mathematical formalisation. Qualitative institutional analysis or descriptive evidence can be alternatives where a formal model is not useful. Mathematical elegance does not validate assumptions or prove empirical fit. No certification implied.
- **Free learning resource:** MIT OCW 18.642 lecture notes/videos/problem sets on linear algebra, probability, stochastic processes and numerical methods applied to finance ([course materials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-642-topics-in-mathematics-with-applications-in-finance-fall-2024/)); free, but finance-focused and advanced. For direct economics prerequisites, NOUN ECO 255/256 courseware is also free and locally contextual ([ECO 255](https://nou.edu.ng/coursewarecontent/ECO%20255%20MATHEMATICS%20FOR%20ECONOMISTS%20I-Copy.pdf)). Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO URI/type; role-task evidence and mathematical depth; choose a lesson at appropriate level; no universal mathematics threshold or job credential established.
