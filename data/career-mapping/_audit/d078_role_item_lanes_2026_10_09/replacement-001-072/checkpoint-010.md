### 001 — `history` / optional (5 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** ESCO skills-pillar query did not resolve a unique concept record for this bare label. Treat as disciplinary knowledge, not as a practical skill; URI and ESCO version-specific identity remain open. ESCO documents that its skills pillar distinguishes knowledge from skill/competence concepts and provides concept descriptions and URIs ([ESCO skills pillar](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/classification/skill-main), taxonomy/type and concept metadata, current v1.2.1; [ESCO API](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/use-esco/use-esco-services-api/esco-web-service-api), URI/version behavior). No credential is implied.
- **Global evidence and independent explanation:** National Archives (US) explains how diaries, letters, records and other primary sources are interpreted with attention to author perspective, contradictions and source reliability ([History in the Raw](https://www.archives.gov/education/research/history-in-the-raw.html), full explanation). This supports historical source work as one possible background for genealogy, anthropology and social inquiry; it does not define a universal D078 role requirement. The same archive's document-analysis sequence (meet, observe, interpret, use as evidence) independently explains a bounded research workflow ([Document Analysis](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/worksheets), four-step sequence).
- **Task/output rationale:** A task-appropriate use is contextualising a social institution, media record, community account or family lineage through dated, sourced records. Output: a short historical-context note or source-based chronology that distinguishes primary/secondary evidence and reports gaps and competing interpretations. This is an optional lens, not a finding from five occurrences.
- **Nigeria context:** Nigeria's National Archives describes holdings spanning political, economic, social and cultural history and records from colonial periods onward ([National Archives of Nigeria](https://nationalarchives.gov.ng/), holdings and research access); the Federal Ministry states the Archives' research and documentary-preservation remit ([Ministry page](https://fmino.gov.ng/departments/national-archives/), Corporate Functions and National Archives Act description). These establish local archival resources, not role requirements or comprehensive public access. Nigerian history must use Nigerian sources and not be substituted with US archive examples.
- **Prerequisites, complements, alternatives, limits:** Define a bounded question and period; source criticism, provenance and triangulation complement interpretation. Oral histories, archives, contemporaneous media and secondary scholarship can complement one another but are not interchangeable. Avoid presentism, treating an archive as complete, or projecting a current category onto the past. Does not establish the truth of a record or expertise in history.
- **Free learning resource:** US National Archives, “Analyze a Written Document,” novice worksheet, prompts on author/recipient/date, main idea, evidence and further corroboration; public-domain material ([resource](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/worksheets/analyze-a-written-document-novice)). This teaches primary-document analysis, not Nigerian history. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO URI/type; role-specific history task and checkpoint owner; Nigeria-focused free lesson; whether any occurrence means historical knowledge rather than an adjacent disciplinary topic.

### 002 — `financial forecasting` / optional (4 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** ESCO concept URI/type not uniquely verified for the exact phrase in this pass. Candidate is a knowledge/method family around forecasting financial/economic quantities; do not equate it with investment advice. ESCO v1.2.1 identifies its taxonomy version and concept URI scheme ([API/version documentation](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/use-esco/use-esco-services-api/esco-web-service-api)).
- **Authoritative global evidence:** IMF's Macroeconometric Forecasting (MFx) course defines a model-based route: time-series properties, dynamic models, evaluation, design, simulation and forecast uncertainty; stated outcomes include time-series forecasts and model evaluation ([IMF MFx](https://www.imf.org/en/capacity-development/training/icdtc/courses/mfx), course description/objectives/qualifications). The course requires undergraduate statistics and basic econometrics and uses EViews. IMF's Macroeconomic Diagnostics course places projections within a broader country diagnostic ([MDSx](https://www.imf.org/en/capacity-development/training/icdtc/courses/mdsx), course scope); this is an independent framing, not a generic occupational standard.
- **Nigeria context:** NBS publishes Nigeria GDP accounts/reports with quarterly reference periods and methodological material ([NBS GDP catalogue](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/147/related-materials), Q1/Q2 2026 reports and dataset purpose). This is a local data context for a forecast exercise, not evidence that the listed roles forecast Nigerian data or that a forecast is official. No nationwide D078 credential or job requirement established.
- **Task/output rationale:** Conditional task: use prior observations, a stated model and explicit assumptions to produce a forecast range/scenario for an economic or financial variable; deliver a forecast table/chart, validation summary and uncertainty note. The role labels and counts alone do not prove that every occurrence owns this task.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Data definitions and time coverage, statistics/econometrics, model diagnostics and a baseline are prerequisites; uncertainty and out-of-sample validation complement the forecast. Econometric time-series methods, expert judgement and scenario analysis are alternative/complementary approaches depending on horizon and evidence. Forecasts are conditional estimates, not facts, causal proof, guaranteed returns or personalised financial advice. IMF courses may have cohort eligibility/enrolment limits and are not assumed open at all times.
- **Free learning resource:** IMF MFx course page, objectives and course description on forecast construction/evaluation; official online-learning materials are described as free-access ([IMF Online Learning](https://www.imf.org/en/Capacity-Development/online-learning), access statement). MFx itself has scheduled offerings and technical prerequisites; availability for self-paced access is not confirmed, so keep as a learning candidate pending access check. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity/type/URI; role-by-role task linkage; whether these occurrences mean macroeconomic forecasting, business projections, or financial-market prediction; free self-paced lesson eligibility; Nigeria-specific forecasting standards.

### 003 — `interview techniques` / optional (4 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** The phrase is plural and broad; no single ESCO URI/type confirmed. Keep as a method/skill family, not a named credential. ESCO's type distinction and stable URI contract are documented in its official skills/API pages cited above.
- **Global evidence:** Open University OpenLearn's free course defines qualitative interviewing as a way to investigate views/experiences and teaches preparation, question asking, consent, distress response, fieldnotes, anonymisation and storage ([Conducting qualitative interviews](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/conducting-qualitative-interviews-introduction), course description, learning outcomes, sections 1–4). This is an instructional source. Its topic-specific research project supplies an independent explanation of participant interaction and the interviewer's role ([Methods in Motion: Acknowledging the active participant](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/course/view.php?id=13767), participant agency and social shaping of interview talk).
- **Nigeria context:** NUC's Social Sciences CCMAS identifies Research Method I/II and project work in its current official curriculum ([NUC Social Sciences CCMAS PDF](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Social-Sciences-CCMAS-FINAL-2023-A.pdf), course list and research-method sections; locator to confirm at page level before any curriculum claim). This is Nigerian programme context, not proof of a national job requirement or a specific interview method. No Nigerian professional interview credential established.
- **Task/output rationale:** When a question needs participants' accounts, prepare a topic guide, recruit appropriately, obtain ongoing consent, conduct and record an interview, then produce a securely handled transcript/fieldnote set and evidence-linked analysis. Interviewing can belong to sociological, media, anthropological or genealogical inquiry only when the actual question and access support it.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Clear research question, sampling/access plan, consent/ethics, language and recording/data-protection plan. Interview design complements analysis and reflexive fieldnotes; survey, observation, archival work or focus groups are alternatives selected for the question. Interview answers are situated accounts, not automatically representative or objectively true; interviewing is not counselling or clinical assessment.
- **Free learning resource:** OpenLearn course above, free Level 1 / approximately 3 hours; direct locator sections 1–4, especially 2 Preparing, 3 Conducting, and 4 After the interview. Course content can be started without signing in; account needed only for progress/activities ([course access details](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/conducting-qualitative-interviews-introduction)). Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept/type; role-specific justification for each occurrence; Nigerian interview-practice learning resource and institutional ethics route. Resource includes health/social-care examples but is used only for method mechanics; it does not support clinical psychology or treatment content.

### 004 — `social network analysis` / optional (4 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Method, not software or generic networking. Exact ESCO concept URI/type not verified. The method studies entities and relations as a network; no software is implied.
- **Global evidence and explanation:** US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration describes mapping relationships and informal communication networks and provides an introductory learning page/PDF ([NOAA Digital Coast: Introduction to Social Network Analysis](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/training/social-network.html), intro and downloadable training PDF). A UK Government guide presents a domain-specific application to intelligence data and gang networks ([SNA: How to guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/social-network-analysis-how-to-guide), intended use); it supports a bounded example, not all social research.
- **Nigeria context:** An open-access peer-reviewed study applies SNA to relationships between internally displaced people and host communities in a Maiduguri IDP camp ([Kamta & Scheffran, GeoJournal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10708-021-10500-8), abstract, study setting and methods). It demonstrates a Nigerian research use case only; its selected camp and study design cannot establish national network structure, causal effects or universal role practice.
- **Task/output rationale:** Conditional method when the question concerns ties among defined actors (e.g. information exchange, referrals, collaboration); collect bounded relational data, define nodes/ties, calculate/interpret network measures, and report a sociogram or network summary with missingness and boundary choices. Candidate output: an anonymised network map plus a method note.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Defined network boundary, unit of analysis, relation/tie definition, consent/privacy and suitable relational data; graph/statistical concepts complement interpretation. Interviews, ordinary survey analysis, qualitative relational accounts or process tracing may be alternatives. Network centrality is not automatically influence, importance or causality; missing ties can distort results. No software certification or general credential implied.
- **Free learning resource:** NOAA Digital Coast page, direct downloadable “Social Network Analysis” training PDF; exact lesson locator is the introduction and PDF instructional sequence for mapping nodes/links and reading network structure. Free public resource; accessed 2026-10-09. Confirm the PDF remains downloadable before display.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity; per-role tasks; Nigeria-specific free training; ethical data-governance conditions for any proposed Nigerian network study.

### 005 — `sociology` / optional (4 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Disciplinary knowledge, not a software skill or credential. Exact ESCO knowledge URI not verified. UNESCO defines social sciences as systematic study of human society and relationships and names sociology among the constituent disciplines ([UNESCO definition](https://www.unesco.org/uk/query-list/s/419467), full entry). ESCO's taxonomy distinguishes knowledge from skills; taxonomy version v1.2.1 ([ESCO skills pillar](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/classification/skill-main)).
- **Independent/global evidence:** American Sociological Association's undergraduate learning outcomes describe sociological theory, social structure, research methods, evidence, ethics and communication; this is education guidance, not an occupational licence ([ASA learning outcomes](https://www.asanet.org/academic-professional-resources/teaching-learning/teaching-resources/learning-outcomes/), outcomes). It helps explain the discipline's analytical scope beyond everyday opinion.
- **Nigeria context:** NUC's official Social Sciences CCMAS includes Nigerian university Sociology programmes and research-method courses ([NUC CCMAS](https://www.nuc.edu.ng/ccmas/), Social Sciences package; direct PDF cited in item 003). This is curriculum context for Nigerian universities, not a role mandate. Nigerian cases must be researched locally; the global definitions do not establish facts about Nigerian society.
- **Task/output rationale:** For roles where sociology is relevant, apply concepts about institutions, groups, inequality or social change to a bounded question and evidence; output could be a literature-grounded social analysis or research report. The four optional occurrences only identify candidate records, not common role tasks.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Concepts and theory plus source appraisal; research design and methods complement disciplinary knowledge. Sociology overlaps with anthropology, political science, economics and social psychology but is not identical to any of them. It does not qualify someone for social work practice, counselling, psychotherapy, diagnosis or licensure (D064 boundary preserved).
- **Free learning resource:** OpenStax, *Introduction to Sociology 3e*, free textbook; use Chapters 1–2 for sociological perspective and research methods, and the relevant topic chapter for the selected question ([OpenStax book](https://openstax.org/details/books/introduction-sociology-3e), contents and chapters). Accessed 2026-10-09. Introductory teaching resource, not professional qualification.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO concept identity/URI; identify which role-task (if any) actually uses sociological disciplinary knowledge; Nigeria-specific free lesson using locally grounded cases.

- **Global explanation:** World Bank spatial/territorial planning material distinguishes spatial planning from broader territorial development and explains coordination of land use and sectoral decisions ([World Bank report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/782491468296462975/pdf/Full%E2%80%91report.pdf), working definition and distinction; exact printed page remains to verify). It supports the planning concept, not a social-science task requirement.

- **Task/output rationale:** Conditional when a role analyses population/place evidence for decisions about land, settlement, services or development. Output: a place-based options/map/report that identifies scale, land-use constraints, affected groups and evidence sources. - **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Human-geography/population concepts, current spatial data and scale; GIS/cartography may support but are not synonyms for planning. No general D078 certification implied.

This is a free reference, not yet verified as a guided beginner lesson; non-displayable pending lesson-fit review. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO “spatial planning” concept identity/type/URI; page locator in World Bank report; exact role tasks; free beginner resource with a clearly scoped social-science application.

### 008 — `multidisciplinary research` / optional (3 occurrences)

- **Identity/type:** Broad research approach/competency phrase; no unique ESCO concept URI/type identified. Distinguish multidisciplinary work (disciplines contribute in parallel) from interdisciplinary integration and transdisciplinary co-production; do not treat these labels as interchangeable.
- **Global evidence:** OECD's Frascati Manual defines R&D disciplines and classification, while UNESCO's transdisciplinary research guidance explains collaboration across disciplines and societal actors ([OECD Frascati Manual 2015](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/frascati-manual-2015_9789264239012-en.html), field classification and R&D definition; [UNESCO transdisciplinary research guidance](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000378006), collaboration/integration framework). These support collaboration concepts, not a universal researcher skill requirement. Independent explanation: National Academies' *Facilitating Interdisciplinary Research* describes integration and collaboration barriers ([National Academies report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/11153/facilitating-interdisciplinary-research), chapters 1–2).
- **Nigeria context:** NBS and Agricultural Research Council of Nigeria describe an institutional agreement to strengthen research-data collaboration ([NBS–ARCN announcement](https://nigerianstat.gov.ng/news/91), 22 June 2026). This is one Nigerian cross-institutional data initiative in agricultural science; it does not establish D078 tasks or nationwide practice. No D078-specific Nigerian requirement found.
- **Task/output rationale:** Relevant only where a question requires evidence or methods from multiple fields. A credible output states each discipline's contribution, how evidence is integrated, disagreements and who is accountable for synthesis. It does not mean assembling a team automatically improves validity.
- **Prerequisites/relationships/limits:** Clear shared question, disciplinary literacy, collaboration and integration plan; communication/data governance complement the research plan. Single-discipline study is a valid alternative when sufficient. Avoid using “multidisciplinary” as a badge, conflating parallel contributions with integrated analysis, or assuming cross-field agreement.
- **Free learning resource:** National Academies open report *Facilitating Interdisciplinary Research*, Chapters 1–2 on what interdisciplinary research means and why it is difficult ([full report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/11153/facilitating-interdisciplinary-research)). Free reading resource rather than skills course; learner output can be a one-page discipline-contribution matrix. Accessed 2026-10-09.
- **Open gaps:** Exact ESCO identity/type; the three role tasks; distinction intended by source label; direct free practical lesson and Nigeria-specific D078 example.
