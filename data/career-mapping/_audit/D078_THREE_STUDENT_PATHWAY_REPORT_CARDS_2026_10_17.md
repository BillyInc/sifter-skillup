# D078 Learning Path Report Cards: Three Student Examples

**Prepared:** 17 October 2026  
**Programme:** Sociology (B.Sc.) ? Social Sciences and Behavioural Research (D078) ? selected area ? optional role

> These are sample learning plans, not grades or completion records. No student progress data was supplied.

## How a student uses this path

The student learns **items**. Tasks are only used to explain where an item is useful; the student does not select a task to enter the path.

1. Start with the programme record as context.
2. Complete the three required shared D078 foundation items below.
3. After the foundations, see **domain learning items that give an edge in particular areas or roles**. Choose one or more that fit the student's interests. Each item card says what it teaches, where it is useful, and what item comes next in that strand. There is no single fourth item for everyone.
4. When the student selects an area, show the additional items owned by that area. Keep domain-owned items marked as domain learning, even when useful in that area.
5. If the student chooses a role, show its role-owned learning items as further useful choices.

The order shown in each example is a recommended learning sequence **for a student who chooses that strand**. It does not make the useful items compulsory. Methods and tools are only selected when the work calls for them.

## What appears after the foundations

The next section is a **menu of domain learning items**. Each is a choice. Here are three examples of the first item a student might choose, depending on the kind of edge they want:

| If the student wants to? | First domain item to consider | In plain language | What can come next |
|---|---|---|---|
| Understand or evaluate a programme | **Understand how a programme is meant to work and how to evaluate it** | Map how a programme's activities are expected to lead to results, then decide what evidence would show whether that happened. | In the Social Research and Evaluation area: describe the programme's expected change ? select evaluation questions, design and evidence ? report recommendations. |
| Work with surveys, evaluations or population data | **Sampling and knowing what evidence represents** | Check who or what the evidence covers and whether it represents the people, cases or records being discussed. | Continue to **Turning ideas into observable measures** if the question needs indicators; then see the relevant area item. |
| Measure social or population patterns | **Turning ideas into observable measures** | Convert an idea into something observable, such as an indicator, variable, survey question or coding rule. | Continue to the selected area's measurement item; pretest a survey measure when that is relevant. |

A student can choose one or more of these domain items. The ?useful for? labels explain their relevance; they are not task assignments or gates.

# Report Card 1 ? Programme Evaluation

**Student's goal:** Learn how to evaluate a social programme and explain what its evidence supports.  
**Selected route:** Sociology ? D078 ? **Social Research and Evaluation** ? optional role: **Social work researcher**

### Recommended item sequence for this goal

| Order | Learning item and owner | Lane | Why it fits | Next item if continuing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Understand how a programme is meant to work and how to evaluate it** ? domain | Useful; skippable | A theory of change is a simple map: programme activities are expected to produce immediate outputs, which are expected to lead to intended outcomes. An evaluation checks the evidence for those links and results. | Area item: **Describe how a programme is expected to produce change**. |
| 2 | **Describe how a programme is expected to produce change** ? Social Research and Evaluation area | Useful; skippable | Applies the idea to a programme the student wants to understand. | Area item: **Select evaluation questions, design and credible evidence**. |
| 3 | **Select evaluation questions, design and credible evidence** ? area | Useful; skippable | Connects the evaluation goal to a suitable design and evidence. | Add **Sampling and knowing what evidence represents** if people, cases or records are sampled. |
| 4 | **Sampling and knowing what evidence represents** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Helps explain who or what the evaluation evidence covers. | Add **Checking reliability, validity and uncertainty** if measures or estimates need checking. |
| 5 | **Checking reliability, validity and uncertainty** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Helps keep conclusions within what the evidence supports. | If the design uses quantitative analysis, choose one suitable workflow; otherwise go to reporting. |
| 6 | **Quantitative analysis workflow** ? area, when useful | Optional; skippable | Choose only if the design and data call for quantitative analysis. | If using software, choose one suitable tool (for example SPSS, R or Stata); then report. |
| 7 | **Turn findings into usable, transparent recommendations** ? area | Useful; skippable | Connects justified findings and their limitations to stakeholder use. | Finish with recommendations proportionate to the evidence. |

**Optional social-work-researcher items:** *Action research* can help when practitioners or affected people take part in inquiry and action. *Apply quality standards in social services* can help when findings inform service quality review. Both are useful, skippable role items; choose them only if they fit the intended role work.

**Possible work sample:** An evaluation plan/report with the programme logic, evaluation question, evidence, coverage, limits and recommendations.

# Report Card 2 ? Political-Opinion Survey

**Student's goal:** Learn to design or interpret a political-opinion survey and explain what its results can support.  
**Selected route:** Sociology ? D078 ? **Politics, Governance and Public Policy** ? optional role: **Political scientist**

### Recommended item sequence for this goal

| Order | Learning item and owner | Lane | Why it fits | Next item if continuing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sampling and knowing what evidence represents** ? domain | Useful; skippable | The survey must define who it represents and account for sampling and nonresponse. | **Turning ideas into observable measures**. |
| 2 | **Turning ideas into observable measures** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Helps translate political concepts into survey indicators and questions. | Area item: **Survey design, sampling and measurement**. |
| 3 | **Survey design, sampling and measurement** ? Politics area | Useful; skippable | Directly linked to political-opinion survey work. | Use **Piloting questions before fieldwork** if wording or response options need checking. |
| 4 | **Piloting questions before fieldwork** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Pretesting can reveal unclear wording before responses are collected. | Revise the survey, then choose a collection approach. |
| 5 | **KoboToolbox for field survey collection** ? area tool | Optional; skippable | Choose only if an electronic field form fits this survey. | If selected, configure/check the form and collect responses. Otherwise skip this tool. |
| 6 | **Checking reliability, validity and uncertainty** ? domain | Useful; skippable | After collection, assess measurement, coverage and nonresponse before interpreting results. | Choose one quantitative analysis workflow only if needed, then explain findings and limits. |

**Optional political-scientist items:** *Politics* adds context about institutions and behaviour. *Government policy* is useful when survey findings relate to a policy question. Both are skippable role items; add them when they help interpret or use the findings.

**Possible work sample:** A survey brief with its population, measures, wording, sample, pretest, nonresponse risks and limits.

# Report Card 3 ? Population Change

**Student's goal:** Learn to explain population change and compare estimates without overstating the evidence.  
**Selected route:** Sociology ? D078 ? **Human Geography and Population Studies** ? optional role: **Demographer**

### Recommended item sequence for this goal

| Order | Learning item and owner | Lane | Why it fits | Next item if continuing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sampling and knowing what evidence represents** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Helps check who or what each census, survey or administrative source covers. | **Turning ideas into observable measures**. |
| 2 | **Turning ideas into observable measures** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Clear definitions are needed before comparing population measures. | Area item: **Population measures and demographic change**. |
| 3 | **Population measures and demographic change** ? Human Geography area | Useful; skippable | Introduces fertility, mortality and migration concepts used in the selected interest. | Area item: **Appraise census, survey and administrative population data**. |
| 4 | **Appraise census, survey and administrative population data** ? area | Useful; skippable | Helps compare source coverage and assumptions before using the data. | Add **Checking reliability, validity and uncertainty** when comparing measures or estimates. |
| 5 | **Checking reliability, validity and uncertainty** ? domain | Useful; skippable | Helps assess comparability and uncertainty. | Area item: **Population estimation and projection with uncertainty**. |
| 6 | **Population estimation and projection with uncertainty** ? area | Useful; skippable | Applies the evidence and measures to estimates or projections. | Report the source, assumptions and limits. |
| 7 | **GIS/spatial analysis** ? area method/tool option | Optional; skippable | Choose only if the student wants to know how population varies by place. | If selected, map and interpret spatial patterns; skip it for a non-spatial population question. |

**Optional demographer items:** *Use data-processing techniques* helps when preparing population data. *Quantitative analysis* helps when the selected work requires it. *Spatial planning* helps when the work is place-based. These are useful, skippable role items; choose according to the intended work.

**Possible work sample:** A population-change note stating measures, sources, coverage, assumptions, uncertainty and limits. Add a map only for a spatial question.
