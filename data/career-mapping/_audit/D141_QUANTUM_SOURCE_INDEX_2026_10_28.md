## Domain learning context

Linear algebra/complex amplitudes, probability and statistics, introductory quantum mechanics and quantum information (states, gates, measurement, entanglement), computational thinking and programming; deeper device physics, control, metrology, optics, networking or QEC are specialization-dependent.

A learner should be able to formulate a quantum information/measurement problem; identify applicable classical baselines and physical/platform constraints; create or characterize a quantum system; test, calibrate, compile, simulate or measure it; report resource/performance/uncertainty limits; and explain whether a claimed use is established, emerging or roadmap-only.

### Distinctions retained

- Quantum computing, quantum sensing/metrology, and quantum networks are connected QIST branches but have distinct outputs and area choices.
- Quantum key distribution is quantum communication; post-quantum cryptography is classical cryptography designed to resist quantum attacks.
- QAOA/VQE, quantum machine learning, and vendor roadmap claims are not proof of general quantum advantage.
- Tools/frameworks implement workflows; they are not methods, algorithms, hardware modalities, or substitutes for foundations.

## Area evidence and relationships

### Quantum Computing Hardware and Devices

**Boundary:** Physical implementations, quantum devices, control/readout, fabrication/material platforms and cryogenic or photonic systems.

**Core claim:** Hardware is a co-designed physical system, not a qubit-count contest: platform physics sets control/readout, connectivity, environmental, fabrication and infrastructure constraints. Compare systems on task-relevant fidelity, logical error behavior, connectivity, cycle time, uptime, control burden, energy/cost and workload-level benchmark; no single platform is established as universally best.

**Prerequisites, alternatives, complements and downstream flow:** Quantum mechanics/device physics and measurement foundations support platform-specific device design; fabrication/materials, cryogenic or ultra-high-vacuum/laser systems and control electronics enable QPU operation. Calibration/control/readout produce characterized gate, measurement, coherence and noise data used by compilation, error correction and algorithm/resource estimation. Platforms are alternatives for a selected design objective, not interchangeable components; their controls/tooling are platform-specific. Classical host/HPC, control electronics, and software are complementary.

**Evidence limits:** A portfolio of roadmaps and selected demonstrations cannot rank modalities universally or establish commercialization. Hardware performance claims need dated, independently inspectable benchmarks and application-level classical baselines. Topological devices and large-scale fault tolerance remain research/roadmap horizons.

**Counter-search result:** The search confirmed the platform families and added QTEdu competence coverage, the IEEE 2026 architecture review, EuroHPC 2026 neutral-atom targets, and DLR's technology-neutral portfolio. No additional materially distinct current platform family was found within the bounded search scope. Roadmap/call milestones remain aspirational; topological and other emerging concepts remain maturity-qualified.

### Quantum Algorithms and Software

**Boundary:** Quantum algorithms, programming, compilation, error mitigation/correction and software tooling for quantum systems.

**Core claim:** Algorithm selection starts with a problem formulation and complexity/resource model, not a tool menu. Teach states, gates, measurement, circuit model and classical simulation/complexity foundations; then algorithm families (e.g., phase estimation, Grover/amplitude estimation, quantum simulation, hybrid variational methods) with workload-specific best classical baselines. Compile/transpile to target connectivity/ISA, estimate resources, execute/simulate, characterize noise, validate outputs and report uncertainty.

**Prerequisites, alternatives, complements and downstream flow:** Mathematics, quantum information and programming feed circuit/algorithm design. Problem structure and a classical baseline constrain whether a quantum algorithm is relevant. Circuit output is transpiled/compiled to the selected QPU; hardware topology/noise/calibration feed compilation and error-control choices. Error suppression and mitigation can improve estimates on noisy runs but do not correct arbitrary errors or guarantee unbiased outputs. QEC encodes logical information, repeated syndrome measurements feed a decoder, and real-time decoding/control supports fault-tolerant computation; resource costs are part of algorithm selection. Hybrid optimization has a classical optimizer/host that supplies parameters and consumes measured expectations. Q#, OpenQASM, Qiskit, Cirq, PennyLane, CUDA-Q and provider APIs are tools/languages/frameworks with different backends, not alternatives to learning algorithms themselves.

**Evidence limits:** No generalized practical quantum advantage follows from asymptotic query complexity or quantum-superposition wording. Current hardware remains noisy and task-limited; algorithm use, advantage, and scale need workload-specific independent baselines. QML and broad commercial optimization advantages are unestablished as general claims.

**Counter-search result:** Counter-search added/confirmed benchmark methodology and explicitly retained Grover's query-complexity-versus-resource caveat, VQA trainability/noise limits, compilation to hardware ISA, QEC as cross-layer dependency and hybrid classical workflows. It found no basis for treating QAOA/VQE or quantum ML as generally superior to classical methods. Candidate methods remain workload- and hardware-dependent.

### Quantum Communication and Networks

**Boundary:** Quantum information transfer, networking, protocols, quantum links and enabling network technologies.

**Core claim:** Quantum networking joins quantum endpoints over quantum channels plus classical signaling/control. QKD generates shared cryptographic keys under protocol/device/security assumptions; wider quantum networking also concerns entanglement distribution, networked computing, distributed sensing, memories, repeaters, transduction and routing. Network engineering must track fidelity, transmission rate/distance, synchronization, node count/connectivity and application requirements.

**Prerequisites, alternatives, complements and downstream flow:** Quantum optics/photon sources, detectors, memories and interfaces create/transfer/store photonic states; classical channels carry synchronization, signaling and control. For QKD, QKD modules feed generated keys to key managers; key management/relay supplies keys to cryptographic applications and controllers route/monitor QKDN links. QKD uses trusted-node/link constraints in current standardized architectures. Entanglement swapping, quantum memory and repeaters are prerequisites to extending some network applications, not required for every point-to-point QKD route. Post-quantum cryptography is a separate classical cryptographic route and can complement/serve as an alternative quantum-safe migration without a quantum channel; it is not a quantum network item.

**Evidence limits:** Standards describe architecture/protocol frameworks, not blanket security guarantees. QKD security depends on implementation, authentication, endpoint security, trusted nodes and network design. Long-distance repeaters, broad networked quantum computing and many applications remain developmental; roadmap forecasts are not deployed capability.

**Counter-search result:** The counter-search confirmed that QKD is only one network service/use case; quantum networks may distribute entanglement or connect computers/sensors. ITU/ETSI provide current QKDN architecture, interworking and key-management/control standards. NIST and QED-C document memories, sources, detectors, interfaces, repeaters and testbeds while emphasizing remaining range/performance/deployment limitations. No evidence supports an 'unhackable internet' or broad deployed quantum internet claim.

Roles appearing in existing area context: quantum cryptography engineer, quantum systems architect.

### Quantum Sensing and Metrology

**Boundary:** Quantum-enhanced measurement and sensors, precision metrology, timing and sensing applications.

**Core claim:** Quantum sensing is measurement engineering: define the measurand and operating environment; choose a physical sensor, measurement protocol and reference; quantify sensitivity, resolution, bandwidth, dynamic range, uncertainty, noise, calibration/traceability, drift, size/power and cost; compare with the classical baseline at the intended task. Modalities include atomic clocks, atom interferometers/gravimeters, atomic-vapor and NV magnetometry, Rydberg RF sensors, optical/photonic and other platform-specific sensors.

**Prerequisites, alternatives, complements and downstream flow:** Quantum-state/control/measurement foundations and metrology/statistics support modality-specific sensor design. The target measurand and conditions (field, temperature, vibration, size, distance, bandwidth) select among sensor alternatives; those are not interchangeable just because each is quantum. Optical/electronic/vacuum/cryogenic interfaces and classical signal processing/calibration are complementary. A sensor produces a traceable measurement/uncertainty or signal stream for navigation, timing, science, medical or industrial tasks; deployment claims need comparison against classical instruments and field conditions. Networked sensing can depend on communications/entanglement infrastructure, but an individual sensor route does not require a quantum network.

**Evidence limits:** Quantum sensing is relatively mature as a broad class, but readiness varies sharply by modality and use case; some atomic clocks and magnetic-resonance technologies are commercial while novel compact sensors may face cost, ruggedization, reliability, supply-chain and integration barriers. Quantum advantage is not automatic and must be demonstrated against classical sensor baselines under relevant operating conditions.

**Counter-search result:** Counter-search surfaced QTEdu's explicit metrology and classical-comparison competencies, and NIST's 2025 atom-based review of atomic-vapor, NV and Rydberg electromagnetic sensors. Combined with GAO's maturity/cost/reliability limits and NIST/NSF scope, no materially distinct established modality was found missing from the bounded matrix. Advanced imaging/radar claims require application-specific validation and are not promoted as mature capabilities.

Roles appearing in existing area context: quantum sensing specialist.
Existing supported area routes recorded: quantum sensing specialist. No routes were changed.
