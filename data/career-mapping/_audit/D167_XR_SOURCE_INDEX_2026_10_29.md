## Domain flow and distinctions

XR learners need digital/programming and 3D/spatial literacy appropriate to the chosen pathway, user-centered design, interactive-system concepts and an understanding of how runtime, tracking, display and input constrain an experience. The depth is pathway-specific; a content designer does not need the same hardware depth as a platform engineer.

Targeted searches found a Lagos XR developer vacancy (Insightful3d), the Imisi 3D XR lab, OAU animation-lab XR activity, and ABU's reported discussion of a proposed VR learning facility. These are concrete local activity examples, not a national labor-demand or degree/certification claim. No Nigeria-specific device-engineering or human-factors job evidence was identified in this pass.

- **experience/user requirements and content → XR application design and implementation (feeds):** Interaction flows, spatial scenarios, content assets and test criteria specify what the application must render and how users act; implementation and user tests return feasibility/usability evidence to design. [Evidence: D167-S18, D167-S25, D167-S26, D167-S32, D167-S33]
- **application/runtime integration → device and platform capabilities (constrained_by):** Runtime APIs expose supported sessions, views, input and device-specific features; application feature detection and compatibility tests must respect the actual runtime/platform. [Evidence: D167-S01, D167-S02, D167-S03, D167-S19, D167-S20]
- **tracking, anchors and scene understanding → spatial placement and interaction (feeds):** Pose/plane/anchor estimates feed content placement and spatial interaction, with environment quality, permission, tracking error and update latency as constraints. [Evidence: D167-S11, D167-S12, D167-S13]
- **server-rendered XR application → compatible client and network (requires_for_this_architecture):** CloudXR requires a compatible server runtime/application and client SDK pair; network latency, bandwidth and reliability become end-to-end experience constraints. This is an optional architecture, not a universal XR prerequisite. [Evidence: D167-S39]
- **human-factors/accessibility evaluation → design, software and device iteration (feeds):** Task performance, comfort, accessibility, sickness, privacy and adverse-event findings inform redesign and acceptance; evaluation instruments must match the construct and population. [Evidence: D167-S07, D167-S23, D167-S36, D167-S37, D167-S40, D167-S41, D167-S42]
- **3D asset interchange → runtime-specific rendering and behavior (does_not_guarantee):** USD and related scene formats support content composition/interchange; target runtime/renderers may not support every asset feature, interaction, material or extension. [Evidence: D167-S14, D167-S15, D167-S20]

## XR Experience, Interaction and Content Design

**Boundary:** Design XR-specific spatial interaction, user experience, content and scenarios. Include general animation, 3D art or media only when an immersive spatial experience is the intended output.

**Expected outputs:** A defined user/application need, spatial scenario and interaction flow; prototypes, immersive assets and evaluation criteria that are ready for implementation and user testing.

**Core claim and learning flow:** Experience work joins spatial interaction, accessible content and fit-for-purpose immersion. The design brief and user test criteria feed software implementation; feedback from usability, comfort and safety evaluation returns to the design.

**Limits:** Design recommendations are often platform-specific; a design standard or employer task example does not establish one universal interaction pattern or a role taxonomy.

**Counter-search:** The counter-search added active IEEE P3791 interaction-fluency work, XR-specific accessibility requirements, local university/lab and employer examples, and user-research tasks. It found no basis for collapsing experience design, content production and application implementation into one undifferentiated skill set. General 3D/animation work remains conditional on an XR deliverable.

## XR Software and Real-Time Application Development

**Boundary:** Build XR applications, interaction logic, rendering/runtime integration and application-specific services, including standalone, mobile and web-delivered experiences.

**Expected outputs:** An executable application, runtime/device integration, real-time rendering and interaction behavior, test evidence and a deployment package for named target platforms.

**Core claim and learning flow:** Application engineering spans an engine/application layer and a runtime/device interface. OpenXR and WebXR provide different deployment interfaces; engine, runtime, feature, performance and privacy constraints shape the resulting application.

**Limits:** Runtime/API support and engine features vary by device, extension, browser and version. Vendor materials describe their own stack; neither API conformance nor a successful build proves user benefit or performance on all targets.

**Counter-search:** The counter-search added a systematic mapping of XR-specific software testing and CloudXR's explicit server-runtime/client-SDK pairing. It confirmed that functional behavior, 6DOF/input, privacy/security, performance/load and usability need distinct checks. Streaming is an optional deployment architecture whose latency/network costs must be measured, not a universal prerequisite.

## XR Devices, Tracking and Platform Engineering

**Boundary:** Develop or integrate head-mounted/near-eye displays, sensors, tracking, input, graphics and system-level XR hardware/software components.

**Expected outputs:** A device/platform design or integration, characterized tracking/display/input behavior, capability and interface descriptions, calibration and verification data, and system constraints consumed by applications.

**Core claim and learning flow:** Device and tracking layers expose pose, input, anchors, scene information and display behavior to runtimes/applications. Environmental conditions, sensing error, calibration, power/thermal limits and latency constrain how spatial content can be placed and rendered.

**Limits:** Apple documentation is an implementation example; active standards projects and research prototypes are not general device capabilities. The local Nigerian sources establish examples of XR infrastructure and a proposed facility, not a local device-engineering occupation market.

**Counter-search:** The counter-search added a 2026–2029 integrated optics/sensing project and a 2025 experimental multifocal waveguide, plus local lab/proposal context. These are emerging research and local infrastructure examples, not evidence that next-generation displays or device-engineering roles are generally available. No materially distinct established device subsystem was found beyond display/optics, sensing/tracking, input/haptics and platform/runtime integration within this search scope.

## Human Factors, Accessibility, Safety and Evaluation

**Boundary:** Assess comfort, usability, accessibility, motion-related risks, privacy and ethical effects of immersive products and systems; this is cross-cutting and a distinct area only where the learning/work output is substantial.

**Expected outputs:** A context-specific evaluation plan, participant/user evidence, accessibility and safety issues, measurement limitations and prioritized design/engineering changes.

**Core claim and learning flow:** XR evaluation combines technical performance and human outcomes. Usability tasks, interviews/think-aloud and construct-matched measures can complement one another; comfort, accessibility, adverse events, movement space, privacy and device/runtime performance shape interpretation and redesign.

**Limits:** Reviews report heterogeneous constructs and instruments and no single universal protocol. A sickness-reduction standard, draft accessibility guidance or one clinical/remote-study review does not establish a complete risk model or legal compliance.

**Counter-search:** The counter-search added scoping/systematic reviews of VR questionnaires, presence, software testing and remote-study safety reporting, plus employer task evidence for XR hardware UX research, device comfort/wearability and an XR engineer role combining user studies with implementation. It found no agreed universal presence/usability protocol and shows that questionnaire validity, task-based observation, qualitative evidence, cybersickness monitoring and privacy/safety procedures answer different questions. No Nigeria-specific occupational evidence for XR human-factors evaluators was found in the targeted local search; employer examples support conditional task contexts, not new canonical role records.
