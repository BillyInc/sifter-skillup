## D028 specialization-set decision

**Recommendation: flatten D028's specialization set (no current student-facing subgroup/specialization cards); keep only role paths that pass the independent D028 task-fit review directly under the domain.** This resolves the prior restructure-versus-flatten question at the evidence level, without editing the taxonomy.

Basis: AACSB's 2026 Global Standards are globally framed, principles- and outcomes-focused, and emphasize curriculum review, disciplinary depth, adaptability, technology, ethics, and experiential learning (Standard 4, clauses 4.1–4.6). The standard does not prescribe general-management specialization labels or a fixed count. Imperial College Business School's MSc Management example describes a broad cross-functional core plus elective concentrations, but explicitly notes there is no standard MiM template; its Strategy & Innovation, Finance, Marketing, and Management & Operations concentrations are an example of one school's elective design, not a universal general-management taxonomy. Those functions also overlap this catalogue's separate root domains. Therefore the evidence supports a broad management foundation and role-specific routes, but not promoting the current D028 job-family buckets or copying one institution's elective concentrations as canonical subgroups.

Sources and locators:

- AACSB, [Global Standards for Business Education](https://www.aacsb.edu/educators/global-standards), page lines 137–151 (global scope and principles/outcomes orientation); Standard 4 lines 247–269 (curriculum design, disciplinary depth, adaptability, digital/analytical literacy, ethics and experiential learning). This is a business-school quality framework, not a list of management specializations.
- EFMD Global interview with Imperial College Business School's MSc Management academic director, [Shaping programmes for the future](https://blog.efmdglobal.org/2024/10/02/shaping-programmes-for-the-future-academic-director-of-msc-in-management-imperial-college-business-school/), lines 47–56 (broad cross-functional MiM versus specialist master's; core plus elective concentrations; named Imperial concentrations; explicit statement that there is no standard MiM template). This is a single-programme example, not a universal taxonomy.
