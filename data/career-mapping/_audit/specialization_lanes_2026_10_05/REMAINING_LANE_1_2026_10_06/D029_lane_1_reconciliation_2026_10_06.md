## Candidate areas

- **Workforce Planning** — Keep workforce forecasting focused on people, roles, skills and capacity. Do not absorb corporate strategy, generic operations planning or general data science.
- **Employee Experience** — Center this area on experience, engagement and employee voice. Keep clinical mental-health treatment in health professions and avoid duplicating specialist wellbeing content.
- **Employee Relations** — Separate globally applicable concepts and negotiation practices from country-specific employment law, dispute procedures and union rules; label jurisdiction-specific requirements explicitly.
- **Equality, Diversity and Inclusion** — Distinguish inclusive practice from jurisdiction-specific equality-law advice and from generic social policy or advocacy roles.
- **Learning and Development** — Keep workplace capability development distinct from general school teaching, subject-matter training delivery, and education-sector curriculum design unless the role's actual work is HR-owned L&D.
- **Organization Development and Design** — Limit this to people, organization design, culture and change dimensions. Do not absorb general business-process engineering or organization-wide management consulting without clear HR/OD task evidence.
- **People Analytics and Human Capital Reporting** — Require a workforce/people question and responsible data handling. Generic BI, data engineering, financial analytics or data science alone belongs to its own domain.
- **Resourcing and Talent Acquisition** — Keep talent sourcing and selection distinct from staffing-agency sales, general marketing and employment-law practice; apply local legal rules to hiring requirements.
- **Reward, Compensation and Benefits** — Separate reward policy, job evaluation and benefits from accounting, payroll processing, tax advice and actuarial work; include those only when the role's actual remit supports them.
- **Talent Management and Succession** — Keep talent pools, mobility and succession distinct from recruitment and L&D while showing their supported overlaps; avoid implying that workforce potential can be measured without valid, fair evidence.
- **Workplace Wellbeing** — Focus on organizational/workplace practice, not diagnosis or treatment. Occupational health, clinical psychology and workplace safety competencies belong to their relevant professions; cross-links require task-specific evidence.

## Residuals and limits

- CIPD is a professional body based in the UK; its Profession Map explicitly describes an international people profession, but it remains one professional framework, not universal global consensus.
- The current page's specialist area list is used as a reference taxonomy, not copied as proof that every listed practice should be an independent sub-group in every organization or country.
- This review establishes candidate specialization areas and boundaries only. It does not populate skills, tools, competencies, certifications or learning links for those areas; that is a subsequent enrichment layer.
- The 16 existing role-to-domain mappings and all role-to-specialization placement remain under their separate task-based audit.
- Human Resources covers organization-wide people practices and specialist HR work. Keep specialist people practices here when the task is specifically workforce/employee-related; do not absorb general management, organizational psychology, occupational health and safety, legal practice, generic data science, education/training delivery or business administration solely because those functions interact with employees. Label legal requirements and professional permissions by jurisdiction.
