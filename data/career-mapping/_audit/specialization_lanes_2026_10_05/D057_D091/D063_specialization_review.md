# D063 — Economics and Policy Analysis: specialization taxonomy review

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** Provisional candidate set; resolve granularity and neighboring-domain boundaries before implementation.  
**Scope:** Domain-level specialization taxonomy only. This review does not assign roles or edit canonical taxonomy.

## Finding

The three current subgroup records—“Teaching professionals,” “Economic Policy & Analysis,” and “Economic Research & Quantitative Policy”—are inherited occupational groupings, not evidence that three student-facing specializations are correct. The teaching bucket is not an economics specialization. The other two are too broad to show the distinct subject and methods routes evidenced by the sources.

I recommend **six candidate areas** as a usable synthesis, not as a globally mandated count:

1. **Economic Research, Econometrics and Forecasting** — empirical questions, economic data, statistical/econometric inference and forecasts.
2. **Computational Economics and Economic Modelling** — programming, numerical methods, simulation, optimization, computational econometrics and model implementation.
3. **Public Economics, Policy and Evaluation** — taxation, public spending, policy options, implementation evidence and evaluation.
4. **Macroeconomic, Monetary and Financial Economics** — economy-wide output, inflation, employment, money, interest rates and financial-economic interactions.
5. **International, Trade and Development Economics** — cross-border trade/finance, integration, growth, structural change and development outcomes.
6. **Applied and Sector Economics** — economic analysis applied to labour, health, education, industry/innovation, agriculture/natural resources, environment, transport and urban/regional contexts. These remain visible themes; split a theme into its own specialization only if it has a distinct, adequately supported learning route.

Computational economics merits an explicit route: the Society for Computational Economics names a coherent field spanning computational econometrics, agent-based models, numerical model solution, simulation and programming. Southampton (UK), Edinburgh (UK) and McGill (Canada) independently publish economics-specific computational-methods curricula. These methods can also support research, policy and sector routes; their appearance should not imply exclusive ownership or duplicate a whole identical foundation on every branch.

## Boundaries and open decisions

- The American Economic Association’s JEL scheme confirms breadth—quantitative methods, micro/macro, international, public, labour, industrial, development, agricultural/environmental and regional fields—but is a literature-indexing classification, **not** a required student taxonomy. The six-area set is a reasoned consolidation, not a direct copy of JEL.
- The “Applied and Sector Economics” umbrella is broad. A more granular alternative would separate labour/demographic, health/education/welfare, industrial/innovation, agricultural/environmental, and urban/regional economics. That would improve specificity but may create an unwieldy card set; test this against the full domain catalogue and user-facing layout.
- The current D063 text includes financial economics. Clarify its boundary against D011 Quantitative Finance and Financial Engineering, investment-analysis domains and Trading before final approval. Economic analysis of financial systems can remain here; pricing-engineering, execution and portfolio-advice practice should not be swept in without explicit catalogue evidence.
- Policy evaluation overlaps public administration and political science when the work is primarily institutional, legal or political rather than economic.
- “Flat” is not recommended: the evidence supports distinct subject/method learning routes. The exact grouping/count remains provisional.

## Evidence and limits

- [AEA JEL classification](https://ftp.aeaweb.org/econlit/jelCodes.php?view=jel): international scholarly classification; supports the field breadth, not a learning taxonomy.
- [Society for Computational Economics](https://comp-econ.com/) and its [scope](https://comp-econ.org/about/): international scholarly/professional community; supports Computational Economics as a recognized methods field, not a license or universal job requirement.
- [OECD evaluation criteria](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/development-co-operation-evaluation-and-effectiveness/evaluation-criteria.html) and [OECD public-policy evaluation recommendation](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0478): international policy frameworks; support evaluation as a distinct evidence-to-decision practice, not an economics-only occupation.
- [World Bank DIME](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/unit/unit-dec/impactevaluation): international institution’s development research/impact-evaluation practice; illustrative rather than exhaustive.
- [Southampton ECON2040](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/courses/2026-27/modules/econ2040), [Edinburgh ECNM10115](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/26-27/dpt/cxecnm10115.htm), and [McGill ECON581](https://www.mcgill.ca/economics/undergraduate/courses/new-500-level-economics-course-econ-581): institution-specific curricula in the UK and Canada, used as cross-region corroboration—not universal requirements.
- [IMF micro/macro explainer](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/micro-and-macro): introductory distinction only; not a full taxonomy.
