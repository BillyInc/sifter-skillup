# D065 — Translation, Interpretation and Linguistics: specialization review

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** Provisional candidate set; reconcile scope with adjacent language, education, and AI/software domains.  
**Scope:** Domain-level specialization taxonomy only; no role assignments or canonical edits.

## Finding

The four existing records are broad ISCO occupation families, mixing managers, teachers, scientists, and legal/social/cultural professionals. They do not define the learner-facing areas.

Six candidate areas offer a compact structure across the combined language-services and language-science scope:

1. **Translation, Localization and Transcreation**
2. **Interpreting**
3. **Terminology and Language-Services Project Practice**
4. **Linguistic Research, Description and Documentation**
5. **Applied Linguistics and Language Learning**
6. **Computational Linguistics and Language Technology**

FIT distinguishes translation, localization and transcreation, while recognizing translator, interpreter and terminologist associations internationally. CIOL qualification examples distinguish translation and public-service interpreting, and identify separate language-education/service-coordination contexts (these are UK-specific qualifications and rules). ACL explicitly describes computational linguistics as an international research/technology field. Linguistics Society of America proceedings illustrate distinct theoretical, social, applied and methodological research directions.

## Boundaries

- Keep legal, medical, technical, literary and scientific translation as text-domain themes unless distinct curricula justify separate routes.
- Keep conference, public service, court, healthcare and signed-language interpreting as nested pathways initially; legal authorization and qualification vary by jurisdiction.
- Distinguish linguistics research from translation practice and applied language education.
- Make computational linguistics visible based on ACL evidence, not the automated scan. The scan found only one noisy lexical hit inside a generic ISCO description. Connect language-specific modeling/data/evaluation to D065; general ML/software engineering remains adjacent.
- No role titles or links were judged. A label or ISCO code is not fit evidence.
