# D059 — Space Technology and Satellite Systems

Date: 2026-10-05. **Status: source-reviewed; recommend a two-area student-facing synthesis.**

## Recommendation

Use two broad areas: **Spacecraft and Payload Systems Engineering**; and **Ground Systems, Space Communications and Mission Operations**. Treat mission objectives, end-to-end architecture, integration/verification and user applications as shared or cross-cutting context, not automatic extra specialization cards.

NASA's SmallSat engineering reference separately organizes spacecraft platform/subsystem engineering (power, propulsion, guidance/navigation/control, structures, thermal, avionics, communications and launch/deployment) and a dedicated ground data/mission operations chapter. NASA §11.1's Figure 11.1 distinguishes space segment, ground segment and final user; §§11.2–11.7 define ground stations, communications, ground software, mission/science operations centers and operations software. The user segment is an endpoint, not automatically a space-engineering specialization. [NASA spacecraft reference](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/), [NASA ground data and operations](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/ground-data-systems-and-mission-operations/)

ESA Academy's Earth-observation satellite design course gives comparative European curriculum corroboration: mission requirements/orbit, payload design and satellite architecture are taught alongside distinct ground-segment/operations and verification/testing responsibilities. ISO 21076, maintained as current in 2023, defines international space-communications cross-support architecture spanning in-space and ground elements. [ESA course](https://www.esa.int/Education/ESA_Academy/Training_Future_Opportunities), [ISO 21076](https://www.iso.org/standard/69836.html)

## Count rationale and alternatives

The two-area synthesis consolidates many spacecraft subsystems under a spacecraft/payload path and treats ground infrastructure/communications/operations as the second major system context. Separate power, propulsion, thermal, avionics, guidance, payload types, and user applications would over-fragment or cross into adjacent fields. The sources establish the engineering/system boundaries, but none mandates this exact count; it remains an internal taxonomy recommendation, not accreditation.

The earlier three mission-segment model (space/ground/user) was not adopted as three subgroups because those are architectural segments, not a curriculum taxonomy. Satellite application/data analysis may be linked to relevant downstream domains rather than embedded automatically here. Exact source locators and limits are recorded in [`D059_specialization_review.json`](D059_specialization_review.json).

No roles, ISCO groupings or memberships were used; no role-to-specialization links or canonical taxonomy were changed.
