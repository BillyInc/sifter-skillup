# D064 — Psychology and Counselling: specialization taxonomy review

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** Provisional; titles and boundaries vary by jurisdiction.  
**Scope:** Domain-level specialization taxonomy only. No roles are assigned or canonical data changed.

## Finding

The current four records are ISCO-derived occupation families—teaching professionals; legal/social/cultural professionals; health associate professionals; and related associate professionals. They combine unlike practice areas and are not a defensible student-facing specialization taxonomy.

Cross-region professional sources support the existence of distinct practice areas, but not one globally standardized count. I recommend **nine candidate areas**:

1. Clinical Psychology
2. Clinical Neuropsychology
3. Counselling Psychology and Psychotherapy
4. Educational, School and Developmental Psychology
5. Health Psychology
6. Community Psychology
7. Forensic Psychology
8. Work and Organizational Psychology
9. Sport and Exercise Psychology

This is a synthesis, not a claim that these titles are legally or educationally equivalent worldwide. Research, assessment and measurement remain cross-cutting capabilities rather than a standalone specialization card. Counselling psychology must not be conflated with broader counselling/psychotherapy routes.

## Naming, boundaries, and count caveats

- US APA recognition, UK protected practitioner titles, and Australian areas of practice share several names (clinical, counselling, educational, forensic, health, work/organizational, sport/exercise) while differing in coverage and legal meaning. Australia separately lists community psychology and clinical neuropsychology; those categories are not universal.
- Work/organizational psychology appears as industrial-organizational in US material and occupational psychology in UK title regulation. Use a plain-language cross-region display label; put jurisdiction-specific title and qualification facts in localized notes.
- Health and community psychology are shown separately because their primary contexts differ: health behaviour, illness and healthcare versus prevention, participation and community systems. Australia lists both separately; the product should retain jurisdiction notes.
- Clinical neuropsychology is shown separately as an advanced brain-behaviour assessment and rehabilitation route where supported by evidence. This does not imply universal licensure or that every psychology graduate may practise it.
- Research, assessment and measurement are cross-cutting capabilities, not automatically a separate practice specialization; testing authority can be regulated.
- The computational scan reports one lexical hit—optional “data analytics” attached to a volunteer-mentor competency. This does not justify a domain-level computational specialization. Any computational psychology, digital mental-health or neuroinformatics route needs independent evidence.
