# D057 — Digital Health and Health Technology

Date: 2026-10-05. **Status: provisional source review; do not promote subgroup cards yet.**

## Finding

Do not treat the current four-card structure as validated. The prior checkpoint reports that it mixes health-informatics labels with broad occupational/job-family buckets. This review does not inspect or use role memberships/counts.

The safest current taxonomy recommendation is a **flat parent domain with health-system/function facets**, rather than converting the five WHO architecture categories into five student specializations. WHO’s 2023 global classification is explicitly a shared language for classifying interventions, health-system challenges, and software/services/application types—not education or occupations. Its computable v2 architecture list has five representations: point of service; health-system/provider administration; registries/directories; data-management services; and surveillance/response. Those are useful system facets, but one deployed system can span them and they do not define additive learning paths. [WHO classification](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/373581/9789240081949-eng.pdf), printed p. v and chapter 1; [WHO computable service/application types](https://smart.who.int/base/1.0.0/CodeSystem-CDSCv2.html), lines 25–26 and A–E definitions.

IMIA’s multiregional education recommendations recognize dedicated health/medical informatics study, specialist education and different entry backgrounds, while explicitly noting country/program variation. The 2022 second revision abstract describes six knowledge/competency domains and HMI user/generalist/specialist education; these are **not six specialization groups**. WHO’s 2026 competency landscape likewise clusters competencies across patient care, data, informatics, communication, technical proficiency, professionalism and administration rather than defining specialist branches. [IMIA second revision](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2022.104908), abstract; [WHO competency landscape](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240119765), overview.

## Candidate routes and alternatives

For subsequent curriculum comparison, three working candidates are recorded: **Clinical and Care-Delivery Informatics; Population and Public-Health Informatics; Health Data Systems, Interoperability and Analytics**. These are internal hypotheses, not claims that WHO or IMIA prescribes three branches. Keep them provisional until multiple independent, cross-regional dedicated curricula confirm their educational boundaries.

Alternatives considered: (1) five WHO system categories as subgroups—rejected because they classify systems, not specializations; (2) retain the current four cards—rejected because their current labels mix concepts and generic job families; (3) three candidate routes—kept for follow-up, not approved yet because current primary frameworks do not establish an exhaustive, non-overlapping curriculum taxonomy. A flat domain with WHO categories as facets is the present safer display.

## Scope limits

This is not accreditation or formal academic sign-off. No role fit, role-to-specialization links, role counts, ISCO groups, canonical data or shared register were assessed or changed. Generic software engineering/data engineering, clinical practice, wet-lab biomedical work and medical-device engineering should not be pulled into D057 without separate parent-domain evidence.
