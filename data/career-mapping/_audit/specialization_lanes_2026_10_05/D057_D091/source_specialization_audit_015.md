## Method and limits

Reviewed the 28 lane-owned source-review records D057–D084, the seven already-reconciled shared-register records D085–D091 (read-only), each source list and candidate-area/source-ID link, and the stated disposition/count rationale. Cross-checked primary international or multiregional sources for representative and contested boundaries using the organizations' own publications/pages. The lane records contain **142 source-register entries** for D057–D084; the seven carried records cite **28 distinct source IDs**, all of which resolve in the shared evidence register. “Source reviewed” means the evidence and its limits were examined; it does not mean each source is an accrediting authority or that the proposed taxonomy has external sign-off.

The audit separates (a) a real academic/practice field, (b) a learner-friendly way to group fields, and (c) a jurisdiction's legally recognized credential/specialty list. Global sources support many of the *areas*, but they generally do **not** prescribe the catalogue's exact subgroup count. Counts below are therefore internal design proposals unless explicitly described otherwise. No ISCO grouping or role count was used to validate subgroup membership.

## Findings requiring lane-level disposition before any canonical use

1. **D077 has a direct evidence mismatch.** Its “Emergency Response, Fire and Rescue” candidate cites ILO OSH conventions and UNESCO's sport charter. Those sources do not establish firefighting/rescue as an area of specialization *within security services*. The cited UN security/law-enforcement sources do not bridge that gap either. Treat this candidate as unsupported under this parent pending an appropriate international emergency-services source and an explicit parent-boundary decision; do not infer that it belongs because it shares protective/security tasks.
2. **D080's four rows are diagnostic partitions, not subgroup recommendations.** The lane text already says so, and the sources place scenography/live performance, exhibition/experience design, and illustration/animation outside or across the interior-design parent. Keep current visible subgroup count at zero until the taxonomy boundary is resolved; do not seed the four candidate rows as subgroups.
3. **D076's word “Naval” is not evidenced by the cited civilian maritime sources.** IMO seafarer/fishing standards and UNCTAD/WTO logistics support civilian maritime functions. A naval/military branch requires separate evidence and scope; otherwise the parent label should be narrowed in the later taxonomy phase.
4. **D067's eight-area set needs a completeness/reconciliation note.** ADA/NCRDSCB currently describes 12 recognized U.S. dental specialties; that list is an explicit U.S. recognition regime, not a global list. The current eight grouped candidates do not individually expose dental anesthesiology, oral/maxillofacial pathology, oral/maxillofacial radiology or orofacial pain. Some may be intentionally consolidated, but the grouping needs to say where they go or keep the set explicitly non-exhaustive. FDI's international policy supports jurisdiction-sensitive specialty recognition, not a universal count.
5. **D064's eight is not cross-regionally complete by assertion.** EFPA's European EuroPsy specialist examples include psychotherapy and clinical neuropsychology in addition to work/organisational and sport psychology. The lane's candidate set should state whether these are nested under existing labels or omitted; neither EFPA nor APA supplies a global exhaustive set.
6. **D075 needs stronger international triangulation for all four labels.** WorldSkills' international Fashion Technology standard supports fashion design, pattern/construction and garment manufacture/finishing, and describes material selection/sourcing as part of the work. It does not by itself validate a distinct textile-materials specialization or a standalone merchandising/supply-chain specialization. UNESCO's Africa-specific textile-value-chain evidence must remain marked regional; the report must not use it as global-only proof.
7. **D091 labels are learner-facing practice options, not universal licence categories.** Vehicle classes, instructor credentials and permissions differ by jurisdiction. Keep the areas task-based and label local licensing requirements as jurisdiction-specific.
