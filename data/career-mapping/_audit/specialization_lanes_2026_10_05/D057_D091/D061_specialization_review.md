# D061 — Insurance: specialization review

**Disposition:** Recommend three candidate areas: **Insurance Underwriting and Risk Selection**, **Claims, Loss Adjustment and Resolution**, and **Insurance Distribution and Policy Service**. This is a source-grounded synthesis, not an externally mandated count. No role assignment or canonical taxonomy edit was made.

## Why the current groups are not the student taxonomy

The six current records classify broad occupations, clerical work and management. They mix job level and occupational family rather than explaining what a learner would specialize in. I used their existence and counts only as a baseline, not as evidence for the proposed number.

Current professional-learning sources repeatedly distinguish underwriting, claims/loss adjustment, and broking/agency functions. The CII’s current UK pathways list claims/underwriting and broking separately; the Insurance Institute of Canada offers distinct course sequences for underwriting, claims/loss adjustment, and broker/agent practice. Columbia’s U.S. insurance-management curriculum separately teaches underwriting and claims management. IAIS’s international supervisory framework separately addresses claims functions, intermediaries and conduct of business. [CII pathways](https://www.cii.co.uk/learning/training/cii-qualifications-training-programme/), [Insurance Institute of Canada courses](https://www.insuranceinstitute.ca/en/Programs/CIP/Courses), [Columbia curriculum](https://sps.columbia.edu/academics/masters/insurance-management/curriculum-courses), [IAIS ICPs and ComFrame](https://www.iais.org/activities-topics/standard-setting/icps-and-comframe/).

## Boundaries and alternatives

- Product types such as life, health, motor, property and marine insurance should appear as contexts within the relevant pathway by default, not as one card per product line.
- Policy servicing and administration remain nested in distribution/service unless later cross-region curriculum evidence supports a distinct learner pathway.
- Fraud controls are relevant within underwriting and claims; the evidence here does not justify a separate fraud-specialization card.
- Actuarial science and enterprise risk management are established adjacent fields, but they may belong to separate parent domains. Pension-scheme management in the current parent description also requires a catalogue-wide boundary check; neither was added as a D061 specialization by assumption.
- Local laws, intermediary licensing and claims deadlines must be labelled by jurisdiction.

## Computational cross-check and evidence limits

The current catalogue scanner reports no declared computational-layer gap for D061. Analytics, actuarial methods and fraud detection may be useful methods, but keyword mentions would not prove a distinct specialization. CII is UK-specific, the Insurance Institute of Canada is Canada-specific, and Columbia is institution-specific to the United States; they corroborate recurring functions, not universal licensing or a universal count. IAIS is global supervisory evidence, not an education taxonomy.
