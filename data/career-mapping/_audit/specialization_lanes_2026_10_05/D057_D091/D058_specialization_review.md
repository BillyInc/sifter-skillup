# D058 — Education Technology and Learning Experience Design

Date: 2026-10-05. **Status: source-reviewed, still provisional at subgroup count/boundaries.**

## Finding

The prior lane identified the four existing buckets as generic job-family groupings, not a validated EdTech learning-specialization set. I reject retaining those four as currently named. I do **not** replace them with a new fixed count yet.

AECT's current field definition spans the ethical study and application of theory/research through design, management, implementation and evaluation of learning experiences and environments. That describes an integrated field lifecycle, not separate subgroups. [AECT definition](https://aect.org/aect/about/aect-definition)

The UNESCO–UNICEF–ITU public digital-learning-platform charter organizes design/governance principles around public purpose, inclusion, pedagogy, complementarity, openness/interoperability, evidence-led focus and trust. UNESCO's ICT Competency Framework for Teachers covers teacher training and ICT integration; it is useful for curriculum/pedagogy/assessment facets but is **not** a professional curriculum for EdTech product or learning-design teams. UNESCO's six-pillar framework connects platforms, content, curriculum, pedagogy and assessment at an education-system level. Together these sources show meaningful dimensions, but they do not define non-overlapping learner specialization groups. [UNESCO–UNICEF–ITU Charter](https://www.unesco.org/en/digital-education/learning-platforms-gateway/charter), [UNESCO ICT CFT](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-ict-competency-framework-teachers?hub=785), [UNESCO Six Pillars](https://www.unesco.org/en/dtc-financing-toolkit/six-pillars)

## Disposition and alternatives

For now, prefer a **flat parent with functional themes** over four unsupported subgroup cards. Four candidate directions are recorded for curriculum triangulation—not approved areas: learning experience/instructional design; educational platforms/learning technology; digital content/accessibility/media; and implementation/evaluation. They overlap and may represent work stages or competencies rather than distinct academic pathways. AECT's preparation standards are U.S.-origin and AECT explicitly says it is no longer an official accrediting body, so they cannot independently establish a global count. [AECT standards](https://aect.org/aect/about/standards)

The next evidence step is compare multiple regions' dedicated educational-technology, instructional-design and learning-science curricula, separating EdTech product/platform development from teachers' classroom use of technology. Until that establishes additive and reasonably distinct student pathways, the count stays provisional. No roles or role memberships were evaluated.
