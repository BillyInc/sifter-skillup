## Source evidence and limits

- [Association for Educational Communications and Technology (AECT) — AECT Definition for Educational Technology](https://aect.org/aect/about/aect-definition) — Definition paragraph: ethical study/application of theory, research and practices to improve learning and performance through strategic design, management, implementation and evaluation of learning experiences/environments using appropriate processes/resources. **Supports:** Educational technology is an integrated field spanning learning experience/environment design, resource/process use, management, implementation and evaluation, rather than simply a collection of teacher/job-family groups. **Limit:** AECT is a professional association; its definition describes the field, not a universal taxonomy or fixed count of specializations. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [UNESCO, UNICEF and International Telecommunication Union — Charter for Public Digital Learning Platforms](https://www.unesco.org/en/digital-education/learning-platforms-gateway/charter) — Overview and seven principles: Public, Inclusive, Pedagogical, Complementary, Open, Focused and Trustworthy. The Inclusive principle addresses multilingualism, disability access, low-cost devices and intermittent connectivity; Open addresses open standards/interoperability; Focused calls for needs- and evidence-led iterative development; Trustworthy addresses accuracy, safety, age appropriateness and learner-data stewardship. **Supports:** Platform work includes governance, inclusion/accessibility, pedagogy, interoperability, iterative design/evaluation and learner safety as connected dimensions; this is cross-regional guidance developed through international consultation. **Limit:** Principles for public platforms do not define private EdTech market roles, an academic curriculum, or distinct specialization groups. Public platform governance contexts may be jurisdiction-specific. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [UNESCO — ICT Competency Framework for Teachers, Version 3](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-ict-competency-framework-teachers?hub=785) — Publication overview and framework structure: intended to guide pre-service/in-service teacher training; organizes teacher competencies across ICT-in-education policy, curriculum/assessment, pedagogy, digital skills, organization/administration and professional learning, at Knowledge Acquisition, Knowledge Deepening and Knowledge Creation levels. **Supports:** Educational technology has distinct pedagogical, curricular/assessment, technical and institutional dimensions; these can be useful content facets and capability themes. **Limit:** This framework is for teachers' use/integration of ICT, not for educational technology product/design professionals; its competency dimensions and progression levels are not specialization areas. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [UNESCO Digital Transformation Collaborative — The Six Pillars Framework](https://www.unesco.org/en/dtc-financing-toolkit/six-pillars) — Digital learning/content pillar overview: learning platforms, application software, curriculum alignment, digital/blended pedagogies and assessment/certification systems; describes platforms and content as components of system-level digital transformation. **Supports:** Platform, content, pedagogy, curriculum, assessment and infrastructure are connected parts of digital education ecosystems. **Limit:** A system-planning framework is not an academic specialization taxonomy and does not decide the number or boundaries of student-facing subgroups. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [Association for Educational Communications and Technology (AECT) — AECT Standards](https://aect.org/aect/about/standards) — Overview of 2012 standards and Standard 4: candidates design, develop, implement and evaluate technology-rich learning environments; associated matrix includes assessing/evaluating, theoretical foundations and methods. AECT notes it is no longer an official accrediting body but maintains guidelines for programs. **Supports:** Professional preparation includes a lifecycle of design/development/implementation/evaluation; the activities are integrated competencies rather than a ready-made four-specialization structure. **Limit:** These are U.S.-origin program standards and not current global accreditation requirements; they do not prescribe the platform's exact learner specialization count. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- This is an internal evidence review, not academic accreditation or external sign-off.
- UNESCO/UNICEF/ITU public-platform guidance is global and multi-agency but focuses on public platforms and system governance.
- AECT provides a field definition and professional-preparation standards, but these are not a universal specialization taxonomy; its program standards are U.S.-origin and AECT states it is no longer an official accrediting body.
- Teacher competency frameworks cannot be used as a proxy for the curriculum of EdTech designers, product teams, platform engineers or learning-experience designers.
- No role IDs, ISCO classes, role counts or current subgroup membership were used as taxonomy evidence.
