# D057 — Digital Health and Health Technology

## Recommendation

Do not retain the current four-card set as validated specialization taxonomy. For this review, retain D057 as a flat parent domain with function/system-type facets rather than claim a globally recognized fixed number of subgroups. Candidate learning routes for a later curriculum validation are (1) clinical and care-delivery informatics, (2) population and public-health informatics, and (3) health data systems, interoperability and analytics. These are an internal synthesis, not a WHO/IMIA taxonomy.

## Candidate areas and boundaries

- **Population and Public-Health Informatics.**

## Source evidence and limits

- [World Health Organization — Classification of digital interventions, services and applications in health, 2nd ed.](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/373581/9789240081949-eng.pdf) — Printed p. v and chapter 1: the classification is organized around health-system challenges, digital health interventions and digital services/application types; it is intended as a shared language for describing uses of digital technology, not a profession or education taxonomy. **Supports:** Digital health spans distinct individual, provider, system and data-service needs, and technology/system architectures should be distinguished from end-user health challenges and the interventions delivered. **Limit:** The WHO classification explicitly describes interventions and software/ICT application types; it does not prescribe student specialization groups, curricula, role titles, or a subgroup count. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [World Health Organization (SMART Guidelines digital representation) — Classification of Digital Health Services and Application Types v2](https://smart.who.int/base/1.0.0/CodeSystem-CDSCv2.html) — Description lines 25–26 and code definitions A–E, lines 36–80: five architecture representations are Point of service; Health system/Provider administration; Registries and Directories; Data Management services; Surveillance and Response. The definitions include EHR, decision support, telehealth, finance/admin, registries, analytics/interoperability and public-health surveillance systems. **Supports:** These are useful facets/examples for describing the kinds of digital systems students may work with and show distinct health-system contexts. **Limit:** The page explicitly calls these types of software, ICT systems/services/channels. Treating them as five areas of specialization would mistake an architecture classification for a professional or academic taxonomy. **Scope screen:** country_or_institution_specific_corroboration.
- [International Medical Informatics Association; published in International Journal of Medical Informatics — Recommendations of the International Medical Informatics Association (IMIA) on Education in Biomedical and Health Informatics: Second Revision](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2022.104908) — Abstract: recommendations cover courses/course tracks and dedicated programs across educational backgrounds; distinguishes HMI user, generalist and specialist education and describes six knowledge-domain areas. The methods include an IMIA task force with representatives from all IMIA regions and an international Delphi study. **Supports:** Health/biomedical informatics is a recognized educational field with multiple entry pathways, specialist education and a broad shared interdisciplinary foundation. **Limit:** The six areas are knowledge/competency domains, not six specializations; the paper does not supply an exhaustive subgroup set for the broader combined Digital Health and Health Technology domain. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [World Health Organization — Competency frameworks and standards for digital health: a landscape analysis](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240119765) — Overview, paragraphs 1–2: narrative review maps frameworks and reports common competency clusters in patient care, data, informatics, communication, technical proficiency, digital professionalism and administration; discusses contextualization and sustainability. **Supports:** Skills/competencies and system enablers span multiple digital-health contexts and should not automatically be treated as separate specializations. **Limit:** This is a landscape of workforce competency frameworks, not a curriculum taxonomy for people building health technology; its clusters do not establish the proposed three-route boundaries. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- This is an internal source assessment, not accreditation or academic-body approval.
- WHO's framework is global but classifies digital-health interventions and system types, not specializations.
- IMIA recommendations are multiregional, but health and education systems vary; they do not prescribe an exhaustive specialization count.
- The three proposed routes are working candidates. They need independent international academic-program comparisons before being displayed as canonical subgroup choices.
- No ISCO member role, role frequency, current subgroup member count or national-only source was used as evidence for taxonomy or count.
