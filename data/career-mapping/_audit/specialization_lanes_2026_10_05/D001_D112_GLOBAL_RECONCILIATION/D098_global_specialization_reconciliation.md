# D098 — Precision Engineering and Toolmaking

Global specialization recommendation reconciliation (2026-10-06). This is an internal recommendation, not canonical taxonomy or external academic sign-off.

- **Candidate areas:** 4
- **Evidence URLs:** 8 unique; 2 source records screened as global/international
- **Canonical changes / role assignments:** none / none

## Recommendation

Adjudication: recommend FOUR candidate areas, not the shared register's three and not the lane's earlier four as composed. Metrology is a distinct learner route, independently demonstrated by formal dedicated curricula and training, and the parent explicitly includes precision testing/instruments. The parent description also explicitly names watchmakers, so retain Horology. Merge component machining and toolmaking into one precision-production area; separate precision instruments/optical devices; add standalone dimensional metrology; retain horology. This resolves the original three-versus-four issue without treating generic quality inspection as D098 specialization.

## Candidate areas and boundaries

- **Precision Machining and Toolmaking.** Precision part/tool/die/fixture production; combine machining and toolmaking because the learner routes share precision production foundations and source evidence does not require separate areas.
- **Precision Instruments and Optical Devices.** Build, service, test or repair precision measuring/optical instruments; metrology methods used within instrument work remain connected but are not collapsed into this route.
- **Dimensional Metrology and Measurement Systems.** Measurement strategy, GD&T, calibration/traceability, uncertainty, CMM/optical measurement and validated results as a standalone learning route; generic QA is not enough.
- **Horology (Clock and Watchmaking).** Making, servicing, repairing and restoring timepieces; explicitly within the current parent description, not held as a parent-scope anomaly.
