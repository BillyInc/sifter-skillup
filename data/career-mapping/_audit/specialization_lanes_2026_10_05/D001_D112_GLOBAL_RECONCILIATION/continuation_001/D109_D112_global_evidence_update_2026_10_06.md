# D109–D112 global specialization evidence update

Date: 2026-10-06. This is a disjoint continuation of the existing D099–D112 source-gap work. It adds a small set of authoritative global sources to four already-reviewed provisional cases; it does not repeat their completed source inventories.

## Scope and controls

- Global evidence only. No Nigerian/local sources or searches.
- Recommendations only: no canonical taxonomy writes, no role-to-domain decisions, and no role-to-specialization assignments.
- Area counts are internal learning-taxonomy proposals. Standards committees, occupational classifications, and course providers establish practice/process boundaries; they do not prescribe the catalogue's exact subgroup counts.
- Current canonical subgroup counts, ISCO memberships, role frequency, and computational keywords were not used to set counts.

## Sources, claims, and limits

- [World Steel Association — The steelmaking process](https://worldsteel.org/about-steel/steelmaking-process/) — its global industry process description runs from raw-material preparation and ironmaking through steelmaking, casting, rolling and finishing. It supports D109's stage boundaries, but covers steel rather than every metal and does not establish an education taxonomy.
- [ILO — Regional Model Competency Standard: Manufacturing industry](https://www.ilo.org/publications/regional-model-competency-standard-manufacturing-industry) — competency material includes casting, moulding and manufacturing processes. It supports the teachable coherence of foundry work; it is explicitly a regional Asia-Pacific model, not global subgroup authority.
- [ILO — ISCO-08 structure and group definitions](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf) — groups distinguish metal-processing plant operators, metal-finishing plant operators, metal moulders/coremakers, chemical-products plant operators, and rubber/plastics-products machine operators. This is task-boundary corroboration only; it is not used to create areas or assign roles.
- [ISO/TC 45 — Rubber and rubber products](https://www.iso.org/committee/48668.html) — global technical scope covers rubber in any form and rubber products; its committee structure separates raw materials, testing/analysis and products. Supports rubber as a distinct technical material domain, not a student pathway by itself.
- [ISO/TC 61 — Plastics](https://www.iso.org/committee/49256.html) — global technical scope includes plastics materials/products and processing, explicitly excludes rubber, and notes the limited joint responsibility for thermoplastic elastomers. Supports separating rubber and plastics as candidate material areas; does not prescribe subgroup count.
- [ISO/TC 72 — Textile machinery and accessories](https://www.iso.org/committee/49924.html) — lists separate committees for spinning machinery, fabric-manufacturing machinery and dyeing/finishing machinery; ISO/TC 148 sewing machines is a liaison. Supports D111's three core process boundaries and signals a separate sewing-machinery technical context, not an independent curriculum.
- [ISO 10821:2005 — Industrial sewing machines](https://www.iso.org/standard/2103.html) — covers professional industrial sewing machines, units and systems used in clothing and related industries. Supports a distinct machinery scope, not a global garment-operator specialization curriculum.
- [ILO — Safety and health in textiles, clothing, leather and footwear](https://www.ilo.org/global/topics/safety-and-health-at-work/normative-instruments/code-of-practice/WCMS_841128/lang--en/index.htm) — sector code distinguishes connected textile/clothing industrial contexts. Supports a boundary caution only; it does not determine subgroup structure.
- [TAPPI Academy — eLearning categories for pulping, paper manufacturing and converting](https://www.tappi.org/home-page-articles/2026/3.16.26/unlock-the-power-of-tappi-academy-elearning/) — offers separate course categories for pulping, paper manufacturing, and winding/web-handling/converting; converting content addresses its own operational practice. This strengthens D112's third candidate route, but an industry provider does not prescribe global academic taxonomy.
- [TAPPI/NPT2 — National Network for Pulp & Paper Technical Training](https://www.tappi.org/publications-standards/npt2/) — offers operations-focused paper-machine and pulp/paper training. Corroborates coherent pulp and paper learning content; not a universal subgroup count.
