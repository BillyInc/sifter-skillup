## Recommendation

Replace the three inherited occupational buckets with six candidate learning areas: Economic Research, Econometrics and Forecasting; Computational Economics and Economic Modelling; Public Economics, Policy and Evaluation; Macroeconomic, Monetary and Financial Economics; International, Trade and Development Economics; and Applied Sector Economics. The sixth area should expose sector themes (industrial/innovation, labour/demographic, health/education/welfare, agricultural/environmental, urban/regional) rather than silently treating every JEL subfield as a separate card. Computational Economics is named explicitly because it has a recognized methods community and dedicated university curricula, although its methods also support other areas. This is a provisional synthesis, not an externally mandated six-area taxonomy.

## Candidate areas and boundaries

- **Economic Research, Econometrics and Forecasting.** Frame economic questions, find and prepare relevant data, apply statistical and econometric methods, test explanations, and communicate evidence or forecasts.
- **Computational Economics and Economic Modelling.** Use programming, numerical methods, simulation, optimization, and computational econometrics to build, solve, test, and interpret economic models and data-intensive analyses.
- **Public Economics, Policy and Evaluation.** Analyze taxation, public expenditure, incentives and policy options; assess implementation and evaluate policy or programme results using suitable economic and social evidence.
- **Macroeconomic, Monetary and Financial Economics.** Study economy-wide output, inflation, employment, money, interest rates and financial-market interactions, and analyze their implications for institutions and policy.
- **International, Trade and Development Economics.** Analyze cross-border trade, finance and economic integration, and study structural change, growth, poverty and development outcomes across economies.
- **Applied and Sector Economics.** Apply economic theory and evidence to a defined sector or place, such as labour, health, education, industry and innovation, agriculture and natural resources, environment, transport or urban and regional economies.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- AEA JEL is a scholarly classification for indexing economics literature, not an accreditation standard or mandated learning-path taxonomy.
- The Society for Computational Economics is an international scholarly/professional society; its descriptions establish a recognized methods field, not a licensing or employment requirement.
- OECD evaluation criteria are an international policy framework, originally for development cooperation and now used more broadly; their use still depends on context and is not a universal economics curriculum.
- World Bank DIME is an institutional impact-evaluation programme; it evidences applied evaluation practice but is not a global occupational standard.
- Southampton, Edinburgh and McGill are institution-specific curricula in the UK and Canada. Their course structures corroborate computational learning content but do not prescribe a universal sequence.
- The six-area count and grouping, especially the Applied and Sector Economics umbrella, are a reasoned proposal. Full catalogue-boundary review and external academic feedback remain outstanding.
