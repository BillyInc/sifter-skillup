## Recommendation

Replace the four occupation-derived buckets with six candidate learning areas: Translation, Localization and Transcreation; Interpreting; Terminology and Language-Services Project Practice; Linguistic Research, Description and Documentation; Applied Linguistics and Language Learning; Computational Linguistics and Language Technology. Treat legal/medical/literary/technical texts, language pairs, interpreting modes, and sign-language modality as contextual pathways or themes unless separate evidence supports an independent, complete learning route. The count is an internal synthesis, not a global standard.

## Candidate areas and boundaries

- **Translation, Localization and Transcreation.** Transfer written content between languages accurately and appropriately, adapting terminology, style, format and cultural references to the audience and use case.
- **Interpreting.** Enable spoken or signed communication across languages in real time or near-real time, using appropriate consecutive, simultaneous or sight-translation methods and professional ethics.
- **Terminology and Language-Services Project Practice.** Build and maintain multilingual terminology resources and coordinate language-service workflows, quality, specialists, clients and delivery.
- **Linguistic Research, Description and Documentation.** Study and document language structure, variation, use and change through descriptive, theoretical, corpus, experimental or field methods.
- **Applied Linguistics and Language Learning.** Use linguistic knowledge to address real language-learning, multilingual communication, assessment and language-in-society needs.
- **Computational Linguistics and Language Technology.** Model human language computationally and build or evaluate language-processing systems, including NLP, speech technologies, machine translation and language data resources.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- FIT is an international federation of member associations; its position papers and descriptions support globally recognized language-service activities but do not create a single global certification or regulator.
- CIOL qualifications and the DPSI are UK-regulated/nationally scoped even when candidates sit internationally; do not present them as universal requirements.
- ACL is an international scientific/professional society; computational linguistics is established, but role titles and curricula vary across employers and institutions.
- LSA proceedings are peer-reviewed scholarly outlets and the cited review/scope sources are not a universal accreditation taxonomy.
- The exact six-area synthesis and the scope of the umbrella applied-linguistics and language-services areas remain internal recommendations; complete cross-domain and student-display review is outstanding.
