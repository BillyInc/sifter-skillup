# D095 — Motor Vehicle Mechanics and Repairs

Global specialization recommendation reconciliation (2026-10-06). This is an internal recommendation, not canonical taxonomy or external academic sign-off.

- **Candidate areas:** 3
- **Evidence URLs:** 5 unique; 4 source records screened as global/international
- **Canonical changes / role assignments:** none / none
- **Geographic scope:** global phase only; Nigerian-specific evidence intentionally deferred

## Recommendation

Replace the five excluded ISCO buckets with three candidate areas: Passenger and Light-Commercial Vehicle Service and Repair; Heavy-Vehicle Service and Repair; and Vehicle Body Repair and Refinishing. The set is grounded in distinct international WorldSkills occupational standards and cross-checked against ASE's separate competency tracks. Keep narrower systems (brakes, tyres, engines, electrical systems) as subject/tool/competency themes within the relevant path unless stronger evidence shows a separate specialization. Do not expand the root to bicycles or rail vehicles; those are not motor vehicles in this domain's stated scope.

## Candidate areas and boundaries

- **Passenger and Light-Commercial Vehicle Service and Repair.** Inspect, diagnose, service and repair passenger cars and light commercial vehicles across their mechanical, electrical and electronic systems.
  - Source claim: WorldSkills Automobile Technology defines a distinct vehicle technology service/repair practice; ASE groups automobile and light-truck competency tests separately from medium-heavy truck and collision repair.
- **Heavy-Vehicle Service and Repair.** Diagnose, maintain and repair heavy road vehicles, including their diesel, drivetrain, brake, hydraulic/pneumatic and electrical/electronic systems.
  - Source claim: WorldSkills Heavy Vehicle Technology explicitly organizes work around heavy-vehicle diesel, hydraulic/pneumatic, brake/undercarriage, electrical/electronic and drivetrain systems; ASE separately identifies medium-heavy truck service tests.
- **Vehicle Body Repair and Refinishing.** Assess collision damage, restore vehicle structures and panels, and prepare and refinish repaired surfaces to safe, specified standards.
  - Source claim: WorldSkills Autobody Repair defines structural/panel diagnosis and repair; WorldSkills Car Painting defines refinishing as a related specialist craft; ASE groups collision repair and refinish as a competency track.

## Source evidence and limits

- [WorldSkills International — WorldSkills Occupational Standards: Automobile Technology (2024)](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1669/) — Occupation profile and standards on vehicle inspection, diagnostic methods, service, repair, mechanical/electrical systems and customer/workshop practice. **Supports:** Passenger/light-vehicle technology service and repair is a distinct occupational practice area with integrated mechanical and electrical diagnostics. **Limit:** International WorldSkills benchmark, not a universal curriculum or mandated taxonomy/count; competition scope represents a bounded occupational profile. Geographic scope: international reference. **Scope screen:** global_or_international_source.
- [National Institute for Automotive Service Excellence (ASE) — ASE Certification Test Series](https://www.ase.com/test-series/) — ASE test-series descriptions distinguish automobiles/light trucks, medium-heavy trucks, collision repair/refinish and systems-level tests such as brakes, electrical/electronic systems, engines and HVAC. **Supports:** Automotive service, heavy-vehicle service and collision/refinish are separately recognized industry competency tracks; narrower vehicle systems can remain within the broader learner specialization rather than each becoming a subgroup. **Limit:** ASE is a U.S.-based voluntary certification framework. Its test categories corroborate work distinctions but are not universal requirements, legal licenses or a global taxonomy. Geographic scope: United States. **Scope screen:** country_or_institution_specific_corroboration.
- [WorldSkills International — WorldSkills Occupational Standards: Heavy Vehicle Technology (2024)](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1679/) — Occupation profile and technical sections covering diesel engines, hydraulic/pneumatic systems, steering, brakes, undercarriage, electrical/electronic systems and drivetrains. **Supports:** Heavy-vehicle maintenance/repair has a distinct systems and equipment context from ordinary passenger/light-vehicle service and is supported as a separate specialization. **Limit:** International occupational benchmark; does not define regulatory permissions or a globally required credential. Geographic scope: international reference. **Scope screen:** global_or_international_source.
- [WorldSkills International — WorldSkills Occupational Standards: Autobody Repair (2024)](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1671/) — Occupation description and sections on structural and panel damage assessment, alignment, repair/replacement, welding/bonding, safe methods and refinishing readiness. **Supports:** Collision/body repair is a coherent vehicle-repair practice distinct from mechanical servicing, with specialized structural diagnosis, panel work and repair methods. **Limit:** International occupational benchmark; some repair rules and OEM procedures vary by vehicle manufacturer and jurisdiction. It does not prescribe a global subgroup count. Geographic scope: international reference. **Scope screen:** global_or_international_source.
- [WorldSkills International — WorldSkills Occupational Standards: Car Painting (2024)](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1718/) — Car-painting occupation profile and standards on surface preparation, paint systems, colour matching/application, finishing and quality inspection. **Supports:** Vehicle refinishing is a recognized specialist craft within collision/body repair; it is grouped with body repair here to avoid splitting one closely linked repair lifecycle into an overly narrow subgroup. **Limit:** WorldSkills occupational competition reference, not a universal training programme or licensing requirement. Geographic scope: international reference. **Scope screen:** global_or_international_source.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- The three-area set is a cross-source synthesis, not a globally ratified taxonomy or academic accreditation decision.
- The WorldSkills standards provide international occupational benchmarks; ASE's framework is U.S.-specific and is corroborative only.
- The canonical role audit must determine whether current D095 rows such as bicycle mechanic, rail/tramway technician, restoration technician and supervisor fit this parent domain at all; this specialization review intentionally does not decide those links.
- EV/hybrid service, motorcycles and commercial fleet specialisms may merit later scoped treatment if the root-domain catalogue and source evidence establish distinct, coherent student areas; no such expansion is inferred from the three reviewed sources.

## Audit boundary

No roles were assigned to these areas. Current subgroup counts, ISCO grouping, role frequency and computational keyword hits were not used to determine candidate count. Nigeria-specific context is explicitly out of scope in this global phase.
