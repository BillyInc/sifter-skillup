# D060 — Banking and Credit Services

Global specialization recommendation reconciliation (2026-10-06). This is an internal recommendation, not canonical taxonomy or external academic sign-off.

- **Candidate areas:** None
- **Evidence URLs:** 5 unique; 0 source records screened as global/international
- **Canonical changes / role assignments:** none / none
- **Geographic scope:** global phase only; Nigerian-specific evidence intentionally deferred

## Recommendation

Do not retain the current four occupationally framed buckets as the student-facing specialization taxonomy. Credit & Lending is independently supported as a coherent learning area by a voluntary international banking-education standard. Payment, clearing and settlement is an independently recognized financial-infrastructure practice area, but BIS/CPMI standards describe infrastructures and controls, not necessarily bank-employee specializations. Basel's governance, risk, capital, credit and abuse-of-financial-services principles are bank-wide supervisory/control topics, not a list of specializations. Recommend a functional taxonomy review with Credit & Lending as a supported candidate; keep customer/account/deposit operations, payment/transaction services and treasury as candidates requiring comparative banking curricula before deciding the final number, exact boundaries or whether they merit separate subgroup nodes. Do not infer a four-area replacement or claim a universal fixed count. Risk, compliance and governance should not be separate subgroups solely because supervisory standards have separate principles; treat them as cross-cutting learning topics unless comparative education evidence demonstrates distinct learner routes.

## Source evidence and limits

- [Global Banking Education Standards Board (GBEStB) — Global Standards](https://www.globalbankingstandards.org/globalstandards) — Page lines/sections: introduction (states there are no definitive internationally agreed answers to all banker knowledge/skills questions); lines 6 and 15 (25 national institutes and international professional education aims); headings for the fourth standard, Credit & Lending, and sixth standard, Treasury Operations; lines 47 and 51 identify the treasury and operational-risk documents as first drafts. **Supports:** Credit & Lending has a named GBEStB education standard; treasury operations has been proposed in a first-draft standard. The Board itself cautions that globally agreed answers to the full banker knowledge/skills question are not definitive. **Limit:** Voluntary professional education standards, not accreditation of a universal subgroup taxonomy. The treasury document is identified as a first draft. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [Global Banking Education Standards Board — Credit & Lending Education and Training for Professional Bankers](https://www.globalbankingstandards.org/s/GBEStB-Credit-Lending-Education-and-Training-Standard-Final-080721-gs84.pdf) — PDF pp. 1-2, Overview/Scope: calls credit and lending a core area of banking and states the standard sets high-level education content without country-wise product/delivery detail; pp. 4-7, Key Aspects and Advanced Learners: sound lending, borrower/facility types, credit appraisal/risk, monitoring/impaired assets, with advanced export/project finance and portfolio topics. **Supports:** Credit and lending is a coherent, separately teachable professional education area and can be considered as a distinct candidate subgroup under this parent. **Limit:** The standard guides member bodies and others; it explicitly leaves country-specific products/regulation to local context. It does not prescribe the subgroup structure/count for this catalogue or establish that every learner needs an advanced credit path. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, hosted by the Bank for International Settlements — Core Principles for effective banking supervision](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework/standard/bcp?allChapters=true) — BCP 01.1-02.3: de facto minimum standard and universal applicability. Principle 14, BCP40.32-33: corporate governance; Principle 15, BCP40.34: comprehensive, bank-wide risk management; Principle 17, BCP40.39: credit risk including the full credit life cycle; Principles 18-26: problem exposures, concentration, related parties, country/transfer, market, banking-book interest-rate, liquidity, operational risk/resilience and internal controls; Principle 29, BCP40.66: customer due diligence/abuse of financial services. **Supports:** Risk, governance, operational resilience and financial-crime controls are broad, connected bank-wide supervisory dimensions; credit lifecycle is distinct enough to be specified separately. The Core Principles apply across bank types and jurisdictions. **Limit:** This is a supervisory framework describing expected bank controls and supervisor assessment, not a professional curriculum, occupational classification or student specialization count. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures (BIS) and International Organization of Securities Commissions — Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures](https://www.bis.org/committees/cpmi/pfmi/overview) — Overview and scope; list of principles, including legal basis, governance, comprehensive risk management, settlement finality and money settlements. The overview defines covered infrastructures as payment systems, central securities depositories, securities settlement systems, central counterparties and trade repositories. **Supports:** Payments, clearing and settlement infrastructures are a distinct technical/operational practice area with specialized standards and risks. **Limit:** The standards govern financial market infrastructures, not all bank payment-service workers; they do not prove that payments must be a separate specialization beneath D060 rather than a neighboring domain or an area within broader banking operations. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.
- [Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, hosted by the Bank for International Settlements — Principles for the Management of Credit Risk](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d595.pdf) — Overview and table of contents, principles grouped into: (i) credit-risk environment; (ii) sound credit-granting process; (iii) credit administration, measurement and monitoring; (iv) adequate controls. The 2025 revised principles page describes these four evaluation areas. **Supports:** Credit management comprises connected lifecycle/control activities and is not four independent banking specializations by default. **Limit:** The four elements are supervisory assessment areas for credit-risk management; copying them into subgroup records would confuse control dimensions with student-facing specialization taxonomy. **Scope screen:** geographic_scope_not_established.

## Open limits / reconciliation notes

- The source set is globally oriented, but sources answer different questions: professional education (GBEStB), bank supervision (BCBS) and infrastructure standards (CPMI/IOSCO). They must not be treated as interchangeable curriculum evidence.
- No country-specific evidence was used to define global specialization structure. Local financial law, products and licensing remain jurisdiction-dependent and should be clearly tagged if added in a later enrichment phase.
- The final count and flat-versus-split decision remain open pending comparative banking curricula that distinguish common bank foundations from additive learner pathways in account/deposit services, payments and treasury.
- No roles, ISCO memberships, role counts or role-to-specialization links were used as evidence.
