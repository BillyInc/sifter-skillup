## Parent scope and disposition

ICOM's museum definition covers research, collecting, conservation, interpretation and exhibition; archive/records and archaeological fieldwork have distinct methods, ethics and standards. ## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Museum Collections, Curation and Interpretation** — Collections research, stewardship, documentation, curation, exhibitions and public interpretation in museums. Boundary: Do not conflate collection-based museum work with archives or library services.
- **Conservation and Collection Preservation** — Preventive/conservation practice for objects, collections and heritage materials, including condition assessment and preservation planning. Boundary: Conservation specialization/qualification differs by material and jurisdiction; conservation is not simply art restoration.
- **Archives and Documentary Heritage** — Appraisal, arrangement, description, preservation, access and stewardship of records and archival collections. Boundary: Records management and archival preservation overlap but may have distinct lifecycle mandates and professional standards.
- **Archaeology and Heritage Field Research** — Material-culture field research, excavation/survey, analysis and stewardship of archaeological heritage. Boundary: Archaeology is a research discipline with legal/ethical permissions; it should not be treated as an ordinary museum subgroup by default.
- **Museum Learning and Community Engagement** — Education, interpretation, accessible visitor learning and community participation connected to collections and heritage. Boundary: The ICOM museum purpose supports these functions; education professions and general event/hospitality management remain distinct.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not keep generic hospitality/retail managers or clerical support as specialist labels. Do not split each collection type/material or visitor segment into a subgroup. Keep libraries, archives, archaeology, conservation and museums distinct where tasks/standards differ, while showing evidence-backed interdisciplinary paths.
- Computational signal: Digital collection documentation, digital preservation, 3D recording/reconstruction, GIS/remote sensing and computational archaeology are meaningful methods. They are not interchangeable and should be attached to the specific collection/research practice they support.
- Jurisdiction note: Heritage ownership, excavation permits, repatriation, archive-access/privacy law, conservation practice and protected titles vary across countries and communities. ICOM is global professional guidance, not national law or a licensing body.

## Sources and limits

- [International Council of Museums (ICOM): Museum Definition](https://icom.museum/en/resources/standards-guidelines/museum-definition/) — The adopted definition names research, collecting, conserving, interpreting and exhibiting tangible/intangible heritage, with education, knowledge-sharing, community participation and accessibility. Supports: Direct global professional definition for major museum practice functions and the museum parent boundary. Limitation: Definition states institutional mission, not a curriculum or exact subgroup count.
- [ICOM Documentation Committee: Museum Documentation Definition](https://icom-documentation.mini.icom.museum/standards/museum-documentation-definition/) — Describes documentation as supporting research, interpretation, collections management, audience engagement, conservation, curation, exhibition, education, administration and access. Supports: Supports documentation as an integrative museum practice and a computational/digital evidence area. Limitation: Committee practice definition, not an exhaustive profession taxonomy.
