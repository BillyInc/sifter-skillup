# D135: Nuclear Engineering and Radiation Science — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_three_candidate_routes_clinical_boundary_explicit`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: Science and engineering professionals (2 roles); Health professionals (2 roles); Science and engineering associate professionals (3 roles); Health associate professionals (2 roles)

Current records are broad science/health ISCO groups; they mix nuclear engineering, radiation applications and healthcare occupations and are not suitable specialization cards.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The title spans engineering of nuclear systems and scientific/technical work involving ionizing radiation. Keep peaceful nuclear systems, fuel-cycle and radiation-science applications in view, but distinguish medical physics/radiation therapy as D137 unless the product later revises domain boundaries with evidence.

Three candidate learning routes are supported as a defensible navigation synthesis: ABET's nuclear/radiological curriculum topics explicitly include systems/processes, fuel cycles, detection/measurement and design; IAEA sources separately emphasize nuclear energy and radiation protection/safety competence. The precise route count is not prescribed by ABET or IAEA and needs curriculum/role validation before canonical implementation.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Nuclear Energy Systems and Reactor Engineering** — Nuclear physics and systems, reactor concepts/design, thermal-hydraulics, plant systems, operation and safety analysis for nuclear energy applications. Boundary: Power/reactor engineering route; not a universal promise that every learner can obtain reactor-site authorization.
- **Nuclear Fuel Cycle, Materials and Waste Systems** — Fuel materials and cycle, fuel fabrication/use, safeguards-relevant technical systems, radioactive waste and decommissioning engineering/science. Boundary: A distinct candidate only when the curriculum/role path includes fuel-cycle and material-system depth; nuclear materials can also support reactor engineering.
- **Radiation Science, Measurement and Protection** — Radiation interactions and detection, dosimetry, measurement/instrumentation, radiation protection and non-medical industrial/research applications. Boundary: Do not absorb clinical medical physics, diagnostic imaging or radiation therapy; those require health-science training and belong to the separately scoped medical-physics domain.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not present nuclear physics, reactor operations, radiation protection, dosimetry and waste management as unrelated cards when they form connected learning routes. Conversely, do not merge clinical medical radiation practice into this engineering/science root. Safeguards, licensing and regulated operator authority need dedicated contextual labels.
- Computational signals: Reactor-physics simulation, Monte Carlo transport, computational materials and risk modeling are cross-cutting methods supporting routes; they do not form a separate specialization here by themselves.
- Jurisdiction note: Nuclear facility authorization, operator licensing, radiation-worker qualification, safeguards implementation and permitted source use depend on national law and the facility regulator. IAEA standards guide Member States but are implemented through national frameworks.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [ABET / American Nuclear Society: Criteria for Accrediting Engineering Programs, 2025–2026](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/accreditation-criteria/criteria-for-accrediting-engineering-programs-2025-2026/) — Lines 726–744: nuclear/radiological programs include mathematics, nuclear physics, radiation interaction, systems/processes, fuel cycles, radiation detection/measurement and system design. Supports: Shows distinct connected technical topic families underpinning candidate engineering/science routes. Limit: US program-accreditation criteria; not a global occupational license or exact subgroup schema.
- [International Atomic Energy Agency: Education and Training Appraisal for Radiation Protection and the Safety of Radiation Sources (EduTA)](https://gnssn.iaea.org/main/Pages/Capacity%20Building/Education_and_Training.aspx) — IAEA describes education/training frameworks for nuclear/radiation safety, radiation protection and safety of radiation sources, including competence-needs assessment for Member States. Supports: Confirms radiation protection and nuclear safety are specialized competence contexts that require systematic training. Limit: IAEA assistance framework; exact authorization and curricula remain Member-State responsibilities.
- [International Atomic Energy Agency: Global Education Resources](https://gnssn.iaea.org/main/getr/Pages/EducationResources.aspx) — Catalogues nuclear education programmes by topic and country, including nuclear engineering/energy, radiation protection and nuclear/radiation safety. Supports: Demonstrates the real-world coexistence of nuclear-energy and radiation-safety educational routes across multiple countries. Limit: Catalogued offerings are examples, not exhaustive or a prescribed global taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
