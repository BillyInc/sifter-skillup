The four records include two potentially relevant engineering/software groups, one broad technical grade and one unrelated trade family. The current architecture blends vehicle engineering, automated-driving software and system safety/validation; role membership alone cannot confirm subgroup fit.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain the technical system domain. Treat functions such as perception, localization, planning/control, vehicle integration, safety assurance and testing as candidate practice areas only after curriculum/role evidence confirms distinct pathways. SAE J3016 levels describe driving-automation system capability and human roles, not a learning ladder or job hierarchy.

SAE's J3016 scope provides a taxonomy for levels of driving automation based on which parts of the dynamic driving task the system performs and when; SAE's summary explicitly states the levels are descriptive, not normative, and imply no order of market introduction. NHTSA separately discusses ADS development, testing, validation and safety. This supports the correction that SAE levels are capability descriptors, not student progression. Four candidate technical routes organize system work, but require role/task and curriculum validation before adoption.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Perception, Sensing and Scene Understanding** — Build and validate sensing systems and algorithms that detect, classify and track road users, objects and driving conditions. Boundary: Sensor/ML software, hardware and operational design domain assumptions must be connected but not presumed to be one role.
- **Localization, Mapping and Vehicle State Estimation** — Estimate vehicle position and motion and maintain/use maps and localization systems for automated driving. Boundary: Mapping and localization are technical functions, not SAE automation levels.
- **Planning, Control and Automated Driving Software** — Plan safe paths and control vehicle motion within system constraints and the operational design domain. Boundary: Software control and vehicle dynamics interfaces are distinct learning additions; no shortcut from generic Python/C++ to job readiness is implied.
- **Vehicle Integration, Safety and Verification** — Integrate automated-driving components with the vehicle and verify system performance, safety and operational constraints. Boundary: ISO 26262 is a functional-safety standard, not a certification of a person; regulatory approval and system assurance are separate.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not use SAE levels as specialization names or course sequence. Do not create a branch for every sensor, vehicle type, autonomy level or software stack. Keep driver assistance, automated driving, robotics and conventional automotive engineering boundaries explicit.
- Computational signal: Machine learning/perception, sensor fusion, state estimation, simulation, planning/control, mapping and safety-data analysis are central computational methods in specific branches; programming languages and platforms are tools, not universal job guarantees.
- Jurisdiction note: Vehicle safety regulation, testing permits, road deployment, liability and type approval are jurisdiction-specific. SAE is an international standards organization; NHTSA material is US regulatory context. ISO 26262 is a standard, not an individual certification.

## Sources and limits

- [SAE International: J3016: Taxonomy and Definitions for Terms Related to Driving Automation Systems](https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/j3016_201609-taxonomy-definitions-terms-related-driving-automation-systems-road-motor-vehicles) — Scope describes taxonomy for systems performing part/all of the dynamic driving task and levels 0–5; classification depends on the engaged feature. Supports: Correct interpretation of automation levels as system-function descriptors, not a human learning or career sequence. Limitation: Standard scope defines terminology, not curriculum, job families or a globally required credential.
- [SAE International: Summary of SAE Levels of Driving Automation for On-Road Vehicles](https://www.sae.org/binaries/content/assets/cm/content/news/press-releases/pathway-to-autonomy/automated_driving.pdf) — States levels are descriptive rather than normative and technical rather than legal, and imply no particular order of market introduction. Supports: Direct evidence against treating SAE levels as a student's progression ladder. Limitation: Summary document is not a technical curriculum or competence framework.
- [NHTSA: Automated Vehicle Safety](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/automated-vehicle-safety) — Explains driver assistance vs automated driving responsibility and notes development, safe testing and validation; consumer availability and restrictions vary by level/context. Supports: System safety, operational limitations and human/system responsibility boundary. Limitation: US regulator source; policy/legal details are US-specific, not global rules.

The sources support review candidates and boundaries, not a universal required subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as evidence of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
