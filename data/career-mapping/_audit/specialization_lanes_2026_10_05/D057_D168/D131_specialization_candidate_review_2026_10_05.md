# D131: Motorsport Engineering — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_routes_not_standardized`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Vehicle & Powertrain Engineering (13 roles); Information and communications technology professionals (1 role); Production & Test Technicians (12 roles)

ISCO-derived job families mix an application domain (vehicle/powertrain), a generic ICT major group and a technician/production family; they do not describe clean motorsport specialization routes.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Keep this as engineering applied to competition vehicles and their competition use. It overlaps aerospace/automotive engineering at the technical-method level, but the competition-vehicle context, regulation, rapid iteration and trackside performance distinguish its application. Separate motorsport engineering from race operations/driver services and general vehicle manufacturing.

Three learner-route candidates are supported by the technical topics in established motorsport-engineering education and FIA technical activity, but these are a product taxonomy synthesis, not an internationally standardized specialty count. Replace the present ISCO-family display with these routes only after a separate taxonomy approval; do not alter canonical records in this review.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Competition Vehicle Design and Performance** — Vehicle architecture, aerodynamics, structures/materials, vehicle dynamics, tyres and simulation as applied to competition vehicles. Boundary: A coherent engineering route; individual topics such as CFD, composites or tyres are learning modules, not separate specialization cards.
- **Motorsport Powertrain and Energy Systems** — Competition power units, drivetrains, controls, electronics and energy-management integration. Boundary: Keep distinct from general vehicle design only where the learner path adds powertrain/energy-system depth; current series rules are time- and series-specific.
- **Race Engineering, Data and Vehicle Operations** — Use telemetry, data acquisition, simulation and engineering analysis to support setup, testing and competition decisions. Boundary: A supported applied route, but team titles and responsibilities vary; do not treat all data/ICT occupations in the domain as motorsport engineering roles.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split aerodynamics, CFD, composites, tyre science, electronics and data acquisition into one-card specializations: they are interacting vehicle engineering themes. Keep race operations, sporting administration and driver coaching outside unless the parent scope is deliberately broadened.
- Computational signals: CFD, vehicle-dynamics simulation, telemetry, data acquisition and optimization are cross-cutting methods/tools used across the first and third routes, not standalone computational domains here.
- Jurisdiction note: Competition eligibility, technical regulations, safety requirements and homologation differ by sanctioning series and change over time. Link regulations to the selected series/year; the FIA is authoritative for its championships, not every motorsport series.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Cranfield University: Advanced Motorsport Engineering MSc](https://www.cranfield.ac.uk/motorsport) — Programme description and technical focus: aerodynamics/CFD, composites, structures, powertrain, vehicle dynamics, simulation, data acquisition, electronics and tyre modelling. Supports: Shows coherent engineering themes and their integration in a specialist motorsport education route. Limit: One UK university programme, not a global professional taxonomy or proof of universal route count.
- [Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile: FIA Activity Report 2024: Safety and Technological Development](https://activityreport2024.fia.com/sport-championships/safety-and-technological-development-2/) — Reports technical expertise and working groups spanning powertrain, electronics, data systems, performance analysis, energy management and aerodynamics. Supports: Confirms these are substantive, interacting motorsport engineering themes in FIA championship contexts. Limit: FIA remit and report reflect particular championships and current regulation cycles; not a universal education standard.
