# D107 — Leatherworking, Shoemaking and Leather Goods: specialization candidate review

**Disposition:** source_gap_closed_candidate_boundary_or_count_remains_provisional

**Parent scope basis:** Leather technology and shoemaking covers turning hides into leather and making footwear, bags and other leather goods. Tanners process hides; makers and machine operators cut, stitch, last and finish shoes, including bespoke and orthopaedic footwear; technicians test quality while designers and managers develop products and run production. Employers include tanneries, footwear factories, workshops and leather goods shops.

## Candidate student-facing areas

1. **Leather Processing and Finishing** — Prepare hides/skins and process, tan and finish leather material.
2. **Footwear Design, Construction and Repair** — Develop patterns and construct, fit, repair or finish footwear, including specialist footwear where evidence supports it.
3. **Leather Goods, Saddlery and Accessories** — Cut, form, stitch, assemble and finish bags, saddlery and other leather accessories.

## Decision

Three output/practice candidates are supported for internal student-facing taxonomy. Footwear is separately corroborated as an occupational learning route, but the three-area count remains an internal synthesis, not a global standard. Source-gap disposition recorded as `footwear_route_now_independently_corroborated_but_three_area_set_remains_provisional`.

## Parent boundaries and exclusions

- Leather processing is upstream material production; footwear is a dedicated product route; other leather goods share construction methods but have distinct product outcomes.
- Do not treat sales, warehouse, generic quality-inspection or management positions as specializations.
- England occupational standards corroborate a route; UK credentials/regulations are not universal.
