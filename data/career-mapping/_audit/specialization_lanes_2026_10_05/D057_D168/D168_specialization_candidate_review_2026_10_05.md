# D168: Esports and Creator Economy — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_conflates_esports_and_emerging_creator_work_split_recommended`. Evidence review only; not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **6**. Current records: Esports Operations & Management (1); Esports Community & Partnerships (2); Esports Broadcast & Content Production (4); Esports Team & Player Management (2); Competitive Performance & Analysis (3); Information and communications technicians (1)

The six groups are mostly esports-oriented and include one generic broadcast technician class. They do not capture creator-economy work as an equivalent specialization system. The parent joins organized competitive gaming (leagues, teams, events, broadcast) with cross-platform individual/collective digital-content businesses; these have overlapping media skills but distinct markets and operating models.

## Parent scope and disposition

Treat the combined root as structurally suspect. Prefer distinct Esports and Digital Creator/Platform Work domains or clearly separated parent branches before accepting subgroup counts. Esports has organized competition and governing/event structures; creator work spans platforms and forms including video, streaming, music, writing and digital services. Creator economy is an emerging umbrella, not a globally standardized profession taxonomy.

IESF describes itself as an international esports federation with member nations and organized championships; Olympic Movement materials recognize organized esports events. These support competition/event structures, not a universal job taxonomy. OECD/ILO/EU guidance provides a conceptual framework for digital-platform work but does not establish a creator-economy profession family. The current six labels are largely esports-specific and omit creator work; therefore the combined parent should be split or clearly partitioned before deciding a count. Four candidate practice areas are provisional; the creator branch has weaker taxonomy evidence and is explicitly not an accepted global subgroup.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Esports Competition and Event Operations** — Organize tournaments, competition calendars, rules, venues/platforms, production logistics and event delivery. Boundary: IESF/competition institutions support organized competitive activity; publisher/league rules can differ by game and event.
- **Esports Teams, Players and Performance** — Support competitive teams and players through coaching, training, performance analysis, team operations and player welfare. Boundary: Do not infer licensed sports-science, clinical or counseling competence from an esports team role.
- **Esports Broadcast, Content and Audience Partnerships** — Produce and distribute competition coverage, commentary/editorial content, audience engagement and commercial partnerships. Boundary: Shares media work with creator practice but remains anchored in organized esports properties/events.
- **Digital Creator and Platform-Based Content Work (provisional; separate-parent review)** — Create, distribute and sustain audience-facing digital content or services through one or more online platforms and related monetization/partnerships. Boundary: OECD/ILO/EU measurement guidance defines platform work broadly, not a creator occupation family. Platform monetization, analytics and eligibility are platform-specific; do not imply guaranteed income or universal career ladders.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not treat game titles, platforms, creator niches, monetization features or content formats as separate specializations. Do not merge esports player, broadcast, community, event and creator roles merely because each can publish online. Do not invent a universal creator role or credential; evidence for creator work is platform- and market-dependent.
- Computational signal: Broadcast pipelines, audience analytics, platform dashboards, moderation systems, competitive game telemetry and creator analytics support selected work. These are use-case-specific and platform-dependent, not common requirements or proof of a formal computational specialization.
- Jurisdiction note: Esports governance, labor status, safeguarding, gambling/anti-doping rules, IP rights, sponsorship disclosure, tax and platform monetization vary by game, country and platform. IESF and Olympic materials have institutional scope; OECD platform-work definitions are statistical, not legal employment determinations. No Nigeria-only evidence is generalized globally.

## Sources and limits

- [International Esports Federation (IESF): International Esports Federation](https://www.iesf.org/) — Identifies IESF as a global esports federation, describes member nations and world championships, and lists official game titles. Supports: Organized competitive esports, federation and event context. Limitation: Federation remit does not define a complete global occupation or subgroup taxonomy; game titles and rules evolve.
- [International Olympic Committee (IOC): The Olympic Esports Games](https://img.olympics.com/images/image/private/fl_attachment/primary/ta3mdy5mqdmxakymp98i.pdf) — IOC document describes esports' participation in organized Olympic Movement events and the development of the Olympic Esports Games concept. Supports: Institutional evidence that esports includes organized competitive events and governance beyond individual content creation. Limitation: IOC initiatives do not define all esports careers or validate a universal specialization structure.
- [OECD, International Labour Organization and European Commission: Handbook on Measuring Digital Platform Employment and Work](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/handbook-on-measuring-digital-platform-employment-and-work_0ddcac3b-en.html) — Provides a conceptual framework and definition for productive work organized through digital platforms; notes the variety of platform work and statistical measurement limits. Supports: Cross-platform employment/work is a broad and varied category, supporting caution about treating Creator Economy as one settled occupation taxonomy. Limitation: Statistical measurement framework does not establish job titles, skill requirements or creator-specific career pathways.
- [YouTube Help: Get started with YouTube Analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9002587?hl=en-GB) — Explains platform-specific channel/video analytics and revenue reporting, including eligibility and programme-dependent features. Supports: Concrete example that creator tools and monetization are specific to platform features and participation rules. Limitation: Single-platform documentation; not generalizable as a universal creator skill or income pathway.

Evidence supports candidate distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
