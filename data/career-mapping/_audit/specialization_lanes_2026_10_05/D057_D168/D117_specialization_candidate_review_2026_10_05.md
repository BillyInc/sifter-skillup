# D117: Railway — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_recommended`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **8**. Current records: 21 Science and engineering professionals; 23 Teaching professionals; 31 Science and engineering associate professionals; 74 Electrical and electronic trades workers; 75 Food processing, wood working, garment and other craft and related trades workers; 82 Assemblers; 83 Drivers and mobile plant operators; 93 Labourers in mining, construction, manufacturing and transport

Eight ISCO-derived occupation families are not railway areas of specialization and include broad cross-sector families. Count is therefore not meaningful as a student taxonomy count.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Railway is a system domain spanning infrastructure, energy/traction, control-command-signalling, rolling stock, and operation/traffic management. Passenger versus freight can be route-specific but is not necessarily a separate foundation-level specialization; these system layers are better supported as the initial candidate partition.

Five system-oriented candidates align with the EU Agency for Railways' named railway subsystems and provide coherent technical/operational routes. They are a system architecture used as a taxonomy aid, not an internationally mandated subgroup count. Verify parent excludes transit modes not represented by 'railway' if local scope intends narrower coverage.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Rail Infrastructure and Track Systems** — Track, civil structures, stations, fixed installations and maintenance engineering.
- **Rail Traction Power and Energy Systems** — Power supply, traction substations, overhead or third-rail systems and energy management.
- **Rail Signalling, Train Control and Communications** — Train detection, interlocking, control-command, communications and safety systems.
- **Rolling Stock and Rail Vehicle Systems** — Locomotive/passenger/freight vehicle subsystems, maintenance, brakes, doors, traction and onboard systems.
- **Rail Operations and Traffic Management** — Train operation, timetable/traffic management, dispatch and railway undertaking/infrastructure-manager procedures.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not make separate child cards for each occupation (driver, dispatcher, conductor, shunter) or railway technology brand. Freight/passenger operations and high-speed/urban rail are route contexts unless distinct learning paths are evidenced.
- Computational signals: Signalling/control, predictive maintenance, traffic optimization and digital railway are computational applications within those system areas, not extra computational-specialization cards by default.
- Jurisdiction note: Rail safety authorization, signaling rules, driver licensing, standards adoption and interoperability vary by rail system and jurisdiction. EU TSI evidence is comparative, not a universal legal rule.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [European Union Agency for Railways: Technical Specifications for Interoperability (TSIs)](https://www.era.europa.eu/fi/node/535) — ERA identifies railway subsystems: infrastructure, energy, control-command-signalling, rolling stock, and operation/traffic management. Supports: System-based candidate areas correspond to separately governed technical subsystems. Limit: EU interoperability framework; subsystem architecture is informative globally but its legal rules apply in the EU scope.
- [WorldSkills International: Rail Vehicle Technology](https://worldskills.org/skills/id/788/) — Skill profile identifies maintenance of rail vehicles and knowledge spanning high-voltage electricity, brakes, air conditioning, doors and wheels. Supports: Rolling-stock learning has a coherent distinct systems focus. Limit: International vocational competition standard, not a complete railway academic taxonomy.
- [European Union Agency for Railways: Operation and Traffic Management TSI](https://www.era.europa.eu/domains/technical-specifications-interoperability/operation-and-traffic-management-tsi_en) — Scope covers train-operation processes/procedures and staff executing safety-critical tasks. Supports: Operations/traffic management is distinguishable from infrastructure and equipment disciplines. Limit: EU regulation; safety-critical competence and authorization details are jurisdiction-specific.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
