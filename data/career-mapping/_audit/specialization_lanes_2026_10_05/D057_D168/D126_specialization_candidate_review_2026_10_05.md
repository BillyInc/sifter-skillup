# D126: Complementary, Alternative and Traditional Medicine — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_no_universal_specialization_set`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: 22 Health professionals; 32 Health associate professionals

Generic occupation-major groups cannot represent the many distinct traditional, complementary and integrative medicine (TCIM) systems, modalities and country-specific legal scopes.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: This umbrella covers systems and practices with different origins, evidence bases, educational traditions and regulatory status. A single universal list of subgroups would flatten culturally distinct knowledge systems and imply false equivalence. The root label should prefer WHO's current umbrella terminology (Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine) while allowing region-specific aliases.

No universal specialization card set is justified at this level. Build a jurisdiction- and tradition-specific route only when the specific system/practice, source curriculum, evidence and legal scope are identified. WHO's global strategy supports a common foundation around evidence, safety, regulation, cultural respect and sustainability, not an agreed universal modality taxonomy.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- No separate specialization is justified by the reviewed evidence; use the explicit disposition below.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Acupuncture, herbal medicine, Ayurveda, traditional Chinese medicine, chiropractic, yoga and other mind-body/manual practices are not interchangeable. Do not combine them solely under CAM; do not invent a common role title or imply efficacy. Modalities should appear as distinct, carefully attributed contexts only when source and regulation justify it.
- Computational signals: Evidence synthesis, pharmacovigilance and digital health are cross-cutting methods; none is a TCIM specialization by itself.
- Jurisdiction note: Practitioner registration, title protection, scope, product regulation, referral and permitted claims vary by country and sometimes subnationally. Every practice route must show jurisdiction and evidence status.
- No-specialization disposition: Do not offer universal TCIM subgroups under the current umbrella. Require an explicitly named tradition/practice and local authoritative curriculum/regulatory context before creating a specialization route.

## Evidence and limits

- [World Health Organization: Global Traditional Medicine Strategy 2025–2034](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/traditional-complementary-and-integrative-medicine/global-strategies) — Four strategic objectives: evidence base, appropriate regulation/safe effective provision, health-system integration and cross-sector value; emphasizes cultural respect and Indigenous Peoples' rights. Supports: Common cross-cutting foundation and need to keep diverse practice systems distinct/contextual. Limit: Global strategy; not an endorsement of every therapy, a credential standard or a subgroup taxonomy.
- [World Health Organization: Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine topic overview](https://www.who.int/Health-Topics/Traditional-Complementary-and-Integrative-Medicine) — Defines integrative medicine as evidence-based combination and reports that Member-State policy/implementation varies. Supports: Umbrella terminology and regulation/evidence variability. Limit: WHO thematic overview does not determine which individual modalities are effective or lawful in a jurisdiction.
- [World Health Organization: Traditional medicine: questions and answers](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/traditional-medicine) — Definitions distinguish traditional, complementary and herbal medicine; notes global variation in use and evidence. Supports: Terminology and diversity boundaries. Limit: Public information source, not a practice credential or clinical efficacy determination.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
