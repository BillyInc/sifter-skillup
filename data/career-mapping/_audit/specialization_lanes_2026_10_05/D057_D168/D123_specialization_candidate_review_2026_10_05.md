# D123: Radiography and Medical Imaging — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_root_boundary_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: 22 Health professionals; 32 Health associate professionals

Both child records are generic occupational families, not imaging specializations. Their presence cannot establish correct imaging roles or child count.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The parent combines diagnostic imaging with radiography and mentions radiation treatment. Diagnostic imaging modalities can form learning routes; radiation therapy/dosimetry is a distinct therapeutic discipline and should be excluded or the root explicitly broadened. Role authorization varies by jurisdiction and modality.

Six modality candidates are useful for learner route choice, but they are an internal display synthesis. ASRT lists modality-specific practice standards and professions, while EFRS offers European comparative material; neither is global law. Mammography may be nested within diagnostic imaging in some systems. Keep radiation therapy/dosimetry out of this domain pending root-scope decision.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Diagnostic Radiography and Fluoroscopy** — Projection X-ray and fluoroscopic imaging, patient positioning, image acquisition and radiation protection.
- **Computed Tomography (CT)** — Cross-sectional X-ray imaging protocols, acquisition, reconstruction basics and patient/radiation safety.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)** — MR acquisition and protocols, patient screening, device/zone safety and image quality.
- **Medical Ultrasound** — Ultrasound acquisition and image optimization for clinically authorized examinations.
- **Nuclear Medicine Imaging** — Radionuclide-based diagnostic imaging and related radiation-safety practice.
- **Mammography and Breast Imaging** — Breast imaging and mammographic technique/quality, with pathway boundaries depending on local training structure.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Interventional, cardiac/vascular, bone densitometry, limited practice and radiologist-assistant pathways are not automatically separate global specializations. Validate whether they warrant routes after comparing multi-region education/professional sources. Do not call modality cards globally licensed occupations.
- Computational signals: Image reconstruction, AI-assisted imaging, PACS/RIS and quantitative imaging are cross-cutting methods/tools, not specializations themselves.
- Jurisdiction note: Scope of practice, modality credentials, radiation permits, contrast administration and supervision are jurisdiction-specific. ASRT and EFRS provide regional evidence, not universal licensing rules.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [American Society of Radiologic Technologists: Practice Standards for Medical Imaging and Radiation Therapy](https://www.asrt.org/main/standards-and-regulations/professional-practice/practice-standards-online) — Names distinct professional practice areas: CT, MRI, mammography, nuclear medicine, radiation therapy, radiography and sonography; standards include specific criteria by discipline. Supports: Modality-specific learning/work pathways exist and some are distinct professions in a major professional framework. Limit: United States professional practice framework; not universal education/licensure law.
- [European Federation of Radiographer Societies: European Qualifications Framework (EQF) Benchmarking Document for Radiographers](https://www.efrs.eu/publications/) — Professional education/qualification resources address radiographer competencies and European qualification context. Supports: Independent regional comparison is needed; radiography education is competency-based and role scope is not identical across regions. Limit: European framework; not a worldwide standard or detailed mandate for the exact proposed six cards.
- [World Health Organization: Global Initiative on Radiation Safety in Health Care Settings](https://www.who.int/initiatives/global-initiative-on-radiation-safety-in-health-care-settings) — WHO distinguishes diagnostic radiology, nuclear medicine procedures and radiotherapy treatment as major medical radiation uses. Supports: Diagnostic imaging and radiation therapy are related but distinct service/practice areas, supporting a root-boundary correction. Limit: Radiation-safety initiative; not a profession or subgroup taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
