# D161: Humanitarian Aid, Emergency Response and International Development — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_blends_response_and_development_candidate_routes_provisional`. Evidence review only; not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **10**. Current records: Production and specialised services managers (1); Health professionals (1); Business and administration professionals (3); ICT professionals (1); Legal, social and cultural professionals (1); Health associate professionals (2); Legal/social/cultural associate professionals (1); Customer services clerks (1); Protective services workers (2); Food/craft trades workers (1)

Ten active groups are ISCO occupation classes rather than areas of specialization. They combine humanitarian coordination, health delivery, procurement, information systems, social protection and security roles; counts do not establish disciplinary fit. The root also joins acute humanitarian response to longer-horizon international development.

## Parent scope and disposition

Keep humanitarian response and development connected only as a broad cross-sector umbrella; expose the operational distinction. Humanitarian work is needs-based, crisis-context response and protection; international development includes longer-term systems, policy and programme work. Do not imply all humanitarian practitioners work across every technical sector.

OCHA describes a six-step cycle (analysis, planning, resource mobilization, implementation, monitoring/evaluation and reporting), while Sphere organizes technical response standards into distinct sectors and cross-cutting quality/accountability foundations. These support functional candidate paths and show why ten occupation-grade groups are not the right student specialization labels. The combined parent needs a visible acute-response vs longer-term development split; four candidates are provisional, not a universal taxonomy.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Humanitarian Needs Assessment, Analysis and Response Planning** — Coordinate needs evidence, prioritization, response objectives and inter-agency plans across a crisis response. Boundary: OCHA's Humanitarian Programme Cycle supports these functions. It is a coordination/management practice, not a single technical sector.
- **Emergency Operations and Humanitarian Coordination** — Coordinate operational response, actors, information, logistics and adaptation during emergencies. Boundary: Operational safety, security and mandated authority depend on context; keep police/military or clinical roles distinct.
- **Humanitarian Sector Programme Delivery** — Design and deliver technical assistance in a defined sector such as health, WASH, shelter, food security or protection. Boundary: Sphere identifies distinct response sectors and standards; each needs its own domain-level technical branch, not one generic aid subgroup.
- **Recovery, Resilience and International Development Programmes** — Plan and manage longer-term development, recovery, resilience and institutional-capacity programmes. Boundary: Development is not simply the next stage of every emergency response; donor, national and community contexts shape practice.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not make every humanitarian cluster, country crisis, aid modality, job title or beneficiary group a subgroup. Keep technical sectors distinct where learning requirements diverge. Do not absorb security forces or licensed health professions solely because they can work in emergencies.
- Computational signal: Needs assessment, geospatial information management, response monitoring, logistics and early-warning analytics are computational use cases. They are not shared prerequisites for every humanitarian or development path.
- Jurisdiction note: Humanitarian principles and global standards are broadly applicable; legal mandates, disaster-management authorities, donor rules, access/security and country development systems are context-specific. Nothing here is Nigeria-specific.

## Sources and limits

- [United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA): About the Humanitarian Programme Cycle](https://knowledge.base.unocha.org/wiki/spaces/hpc/pages/3993075713) — Overview states the six-step HPC: analysis, planning, resource mobilization, implementation, monitoring/evaluation and reporting; describes coordinated humanitarian action. Supports: Global humanitarian programme functions and the distinction between coordination/management work and technical sector delivery. Limitation: Operational cycle, not a prescribed educational specialization taxonomy.
- [Sphere: What are humanitarian standards?](https://spherestandards.org/humanitarian-standards/) — Lists the Sphere technical response areas: WASH, food security and nutrition, shelter and settlement, and health; explains standards as consensus global good practice. Supports: Evidence that humanitarian response contains distinct technical sectors and shared accountability foundations. Limitation: Sphere covers core standards and not every humanitarian, protection or development discipline.
- [OECD: DAC Criteria for Evaluating Development Assistance](https://www.oecd.org/dac/evaluation/daccriteriaforevaluatingdevelopmentassistance.htm) — Defines the international development evaluation criteria and programme-assessment frame. Supports: Longer-horizon development programme evaluation as a distinguishable practice from emergency response operations. Limitation: Evaluation criteria do not define the full development profession or specialties.

The sources support review candidates and boundaries, not a universal required subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as evidence of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
