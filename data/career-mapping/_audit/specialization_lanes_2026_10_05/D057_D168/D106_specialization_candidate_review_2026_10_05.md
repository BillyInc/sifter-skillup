# D106 — Garment Making, Tailoring and Dressmaking: specialization candidate review

**Disposition:** candidate_set_reviewed_with_explicit_residuals

**Parent scope basis:** Garment making, tailoring and dressmaking turns fabric into clothing and covered furnishings. Dressmakers, sewing machinists, patternmakers and embroiderers cut and sew garments, grade clothing sizes and work to standard sizing systems, while upholsterers cover seats for boats and railway cars. Typical workplaces include tailoring shops, garment factories and fashion houses.

## Candidate student-facing areas

1. **Garment Design, Pattern Development and Fitting** — Translate a brief into garment concepts, patterns, size/fit decisions and production-ready specifications.
2. **Tailoring and Custom Garment Construction** — Construct, fit, alter and finish garments to individual measurements, briefs and specialist construction requirements.
3. **Apparel Production and Sewing Operations** — Cut, sew, assemble and finish garments in small-batch, sampling or industrial production workflows.

## Decision

Three candidate routes distinguish design/pattern/fit, bespoke garment making and production operations; the WorldSkills standard covers all three in an integrated occupation, so the split is an internal learning-path synthesis rather than a globally prescribed taxonomy. Validate the learner-route split against additional current curricula before canonical adoption.

## Parent boundaries and exclusions

- Embroidery, knitwear, menswear, children’s wear and alteration are likely techniques/product contexts unless evidence establishes an independent path.
- Upholstery/furnishings are outside garment making; textile handcraft belongs D105; industrial textile machinery belongs D111.
- Fashion business, merchandising and brand work belong elsewhere unless garment-production tasks are central.

## Evidence and claim limits

- [WorldSkills International: Fashion Technology WSOS 2024](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1697/) — Occupation description and standards sections: fashion design, technical drawing, pattern construction/draping, cutting, sewing and finishing. **Supports:** Documents the breadth and connected workflow of garment design and construction. **Limit:** One integrated international competition occupation; it does not itself prescribe three separate student specializations. **Scope:** International/global benchmark.
- [Skills England: Fashion and textiles pattern cutter apprenticeship standard finder](https://skillsengland.education.gov.uk/apprenticeships/) — Search listing for Fashion and Textiles Pattern Cutter; use the current approved standard/version at implementation time. **Supports:** Corroborates pattern cutting as a distinct occupational learning focus. **Limit:** National England apprenticeship framework; broad landing page is not a detailed curriculum locator, so it is supporting rather than decisive evidence. **Scope:** England, UK.

**Evidence limit:** Sources establish practice tasks and boundaries; they do not prescribe universal specialization counts or endorse this catalogue.
