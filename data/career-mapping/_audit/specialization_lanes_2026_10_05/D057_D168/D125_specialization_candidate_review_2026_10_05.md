# D125: Medical Secretarial — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_routes_parent_label_and_granularity_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: 32 Health associate professionals; 33 Medical Office Administrators; 42 Medical Reception & Front Desk

Two student-facing subgroup names are medical-office tasks/role clusters and one is a broad ISCO category; this is already closer to functional practice than many projections but does not establish specialization count.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The root is a narrow health-administration support occupation family, not a clinical discipline. Consider a student-facing root rename such as Medical Office and Clinical Administration Support; do not absorb full Health Information Management or coding professions without boundary decisions and distinct curricula.

Three candidate routes are recognizable task clusters, but their boundaries overlap and evidence does not establish that they must be separate specialization cards. First refine the parent name/scope and distinguish entry-level office administration from credentialed health-information management/coding. Could be best represented as one domain foundation with optional contextual tracks rather than three fixed areas.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Patient Access and Medical Office Coordination** — Appointments, reception, referrals, patient communications and routine workflow coordination in a clinical office.
- **Clinical Documentation and Medical Transcription Support** — Accurate preparation, handling and routing of clinical notes and correspondence under authorized workflows.
- **Health Records and Information Support** — Organize and retrieve records, maintain data quality and confidentiality, and support health-information processes without claiming independent coding/HIM authority.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not equate medical secretary/receptionist with medical coder, clinical documentation integrity specialist or HIM professional. Appointment scheduling and patient reception are tasks within patient-access support, not separate children. Coding systems/credentials are jurisdiction- and payer-specific.
- Computational signals: EHR, scheduling, transcription and coding software are tools; health informatics/data analytics is not a specialization of medical secretarial support by default.
- Jurisdiction note: Health-record privacy, release, coding, billing, transcription and title/credential requirements vary by jurisdiction and employer. Tag specific credentials/regulations locally.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [American Health Information Management Association: Body of Knowledge](https://bok.ahima.org/) — Separates clinical documentation integrity, coding/compliance/revenue cycle and healthcare data lifecycle as distinct topic areas. Supports: Health information/coding has distinct competencies and boundaries from generic office support. Limit: US professional association framework; certification and scope are not global requirements.
- [World Health Organization: International Classification of Health Interventions (ICHI)](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-classification-of-health-interventions) — WHO classification work treats health information/statistical functions as health-system information infrastructure, not clinical secretarial practice. Supports: Health information activity has a different conceptual boundary from patient-office administration. Limit: Classification resource; does not define an occupation or student specialization taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
