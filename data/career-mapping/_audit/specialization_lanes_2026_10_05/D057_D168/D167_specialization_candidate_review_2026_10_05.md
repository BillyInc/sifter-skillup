# D167: Extended Reality (XR) and Immersive Technology — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_xr_functions_supported_but_not_universal_taxonomy`. Evidence review only; not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **7**. Current records: Extended Reality Designers & Hardware Engineers (5); Extended Reality Innovation & Solution Architecture (1); Augmented and Virtual Reality Software Developers (3); Legal, social and cultural professionals (1); Science and engineering associate professionals (1); Extended Reality Technical Support Specialists (1); Food/craft trades workers (1)

Seven records combine three potentially XR-relevant custom groupings, three broad ISCO classes and one unrelated trade group. The custom labels mix user experience/content, hardware, software and architecture; IEEE's standards portfolio reflects multiple technology and human-factors concerns but does not prescribe occupational or learner taxonomies.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain XR as an umbrella for systems combining digital and physical/immersive environments, including VR, AR and MR. Distinguish building XR experiences from device/platform engineering and application integration. Do not equate every 3D, game, visualization or digital-twin role with XR without immersive-system tasks.

IEEE's Digital Reality standards portfolio includes AR/VR device taxonomy, immersive interfaces, content/stream formats, environment safety and XR ethical assessment. This is authoritative evidence that XR work spans device, content/interface, system and human/social concerns, but standards workstreams are not a universal career taxonomy. The three existing custom labels are relevant starting signals but need sharper separation; generic ISCO groups and the unrelated trade group should not define student specializations. Four candidate routes remain provisional pending domain-role task review.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **XR Experience, Interaction and Content Design** — Design immersive interaction, spatial user experience, content and scenarios for a defined XR application. Boundary: Design/content practice may share interaction design with game/media fields; include only if XR-specific spatial interaction and testing are core.
- **XR Software and Real-Time Application Development** — Build XR applications, interaction logic, rendering/runtime integration and application-specific services. Boundary: Separate application engineering from generic software development and from the platform/hardware layer.
- **XR Devices, Tracking and Platform Engineering** — Develop or integrate head-mounted displays, sensors, tracking, input, graphics and system-level hardware/software components. Boundary: IEEE device taxonomies and engineering standards support technical distinctions but do not establish this exact subgroup name.
- **Human Factors, Accessibility, Safety and Evaluation** — Assess comfort, usability, accessibility, motion-related risks, privacy and ethical effects of immersive products and systems. Boundary: Human-factors work cuts across product design and engineering; treat as a specialization only where learning sequence is substantial, otherwise cross-cutting competencies.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create subgroups for every headset, engine, application sector, rendering technique or XR modality. Keep game development, 3D art, simulation, digital twins, industrial design and general IT support linked only where role tasks specifically involve immersive systems.
- Computational signal: Real-time 3D programming, spatial computing, computer vision/tracking, graphics, simulation and interaction analytics are core to some routes. Design, research, production and evaluation paths may use different or less code-intensive toolsets; none is a universal foundation for all XR work.
- Jurisdiction note: Accessibility, health/safety, biometric/privacy, consumer-protection and data rules differ by jurisdiction and application. IEEE standards and projects are technical/professional evidence, not personal certifications or legal approvals.

## Sources and limits

- [IEEE Standards Association (IEEE-SA): IEEE Digital Reality Standards](https://digitalreality.ieee.org/standards/) — Lists active and developing standards/projects for VR/AR device taxonomies, immersive interfaces, video/content, safety and related systems; notes participation spans device makers, content/service providers and technology developers. Supports: Evidence for the range of technical and content-system practices within digital reality/XR. Limitation: Standards project subjects are not occupations, curricula or a settled global subgroup count.
- [IEEE Standards Association: P7030 Recommended Practice for Ethical Assessment of XR Technologies](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/7030/10799/) — Project scope covers definitions/classifications for XR (AR, VR, immersive web and spatial web) and socio-technical/ethical assessment of XR products, services and systems. Supports: XR scope extends beyond software and devices to applications and human/social assessment. Limitation: A recommended-practice project does not define occupational certification or require a standalone specialization.
- [IEEE Standards Association: IEEE 3079-2020: HMD-Based VR Sickness Reduction Technology](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/3079/7283/) — Standard record describes VR sickness reduction; related work includes motion-to-photon latency and mixed-reality motion-training system frameworks. Supports: Human factors, comfort, evaluation and interaction are technically significant XR work concerns. Limitation: Narrow standards example, not a full XR profession taxonomy.

Evidence supports candidate distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
