# D119: Livestock, Poultry and Dairy Production — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_with_conditional_area`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **6**. Current records: 13 Production and specialised services managers; 31 Science and engineering associate professionals; 61 Animal Breeders & Dairy Farmers; 75 Food processing, wood working, garment and other craft and related trades workers; 81 Stationary plant and machine operators; 92 Livestock & Stable Hands

Six records are ISCO occupation groupings; several are generic roles or downstream food processing, not six animal-production areas.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The title and scope concern production animals and husbandry. Exclude food processing/distribution as a separate child (downstream food manufacture); include dairy as animal production where it means herd/milk production, not dairy processing. Keep species-specific rules contextual, not universal regulatory claims.

Five candidate contexts synthesize distinct animal production learning, but the fifth (apiculture/insects) is explicitly conditional and may be better represented as an optional direct route. The count is not prescribed by FAO/WOAH. Remove the generic processing/plant-operator occupation buckets from the specialization proposal.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Poultry Production** — Poultry breeding, hatchery, housing, feeding, flock health, egg and meat production. Boundary: Different poultry species and production systems are route options within this area unless separate evidence shows distinct curriculum pathways.
- **Ruminant Livestock and Dairy Production** — Cattle, sheep and goats; breeding, grazing/feed systems, health, welfare and milk/meat production. Boundary: Do not include dairy processing or retail.
- **Pig and Rabbit Production** — Monogastric livestock breeding, housing, feeding, health, welfare, production and farm management. Boundary: Current grouping is a synthesis; species have distinct production contexts but evidence does not require two top-level cards.
- **Equine Production and Management** — Horse breeding, husbandry, health, welfare and production management. Boundary: Equine sport/training and veterinary clinical practice are adjacent fields, not automatically part of production.
- **Apiculture and Insect Production** — Managed bee/insect production for products and ecosystem services, with husbandry, colony health and biosecurity. Boundary: Potentially too narrow as a top-level area in some curricula; retain only if domain-level evidence establishes a real learner route, otherwise direct domain-to-role/topic.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not add animal breeders/managers as separate subgroups; those are role families. Do not split by breed or farm system. Treat animal welfare, biosecurity, nutrition and genetics as shared or route-specific competencies, not automatically specializations.
- Computational signals: Livestock data, precision husbandry, genomic selection and farm automation are methods/tools within routes, not standalone specializations.
- Jurisdiction note: Veterinary scope, animal movement, food safety, welfare, veterinary-drug use and farm biosecurity are legally regulated differently across jurisdictions.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Global Farmer Field School Platform: Livestock](https://www.fao.org/farmer-field-schools/themes/livestock/en) — Names pastoralism, dairying, poultry, rabbit, pig, beekeeping, beef and small-ruminant systems. Supports: Species and production systems support distinct route examples; also shows great contextual diversity. Limit: Extension platform examples, not a global subgroup list or academic mandate.
- [World Organisation for Animal Health: Terrestrial Animal Health Code index](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2021/en_index.htm) — Dedicated production-system chapters include beef cattle, broiler chickens, dairy cattle and pigs. Supports: Internationally recognized husbandry/production contexts and welfare/biosecurity concerns. Limit: International animal-health standards do not set education taxonomy; implementation and legal duties are jurisdictional.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Global livestock production systems](https://www.fao.org/4/i2414e/i2414e.pdf) — Production-system typology includes dairy/beef cattle, small ruminants, pigs, chickens and water-fowl. Supports: Supports animal/system context distinctions used in the provisional synthesis. Limit: Analytical production-system typology, not a student taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
