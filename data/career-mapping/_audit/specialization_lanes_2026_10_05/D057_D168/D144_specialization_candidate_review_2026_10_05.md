## Scope and disposition

Parent boundary: Retain atmospheric weather and climate science, including observation, physical understanding, prediction and climate analysis. Hydrology and ocean science are closely coupled Earth-system fields but should be cross-linked or included only when the parent explicitly covers them. Separate scientific/operational meteorology from public warning or policy functions where learning differs.

WMO describes its international remit across meteorology (weather and climate), operational hydrology, data exchange, standards, research and applications. Its weather/climate service systems and climate-science programmes support at least operational forecasting, climate analysis/modelling and applied services as distinct practice families. Atmospheric composition is a supported scientific field but needs additional curriculum/role reconciliation before acceptance. Four candidate areas are provisional, not a WMO-prescribed count.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Operational Meteorology and Weather Prediction** — Atmospheric observations, forecasting, nowcasting, warnings and operational weather services. Boundary: Weather prediction timescales and service responsibilities differ from climate projections and long-range assessment.
- **Climate Science and Climate Modelling** — Climate variability/change, attribution, Earth-system modelling, projections and climate datasets. Boundary: Policy and adaptation planning consume climate evidence but are not themselves climate science specialties.
- **Atmospheric Composition and Air Quality** — Atmospheric chemistry, aerosols, greenhouse gases, air pollution and their measurement/modeling. Boundary: Environmental health/regulation may be adjacent; retain only as atmospheric science, not general pollution enforcement.
- **Applied Meteorology and Climate Services** — Translate weather/climate information for sectors such as aviation, agriculture, water, energy and disaster risk. Boundary: Application sectors are cross-cutting service routes; do not make every user sector a separate specialization.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split forecast duration, model family, instrument, hazard or customer sector into separate areas by default. Hydrology/oceanography are adjacent disciplines, not automatic climate-science subgroups; forecast service delivery is not identical to climate research.
- Computational signal: Numerical weather prediction, Earth-system modelling, data assimilation, remote sensing and climate downscaling are core computational methods. They support multiple areas and should not be exposed as one generic 'computational' subgroup.
- Jurisdiction note: Atmospheric and climate science are global; official warnings, service mandates, data standards and national meteorological authority responsibilities operate through state and WMO coordination.

## Sources and limitations

- [World Meteorological Organization (WMO): WMO Overview and Mandate](https://wmo.int/about-wmo/overview) — WMO identifies its remit as meteorology (weather and climate), operational hydrology and related geophysical sciences; it coordinates global observations, data, services and standards. Supports: International source for parent scope and the distinction between weather, climate and connected hydrological services. Limitation: WMO mandate describes international coordination, not a universal student specialization count.
- [WMO: Weather](https://public.wmo.int/themes/weather) — Describes operational weather prediction, warnings, observations, numerical weather prediction and Earth-system modelling across operational centres. Supports: Supports operational meteorology as a distinct practice route and the cross-cutting computational methods. Limitation: Operational arrangements and warning authority belong to national/regional services.
- [WMO: Climate](https://wmo.int/themes/climate) — Describes climate monitoring, prediction, projection, climate services and regional/global coordination. Supports: Supports a climate-science/modelling route distinguishable from short-term weather forecasting. Limitation: WMO programme structure is not an exhaustive educational taxonomy.
