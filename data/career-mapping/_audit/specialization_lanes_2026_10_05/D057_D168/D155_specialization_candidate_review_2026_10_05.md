# D155: Nanotechnology and Advanced Materials — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_scope_and_count_need_validation`. Source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Nanoscience & Advanced Materials Research (11 roles); Nanofabrication & Materials Technicians (1 role); Assemblers (1 role)

The groups include one very broad research umbrella, a technician level and a generic assembly family. The label 'nanotechnology and advanced materials' combines a scale/enabling technology with a broad materials-science field; they are related but not synonymous.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain research, engineering and application of nanoscale systems/materials and advanced materials, but make clear that scale is not the only organizing axis. Materials chemistry, characterization, fabrication and application sectors may have distinct learning paths. Do not treat nanotechnology as every material or every product containing nanoscale features.

ISO/TC 229's current programme distinguishes advanced materials, nano-bio, measurement/characterization, material specifications, products/applications and health/safety/environmental aspects. This international standards work supports meaningful technical themes, but standards-committee workstreams are not a learner taxonomy. Four candidates are provisional; remove technician/assembler job-family labels from the student specialization layer and clarify whether the domain includes non-nanoscale advanced materials.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Nanomaterials and Functional Materials** — Design, synthesis, characterization and behavior of materials whose nanoscale structure produces functional properties. Boundary: Keep material classes as nested topics unless learning sequences diverge; advanced materials beyond nanoscale need an explicit parent boundary.
- **Nanofabrication and Nanoelectronics** — Patterning, fabrication, devices, nanoscale components and relevant nanoelectronic systems. Boundary: Process and device engineering, not generic assembler work.
- **Nanobiotechnology and Biomedical Nanotechnology** — Nanoscale systems for biological measurement, diagnostics, delivery or interaction with living systems. Boundary: Application-specific; medical-device and clinical authorization are separate and must be sourced.
- **Nanometrology, Characterization and Safety** — Measurement, characterization and safety/environmental assessment of nanomaterials and nanotechnology systems. Boundary: ISO's work programme separates measurement/characterization, material specifications and health/safety; candidate path needs learning-outcome validation.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not make every material family, fabrication tool or end-use sector a separate subgroup. Keep advanced materials that are not nanoscale only if parent scope and curriculum support them. Do not equate manufacturing assembly with nanotechnology specialization.
- Computational signal: Atomistic/multiscale simulation, materials informatics, process modeling and image/data analysis are computational methods in materials research. They complement laboratory characterization and are not common to every route.
- Jurisdiction note: Nanomaterial health, environmental and workplace rules vary by jurisdiction; ISO standards and technical guidance are not certification or universal legal requirements.

## Sources and limits

- [International Organization for Standardization (ISO): ISO/TC 229 Nanotechnologies](https://www.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/committee/38/19/381983.html) — ISO/TC 229 work programme lists advanced materials, nano-bio, measurement/characterization, material specifications, products/applications and health/safety/environmental aspects. Supports: International technical-standardization evidence for distinct but interrelated practice themes. Limitation: ISO committee working groups organize standardization work; they do not prescribe career specializations or curricula.
