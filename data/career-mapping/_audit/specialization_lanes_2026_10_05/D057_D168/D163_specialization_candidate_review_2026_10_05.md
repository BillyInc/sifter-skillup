# D163: Intellectual Property Law and Patent Practice — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_occupational_groups_unfit_patent_scope_supported_other_ip_routes_need_more_evidence`. Evidence review only; not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **5**. Current records: Administrative and commercial managers (1); Science and engineering professionals (1); Legal, social and cultural professionals (0); Business and administration associate professionals (2); Legal/social/cultural associate professionals (1)

Five ISCO occupational-grade buckets (including one empty) do not express IP practice. The domain covers legal/strategic protection and technical patent work; patent examination, patent-agent representation, portfolio strategy and licensing are separate functions with differing legal authority.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain a coherent IP practice parent only if patent practice is not treated as all of intellectual property. Make patent prosecution/examination, trademarks/designs/copyright, transactions/licensing and portfolio strategy distinct candidate routes where education and task sequences differ.

WIPO separates patent examiner work and PCT international search/examination guidance, while explicitly noting national patent law and practices may differ. This supports patent examination as a function and jurisdiction caveat, but does not by itself establish four universal specializations. Current subgroup count of five is an artifact of occupation coding, not defensible taxonomy evidence. Four candidates remain provisional pending source checks for non-patent IP tasks and role-level alignment.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Patent Drafting, Prosecution and Patent Information** — Translate inventions into patent applications, manage examination correspondence and analyze patent documents and landscapes. Boundary: Who may represent applicants before an office is set by national/regional law; technical discipline knowledge is common but not itself legal authority.
- **Patent Examination and IP Office Practice** — Search and assess applications against patentability requirements and apply procedures in a patent or IP office. Boundary: WIPO materials describe examiner functions and PCT examination, but offices and legal criteria have jurisdictional limits.
- **IP Transactions, Licensing and Commercialization** — Negotiate or manage licensing, assignments, technology transfer and commercial use of IP. Boundary: Contract, competition, tax and regulatory expertise may be shared with adjacent legal/business domains.
- **IP Portfolio, Strategy and Rights Management** — Develop organizational approaches to identifying, protecting, maintaining and using patents, marks, designs and other rights. Boundary: Do not conflate IP portfolio management with legal advice or inventorship; scope should reflect actual domain roles.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split by every IP right, technology sector, filing office or patent class. Keep trademarks, designs, copyright, trade secrets and licensing as candidate routes only where the product's actual role scope supports them; avoid assuming every IP professional is a lawyer or patent agent.
- Computational signal: Prior-art search, patent classification, portfolio analytics and technology-landscape analysis use databases and computational text/data methods. These are task-specific supports, not universal prerequisites for all IP-law practice.
- Jurisdiction note: Patentability rules, attorney/agent registration, representation rights, court practice and rights protection vary across jurisdictions. WIPO treaties facilitate cooperation; they do not create a universal IP practice license. No Nigeria-specific content is generalized globally.

## Sources and limits

- [World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO): Resources for IP Examiners](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/examiners/resources) — Provides examiner resources and PCT International Search and Preliminary Examination Guidelines; notes national laws/practices may differ. Supports: Patent examination as a distinct technical/legal function and an explicit limit on global generalization. Limitation: Examiner resource set does not classify every IP profession or define a learner subgroup taxonomy.
- [WIPO: PCT International Search and Preliminary Examination Guidelines, Chapter 19](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/ispe/19_01_05) — Paragraphs 19.01–19.04 describe preliminary examination and the non-binding opinion on novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability; national criteria may differ. Supports: Specific patent assessment work and the boundary between international procedure and national grant decisions. Limitation: PCT procedure covers patents and is not evidence for trademark, copyright or all IP practice areas.

The sources support review candidates and boundaries, not a universal required subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as evidence of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
