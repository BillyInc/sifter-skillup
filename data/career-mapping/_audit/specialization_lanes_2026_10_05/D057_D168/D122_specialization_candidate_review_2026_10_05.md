# D122: Medical Laboratory Technology — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_and_root_label_refinement`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: 13 Production and specialised services managers; 23 Teaching professionals; 31 Science and engineering associate professionals; 32 Health associate professionals

Four ISCO occupation buckets do not represent laboratory diagnostic disciplines. The parent name also merits student-facing refinement: global professional bodies more commonly use Medical Laboratory Science/Clinical Laboratory Science.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Recommend a student-facing label such as Medical Laboratory Science, retaining legacy/local title aliases in search. Laboratory disciplines are candidate routes; shared foundations include specimen lifecycle, quality, safety, method validation, data integrity and clinical collaboration. Do not present scope-of-practice or result interpretation as identical across jurisdictions/qualification levels.

Five discipline families are a defensible candidate synthesis from ASCLS's scope and clinical-laboratory curricula; don't treat the count as global consensus. The current role buckets should not be repurposed as specialization names. Keep laboratory informatics, quality and management as shared functions or advanced routes only when domain-level curricula support them.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Clinical Chemistry, Endocrinology and Toxicology** — Laboratory analysis of chemical constituents, endocrine markers and toxicological substances in clinical specimens.
- **Hematology, Hemostasis and Transfusion Science** — Blood-cell and coagulation testing, blood banking/transfusion services and related clinical laboratory methods.
- **Clinical Microbiology and Immunology** — Detection and characterization of infectious agents and immune markers, including culture, serology and related diagnostic methods.
- **Histology, Cytology and Anatomic Pathology Laboratory Science** — Preparation and laboratory examination of tissue and cellular specimens for diagnostic pathology.
- **Molecular Diagnostics and Medical Genetics** — Molecular/genetic testing and laboratory interpretation workflows, within locally authorized scope.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Flow cytometry, cytogenetics, histocompatibility, virology, parasitology and point-of-care testing may be subareas within the named families or local advanced pathways. Their existence in one professional body list does not mandate separate global subgroup cards.
- Computational signals: Bioinformatics, laboratory informatics and computational pathology support multiple laboratory specialties; cross-cutting methods, not separate specializations absent a distinct learning route.
- Jurisdiction note: Professional title, authorization to validate/report results, transfusion scope, certification and laboratory accreditation requirements differ substantially by country. Local rules must be visibly jurisdiction-tagged.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [American Society for Clinical Laboratory Science: Scope of Practice and Personnel Standards](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/) — Defines clinical laboratory testing and lists discipline areas including microbiology, hematology, immunology, transfusion medicine, clinical chemistry, toxicology, cytology, histology and molecular diagnostics. Supports: Globally familiar laboratory discipline candidates and breadth of the profession. Limit: US professional-society scope; not global licensure or a required taxonomy/count.
- [American Society for Clinical Laboratory Science: Becoming a Clinical Laboratory Professional](https://ascls.org/how-do-i-become-a-laboratory-professional/) — Describes clinical chemistry, hematology, immunology, immunohematology, microbiology and molecular biology as laboratory testing disciplines. Supports: Independent corroboration of core clinical laboratory content families. Limit: US education/certification context; national names and practice rights differ.
- [World Health Organization: WHO list of priority medical devices for cancer management](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565462) — Service-unit framing separates laboratory services from imaging/nuclear medicine and other cancer-care units. Supports: Confirms medical laboratory is a distinct health service from imaging and clinical treatment. Limit: Cancer-service planning resource, not a laboratory education framework.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
