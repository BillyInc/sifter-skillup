# D132: Space Science and Exploration — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_routes_parent_boundary_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: Science and engineering professionals (3 roles; excluded from student view); Science and engineering associate professionals (3 roles; excluded from student view)

Both records are broad ISCO occupational groups, not student-facing areas. The root joins scientific inquiry with exploration; its engineering/mission-operations slice is not the same as space science.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: For a research-facing learner taxonomy, define the parent as scientific study using space-based observations, in-situ missions and spaceflight environments. Treat spacecraft engineering, astronautics and mission operations as adjacent distinct domains or an explicitly separate branch, not as science specializations by default.

NASA's scientific portfolio demonstrates established, distinct research pursuits useful as a candidate route structure, but is an agency-specific organizing model rather than a universal global taxonomy. The five candidate paths are defensible if the domain retains both Earth observation and spaceflight science; the title and engineering boundary should be clarified before implementation.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Earth and Space-Based Earth Observation** — Study Earth systems using satellite/spaceborne observations, remote sensing and related physical data. Boundary: Scientific Earth-system interpretation belongs here; spacecraft/platform engineering does not.
- **Planetary Science and Exploration Science** — Study planets, moons, small bodies, solar-system origins and the scientific questions addressed by robotic or human exploration. Boundary: Mission-science analysis is included; designing spacecraft belongs to aerospace/space-systems engineering.
- **Heliophysics and Space Weather** — Study the Sun, heliosphere, planetary space environments and their effects, including space-weather phenomena. Boundary: Forecasting and scientific observation are included; operational satellite engineering is not.
- **Astrophysics and Cosmology** — Study celestial objects, exoplanets, galaxies, the universe's structure, origin and evolution using ground- and space-based evidence. Boundary: Astronomical instrument engineering is supporting/adjacent, not itself the science route.
- **Biological and Physical Sciences in Space** — Investigate fundamental biological and physical systems in microgravity and other spaceflight environments. Boundary: Include only where the domain actually intends to cover space-based laboratory science; do not use as a catch-all for exploration technology.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not retain generic ISCO major groups as specialization cards. Do not silently include aerospace engineering, mission operations, astronaut occupations, space policy or satellite communications under science. NASA's directorate includes management functions as well as science divisions; those are not learner specialties.
- Computational signals: Remote sensing, astronomical data science, numerical modeling and space-weather forecasting are methods used across scientific routes; computational astrophysics is an approach/subfield, not an additional top-level path by itself.
- Jurisdiction note: Agency portfolios and space-program structures are national or intergovernmental organizational choices. Scientific disciplines are globally practiced, but mission access, export controls, licensing and spaceflight employment rules vary by jurisdiction.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [NASA Science Mission Directorate: NASA's Science Vision](https://science.nasa.gov/about-us/smd-vision/) — Lists five scientific pursuits: Earth Science, Planetary Science, Heliophysics, Astrophysics, and Biological and Physical Sciences; distinguishes these from management functions. Supports: Supports candidate disciplinary routes and the boundary between science and directorate administration. Limit: NASA agency portfolio is not a global consensus taxonomy; includes US agency priorities and organization.
- [NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory: Science, Implementation, and Operations Division](https://science.jpl.nasa.gov/division/) — Describes a lifecycle with science formulation, project systems engineering, mission design/navigation, mission systems design, mission operations and science phases. Supports: Shows mission engineering/operations are functions distinct from scientific discipline labels, while connected in practice. Limit: JPL organizational model, not a global career framework.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
