## Final observations

- **D161:** the ten current subgroups are occupation classes, not areas of specialization. OCHA’s humanitarian programme cycle and Sphere’s distinct technical response sectors support separating coordination, technical delivery and longer-horizon development work. The parent currently combines acute response and development.
- **D162:** asylum/refugee protection is a legal-administrative practice anchored in international, regional and national frameworks; immigration procedure, legal authority and qualifications remain jurisdiction-specific. Generic occupation classes and two empty custom groups do not constitute a defensible structure.
- **D163:** WIPO supports patent search/examination as a specific practice and expressly preserves national-law differences. The five existing occupation buckets do not define a student taxonomy; non-patent IP branches still need stronger profession-specific evidence.
- **D164:** UN-Habitat supports integrated urban mobility planning, transit, active/shared mobility and mobility technologies. Existing groups are mixed; “smart city” scope should be bounded to mobility systems unless the domain is intentionally expanded.
- **D165:** current groups mix clinical nutrition/therapy, exercise and nonclinical wellness coaching. WHO population guidance and ACSM clinical exercise material support explicit clinical/nonclinical boundaries; do not infer clinical qualifications from a coaching label.
- **D166:** SAE J3016 describes driving-system capability and task allocation, not student progression. Existing subgroup records include generic/nonmatching families; technical route candidates still need role-task and curriculum validation.
- **D167:** IEEE standards activity covers device, content/interface, software/system and human-factor concerns. These help audit scope but are not an occupational taxonomy; generic ISCO groups must not be student-facing specialization labels.
- **D168:** the root combines organized esports with the much broader, emerging creator-economy umbrella. IESF/IOC support organized esports structures; OECD/ILO/EU describes digital-platform work broadly and statistically, not a settled creator profession taxonomy. Split or partition the parent before accepting creator-related specialization labels.

## Limits and handoff

All 208 proposed labels remain **candidates**, not accepted findings or canonical data. Sources establish useful global scope distinctions but not a universal number of areas for each domain. Country-specific licensing, legal practice, qualifications and regulation need explicit local labels. Computational methods are recorded as branch-dependent signals, not assumed domain-wide requirements. ISCO membership and role counts were diagnostic only; no role-to-specialization links were created.
