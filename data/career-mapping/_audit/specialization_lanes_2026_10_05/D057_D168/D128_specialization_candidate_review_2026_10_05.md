# D128: Social Welfare and Youth Services — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_parent_scope_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **5**. Current records: 13 Production and specialised services managers; 24 Business and administration professionals; 26 Legal, social and cultural professionals; 34 Legal, social, cultural and related associate professionals; 53 Personal care workers

Five ISCO job families are not specialization areas; the root mixes broad welfare services and youth work, adjacent to but not synonymous with social work.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The root contains diverse settings, populations and intervention levels. IFSW/IASSW global standards explicitly recognize practice diversity by setting, population/issues and micro/mezzo/macro methods. A coherent route taxonomy should separate substantive population/service context from role grade and method. The root may need splitting if youth development and statutory/social welfare services have different foundation requirements.

Four candidates are provisional organizing routes based on populations/contexts and are not an internationally approved specialty list. Root scope and distinction from Social Work, Public Health, Youth Work and statutory government services must be resolved before implementation. Keep legal casework and protected titles jurisdiction-specific.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Child and Family Welfare** — Family support, child protection/well-being, safeguarding and coordinated services, subject to local statutory scope.
- **Youth Development and Youth Services** — Youth participation, development, support, prevention and service coordination across community and institutional settings.
- **Community and Social Development** — Community-led development, inclusion, participation, resource coordination and collective intervention.
- **Adult and Community Welfare Support** — Needs assessment, service navigation and support related to social protection, disability, ageing, housing or livelihood needs, as locally defined.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create separate subgroups for generic management, clerical or personal-care occupational families. Do not collapse social work, youth work, community development and welfare administration simply because they serve similar populations. Child protection, homelessness, disability and aging may be contexts within routes unless separate learning pathways are demonstrated.
- Computational signals: Case-management systems, social-service analytics and digital inclusion are tools/methods; computational social science is cross-cutting, not an automatic specialization under this service-delivery root.
- Jurisdiction note: Child protection mandates, social-work registration, safeguarding, benefits eligibility, statutory powers and youth-worker titles vary by law and jurisdiction.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Federation of Social Workers and International Association of Schools of Social Work: Global Standards for Social Work Education and Training](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards-for-social-work-education-and-training/) — Describes diversity by practice setting, field/population/issues and micro/mezzo/macro intervention level. Supports: Population/setting-based route candidates and warning against a single universal specialty count. Limit: Global education standards for social work, not all welfare/youth occupations; not legal scope.
- [International Federation of Social Workers: Global Definition of Social Work](https://www.ifsw.org/what-is-social-work/global-definition-of-social-work/) — Definition emphasizes social change, development, cohesion, empowerment, rights and context, with national/regional amplification allowed. Supports: A global conceptual boundary for adjacent social-work pathways. Limit: Does not make every welfare/youth worker a social worker or prescribe subgroup taxonomy.
- [UNICEF: Adolescent development and participation resources](https://www.unicef.org/adolescence) — UNICEF frames adolescence as a distinct development and participation context for programmes and services. Supports: Youth development/service context has a distinct learner focus. Limit: Programme and age framing varies; not a global occupational qualification standard.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
