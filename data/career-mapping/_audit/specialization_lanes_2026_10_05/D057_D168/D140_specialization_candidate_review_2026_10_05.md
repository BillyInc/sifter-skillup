# D140: Robotics Engineering and Systems Design — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_routes_not_standardized`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: Robotics Engineering & Automation (9 roles); Teaching professionals (1 role); Robotics Technicians & Precision Systems (7 roles); Assemblers (1 role)

One technical umbrella is mixed with a generic teaching aggregate, technicians/precision systems and assemblers. The existing structure is occupational rather than a clean map of robotics-engineering practice areas.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Keep the root on engineering and integration of robotic systems: mechanisms, actuation, sensing, control, software, autonomy, safety and deployment. Robotics is applied across industry, field/service, healthcare and other sectors; an application domain is not automatically a specialization unless it adds a distinct learning path. Teaching is an occupation, not a robotics specialization.

IEEE Robotics and Automation Society technical committees span mechanisms/design, motion planning and control, robot vision, human-robot interaction, manipulation, field robotics, manufacturing automation and medical/service applications. This supports several coherent practice areas but also shows that application contexts and engineering methods intersect. Four candidate routes are a navigation synthesis, not an IEEE-prescribed subgroup count; current generic occupation records are unsuitable as student specializations.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Robot Mechanisms, Mechatronics and Control** — Mechanical/electrical system design, actuation, embedded control, integration and verification of robot platforms. Boundary: Core systems engineering; detailed robotics manufacturing/assembly roles require their own evidence.
- **Robot Perception, Localization and Autonomy** — Sensing, computer/robot vision, state estimation, mapping, planning and autonomous behavior. Boundary: Computational specialization candidates are real technical routes, but computational methods also support other areas.
- **Manipulation and Human-Robot Interaction** — Grasping, manipulation, collaborative robotics, physical interaction and human-centered system design. Boundary: Keep interaction/design and manipulation engineering connected without assuming every collaborative robot has identical safety responsibilities.
- **Robotic Systems Deployment and Application Engineering** — Integration and operation of robots in industrial, mobile/field, service or healthcare settings, including reliability and task-specific system design. Boundary: Applications can diverge greatly; show named application routes only when supported by domain-specific curriculum/evidence and do not duplicate adjacent engineering domains wholesale.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create a separate top-level subgroup for every robot type (drone, surgical robot, humanoid, mobile manipulator) or every algorithm. Do not classify teaching or assembly as engineering specializations; preserve technical pathways for technicians only if explicitly designed as such.
- Computational signals: Perception, planning, estimation, learning, simulation and control software are central computational threads. They are distinct deepening routes where learning goals justify them, while robot computation is also integrated into mechanisms/control and application deployment.
- Jurisdiction note: Machine-safety, industrial, medical and autonomous-system requirements differ by application and jurisdiction; IEEE technical committees establish research communities, not regulatory approval or licensing.

## Evidence and limits

- [IEEE Robotics and Automation Society: List of Technical Committees](https://www.ieee-ras.org/technical-committees/list-of-technical-committees/) — Committee scope includes robot vision, planning/control, manipulation, human-robot interaction, field robotics, medical robotics, manufacturing automation, robot learning, verification and mechanisms/design. Supports: Primary professional-society evidence for distinct and interacting robotics research/practice areas. Limitation: Committee structure is research-community organization, not a mandatory or exhaustive student specialization taxonomy.
- [IEEE Robotics and Automation Society: Technical Committees](https://www.ieee-ras.org/technical-committees/) — Explains technical committees as tracking technical developments across applications, theory, models, metrics, experiments, architectures and products; identifies field and health/medical robotics clusters. Supports: Supports treating application areas as cross-cutting clusters and not equating every application with a standalone discipline. Limitation: Professional-society research organization; standards and safety obligations must be checked with the relevant regulator/standard body.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
