# D118: Crop Production and Horticulture — specialization audit

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Crop production and horticulture overlap in cultivated plants but are not identical. Crop protection/plant health is a cross-crop specialist practice. Keep landscape design/maintenance out unless the root explicitly includes ornamental landscape services; the broader current description says garden plants but does not establish a landscape-design route.

Three candidate routes are supportable as an internal synthesis: two production contexts and a distinct cross-crop protection discipline. FAO/IPPC sources support global field concepts but do not require a three-card count. Replace the earlier 'Horticulture and Landscape Management' label with 'Horticultural Production' unless root scope is expanded with authoritative landscape-practice evidence.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Crop Production** — Plan and manage production of food, fibre and other field/horticultural crops, including crop systems and yield management.
- **Crop Protection and Plant Health** — Diagnose and manage pests, diseases, weeds and phytosanitary risks through integrated, responsible protection approaches.
- **Horticultural Production** — Produce fruits, vegetables, ornamentals and nursery plants using relevant field, protected-cropping and propagation systems.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create top-level areas for individual crop species, farming systems or techniques. Protected/controlled-environment agriculture may be a subroute within horticulture/crop production, not automatically its own specialization. Exclude forestry, aquatic plants and landscape services unless parent boundaries are deliberately revised.
- Computational signals: Precision agriculture, remote sensing, crop models and farm data systems are cross-cutting methods/tools; plant pathology or crop protection is the substantive area, not 'computational agriculture' by default.
- Jurisdiction note: Pesticide authorization, phytosanitary certification, seed rules and plant-health enforcement are jurisdiction-specific; distinguish global agronomic concepts from legally required local practice.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

Supports: Crop production, horticulture and plant protection are internationally legible subject/practice areas. Limit: Bibliographic subject scheme organizes information; it does not prescribe student specialization count.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Pest and pesticide management](https://www.fao.org/platforms/green-agriculture/areas-of-work/natural-resources-biodiversity-green-production/pest-and-pesticide-management/) — Describes integrated crop protection and IPPC-linked phytosanitary responsibilities across cultivated plants. Supports: Plant health/crop protection is a coherent cross-crop practice area. Limit: FAO technical scope, not a curriculum mandate; national plant-health controls vary.
- [International Plant Protection Convention: About the IPPC](https://www.ippc.int/en/about/) — International treaty framework for plant protection and phytosanitary measures. Supports: Plant health is an internationally recognized policy/practice field. Limit: Treaty standards are implemented by contracting parties; does not prescribe academic route structure.
