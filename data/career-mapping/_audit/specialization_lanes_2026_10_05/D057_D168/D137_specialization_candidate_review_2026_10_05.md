# D137: Medical Physics and Radiation Therapy — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_specialties_parent_scope_needs_clinical_role_boundary`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: Science and engineering professionals (1 role); Health professionals (3 roles); Science and engineering associate professionals (1 role); Health associate professionals (2 roles)

The four child labels are broad ISCO occupation families, not areas of specialization. They obscure distinct clinical physics practices and combine professional/associate levels rather than learning areas.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The root combines medical physics with radiation-therapy and diagnostic-imaging occupations. Keep the science/technical scope explicit: application of physics to clinical imaging, nuclear medicine, therapeutic radiation and radiation protection. Radiographers and radiation therapists are distinct regulated clinical professions, not automatically medical-physics specializations; their learning routes need separate profession-specific ownership or carefully bounded cross-links.

IOMP explicitly names six medical-physics specialties: radiation oncology, medical imaging, nuclear medicine, medical health physics/radiation protection, non-ionizing medical radiation physics and physiological measurement. These are the evidence-backed candidate areas, though the last two need parent-scope reconciliation because the present domain description is radiation-focused. The current four ISCO groups should not stand as student specializations. Radiation therapists and radiographers are distinct clinical professions with separate competency and authorization pathways, not medical-physics specialties.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Radiation Oncology Physics** — Physics, treatment planning, dosimetry, equipment quality assurance and safety for external-beam and brachytherapy services. Boundary: Distinguish the medical physicist's planning/QA remit from the radiation therapist's patient setup and treatment-delivery remit; local authorization is jurisdiction-specific.
- **Diagnostic and Interventional Imaging Physics** — Image formation, equipment performance, optimization and radiation protection in diagnostic/interventional X-ray imaging and related modalities. Boundary: Clinical image acquisition by radiographers is a connected profession, not the same specialization.
- **Nuclear Medicine Physics** — Radiopharmaceutical imaging and measurement systems, quantitative imaging, instrumentation and associated radiation safety. Boundary: Separate from nuclear medicine physician practice and from general radiation oncology.
- **Medical Health Physics and Radiation Protection** — Protection of patients, staff and the public from medical ionizing-radiation hazards, exposure assessment and safety practice. Boundary: This is medical-context radiation protection; broader environmental or industrial health physics is outside the parent.
- **Non-Ionizing Medical Radiation Physics** — Physics and safety of non-ionizing medical radiation and associated clinical measurement technologies. Boundary: Keep modality-specific clinical authorization and the relationship to imaging professions explicit.
- **Physiological Measurement** — Application of physics and engineering measurement methods to physiological signals, systems and clinical measurement. Boundary: IOMP recognizes this specialty, but confirm inclusion in this product's student-facing parent scope before promotion; the present description emphasizes ionizing radiation.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split every modality, machine, isotope, imaging technique or treatment technology into a separate top-level area. Do not infer that every radiographer or radiation therapist is a medical physicist. Keep therapeutic, diagnostic and nuclear-medicine responsibilities distinct where competencies differ.
- Computational signals: Treatment-planning optimization, image reconstruction, Monte Carlo simulation, quantitative imaging and dose calculation are important computational methods within the relevant branches; they do not by themselves justify a separate computational subgroup.
- Jurisdiction note: Clinical scope, protected titles, licensing, training stages and permitted tasks vary substantially by country. IOMP/IAEA guidance is international professional guidance, not a universal license.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Organization for Medical Physics (IOMP): Medical Physics](https://www.iomp.org/medical-physics/) — Defines medical physics and lists specialty fields including radiation oncology physics, medical imaging physics, nuclear medicine physics, medical health physics/radiation protection, non-ionizing medical radiation physics and physiological measurement. Supports: Direct international professional-body evidence for distinct medical-physics fields and against generic ISCO groups as specialization labels. Limitation: Professional overview; does not prescribe one universal student taxonomy or jurisdictional authorization.
- [International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA): Roles and Responsibilities, and Education and Training Requirements for Clinically Qualified Medical Physicists](https://www.iaea.org/publications/13493/roles-and-responsibilities-and-education-and-training-requirements-for-clinically-qualified-medical-physicists) — IAEA publication describes clinical medical-physics specialties and role/education expectations across radiotherapy, diagnostic/interventional radiology and nuclear medicine. Supports: Supports the distinction between clinical physics specialties and adjacent clinical professions, and the need to keep training/authorization explicit. Limitation: International technical guidance; implementation and protected scopes remain national.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
