Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_routes_with_local_legal_variance`. Source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

One meaningful geospatial/cadastral umbrella is paired with generic occupational-grade groups. Surveying, mapping and cadastre overlap, but the latter includes legal/administrative land records beyond measurement alone.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain land measurement and geospatial representation for land administration, engineering and land information. Explicitly distinguish licensed boundary/cadastral surveying from engineering/topographic survey and geospatial mapping. Cadastre is a public land-information system whose legal meaning and surveyor authority differ across countries.

FIG's global Statement on the Cadastre describes parcel-based land information, legal/administrative purposes and surveyors' roles, while emphasizing that cadastral systems vary by jurisdiction and no uniform model is recommended. Four candidate practice routes make measurement, mapping and land-administration workflows visible; they remain a learning-path synthesis and should not be presented as globally licensed specialties.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Cadastral and Boundary Surveying** — Survey and document parcel boundaries and spatial descriptions that support rights, restrictions, land registration and land administration. Boundary: Legal authority and who may certify boundaries are jurisdiction-specific; FIG explicitly rejects a uniform cadastre for every country.
- **Engineering and Topographic Surveying** — Measure and set out terrain, structures and project control to support construction, infrastructure, mining or land development. Boundary: Construction engineering delivery and professional signing authority are separate context requirements.
- **Geospatial Mapping, GIS and Photogrammetry** — Create, analyze and maintain mapped/geospatial information using survey, imagery, GIS and related spatial-data methods. Boundary: Cartography and GIS support many domains; include here only for land-surveying mapping work, not all geospatial careers.
- **Land Information and 3D Cadastre** — Model, integrate and maintain land parcels, rights and spatial information, including multi-level/3D land administration where supported. Boundary: Land registry, valuation, planning and public administration roles have distinct authority and may need adjacent-domain paths.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not use associate-professional or business-administration groups as specialization labels. Do not create subgroups for each instrument, coordinate system, parcel type or GIS platform. Hydrographic surveying and geodesy may need cross-links or separate domains rather than being silently absorbed.
- Computational signal: GIS, spatial databases, photogrammetry, remote sensing, 3D cadastral models and spatial analytics are core computational tools/methods in applicable branches; not every cadastral/legal task is computational in the same way.
- Jurisdiction note: Boundary law, land registration, cadastre structure, reserved survey titles and signing/certification authority are country-specific. FIG provides global professional collaboration, not a universal cadastral law or license.

## Sources and limits

- [International Federation of Surveyors (FIG), Commission 7: FIG Statement on the Cadastre](https://www.fig.net/organisation/comm/7/library/reports/cadastre/statement_on_cadastre.html) — Defines cadastre as a parcel-based land information system linked to interests/rights and discusses technical, legal, organizational and jurisdiction-dependent models; expressly says it does not recommend one uniform cadastre for all countries. Supports: Authoritative global-professional evidence for parent boundaries, cadastral practice and jurisdiction-specific variation. Limitation: Policy statement is not a universal curriculum or licensing standard.
- [FIG: About FIG](https://www.fig.net/about/) — Describes FIG's global surveying remit, including spatial information about land/property/marine data, collection, analysis, visualization, standardization and dissemination. Supports: Supports surveying/geospatial mapping breadth beyond cadastral boundary work. Limitation: Association remit does not define exact student subgroup count.
