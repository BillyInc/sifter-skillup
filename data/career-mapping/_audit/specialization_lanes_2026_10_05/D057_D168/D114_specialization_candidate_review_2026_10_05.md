# D114: Assembly and Packaging Operations — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_recommended`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **1**. Current records: 82 Assemblers

The single ISCO assembler bucket is excluded from student taxonomy and conflates product assembly with packaging production.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Both activities fit the factory-operation parent at a high level, but assembly/product integration and packaging engineering are distinct technical practices. Parent currently risks mixing production operators with engineering/design pathways; retain this distinction in route descriptions.

Two candidate areas are defensible: they differ in output, process engineering, equipment and validation tasks. Do not treat one ISCO group as proof of fit or completeness.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Assembly Systems and Product Integration** — Sequence, fixture, join and verify components/products in manual, semi-automated or automated manufacturing systems. Boundary: Excludes general machine maintenance and product design unless specifically part of an assembly engineering path.
- **Packaging Engineering and Production** — Design/select packaging systems and materials, validate protection and distribution performance, and run filling, sealing, coding and packaging lines. Boundary: Excludes graphic/brand design as a separate creative pathway unless supported by domain scope.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Packaging materials, line operation, package testing and sustainability can remain within the packaging route until evidence supports separate routes. Product-family assembly (electronics, vehicles, medical devices) should be examples, not automatic new specializations.
- Computational signals: Robotics, PLCs, machine vision, digital twins and statistical process control are cross-cutting methods/tools, not specializations by themselves.
- Jurisdiction note: Product-contact packaging and safety compliance varies by market; do not present any one jurisdiction's packaging regulation as globally required.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Rutgers University Packaging Engineering: Packaging Engineering program](https://packaging.rutgers.edu/) — Program scope and curriculum description for packaging materials, package design, testing and distribution. Supports: Packaging engineering has a coherent, distinct technical learning route. Limit: One university curriculum; illustrative rather than a global required structure.
- [Institute of Packaging Professionals: Packaging education and professional development](https://www.iopp.org/) — Professional education and knowledge resources for packaging professionals. Supports: Packaging practice is a recognized professional field distinct from assembly work. Limit: Professional association scope does not set an accreditation standard or universal taxonomy.
- [ABET: Criteria for Accrediting Engineering Programs 2025–2026](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/accreditation-criteria/criteria-for-accrediting-engineering-programs-2025-2026/) — General curriculum criteria require engineering design and modern tools; no dedicated assembly/packaging subgroup mandate. Supports: Engineering learning routes need outcomes/evidence; ABET does not validate this exact count. Limit: US-based voluntary accreditation and general criteria, not a global taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
