# D116: Heavy Equipment and Earthmoving Operations — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_parent_boundary_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **1**. Current records: 83 Drivers and Mobile Plant Operators

One broad ISCO-derived occupation projection is not a specialization taxonomy. It includes earthmoving, road construction/compaction and crane/hoisting tasks.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Earthmoving and heavy equipment operation is a coherent operational parent if it is bounded to mobile construction/earthmoving plant. Crane/hoisting work shares plant-operation context but has a distinct load-handling hazard/task set; validate whether the parent intends to include it. Dredging is equipment-specific and should not become a separate area by default.

Three candidate operational routes are supported by the task split in ILO occupational descriptions and vocational standards; exact subgroup count remains an internal synthesis, not a globally standardized taxonomy. The parent boundary should explicitly say whether crane/hoisting is included.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Earthmoving and Excavation Plant Operations** — Operate excavators, dozers and loaders for excavation, grading, trenching and bulk earth movement. Boundary: Different machine endorsements/site authorizations can be jurisdiction-specific; not a separate specialization per machine.
- **Road Construction and Ground-Compaction Plant** — Operate graders, pavers and rollers for road formation, paving and compaction. Boundary: Work tasks overlap with earthmoving; candidate for an area only where the learner route includes distinct paving/compaction competencies.
- **Lifting and Crane Plant Operations** — Operate mobile or construction cranes/hoists for controlled lifting, placement and load movement. Boundary: Consider separate from earthmoving because lift planning, rigging, signaling and load control differ; licensing/certification is jurisdiction-specific.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create separate areas for every equipment type, jobsite sector or machine brand. Maintenance/repair is a separate technical learning route only if domain scope explicitly includes equipment service; the current root emphasizes operations.
- Computational signals: Machine control systems, grade-control/GNSS, telematics and automation support tasks; computational methods are cross-cutting, not a specialization on their own.
- Jurisdiction note: Operator permits, competency cards, lift plans, signaling requirements and equipment-specific authorizations differ by jurisdiction and employer/site; never imply one universal certificate.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Labour Organization: ISCO-08 Volume 1: Unit Group 8342/8343](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf) — Minor Group 834 tasks distinguish earthmoving (8342), crane/hoist (8343) and related plant operation. Supports: Earthmoving and lifting have different task bundles; used to identify candidate boundaries, not to infer subgroup correctness. Limit: ISCO is an occupational classification, not a curriculum/specialization framework.
- [Skills England: Construction plant operative OCC0736](https://skillsengland.education.gov.uk/occupations/OCC0736) — Occupation summary: prepare, set up and operate construction plant; lists excavator, dumper, forklift and roller as common training machines, with progression to specialized plant. Supports: A broad shared plant-operator foundation can coexist with machine-specific routes; do not turn every machine into a subgroup. Limit: England occupational/apprenticeship standard, not a global legal requirement.
- [Government of Canada, Red Seal Program: Heavy Equipment Operator (Excavator) Interprovincial Program Guide](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/9.827471/publication.html) — Publication record identifies a national curriculum guide for excavator operator occupation. Supports: Excavator operation has a structured occupational training route. Limit: Canadian interprovincial curriculum; not global taxonomy or licensing law.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
