# D124: Community Health Extension — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_no_global_specialization_set_parent_title_ambiguous`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: 32 Health Associate Professionals; 53 Personal Care Workers

Current ISCO buckets are not a coherent global specialization scheme and may mix different professional grades, occupations and scopes of practice.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The phrase 'community health extension' is not a universally standardized global profession title. WHO guidance explicitly says community-health worker roles and required competencies vary widely by country, health system, program and specific role. Do not equate this domain automatically with every CHW, community nurse, public-health worker or community-based practitioner.

No globally stable student-facing specialization set is justified for this parent title from the authoritative evidence reviewed. Retain a broad community-health foundation only if its domain definition is made profession-neutral; then show locally defined, jurisdiction-tagged role routes. If it denotes a regulated/national cadre, label that jurisdiction explicitly and derive pathways from its regulator/curriculum rather than inventing global child categories.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- No separate specialization is justified by the reviewed evidence; use the explicit disposition below.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Maternal/child health, infectious disease, health promotion, environmental health, referral and home visiting are important competencies/program areas, but WHO evidence does not establish these as universal specialization cards for one common occupation. Avoid presenting disease/program modules as separate professions.
- Computational signals: Community health data, digital referral, surveillance and telehealth are cross-cutting methods; no distinct computational specialization is justified.
- Jurisdiction note: Community health worker/professional title, prescribing/referral tasks, cadre, training duration, supervision and legal scope are country- and sometimes subnational-specific. Label explicitly.
- No-specialization disposition: Evidence supports no universal specialization cards for the current ambiguous parent title. Establish the exact profession/jurisdiction or revise the domain scope before adding child areas; a direct domain-to-context-specific-role route is safer.

## Evidence and limits

- [World Health Organization: Global curriculum guide for community health workers](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240118065) — Overview states CHW roles vary widely across countries/settings and competencies are context-specific; guide should be adapted to country, health system, program and role. Supports: An explicit no-global-subgroup disposition; local/role-specific pathways are required. Limit: WHO global curriculum guidance does not define the meaning or regulatory status of the specific phrase 'Community Health Extension'.
- [World Health Organization: WHO guideline on health policy and system support to optimize community health worker programmes](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/275474/9789241550369-eng.pdf) — Implementation guidance says training content should be established at country/subnational level based on needs, roles and responsibilities. Supports: Local scope and curriculum must drive any child routes rather than importing a universal taxonomy. Limit: Guideline concerns CHW programmes broadly and does not establish this domain title as a single global cadre.
- [International Federation of Social Workers: Global standards for social work education and training](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards/) — Discusses national/regional diversity and difficulty of identifying universal scope across social-work/welfare personnel categories. Supports: Adjacent welfare/community roles should not be collapsed without a defined professional boundary. Limit: Social-work standards are adjacent context, not direct authority for community-health practice.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
