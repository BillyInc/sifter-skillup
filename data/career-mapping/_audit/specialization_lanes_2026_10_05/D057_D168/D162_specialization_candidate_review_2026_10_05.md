## Parent scope and disposition

Retain only if the product makes the international protection vs general immigration-law boundary explicit. The core areas are legal advice/representation, administrative case processing, refugee-status determination and policy/compliance. These are practice areas, not universally licensed titles; legal representation rules vary by country.

UNHCR's global guidance distinguishes refugee-status determination, identifies the 1951 Convention/1967 Protocol and regional or national law as applicable bases, and says states have primary responsibility. This supports a clear asylum/protection practice route but not an assumed global lawyer license. Existing subgroup labels are partly empty and partly generic occupation classes; four candidate practice areas are useful for review, while credentials and authority must be localized.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Immigration Law and Status Applications** — Interpret immigration statutes and advise or assist with visas, residence, work, family and status applications. Boundary: Substantive law, permitted advisers, reserved activities and filing procedures are national/regional.
- **Asylum and International Protection Casework** — Assess protection claims, gather and test facts, apply refugee definitions and support fair case decisions or appeals. Boundary: UNHCR states refugee-status determination is primarily a state responsibility and may be based on international, regional or national law.
- **Refugee Status Determination and Protection Operations** — Conduct or support administrative eligibility assessment, procedural safeguards, referrals and protection case management. Boundary: Separate legal advocacy from decision authority; UNHCR mandate procedures apply only in their defined contexts.
- **Immigration Compliance, Policy and Programme Administration** — Develop or administer lawful entry, residence, migration and protection programmes and related case systems. Boundary: Government powers and compliance requirements are jurisdiction-specific; do not present policy administration as legal practice.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split by nationality, visa category, protected ground or individual form. Do not collapse asylum adjudication, legal representation, case support and general visa processing. Border enforcement/security is adjacent public administration and should not be included automatically.
- Computational signal: Case-management systems, document/evidence workflows, legal research, translation and caseload analytics may support specific functions; these are not universal foundations and must not displace privacy, due-process or human review.
- Jurisdiction note: International refugee instruments provide a shared framework, but asylum procedure, immigration law, reserved legal work, decision authority and appeals are jurisdiction-specific. Label a Nigerian immigration-law path explicitly Nigeria-specific if later added.

## Sources and limits

- [UNHCR: Refugee Status Determination (RSD)](https://emergency.unhcr.org/protection/legal-framework/refugee-status-determination-rsd) — Defines RSD as legal/administrative assessment under international, regional or national law; states states primarily have responsibility and explains emergency-context variation. Supports: Global basis for distinguishing asylum/refugee-status casework and its legal/administrative functions. Limitation: Guidance does not define national lawyer/adviser qualifications or one global subgroup count.
- [UNHCR: Asylum and refugee status: frequently asked questions](https://help.unhcr.org/global/asylum-and-refugee-status/) — Explains refugee-status determination/asylum procedures and that states establish their own procedures; UNHCR may provide support or act in defined situations. Supports: Confirms international common concepts while preserving national procedural variation. Limitation: Public explainer, not a national immigration law or qualification standard.
