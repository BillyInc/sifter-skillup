# D130: Recruitment and Employment Services — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_recruitment_vs_intermediation`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **6**. Current records: 11 Chief executives, senior officials and legislators; 12 Administrative and commercial managers; 24 Business and administration professionals; 26 Legal, social and cultural professionals; 33 Business and administration associate professionals; 44 Other clerical support workers

Six occupational major/minor group labels do not describe recruitment or employment-service specializations and should not be presented as six learning areas.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Root combines employer-side talent acquisition/recruitment and intermediary employment services/job matching. These are related but have different clients, duties, worker-protection obligations and public/private contexts. Keep them distinguishable; recruitment research, sourcing and selection methods are techniques within talent acquisition unless a separate evidence-backed route emerges.

Two core routes are supportable: employer-side recruitment and employment-service intermediation. Executive search is a plausible specialization practice but may be better represented as an advanced recruitment pathway, not a separate subgroup, absent separate curriculum evidence. Count remains provisional.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Talent Acquisition and Recruitment** — Employer-side role intake, attraction, sourcing, assessment, selection and candidate experience.
- **Employment Services and Job Matching** — Public/private employment service delivery, jobseeker support, matching, referrals and labour-market intermediation.
- **Executive Search and Specialist Recruitment** — Retained or specialist search for senior/scarce roles, with distinct client engagement and market-mapping methods; candidate route only if supported by the catalogue's intended level.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create child cards for recruiter seniority, industry verticals, interview formats, applicant tracking systems or individual sourcing channels. HR business partnering and career coaching are adjacent domains; do not absorb them without scope evidence.
- Computational signals: Recruitment analytics, matching algorithms and ATS/CRM are tools/methods, not standalone specializations. Algorithmic bias, privacy and employment law are cross-cutting competencies.
- Jurisdiction note: Recruitment agency licensing, fee restrictions, worker protection, background checks, privacy, anti-discrimination and algorithmic hiring law vary by country.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development: Recruitment process factsheet](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/recruitment-factsheet/) — Recruitment process spans defining the role through making the appointment. Supports: Employer-side recruitment has a coherent end-to-end practice path. Limit: UK professional-body guidance; not a global legal standard or formal specialization taxonomy.
- [International Labour Organization: Private Employment Agencies Convention No. 181 and employment services](https://www.ilo.org/publications/promoting-ratification-private-employment-agencies-convention-no-181) — C181 discusses private agency activity and cooperation with public employment services for matching and labour-market support. Supports: Employment intermediation is distinct from an employer's internal recruitment function. Limit: Convention obligations bind ratifying states; national implementation and agency regulation vary.
- [Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development: The Profession Map](https://www.cipd.org/en/the-people-profession/the-profession-map/) — Describes the people profession's knowledge/behaviour framework and specialist/generalist practice contexts. Supports: Recruitment belongs within a broader people-profession context; adjacent HR specialties should not be conflated. Limit: CIPD is a UK-origin professional body; not an international regulator or required credential.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
