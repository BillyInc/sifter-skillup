# D149: Diplomacy and International Relations — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_practice_routes_not_global_standard`. Source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Ambassadors & Diplomats (5 roles); Foreign Affairs Officers (6 roles); Legal, social and cultural professionals (1 role)

The first two children are overlapping diplomatic job-title families (ambassador is usually a senior appointment/title, not a distinct specialization); the third is a generic ISCO family. They do not define distinct learner areas.

## Parent scope and disposition

Retain the study and practice of relations among states and international actors, including diplomatic representation, negotiation, foreign-policy analysis and multilateral cooperation. Distinguish academic international relations from state diplomatic service. Intelligence, international law, development and humanitarian work are connected but have distinct professional ownership; not every such role is a diplomat.

UN treaty sources establish diplomatic and consular functions as distinct legal contexts; UN peace/security materials identify preventive diplomacy, mediation and multilateral processes. Four candidate practice areas clarify those functions, but they are a learner-oriented synthesis rather than a global job taxonomy. The current three subgroups should not be retained as specialization labels; diplomats/ambassadors are not separate fields by seniority.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Foreign Policy and International Affairs Analysis** — Analyze international political, economic and security developments and advise on external policy choices. Boundary: Analytical/advisory practice, not a diplomatic rank or country-specific civil-service title.
- **Bilateral Diplomacy and Consular Affairs** — Represent a state in another state and provide consular functions under applicable international agreements and national service rules. Boundary: The Vienna conventions describe legal functions/status; staffing, recruitment and consular procedures are state-specific.
- **Multilateral Diplomacy and International Organizations** — Negotiation, representation and coordination in intergovernmental and multilateral forums. Boundary: International organizations may employ their own civil servants; their work is not the same as a national diplomat's authority.
- **Conflict Prevention, Mediation and Peacebuilding** — Diplomatic negotiation, preventive diplomacy, mediation and political support for peaceful settlement and peacebuilding. Boundary: UN practice is a strong illustration, not the only form of diplomacy; specialist mediation credentials or national mandates vary.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create subgroups by country, rank, embassy size or every foreign-policy topic. Keep international law, intelligence, trade, development and humanitarian operations as linked adjacent disciplines unless the learning scope explicitly establishes them as diplomacy specializations.
- Computational signal: Policy data analysis, conflict/event data, geospatial evidence and digital diplomatic communications are tools/methods, not standalone specialization families without distinct learning routes.
- Jurisdiction note: Diplomatic appointment, foreign-service entry, consular duties and privileges are state-specific; the Vienna conventions establish international legal frameworks but do not prescribe national career pathways.

## Sources and limits

- [United Nations Office of Legal Affairs: Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961)](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_1_1961.pdf) — Treaty text defines diplomatic mission functions and status, including representing the sending State, protecting its interests, negotiating and reporting/relations under international law. Supports: Authoritative international legal boundary for diplomatic representation; supports a diplomatic-practice route, not ambassador rank as a separate specialty. Limitation: Treaty does not define educational areas or national foreign-service roles.
- [United Nations Treaty Collection: Vienna Convention on Consular Relations (1963)](https://untreaty.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/9_2_1963.pdf) — Treaty provisions address establishment/conduct of consular relations and consular-post functions/status. Supports: Shows consular practice is related to but distinguishable from general diplomatic representation. Limitation: Legal framework does not standardize domestic recruitment, training or every state's consular functions.
- [United Nations: Maintain International Peace and Security](https://www.un.org/en/our-work/maintain-international-peace-and-security) — UN overview describes preventive diplomacy, mediation, special political missions and peacebuilding as tools in conflict prevention and peaceful settlement. Supports: Supports mediation/peacebuilding as a substantive diplomatic practice family. Limitation: UN institutional mandate is one intergovernmental context, not a universal organization chart.

Evidence supports candidate distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Current role membership is not used as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
