# D142: Bioinformatics and Computational Biology — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_internal_routes_needs_learning_path_validation`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **1**. Current records: Computational Biology & Bioinformatics (36 roles)

One combined group reflects the umbrella's close relationship but hides different learner emphases and spans all 36 roles. It may remain an umbrella specialization if routes are not genuinely separable, but role volume/count is not evidence either way.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Retain the joint field as computational and quantitative analysis of biological systems and data. It overlaps health/biomedical informatics, statistics, software engineering and laboratory science; keep those boundaries task-based. Separate bioinformatics (data/information pipelines and interpretation) from computational biology (models and computational investigation of biological mechanisms) only where educational objectives and actual practice distinguish them.

ISCB's curriculum-guideline work defines bioinformatics core competencies from career opportunities, core-facility needs and existing curricula, and describes practice in research, medical labs, software organizations and pharma/instrument companies. This supports differentiating data-centric bioinformatics from model-centric computational biology, while infrastructure/service competencies may form a distinct route if student learning objectives validate it. Four candidates are provisional; a single broad umbrella remains defensible if the local evidence does not support independent pathways.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Genomics and Omics Bioinformatics** — Computational handling, analysis, integration and interpretation of genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic and other high-throughput biological datasets. Boundary: Omics types can be nested topics; avoid one subgroup per assay or data modality.
- **Molecular and Structural Bioinformatics** — Computational analysis of biological sequences, structures, molecular interactions and related biological databases/tools. Boundary: Keep distinct from wet-lab structural biology and from general software/database administration.
- **Systems and Computational Biology** — Mathematical/computational modeling and analysis of biological systems, networks, dynamics and mechanisms. Boundary: A computational-science pathway; methods support biology questions and do not imply a specific clinical or laboratory occupation.
- **Bioinformatics Infrastructure and Reproducible Data Science** — Biological data resources, workflow engineering, reproducible computing, software and computational support for research teams. Boundary: Candidate practice area supported by core-facility/professional competency evidence; confirm it is a learner specialization rather than a role-only service function before promotion.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split every organism, disease, assay, omics type or algorithm into an area. Do not classify all biology researchers who use statistics or a programming language as bioinformaticians. Keep clinical health informatics, general data engineering and experimental laboratory specialisms distinct unless the learning path explicitly crosses them.
- Computational signals: Computation is constitutive of this domain rather than an optional overlay: programming, statistics, data management, workflows, algorithms and modeling are core. Distinguish bioinformatics data analysis/infrastructure from computational biology modeling, and do not assume each role uses the same tool stack.
- Jurisdiction note: The discipline is global; privacy, clinical-data access, human-subjects rules and genomic-data governance vary by jurisdiction. ISCB curriculum guidelines are professional/educational guidance, not licensing requirements.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Society for Computational Biology (ISCB): Bioinformatics Curriculum Guidelines: Toward a Definition of Core Competencies](https://www.iscb.org/about-iscb/society-communications/announcements/2014apr21-bioinformatics-curriculum-guidelines) — ISCB Education Committee describes curriculum guidance informed by career opportunities, bioinformatics core facilities and existing education programmes; names practice settings in research, medical labs, software development and pharma/instrument companies. Supports: Primary professional-society evidence that the umbrella spans distinct competency contexts and that curriculum routes must be based on actual practice/learning objectives. Limitation: Guidelines are from 2014 and are an educational framework, not a current exhaustive global specialization count.
- [National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), US National Library of Medicine: Computational Biology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK550339/) — NCBI Bookshelf collection includes distinct chapters/workflows on biological sequence analysis, high-throughput/omics analysis, systems biology and computational modeling. Supports: Technical corroboration that data-centric bioinformatics and model-centric computational biology contain connected but distinguishable work themes. Limitation: US public biomedical reference collection; it is explanatory and not an occupational or curriculum standard.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
