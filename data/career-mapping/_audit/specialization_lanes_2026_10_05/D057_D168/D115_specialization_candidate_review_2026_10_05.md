# D115: Road Transport and Driving Operations — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_recommended`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **1**. Current records: 83 Drivers and Mobile Plant Operators

Current ISCO bucket combines road drivers with mobile plant operators; the latter do not automatically belong in road transport. The current child is not an acceptable specialization label.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The parent definition is transport by road. Passenger carriage and freight/goods carriage represent separate operational contexts; mobile plant operation should be separated to the heavy-equipment domain unless role evidence proves a road-transport function.

Two learner routes are supported by distinct passenger-versus-goods tasks and the explicit separation in EU professional-driver rules. Treat as a useful global route model, not universal licensing categories.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Passenger Road Transport** — Operate road passenger services, including passenger safety, route/service operation and accessibility duties. Boundary: Vehicle classes, public-service rules, school transport and passenger-service permits vary by jurisdiction.
- **Freight and Goods Road Transport** — Move goods by road, including load security, delivery/route operations and cargo-specific safety duties. Boundary: Dangerous-goods, vehicle-weight and commercial-carrier authorization requirements are jurisdiction-specific.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Bus, coach, taxi, delivery van, heavy goods and hazardous-goods categories may be qualifications or vehicle/service contexts, not necessarily top-level areas. The EU rule itself recognizes shared common learning plus category-specific extension.
- Computational signals: Fleet telematics, dispatch optimization and vehicle diagnostics are cross-cutting tools; not standalone specializations.
- Jurisdiction note: Professional driving age, licence class, driver CPC, medical fitness, commercial permits and hazardous-cargo rules are local/regional law; present these as jurisdiction-tagged requirements.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [European Union, EUR-Lex: Directive (EU) 2022/2561: qualification and training of professional drivers](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/drivers-of-road-vehicles-for-the-carriage-of-goods-or-passengers-qualification-and-training.html) — Directive summary explicitly covers carriage of goods and passengers, different vehicle categories, initial and periodic training, and extension between categories. Supports: Passenger and freight routes have distinct requirements while sharing some foundation. Limit: Binding only within the EU legal regime and its territorial scope; does not establish licensing rules elsewhere.
- [International Labour Organization: ISCO-08 Volume 1](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf) — Group 83 and mobile-plant descriptions; unit groups distinguish road vehicle drivers from earthmoving/crane plant operators. Supports: The current broad combined bucket masks task distinctions, but classification is context only. Limit: Occupational classification describes jobs; it does not define student specialization paths.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
