# D165: Wellness Coaching and Lifestyle Medicine — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_mixes_clinical_and_nonclinical_practices_requires_scope_restructure`. Evidence review only; not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Therapists & Nutrition Specialists (8); Fitness & Wellness Coaches (4); Personal service workers (1)

Three groups combine clinical nutrition/therapy, exercise and non-clinical coaching; the domain description also includes dietetics, fitness, Pilates, music/dance therapy. These professions have materially different training, evidence, supervision and legal scopes.

## Parent scope and disposition

Split or sharply qualify the clinical lifestyle-medicine/clinical exercise/registered nutrition scope from general wellness coaching. Lifestyle medicine commonly denotes clinician-led prevention/treatment; a coach is not thereby qualified to diagnose or treat. Therapy modalities such as music/dance therapy require separate recognized professional and clinical scope evidence.

WHO's physical-activity guidelines are population-level recommendations, while ACSM distinguishes exercise physiologists from clinical exercise physiologists and documents education/experience eligibility. This confirms important clinical/nonclinical boundaries; it does not support collapsing nutrition, therapy and coaching into one route. Current three groups are too broad and do not explain the parent. Four candidates are provisional; formal dietetics and music/dance therapy require their own primary professional-body evidence before acceptance under this root.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Health and Wellness Coaching** — Support client-selected behavior change, goal setting, accountability and health-promoting routines within a non-diagnostic scope. Boundary: Avoid medical diagnosis, treatment and nutrition prescriptions unless separately licensed/authorized.
- **Exercise Science and Physical Activity Promotion** — Apply exercise science and physical-activity guidance in health, fitness and performance settings. Boundary: WHO guidelines provide population guidance; professional scopes and clinical competence vary.
- **Clinical Exercise Physiology** — Assess and prescribe exercise for people with health conditions in appropriately supervised clinical settings. Boundary: ACSM describes education and clinical experience prerequisites for its US credential; that credential is not a global license.
- **Lifestyle Medicine and Preventive Health Practice** — Use evidence-based lifestyle interventions in clinical prevention or care, typically as part of a qualified health professional's practice. Boundary: Keep clinician status, prescriptive authority and each health profession's scope explicit; do not place uncredentialed wellness coaching under clinical medicine.

## Boundaries and cross-cutting considerations

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split by each exercise format (Pilates, yoga, dance), client demographic or lifestyle behavior. Do not treat general coaching certificates as clinical qualifications. Dietetics, psychotherapy, physiotherapy and expressive-arts therapy should not be inferred from a broad wellness label.
- Computational signal: Wearables, activity tracking, remote coaching and behavior data can support selected services; they are optional methods and require privacy, accessibility and evidence safeguards.
- Jurisdiction note: Clinical practice, dietetic titles, exercise testing/prescription, therapy licensure and protected professional titles vary nationally. WHO guidance is global; ACSM certifications and eligibility rules are US-based examples and must be clearly labeled, not presented as universally required.

## Sources and limits

- [World Health Organization (WHO): WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour: at a glance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240014886) — Guideline gives evidence-based recommendations by age and population group for frequency, intensity and duration, including sedentary behaviour. Supports: Global public-health and physical-activity foundation, distinguished from individual clinical prescription. Limitation: Population guideline is not a professional qualification or complete clinical scope standard.
- [American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM): Clinical Exercise Physiologists in the Workforce](https://acsm.org/cep-in-the-workforce/) — Defines clinical exercise physiologists as allied health professionals serving people with chronic disease, typically in medically supervised environments and under physician direction. Supports: Clinical vs general wellness/exercise boundary. Limitation: US professional association evidence; scope/licensure and credential recognition differ internationally.
- [ACSM: Exercise Physiologist certification](https://acsm.org/certification/get-certified/exercise-physiologist/) — Describes exercise assessment and prescription and the ACSM-EP eligibility route including a relevant bachelor's degree and CPR/AED; current exam alignment noted on page. Supports: A concrete competency/credential example for exercise physiology, not a universal requirement. Limitation: ACSM is not a global regulator; this certification should be labeled optional/context-specific outside its recognition jurisdiction.

The sources support review candidates and boundaries, not a universal required subgroup count. Existing role membership is not treated as evidence of fit. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
