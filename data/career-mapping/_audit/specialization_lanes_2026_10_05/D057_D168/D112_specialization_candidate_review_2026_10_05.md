# D112 — Wood, Pulp and Paper Processing: specialization candidate review

**Disposition:** source_gap_closed_candidate_boundary_or_count_remains_provisional

**Parent scope basis:** Wood processing and pulp work converts logs into sawn timber, veneer, pellets, pulp and paper. Machine operators run saws, debarkers, planers, bleachers and paper machines, perform test runs, troubleshoot faults and work safely in protective gear, while supervisors oversee production lines. Sawmills, paper mills and wood products factories are the main employers.

## Candidate student-facing areas

1. **Timber and Engineered-Wood Processing** — Operate industrial processes that convert logs and wood inputs into sawn timber, veneer, panels, pellets and related products.
2. **Pulp and Paper Manufacture** — Operate pulping, bleaching, stock preparation and paper-machine processes to make pulp and paper.
3. **Paper Converting and Finishing (not a separate area yet)** — Convert paper stock into finished products through cutting, folding, coating or related finishing operations.

## Decision

Two primary process areas are supported. Paper converting/finishing remains a topic or downstream process under pulp/paper until a distinct curriculum/industry route demonstrates independent learner needs; do not display it as a confirmed third specialization. Source-gap disposition recorded as `two_primary_process_families_supported_converting_not_separately_established`.

## Parent boundaries and exclusions

- Furniture/joinery belongs D102; handicraft paper/fibre work belongs D105.
- Logging/forestry and forest management are upstream forestry domains unless the parent explicitly includes them.
- TAPPI and Japanese industry sources corroborate technical practice, not universal student requirements or subgroup counts.
