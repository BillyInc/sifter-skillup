# D134: Naval Architecture and Marine Engineering — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_two_candidate_routes_remove_isco_noise`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **6**. Current records: Naval Architects & Marine Engineers (5 roles); Ship & Marine Engineering Technicians (9 roles); Business and administration associate professionals (1 role); Protective services workers (1 role); Metal, machinery and related trades workers (2 roles); Electrical and electronic trades workers (2 roles)

Six ISCO occupation families mix two discipline names, technician/trade roles, administration and protective services. Those are not six areas of specialization; some jobs may support the sector without belonging to this engineering domain.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Retain ship/naval design and marine engineering systems as two closely coupled but distinguishable disciplines. Do not automatically expand to ocean engineering, offshore petroleum/subsea engineering, vessel operations or maritime security. Review those separately against their own domain boundaries.

Two candidate routes are well supported by the distinction in ABET's program criteria between naval-architecture curriculum topics and marine-engineering system topics, and by the discipline's professional reference literature. This is a synthesis for student navigation, not a claim that every country accredits two separate programs. Remove non-engineering ISCO aggregates from student-facing specialization labels and preserve technician routes only where their own learning requirements are explicitly scoped.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Naval Architecture and Ship Design** — Ship form and concept/design integration, hydrostatics, stability, structures, hydrodynamics, resistance, propulsion interfaces and safety/performance trade-offs. Boundary: Owns vessel-level design decisions; detailed machinery/system design may be a marine-engineering branch.
- **Marine Engineering and Ship Systems** — Design, integration, operation and lifecycle support of propulsion, energy, machinery, electrical/control and other onboard systems. Boundary: Owns ship systems and plant; vessel hull/form architecture remains naval architecture.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not add ocean engineering just because it is adjacent: ABET identifies distinct ocean-engineering topics (oceanography, waves, underwater acoustics). Offshore energy/subsea systems overlap but should not be swept in unless the domain scope expressly includes them. Administration and protective services are supporting occupations, not engineering specializations.
- Computational signals: Computational fluid dynamics, structural analysis, hydrodynamic modeling and digital twins support both paths; they are methods/tool themes, not separate specialties by default.
- Jurisdiction note: Accreditation, professional registration, classification society rules, flag-state requirements and vessel certification are jurisdictional/industry-specific. ABET is a US-based accreditor with international reach, not a global regulator.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [ABET / Society of Naval Architects and Marine Engineers: Criteria for Accrediting Engineering Programs, 2025–2026](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/accreditation-criteria/criteria-for-accrediting-engineering-programs-2025-2026/) — Lines 708–721 distinguish naval architecture (hydrostatics, structural mechanics, materials, energy/propulsion, instrumentation) from marine engineering (energy/propulsion, materials, instrumentation) and ocean engineering topics. Supports: Strong evidence that these are related but distinguishable curricular domains and that ocean engineering has a separate boundary. Limit: US accreditation criteria; criteria describe program curricula, not a binding global specialization map.
- [Society of Naval Architects and Marine Engineers: Principles of Naval Architecture](https://sname.org/principles-naval-architecture) — Professional reference volumes cover ship stability/strength, resistance/propulsion/vibration and ship motion in waves. Supports: Supports the technical scope of naval architecture and its distinct but coupled topic clusters. Limit: Professional reference scope is not itself a learner taxonomy or occupational licensing rule.
- [Society of Naval Architects and Marine Engineers: Marine Engineering](https://sname.org/marine-engineering) — Describes integrated ship-system architecture/sizing and marine engineering installations in ship design. Supports: Supports systems integration as a distinct marine-engineering learning route. Limit: Professional technical material, not a global program accreditation standard.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
