# D121: Forestry and Wildlife Management — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_parent_scope_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

All thirteen current subgroups are broad occupational codes; count is not defensible as specialization count. The prior five-area proposal requires scope and overlap review.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The parent combines forestry and wildlife management, two related but distinct natural-resource disciplines. Preserve parent in this lane, but make shared foundations explicit and avoid implying that forestry operations qualify a learner for wildlife science or vice versa.

Four candidate areas have distinct subject/practice outcomes in FAO forestry-education sources. Forestry and wildlife are not interchangeable; this parent may ultimately need two parent domains or a clearly shared natural-resource foundation with separate branches. The four-card count is a lane synthesis, not universal.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Forest Ecology, Silviculture and Regeneration** — Forest ecology, regeneration, stand development and silvicultural interventions.
- **Forest Management, Inventory and Planning** — Forest measurement, inventories, management plans, sustainable harvest scheduling, policy/economics and monitoring.
- **Forest Operations and Products** — Harvesting/logging systems, forest engineering, wood science, utilization and forest-products value chains.
- **Wildlife Ecology and Conservation Management** — Wildlife ecology, habitat/population management, protected-area/conservation practice and human-wildlife considerations.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Fire, agroforestry, forest pathology, extension, watershed management, range management and forest policy can be modules/routes inside the four areas depending on parent boundary, not automatic additional cards. Do not turn ILO occupations into specializations.
- Computational signals: Forest inventory/GIS, remote sensing, biometrics, growth models and wildlife telemetry are cross-cutting tools/methods; no 'computational forestry' child is established by this evidence.
- Jurisdiction note: Forestry tenure, harvest permissions, protected species, wildlife use, fire regulation and Indigenous/community rights are governed locally and by applicable international instruments.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Forestry Curriculum Development and Revision](https://www.fao.org/4/y1842e/y1842e22.htm) — Curriculum modules include wildlife ecology/management, silviculture, forest management planning, inventory, integrated land use and forest products. Supports: Independent disciplinary learning areas and boundaries; also illustrates region-specific implementation. Limit: Namibia curriculum-development case; content is comparative, not a global mandated curriculum.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Sustainable Forest Management Toolbox: Silviculture in natural forests](https://www.fao.org/sustainable-forest-management-toolbox/modules/silviculture-in-natural-forests/en) — Defines silviculture, forest management unit, regeneration, growth, composition, health and quality; links production, biodiversity and ecosystem services. Supports: Silviculture is a coherent practice area while objectives may vary. Limit: Technical guidance, not a student taxonomy.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Forestry education: new trends and prospects](https://www.fao.org/4/ap438e/ap438e00.pdf) — Curriculum examples distinguish forest ecology, silviculture, measurement/inventory, operations/products, wildlife and policy/economics options. Supports: Cross-curriculum corroboration of differentiated forestry learning routes. Limit: Review of selected curricula; does not prescribe global child count.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
