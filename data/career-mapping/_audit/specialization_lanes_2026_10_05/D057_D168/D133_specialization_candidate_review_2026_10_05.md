# D133: Luxury and Bespoke Specialist Interiors — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_no_universal_set_parent_scope_inconsistent`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: Fashion & Product Designers (5 roles); Interior & Visual Designers (4 roles); Jewellers & Watchmakers (10 roles); Furniture & Garment Makers (14 roles)

The records span interior/visual design, fashion and product design, jewellery/watchmaking, furniture and garment trades. Several are not interior practice; the adjective 'luxury/bespoke' is a market/commission context, not evidence for a coherent field-wide specialization structure.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The parent title conflates a market positioning/quality level ('luxury'), a way of commissioning/making ('bespoke') and interior design. First decide whether this is interior design in premium contexts or a broader luxury craft/product domain. Until then, its current contents do not support a reliable student-facing specialization taxonomy.

No distinct universal specialization set is defensible under the current parent. Existing subgroups show scope drift, not four validated areas. If narrowed to luxury/bespoke interiors, routes such as residential, hospitality, retail or yacht interiors are plausible context labels but the evidence gathered here does not establish a globally recognized taxonomy or justify selecting a fixed list. If the intended parent includes luxury products/crafts, split or rename the parent before reviewing its specializations.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- No separate specialization is justified by the reviewed evidence; use the explicit disposition below.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not treat 'luxury' as a separate occupation, or assume jewellery, garment making, furniture making and interior design share a common learner foundation beyond design/materials/business basics. Do not turn client sector examples into formal specializations without route-level evidence.
- Computational signals: 3D visualization, CAD/BIM, digital fabrication and rendering are tools/methods used across design and making; digital luxury retail or visualization is not a separate specialization without a defined scope.
- Jurisdiction note: Professional title protection, building/fire/accessibility codes and interior-design licensure differ by jurisdiction. Project market tier and bespoke commissioning are not legal credentials.
- No-specialization disposition: Hold all subgroup candidates at the review layer: the present parent scope is internally inconsistent, and 'luxury/bespoke' alone cannot determine a globally valid set of interior or craft specializations. Clarify parent scope first; then source candidate routes from international design-professional and education frameworks plus cross-market practice evidence.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Federation of Interior Architects/Designers: IFI Interior Architecture/Design Definition](https://ifiworld.org/about/ifi/what-is-interior-architecture-design/) — Professional definition frames interior architecture/design around the designed interior environment and its human, spatial and technical dimensions. Supports: Provides a discipline boundary for interior design distinct from apparel, jewellery and general product manufacture. Limit: Professional federation definition does not establish luxury-market specialization labels or universal subgroup count.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
