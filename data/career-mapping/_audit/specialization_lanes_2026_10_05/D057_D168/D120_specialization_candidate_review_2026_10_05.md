# D120: Fisheries and Aquaculture — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_refined_candidate_set_parent_boundary_open`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: 62 Fish Farm Technicians & Masters; 75 Food processing, wood working, garment and other craft and related trades workers; 92 Aquaculture Field Workers

Current occupation buckets do not express the full fisheries/aquaculture discipline. Generic processing and field-worker categories do not define coherent learner areas.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Keep fisheries (capture, resource and ecosystem management) distinct from aquaculture production while allowing shared aquatic science. Processing/value chain is a downstream but established sector pathway; keep it only if this domain's scope includes post-harvest and product use. Extension/economics and feed/nutrition appear important but may be embedded competencies rather than separate specialization cards.

Four candidates provide clearer boundaries than the earlier six-way split. Nutrition/feed, extension and fisheries economics are material topics that should not become separate top-level areas without evidence of distinct routes. The post-harvest card depends on parent scope and should be removed if the root is limited to harvest/production.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Aquaculture Production and Systems** — Plan and operate aquatic animal/plant production systems, hatchery/nursery, grow-out, water quality and biosecurity.
- **Fisheries and Aquatic-Resource Management** — Capture fisheries, aquatic resources/ecosystems, stock monitoring, governance and sustainable management.
- **Aquatic Animal Health and Biosecurity** — Health surveillance, diagnosis, prevention and control for farmed aquatic animals, within authorized scope.
- **Fisheries Post-harvest and Value Chains** — Handling, preservation, processing, quality/safety and value-chain activities for aquatic products.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create separate cards for each species, gear, waterbody or production technology. Keep fisheries management distinct from aquaculture; keep fish-health competencies distinct from general veterinary medicine but state professional authorization boundaries.
- Computational signals: Stock assessment, GIS/remote sensing, sensor telemetry and feed optimization are cross-cutting methods. They support fisheries/aquaculture work and are not themselves specializations.
- Jurisdiction note: Aquatic animal diagnosis/treatment, fisheries access, vessel safety, food processing and export certification are jurisdiction-specific. State local regulatory requirements separately.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: FAO Voluntary Guidelines for Securing Sustainable Small-Scale Fisheries: value chains, post-harvest and trade](https://www.fao.org/voluntary-guidelines-small-scale-fisheries/key-thematic-areas/value-chains--post-harvest-and-trade/8/en) — Chapter 7 covers value chains, post-harvest operations and trade as a distinct fisheries activity set. Supports: Post-harvest/value-chain practice is a recognizable pathway when within the parent scope. Limit: Guidance focuses on small-scale fisheries and is not a universal curriculum or taxonomy.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: Reducing food loss and waste in aquatic value chains](https://www.fao.org/flw-in-fish-value-chains/overview/en) — Value-chain overview covers aquatic-food production through post-harvest quality, safety and loss issues. Supports: Production and post-harvest are distinguishable segments of the aquatic food value chain. Limit: FAO sector framing; exact training organization varies by country and institution.
- [World Organisation for Animal Health: Aquatic animals and health standards](https://www.woah.org/en/what-we-do/animal-health-and-welfare/aquatic-animals/) — WOAH describes global aquatic animal health/welfare standards, diagnostic methods, biosecurity and disease surveillance. Supports: Aquatic health and biosecurity have identifiable technical practice boundaries. Limit: Standards support animal-health governance, not a subgroup count; competent-authority rules vary.
- [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations: FAO thematic areas: fisheries and aquaculture](https://www.fao.org/biodiversity/topics/en) — Separate thematic work areas include fisheries and aquaculture, land/water, biodiversity and related production concerns. Supports: Fisheries and aquaculture are globally legible sector areas. Limit: Thematic index, not formal education taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
