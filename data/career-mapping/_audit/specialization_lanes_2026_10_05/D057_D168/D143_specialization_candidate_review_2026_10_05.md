# D143: Forensic Science, Digital Forensics and Criminal Investigation — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_mixed_parent_and_candidate_discipline_boundaries`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **4**. Current records: Forensic Medicine (2 roles); Cybersecurity & Digital Forensics (9 roles); Laboratory Science Technicians (8 roles); Criminal Investigation (3 roles)

The four records name mixed fields, occupations and job families. Forensic medicine is a clinical specialty; cybersecurity is much broader than digital evidence examination; laboratory technician is a skill level rather than a forensic discipline; investigation is a separate evidence-gathering practice.

## Scope and disposition

Parent boundary: The root joins forensic science, digital forensics and criminal investigation. A broad evidence-science parent can cover laboratory examinations, digital evidence and crime-scene/investigative methods, but medical pathology and general cybersecurity require explicit boundaries. Preserve separate clinical/medical ownership and do not imply that incident-response cybersecurity is criminal digital forensics.

ENFSI's discipline working groups and best-practice manuals distinguish domains such as DNA, digital imaging, documents, drugs, fingerprints, firearms/GSR and fire investigation. That evidence supports multiple forensic disciplines, not the current mixed ISCO occupation buckets. Five candidate practice areas are a synthesis; forensic medicine and criminal investigation require parent-ownership decisions before student-facing promotion.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Forensic Biology and DNA Analysis** — Biological trace and DNA examination for evidential questions using validated laboratory methods. Boundary: Forensic lab work; clinical diagnostics and population genetics are different purposes.
- **Forensic Chemistry and Trace Evidence** — Chemical, toxicological, materials, drugs, explosives, firearms-residue and trace-evidence examination where the lab scope supports it. Boundary: Several methods may remain nested topics; do not create one area per evidence type without distinct training paths.
- **Digital and Multimedia Forensics** — Forensic acquisition, preservation, examination and reporting of digital devices, data, images and related electronic evidence. Boundary: Evidence integrity and forensic method distinguish this from general cyber defense, penetration testing or incident response.
- **Crime-Scene Examination and Investigative Evidence** — Scene documentation, collection, preservation and interpretation of physical evidence in investigation. Boundary: Investigative authority and procedures are jurisdiction-specific; general detective work is not automatically forensic science.
- **Forensic Pathology and Medical Examination** — Medical examination and interpretation of injury, death and biological evidence for legal proceedings. Boundary: A distinct medical specialty; retain only if the domain explicitly includes clinical/medicolegal professions and explain jurisdictional qualifications.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not make each forensic evidence class a separate top-level specialization when the same laboratory learning route covers it. Do not include generic cybersecurity, police leadership, prosecution or clinical diagnosis as forensics merely because those occupations may touch a case.
- Computational signal: Digital evidence examination, multimedia forensics, forensic data recovery and analytical/statistical interpretation are computationally intensive; they are not equivalent to broad cybersecurity. Computational methods also support non-digital forensic fields.
- Jurisdiction note: Evidence law, chain-of-custody procedure, police powers, forensic laboratory accreditation and medical qualifications vary by jurisdiction. ENFSI is a European network and its methods are not global law.

## Sources and limitations

- [European Network of Forensic Science Institutes (ENFSI): Expert Working Groups and Best Practice Manuals](https://enfsi.eu/) — ENFSI expert-working-group and publication catalogue identifies forensic disciplines including DNA, digital imaging, documents, drugs, fingerprints, firearms/GSR and other evidence domains; digital-technology BPM provides method-specific practice guidance. Supports: Professional forensic-science network evidence that forensic work divides by evidence discipline and validated methods, not generic occupational rank. Limitation: Regional European network; its groups are not a complete global education taxonomy and investigation/medical practice remain legally distinct.
- [ENFSI: Best Practice Manual for the Forensic Examination of Digital Technology](https://enfsi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/1._forensic_examination_of_digital_technology_0.pdf) — Digital forensic examination manual, ENFSI-BPM-FIT-01, version 01 (2015). Supports: Documents a distinct forensic digital-evidence workflow separate from generic cyber-security work. Limitation: European guidance and dated edition; use current validated methods and local evidentiary rules.

Evidence supports candidate discipline/practice distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Existing role-group membership is not used as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization assignment is made.
