## Parent scope and disposition

Keep academic study of religion/theology distinct from faith-specific ministry and spiritual-care practice. Interfaith leadership is a cross-tradition civic/community practice, not a universal credential or single theology. Do not assume one tradition's ministry model, titles or qualifications apply globally.

AAR materials support academic religious-studies work and engagement with public/chaplaincy contexts; World Council of Churches materials discuss theological education and pastoral formation in particular ecumenical contexts. These sources show several distinct practices but do not represent every religion globally. Four candidate routes are provisional; the current ISCO aggregates are unsuitable, and the parent must distinguish scholarship from faith-specific ministry and regulated institutional chaplaincy.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Theology and Religious Studies** — Academic study of religious traditions, texts, histories, beliefs, ethics and lived religion using the relevant scholarly methods. Boundary: Theology may be tradition-specific while religious studies may be non-confessional/comparative; do not collapse them into ordained ministry.
- **Congregational and Community Ministry** — Faith-community leadership, pastoral care, worship/service planning, teaching and community support within a tradition or organization. Boundary: Ordination, authorization and titles are tradition- and institution-specific; never imply a universal license.
- **Chaplaincy and Spiritual Care** — Provide ethically bounded spiritual/religious support in settings such as hospitals, prisons, education, military or community services. Boundary: Institutional endorsement and professional requirements vary; clinical mental-health care remains a separately qualified profession.
- **Interfaith Engagement and Community Leadership** — Facilitate dialogue, cooperation, conflict-sensitive engagement and service across religious/worldview communities. Boundary: A practice area may exist across traditions, but avoid implying a standardized global credential or single institutional form.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not split the taxonomy by religion/denomination or treat theological subdisciplines as a universal student taxonomy. Do not classify counselling/therapy as ministry merely because pastoral care includes support; clinical diagnosis/treatment requires separate qualifications.
- Computational signal: Digital humanities, scripture/text analysis, religious archives and online community practice can be computationally supported, but computation is not a defining common requirement for all religious ministry or theological study.
- Jurisdiction note: Ordination, clerical status, chaplaincy endorsement, education requirements and institutional access are set by religious bodies, employers and governments, varying by tradition and jurisdiction. The sources cited are not universal religious authority.

## Sources and limits

- [American Academy of Religion (AAR): Public Engagement in Religious Studies](https://aarweb.org/religious-studies-matters/public-engagement/) — AAR describes religious-studies scholar engagement, including training/dialogue with government and prison/military chaplaincy leaders and public contexts. Supports: Supports scholarship and applied chaplaincy/public-engagement as distinct contexts. Limitation: AAR is a US scholarly organization and its examples are not globally representative or a ministry credential standard.
- [World Council of Churches (WCC): Keynote address on theology and unity in Oslo consultation](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/keynote-address-on-theology-and-unity-in-oslo-consultation) — Discusses theological education's role in pastoral ministry formation and joint witness/service across Christian denominational and cultural contexts. Supports: Corroborates ministry formation and interfaith/ecumenical public practice as distinct from academic religious studies. Limitation: WCC is an ecumenical Christian organization; it cannot define global practice across religions.
- [AAR: Stages of Education](https://aarweb.org/resource/stages-of-education/) — AAR resource addresses religious studies/theology education through university departments and professional/education pathways. Supports: Supports treating academic religious studies/theology as a distinct education pathway. Limitation: US educational context and primarily academic guidance; not a global ministry structure.
