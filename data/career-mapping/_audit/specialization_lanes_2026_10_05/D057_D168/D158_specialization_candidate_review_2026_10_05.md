# D158: Corporate Finance and Investment Banking — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_is_two_adjacent_fields_and_labels_too_broad`. Source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: Production and specialised services managers (2 roles); Business and administration professionals (18 roles)

The two broad occupational groups obscure distinct corporate finance and investment-banking practices. The domain description additionally includes controllers, budget analysts and bank managers, which are generic finance/banking functions rather than evidence of investment banking specialization.

## Parent scope and disposition

Separate corporate financial decision-making inside companies from client-facing investment banking and capital-markets transactions. These interact but are not synonyms: one is a corporate function, the other an intermediary/advisory and markets business. Clarify if the parent intends both, with shared finance foundations and distinct learner branches.

CFA Institute's practice-informed body of knowledge distinguishes corporate financing decisions, equity investment analysis, fixed income/structured products and credit analysis. That supports different learning routes but not a definitive four-subgroup standard. The current groups and description should not be promoted as student specializations until corporate finance is separated from investment banking and generic controller/budget/bank-manager roles are reassigned by task evidence.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Corporate Financial Planning and Treasury** — Capital allocation, financing choices, liquidity, cash/treasury and financial planning for an operating organization. Boundary: Corporate control/accounting roles may be adjacent; do not treat all accounting/finance operations as corporate-finance specialization.
- **Mergers, Acquisitions and Corporate Advisory** — Transaction analysis, valuation, due diligence, structuring and advisory for mergers, acquisitions, divestitures and restructuring. Boundary: Legal, tax and accounting due diligence require distinct professional scopes; banking advice is not legal counsel.
- **Capital Markets and Underwriting** — Help issuers raise debt/equity capital through securities markets, underwriting, structuring and execution. Boundary: Market-specific authorization and underwriting rules vary; distinguish from portfolio investing and sales/trading.
- **Financial Modeling, Valuation and Deal Execution** — Build transaction/corporate valuation analysis and financial models that support corporate financing and investment-banking decisions. Boundary: Modeling is a shared task cluster across branches and may be a learning theme rather than a separate specialization card.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not create a subgroup for every transaction type, industry, security, bank department or valuation technique. Keep corporate treasury, investment banking, asset management, commercial lending and accounting distinct where tasks differ; do not claim a CFA credential is required for every role.
- Computational signal: Financial modeling, scenario analysis, valuation, deal databases and market-data analysis are core tools/use cases. They are branch-specific requirements, not a generic promise of coding skills across all corporate-finance roles.
- Jurisdiction note: Securities advice, underwriting, investment banking conduct, fiduciary duties and professional registrations vary by market. CFA curriculum is global professional education but not a legal permission to practise.

## Sources and limits

- [CFA Institute: Candidate Body of Knowledge](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/cbok) — Practice-informed curriculum topics include corporate financing decisions, equity analysis, fixed income, credit risk and structured products. Supports: Professional curriculum evidence for distinct financial tasks and topics requiring explicit parent/branch boundaries. Limitation: CFA curriculum covers investment analysis broadly and is not an investment-banking taxonomy or required credential for every finance role.
- [CFA Institute: Credit Analysis for Corporate Issuers](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/credit-analysis-for-corporate-issuers) — Describes qualitative borrower analysis, cash-flow/financial statement analysis, ratios, seniority and credit risk as credit-analysis learning outcomes. Supports: Shows credit analysis is a separate detailed function, which should not be folded indiscriminately into corporate-finance/investment-banking branches. Limitation: One current CFA curriculum reading, not a global occupational classification.
