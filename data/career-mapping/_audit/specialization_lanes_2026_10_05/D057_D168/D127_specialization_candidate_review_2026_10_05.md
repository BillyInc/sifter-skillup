# D127: Early Childhood Education, Nursery and Childcare — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_candidate_set_age_bands_jurisdictional`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **5**. Current records: 13 Nursery Leadership; 23 Preschool Teaching; 26 Legal, social and cultural professionals; 34 Legal, social, cultural and related associate professionals; 53 Childminding & Care

Two existing labels are plausible practice clusters; three others are generic occupational-family groupings. The present five-card count is not justified by ISCO.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The parent combines education and care from birth into early primary years, across centre-, school- and home-based settings. Age/development stage is a clearer student route axis than job title or setting alone, but exact age bands differ across countries and curricula.

Three age-stage routes are a useful candidate for student navigation, supported by NAEYC's birth–3, 3–5 and 5–8 groups, but those bands come from a US professional framework and are not global legal/education divisions. Preserve local age-stage and qualification mappings; childcare setting/leadership can be contextual routes, not automatic universal specialization cards.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Infant and Toddler Care and Development** — Development, responsive caregiving, health/safety, relationships and early learning for infants and toddlers.
- **Preschool and Early Learning** — Play-based learning, curriculum, observation/assessment, family engagement and care for preschool-aged children.
- **Early Primary Learning and School Transition** — Learning and development in the early primary years and continuity between early-learning and school settings.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Special education/inclusion, curriculum leadership, family engagement, home-based childcare and administration can be cross-cutting pathways. Avoid making teaching versus caregiving mutually exclusive; young children need integrated care and learning. Do not infer ages from local school-entry law globally.
- Computational signals: Learning technology, digital observation records and assistive technology are supporting tools; not independent early-childhood specializations by default.
- Jurisdiction note: Childcare licensing, safeguarding, staff-to-child ratios, teacher qualifications, mandatory reporting and school-entry ages vary by jurisdiction.
- No-specialization disposition: Not applicable: evidence supports candidate area(s), subject to the stated residuals.

## Evidence and limits

- [National Association for the Education of Young Children: Professional Preparation Standards summary](https://www.naeyc.org/node/529) — Preparation spans at least two of birth–3, 3–5 and 5–8 years and multiple early-learning settings. Supports: Age-stage route separation and the need to cover settings, not a universal global age taxonomy. Limit: US professional association framework; terminology and ages are not binding globally.
- [National Association for the Education of Young Children: Developmentally Appropriate Practice: definition and position statement](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/definition/) — Lists linked practice functions: learning community, relationships, assessment, teaching, curriculum and professionalism. Supports: Shared foundations and cross-cutting competencies should not be split into over-narrow subgroups. Limit: US professional position statement; comparative evidence, not universal regulation.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
