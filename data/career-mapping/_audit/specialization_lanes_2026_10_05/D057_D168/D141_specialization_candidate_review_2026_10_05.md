## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: Treat quantum information/technology as an interdisciplinary technical domain spanning quantum computation, communication/networking and sensing/metrology, grounded in quantum physics and information theory. Policy, standards, teaching and general operations are functions around the field, not equivalent technical specializations.

NIST's description of quantum information science distinguishes quantum computing, sensing and networks/communications; the existing source-backed QT_CFQT subgroup structure already separates software/algorithms and hardware/sensing. Four candidate routes make the omitted communications/network dimension visible and move teaching/policy/operations out of technical specialization labels. This is a global discipline synthesis, not a universal taxonomy or evidence of a mature labour market for every pathway.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Quantum Computing Hardware and Devices** — Physical implementations, quantum devices, control/readout, fabrication/material platforms and cryogenic or photonic systems. Boundary: Hardware platform topics are specialisms within this area; do not treat every physical platform as a separate root area.
- **Quantum Algorithms and Software** — Quantum algorithms, programming, compilation, error mitigation/correction and software tooling for quantum systems. Boundary: Algorithms and software often depend on hardware constraints but remain a coherent learning path.
- **Quantum Communication and Networks** — Quantum information transfer, networking, protocols, quantum links and enabling network technologies. Boundary: Post-quantum cryptography is classical cryptography designed for quantum-era threats and should not be mislabeled quantum communication.
- **Quantum Sensing and Metrology** — Quantum-enhanced measurement and sensors, precision metrology, timing and sensing applications. Boundary: Applied deployments in medicine/geology/navigation are contexts; core quantum sensing is the technical field.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not present application sectors (medicine, chemistry, materials, finance) as quantum specialties unless their learning outcomes are distinct. Do not merge standards/policy or operations with engineering tracks; show them as contextual roles or separate domains as appropriate. Distinguish quantum-safe classical cryptography from quantum information technology.
- Computational signals: Quantum software, algorithms, compilation and error correction are central computational work. Classical simulation and high-performance computing are enabling methods; their presence alone does not make all quantum roles software roles.
- Jurisdiction note: Standards, export controls, investment programmes and national quantum strategies vary by country. NIST is a US standards agency; its science overview supports technical scope, not a global policy or credential framework.

## Evidence and limits

- [National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST): Quantum Information Science](https://www.nist.gov/quantum-information-science) — Defines QIS at the intersection of quantum physics and information theory; identifies quantum computing, quantum sensors/measurement and quantum networks as major focus areas. Supports: Authoritative technical evidence for the four candidate routes and their distinct computing/sensing/network dimensions. Limitation: US national metrology agency overview; it does not prescribe a global education taxonomy or show labour-market maturity.
- [National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST): Quantum Sensing Explained](https://www.nist.gov/quantum-information-science/quantum-sensing-explained) — Explains quantum-enhanced measurements and examples/applications across physical quantities and sectors. Supports: Substantiates quantum sensing/metrology as a distinct technical focus and separates it from computing alone. Limitation: Explanatory agency publication; not a career or curriculum standard.
