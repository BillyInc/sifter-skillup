# D102 — Carpentry, Joinery, Wood Technology and Furniture Making: specialization candidate review

**Disposition:** candidate_set_reviewed_with_explicit_residuals

**Parent scope basis:** Carpentry and wood technology is the craft of shaping wood into buildings, furniture and everyday objects. Carpenters build frames and install doors, windows and staircases, machine operators shape and dry timber, furniture makers craft tables and chairs, and artisans handcraft baskets, carvings and toys. Construction firms, furniture workshops and joinery factories are the usual employers.

## Candidate student-facing areas

1. **Site Carpentry and Structural Timber** — Measure, cut, assemble and install timber components and structural/site work from construction drawings.
2. **Joinery and Fitted Woodwork** — Make and install accurately jointed doors, windows, stairs, cabinets and fitted interior/exterior woodwork, commonly with workshop processes.
3. **Furniture and Wood-Product Manufacturing** — Design-to-production, machine, assemble and finish furniture or other wood products.

## Decision

Three practice families are a reasonable provisional synthesis. Carpentry and joinery are directly distinguished by international occupational standards; furniture/wood-product production is retained as a candidate because the parent explicitly names furniture making, but requires curriculum/industry evidence beyond occupational classification before canonical approval.

## Parent boundaries and exclusions

- Timber building/site installation differs from workshop joinery; they can share foundational measurement/material skills but have different outputs and workflows.
- Cabinetmaking/furniture are related; merge them if the final evidence does not show a coherent separate furniture learner route.
- Basketry and fibre crafts belong under D105; industrial forestry/pulp processing under D112.

## Evidence and claim limits

- [WorldSkills International: Carpentry WSOS 2024](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1698/) — Associated role: site/building timber tasks, setting out, cutting, joining, assembly and installation. **Supports:** Supports site carpentry/structural timber as a distinct output context. **Limit:** International competition standard; not universal taxonomy. **Scope:** International/global benchmark.
- [WorldSkills International: Joinery WSOS 2024](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1717/) — Associated role: workshop production, drawings, joints, assembly, installation and finishing. **Supports:** Supports a distinct joinery/fitted-woodwork practice. **Limit:** International competition standard; does not settle furniture subgroup boundary. **Scope:** International/global benchmark.
- [WorldSkills International: Cabinetmaking WSOS 2024](https://worldskills.org/what/projects/wsos/2024/events/579/skills/1704/) — Associated role and description distinguishing cabinetmaking by product quality and intricacy. **Supports:** Corroborates specialist workshop furniture/cabinet product work. **Limit:** Competition standard; not a global academic taxonomy. **Scope:** International/global benchmark.
- [International Labour Organization: ISCO-08 Volume I](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf) — Unit groups 7521–7523: wood treaters, cabinet-makers and related workers, and woodworking-machine tool setters/operators. **Supports:** Provides international occupational-context corroboration for woodworking task families. **Limit:** ISCO classifies occupations; it does not validate student specializations or role mapping. **Scope:** International/global benchmark.

**Evidence limit:** Sources establish practice tasks and boundaries; they do not prescribe universal specialization counts or endorse this catalogue.
