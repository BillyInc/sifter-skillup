# D139: Game Design — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_label_scope_mismatch_candidate_routes`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Game Art & Animation (22 roles); Gameplay Programming (12 roles); Creative Direction (7 roles)

The three groups are plausible parts of a game-production ecosystem but omit design/narrative, audio, production, QA and other established disciplines. The root label 'Game Design' is narrower than its art, programming and direction content.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The existing coverage is game development/production, not game design alone. Rename or narrow the parent before finalizing subgroup count. If broad coverage is intended, use game development as the parent and distinguish design, art, engineering, audio, production and quality disciplines; not every game-adjacent business/legal/support role belongs in the same specialization tree.

IGDA's global professional communities and discipline awards recognize game art, writing/narrative, engineering/technology, audio, design, production, QA and localization among distinct contributions. These support a wider parent scope than 'Game Design'. Six candidate routes organize major student learning pathways but are product synthesis, not a standardized global taxonomy; confirm domain boundaries and avoid overfitting award categories.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Game Design and Narrative** — Rules, systems, levels, player experience, interaction, progression, writing and narrative/world design. Boundary: Game-design specialization; distinct from implementing the game systems in code.
- **Game Art, Animation and Visual Development** — 2D/3D art, characters, environments, animation, visual effects and game-facing visual assets. Boundary: Keep specialist production techniques within a coherent art route unless learning paths materially diverge.
- **Game Engineering and Technology** — Gameplay, engine, graphics, tools, networking, systems and platform programming for games. Boundary: Technology implementation, not game design as a whole.
- **Game Audio** — Interactive sound, music, dialogue and audio implementation for games. Boundary: IGDA identifies interactive audio as a distinct discipline community; separate from general audio production when game interactivity changes the work.
- **Game Production and Team Delivery** — Planning, coordination, production management and delivery across multidisciplinary game teams. Boundary: A practice area supporting the product lifecycle rather than a creative direction umbrella.
- **Game Quality, User Research and Accessibility** — Testing, player research, usability/accessibility evaluation and quality processes for playable products. Boundary: Include only where this domain intentionally covers production-quality pathways; do not imply QA is identical to design.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not promote creative director automatically as a separate specialization: it is a senior leadership role spanning disciplines. Do not create a top-level area for each engine, platform, art technique, genre or production stage. Localization, business, legal and publishing may be adjacent pathways or separate domains.
- Computational signals: Programming, tools/engine technology, graphics, networking, procedural systems and gameplay AI are computational practices; computational work is concentrated in the engineering branch, not shared equally by every learner or role.
- Jurisdiction note: The field is global and studio structures vary by production size, platform and market. IGDA categories describe a professional community, not required credentials or a universal studio org chart.

## Evidence and limits

- [International Game Developers Association (IGDA): Discipline Special Interest Groups](https://igda.org/sig-type/discipline/) — Lists Game Art, Game Design, Engineering, Interactive Audio, Production and other discipline-based communities. Supports: Confirms game-development work contains distinct professional disciplines beyond the three current subgroup labels. Limitation: Voluntary professional communities are not an exhaustive, standardized education taxonomy.
- [IGDA: Global Industry Game Awards](https://igda.org/global-industry-game-awards/) — Discipline categories include art, writing/narrative, engineering/technology and subdisciplines such as design, audio, production support, QA/localization in the broader IGDA ecosystem. Supports: Cross-checks actual multidisciplinary game-production contributions and guards against a design-only parent label. Limitation: Award categories reflect a particular award framework and should not be copied as a complete subgroup set.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
