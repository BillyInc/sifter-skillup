# D148: Offshore and Subsea Engineering — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_scope_boundary_open_offshore_energy_conditional`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **2**. Current records: Science and Engineering Professionals (12 roles); Science and Engineering Associate Professionals (6 roles)

The two children are generic occupational-skill levels. The parent description is confined to oil/gas, even though offshore/subsea engineering can include broader low-carbon/offshore energy and marine infrastructure contexts; the actual product scope needs validation.

## Scope and disposition

Parent boundary: Retain engineered systems installed/operated offshore and subsea, but decide whether this is intentionally oil-and-gas subsea production or a broader offshore engineering domain. ISO's 2025 subsea production-system standard covers concept through decommissioning and includes low-carbon energy in its title; offshore renewables and marine construction require deliberate scope and source coverage, not silent exclusion.

ISO 13628-1:2025 is an international standard for subsea production/injection-system development and operation from concept to decommissioning; its title expressly extends to low-carbon energy. SPE technical communities distinguish subsea systems, platforms/floating systems, processing/flow assurance, measurement/control and facilities. These support technical route candidates but do not settle whether D148 should encompass offshore renewables, nor whether the domain should be renamed oil-and-gas subsea engineering. Five candidates therefore include one explicitly conditional route; scope decision is required before final count.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Subsea Production Systems and Controls** — Subsea production/injection system architecture, equipment, controls, integration, lifecycle operation and abandonment. Boundary: ISO 13628 explicitly scopes system lifecycle; pipeline/riser and vessel scopes may be covered by other standards and domains.
- **Offshore Wells, Drilling and Completions** — Well construction, drilling/completion systems and subsea well access/interfaces for offshore operations. Boundary: Oilfield discipline evidence supports this candidate; local regulator and operator-specific authorizations remain contextual.
- **Offshore Structures and Floating Systems** — Fixed/floating offshore structures, platform/floating production systems, structural integrity and marine environmental loading. Boundary: Naval architecture and offshore structural engineering overlap; distinguish fixed/floating production assets from ships and marine vessels.
- **Offshore Installation, Survey and Remote Operations** — Marine construction, positioning/survey, installation, inspection and remotely operated subsea intervention systems. Boundary: Include as engineering/technical practice only if the parent covers project execution; diving/ROV operations have distinct competence and safety schemes.
- **Offshore Renewable and Low-Carbon Systems (conditional)** — Engineering of offshore renewable-energy or low-carbon infrastructure where the domain explicitly includes non-hydrocarbon systems. Boundary: Conditional branch: verify actual intended parent scope and learning path before inclusion; avoid making 'offshore' itself a sufficient competency claim.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not retain ISCO skill-level groups as specializations. Do not treat refinery operation onshore, commercial diving, ROV piloting, marine navigation, offshore wind installation and subsea production as one undifferentiated route. Do not include renewables merely from the word 'offshore'; validate curriculum/tasks and parent-domain ownership.
- Computational signal: Subsea control/automation, digital twins, reservoir/flow simulation, remote inspection, positioning and integrity analytics are computational methods used in relevant branches. Their existence does not make every offshore role a software/AI role.
- Jurisdiction note: Offshore safety, well control, environmental approval, marine operations and competency certification differ by jurisdiction, asset type and operator. ISO is international standards evidence; standards and industry certificates are not interchangeable with professional licensure.

## Sources and limitations

- [International Organization for Standardization (ISO): ISO 13628-1:2025 — Design and operation of subsea production systems](https://www.iso.org/standard/86664.html) — International Standard, published January 2025; scope covers development and operation of subsea production/injection systems from concept development to decommissioning and abandonment; title includes low-carbon energy. Supports: Direct authoritative evidence for a lifecycle-based subsea systems area and a broader low-carbon boundary worth evaluating. Limitation: Specific to subsea production/injection systems; it does not establish all offshore engineering specializations or student role mappings.
- [Society of Petroleum Engineers (SPE): Technical Communities](https://oslo.spe.org/communities2/communities/tdhome) — Technical-community descriptions include subsea systems, measurement/control, platforms and floating systems, processing/flow assurance and facilities operations. Supports: Professional discipline evidence for distinct offshore/subsea engineering themes. Limitation: SPE is petroleum-focused and cannot alone establish global offshore-renewable taxonomy.
- [International Marine Contractors Association (IMCA): Technical Library: Offshore Survey and Remote Systems/ROV](https://www.imca-int.com/resources/technical-library/document-catalogue/?pageNumber=2&series=Offshore+Survey) — Technical catalogue separates offshore survey, remote systems/ROV, diving, marine, competence/training and related practice documentation. Supports: Shows offshore subsea execution includes distinct survey/remote-operations and marine-safety practices beyond design engineering. Limitation: Industry association guidance is activity-specific and does not establish a universal academic subgroup structure.

Evidence supports candidate discipline/practice distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Existing role-group membership is not used as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization assignment is made.
