# D129: Military and Armed Forces Careers — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_no_global_specialization_set`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: 01 Commissioned Armed Forces Officers; 02 Non-commissioned Armed Forces Officers; 03 Armed Forces Occupations, Other Ranks

The three existing child records are status/rank categories, not areas of specialization. National force structures, services, occupational systems and rank terms are sovereign and vary materially.

## Scope and proposed disposition

Parent boundary: The broad parent spans national armed services and many military occupations; no single global learner taxonomy is established by the evidence reviewed. Branches such as land, maritime or air forces are national organizational choices and are not universal specializations; operational/technical specialties are also force-specific.

No globally standardized specialization cards are justified. Keep only a carefully scoped global foundation (e.g., public/organizational context and transferable pre-service learning if product scope supports it), and represent actual career/selection/training paths as country- and service-specific. Do not present military eligibility, ranks, commissions or credentials as globally portable.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- No separate specialization is justified by the reviewed evidence; use the explicit disposition below.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not infer global branches, combat/non-combat taxonomies, rank pathways or military role families from one national force. Civilian defence, UN police/civilian missions and military service must remain distinct contexts.
- Computational signals: Cyber, intelligence analytics, autonomy and simulation are methods/specialties in some forces, but their existence does not establish a global armed-forces specialization catalogue; show them only with explicit service/jurisdiction evidence.
- Jurisdiction note: Recruitment eligibility, rank structure, branches, training, security clearance, service obligations and professional recognition are entirely jurisdiction/service-specific.
- No-specialization disposition: No global child taxonomy: military subgroups must be derived from a named country's/service's official occupational and training framework. Current rank-category subgroups are not global areas of specialization.

## Evidence and limits

- [United Nations Peacekeeping: Work with us](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/work-with-us) — States the UN has no standing military; military personnel are members of national armies contributed by Member States, while police and civilian functions have separate routes. Supports: Military service pathways are nationally owned and distinct from international civilian/police work. Limit: UN peacekeeping employment context; does not catalogue national armed forces or professions.
- [United Nations Careers: Careers outside of Headquarters](https://careers.un.org/careers-in-the-field?language=en) — Separates military, police and civilian mission career routes; military applicants must belong to a country's military. Supports: Avoid collapsing military and civilian international service pathways. Limit: UN employment context, not a global military career taxonomy.

Sources support discipline and practice distinctions; they do not by themselves establish a universal number of student-facing areas. Current occupational-group membership is not used as proof of domain/subgroup correctness. No role-to-specialization mapping is made.
