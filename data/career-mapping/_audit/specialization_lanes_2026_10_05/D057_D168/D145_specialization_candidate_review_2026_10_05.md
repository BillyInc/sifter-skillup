# D145: Neuroscience, Cognitive Science and Brain-Computer Interfaces — specialization audit

Status: `source_reviewed_parent_scope_should_be_split_or_explicitly_branched`. This is a source-grounded candidate review, not canonical taxonomy approval or academic-body sign-off.

## Existing structure

Current subgroup-record count: **3**. Current records: Neuroscience & Cognitive Research (3 roles); Clinical Psychologists & Therapists (5 roles); Health associate professionals (1 role)

The current records mix brain/cognition research with clinical psychology and a generic health occupation family. Brain-computer interfaces are an engineering/technology application not reflected by these labels. The domain's three-part title joins related but distinct disciplines.

## Scope and disposition

Parent boundary: The parent needs an explicit scope decision: neuroscience studies the nervous system; cognitive science is interdisciplinary study of mind/cognition; BCI/neurotechnology develops engineered interfaces. Clinical assessment and psychotherapy belong to appropriately governed psychology/health professions, not automatically to this research/engineering domain. Consider separating parent domains or maintaining clearly distinguished branches with no implied shared job qualification.

Cognitive Science Society evidence describes the field as interdisciplinary across psychology, linguistics, philosophy, AI/computer science, neuroscience and anthropology; BCI is an engineering/technology area and neuroscience is a separate scientific discipline. Four branches are candidates only if the parent is deliberately an interdisciplinary umbrella. Current clinical-psychologist/therapist subgroup is unsupported by that umbrella absent a defined clinical psychology parent and proper licensing context; root naming and scope should be resolved before accepting a final subgroup count.

## Candidate areas of specialization

- **Cellular, Molecular and Systems Neuroscience** — Neural cells, circuits and systems, development, physiology and mechanisms of nervous-system function. Boundary: Biological research pathway, distinct from clinical treatment and from general psychology.
- **Cognitive and Computational Neuroscience** — Neural mechanisms of perception, memory, language, action and cognition using behavioral, computational and brain-measurement methods. Boundary: Computational modeling is a method-rich area; not all cognitive science is neuroscience and not all neuroscience is cognitive.
- **Cognitive Science and Human Cognition** — Interdisciplinary study of cognition drawing on psychology, linguistics, computer science/AI, philosophy, neuroscience and related fields. Boundary: This is broader than brain science; keep its specific learning route distinct rather than treating it as a neuroscience subtopic.
- **Neurotechnology and Brain-Computer Interfaces** — Engineering and evaluation of systems that measure, decode or modulate neural signals and connect nervous-system activity with external devices. Boundary: Clinical use, human-subject research and medical-device approval require jurisdiction-specific governance; BCI is not a general neuroscientist job title.

## Boundary audit

- Omissions / over-splitting: Do not treat all mental-health practice as neuroscience or imply a neuroscience degree authorizes clinical practice. Do not split each cognitive function or neural technology into top-level areas. Keep neuroscience, cognitive science, clinical psychology and neuroengineering boundaries explicit.
- Computational signal: Computational neuroscience, cognitive modeling, signal processing and machine learning for neural data are established computational approaches. Their presence does not make general psychology clinical roles computational, nor does a BCI label prove an independent occupation family.
- Jurisdiction note: Clinical psychology, psychotherapy, neurodiagnostic practice, human-subject research and medical-device authorization are governed differently by jurisdiction. The cited society framework is international scholarly evidence, not licensure authority.

## Sources and limitations

- [Cognitive Science Society: Where is Cognitive Science Now?](https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/cogsci20/papers/0154/index.html) — Society-hosted symposium describes cognitive science as interdisciplinary, drawing on cognitive psychology, linguistics, philosophy, AI, neuroscience and anthropology, while discussing limits of disciplinary integration. Supports: Shows cognitive science is not simply a neuroscience subgroup and supports a separate branch/scope decision. Limitation: Scholarly symposium abstract, not a professional standard or exhaustive curriculum taxonomy.
- [Cognitive Science Society: Cognitive Science Journal scope](https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/csj-executive-editor-nominations/) — Society journal scope identifies anthropology, education, psychology, philosophy, linguistics, computer science, neuroscience and robotics as contributors to interdisciplinary cognitive-science research. Supports: Corroborates interdisciplinary scope and the need to avoid conflating cognitive science with clinical psychology or neuroscience alone. Limitation: Journal scope is a scholarly-community description, not formal occupational or clinical authorization.

Evidence supports candidate discipline/practice distinctions, not a universal subgroup count. Existing role-group membership is not used as proof of fit. No role-to-specialization assignment is made.
