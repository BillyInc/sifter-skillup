# D034 Supply Chain Management — specialization structure review

**Status:** Source-reviewed candidate set; pending catalogue-wide reconciliation. This is not a universally fixed subgroup taxonomy.

## Finding

D034 currently has four visible ISCO-derived groups across 157 role mappings: Logistics & Transport Managers (83), Supply Chain Analysts (8), Import, Export & Shipping Agents (40), and Transport & Cargo Coordinators (26). They mix leadership level, an analytic function, trade/forwarding activities and cargo execution. The current groups do not prove the right number of student areas or the fit of each role.

CSCMP describes supply-chain management as broader than logistics: it spans planning, sourcing/procurement, production/conversion, logistics and coordinating information flows. ASCM's SCOR Digital Standard gives an end-to-end process model covering Orchestrate, Plan, Order, Source, Transform, Fulfill and Return. These are processes, not a mandated student subgroup list or role classifier.

## Proposed student-facing areas

1. Supply Chain Strategy, Network and Resilience
2. Demand, Supply and Inventory Planning
3. Procurement, Sourcing and Supplier Management
4. Logistics, Warehousing and Fulfilment
5. Supply Chain Transformation, Returns and Circular Flows

Analytics and digital systems remain cross-cutting unless later evidence shows they form a coherent student branch with distinct learning and role pathways. “Transform” means supply-chain coordination around conversion/material flows here—not that manufacturing engineers automatically become supply-chain roles.

## Fix and boundary disposition

- Replace the current occupation-family subgroups only after catalogue-wide ID/naming reconciliation.
- Do not infer that 4 current rows mean 4 correct specializations, or set candidate count from their member counts.
- Broaden the student-facing description of SCM beyond transport and warehousing to include planning, sourcing, supplier management, network strategy, fulfilment and returns, consistent with professional definitions; preserve exact cross-domain boundaries.
- Treat customs/trade, shipping-agency, freight-forwarding and transport roles individually. Their labels alone do not establish a single specialization or parent fit.
- Keep hands-on manufacturing/industrial engineering in their technical parents; keep seller-side sales and general legal practice distinct. Show supply-chain interfaces, not duplicate ownership.
- Do not map roles to the five candidate areas from ISCO/subgroup membership or current counts. The independent role audit must compare actual tasks to the domain and selected branch.
- Keep customs procedures, trade controls, procurement law and transport safety jurisdiction-specific, while retaining globally recognizable specialization labels.
