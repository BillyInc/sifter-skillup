# D011 specialization review — Quantitative Finance and Financial Engineering

**Disposition:** Recommend three areas of specialization: quantitative pricing/financial engineering; quantitative financial risk; and systematic investment/quantitative trading. The professional sources support these different application purposes, not an exact universal subgroup count. No role mapping was reviewed.

## Existing structure checked

The current cards mix quantitative/statistical methods, broad investment/risk analysis, and trading/brokerage job groupings. Their memberships are unverified and were not used to define the areas. A title or securities transaction alone is not proof of quantitative-finance work.

## Recommended areas

1. **Quantitative Pricing and Financial Engineering** — mathematical/computational valuation and model construction/calibration for financial instruments and products.
2. **Quantitative Financial Risk Modeling** — quantitative measurement, stress/scenario analysis and modeling of market, credit, liquidity and related financial exposures.
3. **Systematic Investment and Quantitative Trading** — rules-based quantitative investment/trading, strategy testing, portfolio construction and execution/microstructure constraints.

Statistics, probability, optimization and programming are shared methods, not separate specializations. Model governance is cross-cutting. The boundary with general investment analysis/asset management, insurance/actuarial, trading and FinTech must be assessed separately; no roles were assigned here.

## Evidence and limits

- **GARP FRM 2026**: separates quantitative analysis and valuation/risk models from applied market, credit, operational, liquidity and treasury risk areas. [Source](https://www.garp.org/frm/program-exams)
- **CFA Institute CBOK**: includes quantitative methods, time-series, backtesting/simulation, portfolio construction, execution and market microstructure. [Source](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/cbok)
- **CFA Active Equity Investing (2026)**: distinguishes quantitative/systematic from fundamental/discretionary methods and describes model/backtest/portfolio/execution workflows. [Source](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/active-equity-investing-strategies)
- **CFA Portfolio Mathematics (2026)**: portfolio return, variance, covariance/correlation and risk measurement. [Source](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/portfolio-mathematics)
- **CFA curriculum structure**: separates quantitative methods, derivatives/risk and portfolio construction in its broad investment curriculum. [Source](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/curriculum)

GARP and CFA are professional curricula, not a universal occupational taxonomy; neither mandates these exact labels/count. These routes are not required for every finance learner. No role IDs, ISCO membership or role counts were used to infer fit.
