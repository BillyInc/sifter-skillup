# D042 — Media Production and Broadcasting: specialization review

**Disposition:** the parent scope needs correction before its areas of specialization can be finalized. The description combines stage/live performance with recorded audiovisual production and broadcasting. UNESCO classifies performance/celebration separately from audiovisual and interactive media. The three visible subgroup labels are mostly ISCO-derived occupation/work-function clusters, not a defensible final set of practice areas.

## Current structure

The current data has **101 active role mappings**, six stored subgroup records, and three visible subgroups. Three additional managerial subgroups (ISCO 11, 12, 13) are excluded with zero active mapped roles. The 101 count is a data check—not a justification for three specialization areas.

| Visible subgroup | Stored roles | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| Performers & Creative Directors | 52 | Mixes roles, and the parent merges live performing arts with screen/broadcast production. Recorded acting/directing may be part of a screen-production route when the actual task is audiovisual; general live performance should be adjudicated against the performing-arts domain. |
| Stage & Set Production Crew | 32 | Blends live-stage operations with screen art-department/craft work. Determine placement from production context and actual duties. |
| Camera & Sound Technicians | 17 | Too broad for learning navigation: capture, production sound, post-production sound and broadcast engineering have different task requirements. |

## Parent boundary recommendation

Define D042 around **audiovisual content production and broadcasting**: recorded film, television and video; radio/audio broadcast production; their production workflows, technical operations and distribution. Separately adjudicate theatre/live-stage roles against D070 Performing Arts and Entertainment and music-recording roles against D153 Music Production. Editorial/news roles should be checked against D043 Journalism and Digital Publishing, retaining only context-specific routes supported by tasks.

This is a boundary recommendation, not a canonical rename/change. If this parent scope is not accepted, do not finalize a subgroup count under the current umbrella.

## Provisional practice areas after the boundary correction

1. **Audiovisual Content Development and Editorial** — research, develop, shape and prepare programme/script/editorial material; distinguish journalism where that is the primary work.
2. **Audiovisual Production and Direction** — plan, lead and coordinate recorded productions, including directing, producing and production management.
3. **Audiovisual Production Design and Craft** — screen-focused design/build of visual environments, costumes, make-up, props and other physical production elements.
4. **Camera, Production Sound and Lighting** — capture image and production audio and provide lighting/grip work during production.
5. **Post-Production and Content Finishing** — edit and finish recorded content for release/transmission.
6. **Broadcast Operations and Engineering** — operate/maintain technical workflows for broadcast scheduling, playout, contribution and distribution.

These six are a provisional navigation synthesis from industry workflow maps, **not** a universal or mandated classification. They must be tested against every exact role task and the adjacent catalogue domains before acceptance. ScreenSkills is a UK industry-body comparator; its role titles are not automatically global or Nigerian role evidence. EBU materials corroborate production-technology and distribution practice, not a role taxonomy.

## Computational interpretation

Production/broadcast technology is not automatically a computational specialization. Software, digital workflows, signal processing and media systems should appear as tools/skills attached to the particular practice or role requiring them. A separate computational area would need evidence of a distinct subject/practice field, not just use of technology.

## Evidence and limits

- [UNESCO UIS Framework for Cultural Statistics](https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/methods-and-tools/framework-cultural-statistics): describes its international statistical purpose and the 2025 framework update. This is context for cultural-scope boundaries, not an occupational taxonomy.
- [UNESCO UIS cultural-domain definition](https://uis.unesco.org/en/glossary-term/percentage-persons-employed-cultural-occupations-are-working-specific-cultural-domain): lists performance and celebration separately from audio-visual and interactive media. It supports testing the parent split; it does not dictate the product's exact domain boundaries.
- [ScreenSkills career maps](https://www.screenskills.com/starting-your-career/lesson-plans-and-classroom-resources/career-maps/): separately publishes film/TV production, editorial, craft/technical, post-production and broadcast-engineering maps. These maps support distinct practice-area candidates, but are UK-industry references and cannot alone establish a global taxonomy or Nigerian-specific titles/demand.
- [European Broadcasting Union, Production Technology](https://www.ebu.ch/topics/production-technology): describes broadcasters' content-production technologies, workflows and transition from linear radio/TV toward multiservice digital distribution. This supports a production/broadcast technology distinction, not universal role requirements.
