# D004 — Cybersecurity

Status: source-reviewed candidate set recommended; no role placement or canonical taxonomy implementation in this review.

## Finding

The inventory shows no visible D004 specialization cards and two excluded stored job-family-style records. Neither those records nor the current role distribution determined the recommendation.

## Proposed student-facing areas

| Area | Scope | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Cybersecurity Governance, Risk and Assurance | Security policy, accountability, risk treatment, compliance and assurance | Security-focused governance; not general privacy/legal or enterprise governance by default. |
| Security Architecture, Engineering and Secure Development | Build security into systems, products, infrastructure and software lifecycles | Secure development belongs here as a build-lifecycle specialty, not generic software engineering. |
| Network, Cloud and Infrastructure Security | Protect communications, cloud, endpoints and deployed infrastructure | Security of those platforms, not general network engineering or cloud operations. |
| Identity, Access and Data Protection | Manage identities, authentication, privileges and information protection | Keep general data/records management and privacy administration out unless security is the work. |
| Security Testing and Adversarial Assessment | Authorized vulnerability assessment, penetration testing and control testing | Explicitly authorized testing; distinct from live defense and unauthorized activity. |
| Security Operations and Incident Response | Monitor, detect, contain, respond and restore resilient operations | Operational defense; forensic evidence work is a separate branch when that is the primary purpose. |
| Digital Forensics and Cyber Investigation | Preserve and examine digital evidence to reconstruct cyber events or technology-enabled crime | General criminal investigation and non-digital forensic science are not automatically included. |
| Cyber Threat Intelligence and Analysis | Analyze threat information to inform cyber defense and risk decisions | Cyber-specific intelligence, distinct from monitoring/incident-response execution. |

The recommendation is **eight** areas. This is an internal synthesis, not a count required by any source. It happens to equal the number of CISSP exam domains, but the proposed labels and boundaries are not a one-to-one copy of that certification outline.

## Evidence and limits

- [ISC2 CISSP Exam Outline](https://www.isc2.org/certifications/cissp/cissp-certification-exam-outline), domains 1–8. An international professional certification body separates risk, assets, architecture, network, identity, assessment/testing, operations and secure-development knowledge. These are exam domains for an experienced-professional credential—not a prescribed student subgroup taxonomy.
- [NIST NICE Framework overview](https://www.nist.gov/document/niceframework062017pdf-0), NIST SP 800-181 summary. It explicitly distinguishes categories, concentrated-work “specialty areas,” and work roles; examples cover governance, protect/defend, analysis, investigation, collection/operations, operation/maintenance and secure provisioning. This is a US workforce framework, not global law or an academic taxonomy.
- [ENISA ECSF role profiles](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-role-profiles) and its [ESCO–ECSF crosswalk](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/skills-and-competences/skills-development/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-ecsf/crosswalk-between-esco-and-ecsf). The EU framework distinguishes profiles including risk, architecture, incident response, forensics, penetration testing, audit and threat intelligence; it supports function boundaries, not a direct copy of its roles as subgroup cards.

## Decision caveat

The candidate areas remain an internal synthesis; no source mandates this exact student-facing count. No role is assigned here; domain fit and area fit remain separate audits.
