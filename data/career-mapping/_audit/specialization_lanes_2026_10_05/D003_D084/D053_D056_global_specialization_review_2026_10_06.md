## Domain findings

### D053 — Hospitality Services and Management

The three stored subgroups have historical evidence counts of 15, 5 and 22, but all have zero active mappings and are excluded. Their source labels are ISCO occupational classes, not areas of specialization. One record labelled “Professional Chefs” derives from ISCO sub-major 34, whose broad classification title is not a culinary specialization taxonomy. Counts and inherited ISCO membership therefore cannot establish the number or fit of student specializations.

The checked-in domain description spans hotels, restaurants, tourism, tour operations and hospitality events. The Institute of Hospitality's international accreditation scope explicitly covers hospitality, leisure, tourism and events; the UN Tourism Digital Library/AHLEI hospitality education framework separates Lodging Operations, Food and Beverage Operations, Event Management, and Travel/Tourism/Recreation. ILO occupational definitions independently distinguish hotel operations, restaurant/catering work, culinary work, food-and-beverage service and visitor guiding. Together these support four broad professional-education areas within the current parent scope. They do not prescribe Sifter's taxonomy or a universal count; four is an internal synthesis, not an external standard.

1. **Accommodation and Guest Operations** — lodging operations, front office/reservations, rooms, housekeeping, accommodation engineering and guest services. Generic real-estate/property management is outside unless it directly operates guest accommodation.
2. **Food, Beverage and Culinary Operations** — restaurant, catering and hospitality food-service operations, including kitchen/culinary production and beverage service. Keep these together as one broad area; split them only if later student-route evidence demonstrates distinct learning choices rather than different tasks within one service line.
3. **Travel, Tourism and Visitor Services** — travel and tour operations, tourism services, visitor guidance and interpretation. Destination policy/planning and conservation science are outside unless the D053 root scope is explicitly broadened.
4. **Hospitality Events and Meetings** — meetings, conventions, banqueting and special events delivered through hospitality services. Standalone performance production, sports-event operations and general venue management are not included by adjacency alone.

**Disposition:** retain a four-area global internal recommendation for final taxonomy reconciliation. This corrects the overbroad “venue” label and places hospitality events within the root's stated services. Do not restore the inherited three ISCO occupation buckets. D053 still has zero active role mappings: the role-coverage audit must establish a defensible domain-role set before any role path is activated or linked to these areas. The later Nigerian-context pass may add explicitly labelled local variants; it should not replace the global structure.

### D054 — Oil and Gas Operations

The four stored subgroups have historical counts of 1, 3, 6 and 8, zero active mappings, and excluded status. They mix management, engineering, plant operation and rig-work occupational classes. Their counts do not determine specialization structure.

SPE’s technical communities distinguish drilling, completions, production, reservoir, projects/facilities/construction and HSE. The U.S. EIA describes upstream, midstream and downstream, and the Energy Institute independently presents those as international industry sectors: its course outline separates exploration/production, transport/distribution, and refining/petrochemicals. The Energy Institute corroboration reduces reliance on a U.S.-framed source for the value-chain distinction. These are useful global technical/value-chain boundaries, not a mandate to reproduce either organization’s internal taxonomy. Provisional global candidates:

1. **Subsurface Exploration and Reservoir Development** — geology/geophysics, formation evaluation, reservoir characterization and recovery planning.
2. **Drilling and Well Construction** — well planning/design, drilling systems and operations, casing and cementing.
3. **Well Completions and Intervention** — completion design/installation, stimulation, intervention and well integrity.
4. **Production and Field Operations** — production systems, artificial lift, surveillance, optimization and field operations.
5. **Petroleum Facilities, Projects and Construction** — surface/subsea facilities, flow assurance, processing systems, projects and construction.
6. **Midstream Gas Processing, Transport and Storage** — gas processing, pipelines/transport, terminals and storage; validate the parent boundary because midstream can be organizationally and regulatorily distinct from upstream.
7. **Refining and Petroleum Processing** — crude refining and petroleum-product processing; keep general chemical engineering/product manufacturing in D055 unless the role is specifically petroleum processing.

Health, safety, environment, sustainability, asset integrity and digital/data methods are cross-cutting in the cited professional taxonomy. Do not make each a separate specialization merely because it has a professional community or toolset; do so only if a distinct student route and role family are supported.

**Parent-domain decision:** the checked-in D054 description explicitly runs from finding petroleum and planning wells through pumps, gas plants and refinery control rooms. Upstream, midstream and downstream operations therefore fit the current parent scope; this slice does not recommend splitting those segments into new root domains. Because “Oil and Gas Operations” can be read as upstream-only, the student-facing domain orientation must state that it includes gas transport/processing and petroleum refining.

**Disposition:** retain seven technical areas as an internally recommended global specialization structure: subsurface/reservoir; drilling/well construction; completions/intervention; production/field operations; facilities/projects; gas midstream; petroleum refining/processing. SPE and Energy Institute sources corroborate the underlying practice/segment distinctions, but do not prescribe seven learner-facing areas. Role-path activation is still pending the independent domain-role audit and final global-plus-Nigerian reconciliation. Do not reinstate the four inherited occupational buckets.

### D055 — Chemical Engineering

The three stored subgroup records are excluded occupation-class groupings. AIChE and IChemE describe different technical communities in process systems/control, reaction/catalysis, separations, materials/particle technology, biochemical engineering, food, pharma, clean energy and environmental/water technology. These bodies establish meaningful professional distinctions but their organization charts are not a learner taxonomy. The earlier five-area proposal compressed areas whose core methods and added learning are materially different; the revised nine-area set exposes those differences as candidates for review rather than treating professional-body units as a mandatory count.

Provisional global candidates (nine-area internal synthesis):

1. **Process Design, Systems and Control** — process modelling, simulation, design, control and operations systems.
2. **Reaction, Catalysis and Process Development** — reaction engineering, catalyst/process development, scale-up, pilot operation, intensification and technology transfer.
3. **Separation and Purification Engineering** — distillation, extraction, membranes, adsorption, crystallization, filtration, chromatography and related separation systems.
4. **Materials and Particle Process Engineering** — polymers, particles, powders, composites and materials processing; excludes materials science research without process engineering.
5. **Biochemical and Bioprocess Engineering** — biological production systems, bioreactors, fermentation and bioprocess scale-up; excludes clinical biomedical practice.
6. **Food and Bioproduct Process Engineering** — industrial food/drink and bioproduct processing, preservation and manufacture; excludes hospitality food service and dietetics.
7. **Pharmaceutical Process Engineering** — pharmaceutical/biopharmaceutical manufacturing, formulation, scale-up and process control; excludes clinical pharmacy and drug-discovery chemistry as such.
8. **Energy and Low-Carbon Process Engineering** — energy conversion, fuels, carbon capture and low-carbon process systems; excludes D054 petroleum operations.
9. **Environmental, Water and Clean Process Engineering** — water/wastewater, emissions and process-waste treatment, pollution prevention and cleaner production; excludes environmental policy/ecology without process engineering.

Process safety, loss prevention, sustainability and computational modelling are cross-cutting by default, not extra specializations solely because they have professional communities. The nine candidates are intentionally provisional: test whether each yields a distinct specialization body of learning, whether food/bioproduct or energy/environment should be merged, and whether role/domain evidence supports the parent boundaries. Neither AIChE nor IChemE prescribes nine student-facing areas.

**Disposition:** retain nine as a provisional global candidate set for explicit split/merge review. The prior combination of separations with materials and of bioprocess with food/pharma is not retained without evidence that they share the same specialization learning. Do not expose the inherited occupation-class records as specializations; the three active roles are not a complete-path gate or proof that nine roles/groups are correct.

### D056 — Blockchain and Decentralised Systems

The stored single subgroup, “Blockchain Software & Protocols,” has a source-backed role count of 25 but is excluded under the direct-domain-to-role contract. One broad software/protocol bucket does not show whether every role shares the same specialization. NISTIR 8202 separates core blockchain concepts including distributed ledgers, consensus, cryptography, smart contracts and oracles; it is a technical overview, not a career taxonomy. ISO/TC 307 and IEEE Computer Society's Blockchain and Distributed Ledger Standards Committee independently separate technical work around foundations/protocols, smart contracts, security/privacy/identity, interoperability and governance/use cases. Hyperledger Fabric documentation shows permissioned consortium membership, channels and participant policies as a concrete system architecture. None prescribes a student-facing subgroup count.

Provisional global candidates:

1. **Distributed-Ledger Protocols and Infrastructure** — ledger/protocol design, consensus, node/client software, distributed infrastructure, scaling and upgrade mechanisms. Cross-network interoperability is separated where it requires distinct protocols and integration skills.
2. **Smart Contracts and Decentralized Applications** — on-ledger program design, contract development, testing, verification and deployment; dApp architecture and integration with external data/services. Business-sector applications without DLT engineering are outside this area.
3. **DLT Security, Privacy and Digital Identity** — threat modelling and security assurance for ledger protocols/contracts, key and identity systems, privacy controls and forensics. General cybersecurity is outside unless the work is specifically DLT-related.
4. **DLT Interoperability and Integration** — cross-ledger communication, interoperability protocols, bridges/connectors and integration of DLT systems with other digital systems; distinct from building one ledger's internal protocol or dApp.
5. **Permissioned and Consortium DLT Systems** — architecture and operation of permissioned/consortium networks, organizational membership, access policies, channels, governance and participant coordination. Retain this as a distinct candidate only where consortium design/governance is the learner's focus, not merely because an application is enterprise-branded.

Cryptocurrency trading, investment, legal/regulatory advice and token business operations are not automatically blockchain-engineering specializations; map them to the relevant finance, law or business domain unless the exact work is technical DLT engineering.

**Disposition:** retain five global candidates for boundary review, not as an approved count. Compare all 25 exact role tasks with these boundaries and test whether permissioned/consortium architecture is a distinct learning route rather than a deployment context. Do not assign roles to candidates until the separate role-domain audit is complete. The current single-group record is not sufficient evidence of the correct number of specializations.
