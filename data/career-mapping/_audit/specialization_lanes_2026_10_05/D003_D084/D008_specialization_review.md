# D008 specialization review — Telecommunications and Network Engineering

**Disposition:** Recommend four student-facing areas of specialization as an internal synthesis. The sources distinguish the technical concerns, but none mandates this exact taxonomy or count. No role mappings were reviewed or approved.

## Existing structure checked

The inventory contains four visible cards: Telecom & IT Leadership; Radio & Network Engineering; Network Architecture & Planning; and Network Support Technicians. These derive from existing records whose memberships are unverified. They mix leadership, technical layers, planning and a worker grouping; they are not adopted as the specialization taxonomy, and neither their count nor role counts set the recommendation.

## Recommended areas

1. **Telecommunications Access Networks** — fixed and radio-access systems connecting users/devices or local networks to provider networks. Wireless/fixed are technologies inside the area, not automatically separate top-level specializations.
2. **Core and IP Network Engineering** — provider/core and packet network architecture, routing, forwarding, addressing, interconnection and traffic engineering.
3. **Transport and Optical Networks** — high-capacity transmission between access, core, data-centre and service-network locations, including optical/carrier transport, capacity and resilience.
4. **Network Operations and Service Assurance** — operation of live networks: monitoring, faults/incidents, configuration/change, performance, reliability and operational controls across the other layers.

Network architecture/capacity planning, security, automation/SDN and network slicing are cross-cutting practices rather than separate top-level specializations on the evidence reviewed. Generic ICT leadership, project or product management is not automatically a technical specialization here.

## Evidence and limits

- **ITU-R M.2012-7**, network architecture section: radio access functions and their interfaces to core entities. [Source](https://www.itu.int/epublications/en/publication/recommendation-itu-r-m-2012-7-02-2026-detailed-specifications-of-the-terrestrial-radio-interfaces-of-international-mobile-telecommunications-advanced-imt-advanced/en)
- **3GPP RAN3 scope**: UTRAN/E-UTRAN/NG-RAN architecture and associated interfaces. [Source](https://www.3gpp.org/component/sppagebuilder/?Itemid=130&id=27&view=page)
- **ITU-T G.8310 summary**: metro transport architecture, path/section layers, multiplexing/routing/supervision and transport of radio-access traffic. [Source](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/g/T-REC-G.8310-202012-I%21%21SUM-HTM-E.htm)
- **IETF Areas**: routing/forwarding architectures and separate Operations & Management concerns, including network management and operational issues. [Source](https://www.ietf.org/process/areas/)
- **IEEE ComSoc Technical Committees**: global professional communities include wireless, mobile networks, optical networks, next-generation networking and QoS/reliability. [Source](https://www.comsoc.org/engagement-community/boards-councils-committees/board/technical-committees-board)
- **ITU Network 2030 Architecture Framework**: access/edge, routing/addressing, QoS, slicing, edge federation and management. [Source](https://www.itu.int/hub/publication/t-fg-net2030-2020-3/)

These primary sources establish meaningful technical boundaries, not a universal job or education taxonomy. The labels/count are a compact learner-facing synthesis; network functions overlap and interoperate. Academic sign-off is not claimed. No ISCO-derived membership or role title was used to infer area fit.
