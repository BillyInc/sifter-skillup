# D037 — Legal Practice: specialization review

**Disposition:** the existing three subgroup rows are not a defensible final specialization structure. Seven provisional global practice families are proposed for later reconciliation, but the catalogue currently has **zero active role mappings**. Do not expose the proposed branches as working learner pathways until the role population and domain scope are repaired.

The zero count is a catalogue gap, not evidence that legal practice has no specializations. It also prevents validating role pathways or choosing a final subgroup count from this data.

## Provisional global practice families

1. Business, Corporate and Financial Law
2. Dispute Resolution
3. Criminal Justice
4. Public, Constitutional and Human Rights Law
5. Employment, Family and Immigration Law
6. Intellectual Property, Technology and Data Law
7. Property, Construction and Infrastructure Law

These are navigation candidates synthesized from the International Bar Association's practice divisions and committee fields—not a prescribed universal taxonomy. The IBA separately lists other recognized practices such as tax, antitrust, environment/energy, maritime/aviation and international trade; these must remain eligible for distinct branches if later role-path evidence supports that level of granularity.

## Boundaries and student safety

Legal operations, records, court administration and enforcement are not assumed to be legal-practice specializations. A job's location in a law firm or court does not establish its domain. Role placement must use its primary tasks. Practice families may be globally understandable, but governing law, qualification, reserved legal activities and procedure are jurisdiction-specific and must be labelled as such. A shared legal foundation or specialization does not itself confer eligibility to practice.
