# D009 specialization review — Data Science

**Disposition:** Recommend four learner-facing areas as a compact synthesis of data-science competency clusters. The sources support the distinctions, but they do not mandate these labels or a four-part taxonomy. No role mappings were examined.

## Existing structure checked

The current three labels are Applied Statistics & Computational Modelling; Data & AI Engineers; and Statistical Operations & Data Support. Their records have unverified memberships and combine methods, broad job families and operational support. They are context only; their count and role counts do not determine this recommendation.

## Recommended areas

1. **Data Engineering and Governance** — acquire, organize, validate, integrate, secure and manage data for analysis and reuse.
2. **Statistical and Scientific Modeling** — study design, statistical inference, uncertainty and explanatory/predictive models for analytical questions.
3. **Machine Learning and Data Mining** — computational pattern discovery and data-driven model development/evaluation within the data-science scope.
4. **Data Analysis and Communication** — interpret and communicate evidence, visualizations and uncertainty so findings can be used responsibly.

Application-domain knowledge and privacy/security/ethics cut across these areas. Visualization/storytelling is included in analysis and communication rather than automatically made a separate top-level specialization. Keep the adjacent AI/ML and Business Intelligence domains distinct: domain fit depends on work purpose and scope, not merely on tools.

## Evidence and limits

- **ACM Data Science Task Force Final Report**, Body of Knowledge and methodology: data acquisition/management/governance, analysis/presentation, big-data systems, data mining, machine learning and related areas; report draws on academic/industry input and global consultation. [Report](https://dstf.acm.org/DSTF_Final_Report.pdf)
- **ACM Data Science Task Force Draft 2**, Chapter 3, §3.1: lists data-science knowledge areas and says a complete curriculum also requires mathematics/statistics and an application context. [Report](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/education/curricula-recommendations/dstf_ccdsc2021.pdf)
- **NIST Big Data Interoperability Framework**, §5.3: distinguishes end-to-end data science from analytics alone and describes capture, preparation, analysis, visualization, action and lifecycle concerns such as governance/security. [PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1500-1r2.pdf)
- **ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI CS2023**, knowledge-model discussion: data-science computing topics are spread across AI, graphics/interactive techniques, data management and mathematical/statistical foundations. This guards against treating DS as equivalent to AI/ML. [Report](https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/CS2023-Report.htm)
- **ASA statistical-science guidelines**, §§3–4: data production, applied modeling, statistical theory, computing, synthesis and communication. These are recommendations from a workshop, not an adopted occupational taxonomy. [Article](https://jse.amstat.org/v10n2/tarpey.html)

These sources establish coherent competency distinctions, not a universal job taxonomy. The four-area set is an internal synthesis; academic sign-off is not claimed. No role IDs, ISCO groups or role counts were used to determine areas.
