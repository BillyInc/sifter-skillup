# D025 Pharmacy — specialization structure review

**Status:** Source-reviewed candidate set; provisional pending role-to-domain reconciliation. This is a taxonomy review, not external academic sign-off.

## Finding

The inventory has **six current subgroup records** under Pharmacy, all derived from ISCO sub-major families: production/services managers, science and engineering professionals, licensed pharmacists, teaching professionals, pharmacy support staff, and business/administration associate professionals. These describe occupational families, not six pharmacy areas of specialization. Their presence and member counts cannot justify a student-facing specialization count or prove that each member belongs to Pharmacy.

FIP's international practice framework currently lists eight pharmacy practice sections. Its 2025 global report explicitly says there is no globally agreed definition of pharmacy specialization; it groups examples across clinical/disease areas, services, technical/system areas and emerging practice, with substantial regional variation. Therefore, the source supports a global coverage framework—not a universal mandated list or credential equivalence.

## Recommended provisional student labels

Use an eight-area internal synthesis that tracks the FIP global coverage while translating sections into clear student-facing language:

1. Academic Pharmacy and Education
2. Clinical Biology and Laboratory Pharmacy
3. Community and Primary-Care Pharmacy
4. Medicines Information and Medication Safety
5. Hospital and Clinical Pharmacy
6. Industrial Pharmacy and Medicines Manufacturing
7. Emergency and Military Pharmacy
8. Public Health, Policy and Pharmacy Administration

Pharmacy research and pharmaceutical sciences are included within academic/research contexts and product-science routes, not presented as a separate default subgroup. Digital health, AI, genomics and precision pharmacy remain emerging/cross-cutting candidates, not universal default subgroups. Disease-specific specialties can be nested under clinical routes or attached to specific roles where evidence and local credential rules support them.

The labels are a **Sifter synthesis**, not a claim that FIP prescribes eight specialties. Each should be described by its work context, whether it is a setting/service/system/science area, and what it adds to the shared Pharmacy foundation.

## Fix disposition

- Do not carry the six ISCO-family labels into the student-facing specialization taxonomy.
- Keep source/ISCO occupational records available for staff audit and role identification; do not delete them merely because they are unsuitable specialization names.
- Do not assign roles to the eight candidates yet. The independent role lane must first decide every D025 role's fit to the Pharmacy parent from role-task evidence. Then assign only roles whose tasks support both the parent domain and a specific specialization.
- The eight-area candidate set remains provisional until that parent-domain reconciliation and exact role membership review are complete. Jurisdictional licensure, prescribing powers, and formal specialist credentials must be explicitly labeled by jurisdiction.
