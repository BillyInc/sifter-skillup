# D024 Nursing — specialization review checkpoint

**Disposition:** no final specialization count yet. Nursing-specific frameworks do not agree on one global count, and the current parent-role list is not clean enough to define its specialization branches.

The deterministic inventory records **38 active role mappings**, **zero visible specialization cards**, and **three excluded ISCO-derived occupation groups**. The role population includes nurse and midwife roles but also paramedics, medical-records staff, clinical technicians, medical assistants, and personal-care occupations. Those links need task-based review against Nursing and neighboring domains before the group count is chosen.

The ICN's current global definition supports a broad, internationally usable description of nursing. WHO's regional nursing-specialist guide explicitly notes the absence of agreement on what constitutes a nursing specialty. The UK NMC recognizes four nursing practice fields, while WHO Europe discusses specialist nursing categories whose scope and authorization vary by country. These sources show why we should synthesize a clear global-facing set rather than copy a national list or claim a universal count.

I also changed the D024 description to a global profession scope and moved the Nigerian registration rule into a separate `jurisdiction_specific_context` field. The NMCN source supports that local note only; it does not define global nursing or prove the D024 role links.
