# D036 — Procurement and Contract Management: specialization review

**Disposition:** recommend four provisional learner-navigation areas, pending catalogue-wide reconciliation. This is not an official fixed taxonomy, and it does not approve any role-to-domain or role-to-area link.

## Existing structure reviewed

The current three subgroups are generated from ISCO-derived groupings, not validated as three areas of specialization:

| Current label | Existing role count | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| Logistics & Transport Managers | 22 | Logistics/transport-led; not automatically procurement or contract management. |
| Procurement Analysts & Consultants | 3 | Analysis/consulting is a function, not a complete specialization boundary. |
| Buyers, Traders & Brokers | 19 | Mixes buyer-side purchasing with seller-side trading and brokerage. |

The 44 role mappings are not evidence that all 44 roles belong in D036. Each still needs its own task-based parent-domain disposition.

## Recommended provisional areas

1. **Procurement Strategy and Category Management** — organise demand and supply-market evidence into procurement/category strategies and value, cost and risk priorities.
2. **Sourcing and Supplier Management** — identify and assess sources, design sourcing events, evaluate offers, negotiate, and manage supplier relationships and performance.
3. **Contract and Commercial Management** — prepare, award, administer and monitor procurement contracts and commercial obligations.
4. **Public and Regulated Procurement** — apply public/regulated tendering, competition, transparency and integrity procedures, with local law explicitly labelled by jurisdiction.

Routine purchasing, expediting, procurement analytics and digital systems are treated as cross-cutting capabilities for now, not extra areas. Reconsider only if later evidence establishes a distinct learner route.

## Evidence and limits

ISM's capability model and CPSM task specification distinguish sourcing, category management, supplier relationships and legal/contractual work. CIPS describes category management as a supply-market-focused procurement practice. UNCITRAL's Model Law supplies a separate public-procurement process context and principles, but is not a country's domestic law. These sources support meaningful distinctions; none dictates four subgroups or validates the repository's 44 role links.

The current parent description also mixes buying with commodity trading, freight, imports and brokerage. Keep buyer-side procurement distinct from seller-side commerce, logistics, customs/trade and transport intermediation. No role placements are decided here. See the JSON artifact for locators, source support, boundaries and audit criteria.
