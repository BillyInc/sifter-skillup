# D029 Human Resources — specialization structure review

**Status:** Source-reviewed candidate structure; pending catalogue reconciliation. Not external academic sign-off.

## Finding

D029 currently exposes three subgroups: “People & Culture Leadership,” “HR & Talent Advisory,” and “Other clerical support workers.” Their provenance is ISCO occupational submajor group membership. They mix management seniority, a broad business-professional family and a generic clerical group; they do not give students a coherent, consistently named set of HR areas of specialization.

The source-grounded candidate set contains **11 areas**, corresponding to the current specialist-knowledge areas listed by the CIPD Profession Map: Workforce Planning; Employee Experience; Employee Relations; Equality, Diversity and Inclusion; Learning and Development; Organization Development and Design; People Analytics; Resourcing; Reward; Talent Management; and Wellbeing. CIPD describes the people profession as international. This is a usable professional reference taxonomy, not proof that 11 is a universal count or that every employer needs 11 separate teams.

ISO sources corroborate several distinct practice boundaries: workforce planning (ISO 30409), recruitment (ISO 30405), workplace learning and development (ISO 30422), and human-capital reporting (ISO 30414:2025). ISO/TC 260 also documents the D&I lifecycle framework (ISO 30415). ILO sources ground employee/labour relations and collective bargaining. They support the content and scope of these practices; they do not prescribe a single HR subgroup catalogue.

The label expansions are for clarity; the candidate keys and source mapping are in the JSON review. Shared HR foundations should be shown once. The area pages should add their distinct learning and disclose supported overlaps—for example, workforce planning with resourcing, L&D with talent management, and people analytics across workforce practices—rather than making those shared items look exclusive to one area.

## Fix and boundary disposition

- Replace the three occupation-classification buckets as student-facing specialization labels with the reviewed HR practice areas, after the catalogue-wide naming and cross-domain pass confirms identifiers and counts. Keep ISCO as occupational provenance, not as the definition of a student's specialization.
- Do not derive area membership from role count, ISCO code, role title or current subgroup placement. The independent role lane must first determine whether each of the 16 roles actually belongs to Human Resources from its tasks; only valid parent-domain roles may later be placed on these branches.
- Keep employment-law content explicit about jurisdiction. The concepts of employee relations and collective bargaining travel across borders; statutes, protected categories, dispute processes and professional permissions do not.
- Keep People Analytics tied to workforce questions and responsible employee data; generic BI/data science remains in its relevant domain.
- Keep workplace wellbeing focused on organizational practice and coordination. Do not imply clinical diagnosis/treatment or occupational-health expertise.
- Do not add “HR Generalist/People Operations,” “HR Technology” or “Payroll” as extra specializations based only on current roles or topic mentions. The first can be a cross-specialism route; HR technology is a tool/use-case theme unless broader evidence establishes an area; payroll spans HR policy and finance/operations depending on actual tasks.
- Standards are frameworks, not automatically individual certifications. No credential is inferred by this specialization audit.

## Sources

- [CIPD — Profession Map: Specialist knowledge](https://www.cipd.org/en/the-people-profession/the-profession-map/explore-the-profession-map/specialist-knowledge/): current named specialist-knowledge areas; describes an international people profession.
- [CIPD — People practice](https://www.cipd.org/en/the-people-profession/the-profession-map/explore-the-profession-map/core-knowledge/people-practice/): distinguishes shared/core practice knowledge from specialist knowledge.
- [CIPD — Profession Map update history](https://www.cipd.org/en/the-people-profession/the-profession-map/update-history/): documents the addition of workforce planning in the 2025 review and the distinction between employee experience and wellbeing.
- [ISO — ISO 30409:2016, Workforce planning](https://www.iso.org/standard/64150.html): scalable across organization sizes, industries and sectors; current edition was confirmed in 2022.
- [ISO — ISO 30405:2023, Recruitment](https://www.iso.org/standard/79488.html): recruitment process guidance usable by any organization regardless of type or size.
- [ISO — ISO 30422:2022, Learning and development](https://www.iso.org/standard/76365.html): workplace learning for operational, organizational and individual career/lifelong-learning needs.
- [ISO — ISO 30414:2025, Human capital reporting and disclosure](https://www.iso.org/standard/30414?browse=tc): current international standard lists workforce composition, diversity, recruitment, succession, costs, wellbeing and skills/development among reporting areas.
- [ISO/TC 260 — ISO 30415:2021, Diversity and inclusion](https://committee.iso.org/sites/tc260/home/news/content-left-area/news-and-updates/iso-304152021-hr-management--d-1.html): lifecycle-based organizational D&I guidance, contextualized to objectives and risks.
- [ILO — Collective bargaining and labour relations](https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/collective-bargaining-and-labour-relations): global labour-relations framework and bargaining topics; not a substitute for national law.
