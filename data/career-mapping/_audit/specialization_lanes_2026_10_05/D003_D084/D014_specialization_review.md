# D014 — Actuarial Science: specialization taxonomy review

Date: 2026-10-05  
Scope: specialization/area-of-practice taxonomy only. No role-to-domain or role-to-specialization conclusions are made.

## Recommendation

Recommend six broad student-facing practice areas: **Life and Annuities; Health and Care; General Insurance and Reinsurance; Pensions, Retirement and Social Security; Investment and Finance; Enterprise and Financial Risk.** This is a compact internal synthesis across global professional frameworks, not a universal taxonomy prescribed by any one body.

The existing three cards combine an analytical-method label with insurance/occupational groupings and omit well-established actuarial contexts. Their labels are recorded in the JSON checkpoint but are not used to set the recommended count; their memberships remain outside this lane.

## Evidence and interpretation

The IFoA lists Life, Health and Care, General Insurance, Pensions, Finance and Investment, and Risk Management as actuarial practice areas. It also separately recognizes AI/emerging technology, sustainability and cross-practice work, which supports treating those topics as cross-cutting rather than automatically as core branches. The [IAA international forums](https://actuaries.org/forums/) provide cross-regional corroboration for General Insurance, Health, Pensions/Benefits/Social Security, Enterprise and Financial Risk, mortality, data analytics and banking. The IAA explicitly notes that general insurance is also called non-life, P&C, or short-term insurance in different regions. [IFoA practice areas](https://actuaries.org.uk/practice-areas/)

The SOA qualification pathway distinguishes life/annuities, health, investment and retirement courses, and shows that a learner may follow one practice area or combine related areas. CAS defines its actuarial scope around general insurance, including property, casualty and similar exposures. The IAA education guidelines describe shared core actuarial education while allowing specialist and elective packaging to vary by association. [SOA pathway](https://fsa2025.soa.org/flexible-pathway/), [CAS scope](https://www.casact.org/about), [IAA education guidelines](https://actuaries.org/app/uploads/2025/06/20250525_EducationGuidelines_Final.pdf)

The six branches are distinguished by the system and mandate being analyzed—not by methods. Mortality and longevity, probability/statistics, modelling, analytics, AI, climate risk and sustainability can cross multiple branches. Banking is recognized by the IAA but described in its governance material as an emerging area; it is therefore recorded as an adjacent candidate rather than a default seventh area. Data analytics is explicitly cross-practice in that same IAA record, so it is not promoted to a default standalone subgroup here. [IAA forum recommendations](https://www.actuaries.org/IAA/Documents/COUNCIL/Meetings/2021_Q2_Virtual/05a_Forums_RecommendationstoCouncil_13May2021_Final.pdf)

## Boundaries and caveats

- “General insurance,” “non-life,” and “property and casualty (P&C)” are regional terminology variants for overlapping practice, not separate areas.
- Life/annuities, health/care and pension/social-security systems are separate problem contexts, even where mortality, longevity, demographics or asset-liability methods overlap.
- Investment/finance here means actuarial work on institutional investment and financial risk; it is not a claim to absorb all quantitative finance (D011).
- Banking, analytics/technology, climate/sustainability and mortality are retained as emerging, cross-cutting or nested topics based on current evidence, not dismissed as non-actuarial work.
- Product rules, solvency requirements, benefit structures and licensing are jurisdiction-specific and must be labelled as such in later learning content.
