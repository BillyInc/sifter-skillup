# D048 — Food Science: specialization review

**Disposition:** source-reviewed; the current name understates the production/technology scope, and the four inherited subgroups are occupation-family buckets rather than clear specializations. No canonical data was changed.

## Current data and boundary

D048 contains **43 active role mappings** across four visible subgroups:

| Current subgroup | Roles | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| Food Scientists & Biotechnologists | 5 | Scientist/biotechnologist occupation family; generic life-science titles require a food-specific assignment. |
| Food Processing Technicians | 1 | “Technician” is a job level, not an area of specialization. |
| Artisan Food Crafters | 9 | Specialty food production, but the boundary with culinary/food service (D092) depends on product and task. |
| Food & Beverage Machine Operators | 28 | Manufacturing is a valid practice area; operator status is not a specialization. |

The 28 operators and 9 artisans make the mapping set manufacturing-heavy, while the parent is called simply “Food Science.” IFT defines food science through engineering, biological and physical sciences applied to food properties, deterioration, processing and improvement. Its career overview also identifies product development, food analysis, safety/quality, packaging and consumer research. IUFoST recognizes food science and technology and describes processing as an applied set of physical, chemical and biological practices. These support renaming the domain **Food Science and Technology** (or **Food Science, Technology and Processing** if explicit scope is preferable).

Keep D048 for food-product science and technology, including food processing/manufacturing. Keep meal preparation, catering and restaurant service in D092. Keep agricultural primary production in D118–D120. For artisan roles, distinguish packaged/specialty product manufacture from made-to-order meal service by actual tasks and output, not title alone.

The separate parent-role review covers 43/43 current links (39 supported, 3 conditional, 1 unsupported). It does not validate subgroups or assign roles to them. The unsupported aquaculture-biologist link is not a food-science task; generic biochemistry/bioinformatics roles remain conditional on a demonstrated food assignment.

## Provisional areas of specialization

Four candidate areas synthesize professional practice evidence and the actual data scope; **no source prescribes this exact count**:

1. **Food Science and Product Development** — food composition, chemistry, microbiology and sensory properties; design, improve and evaluate products.
2. **Food Safety, Quality and Analysis** — food hazards, composition, hygiene, sampling, testing and risk-based controls.
3. **Food Processing, Preservation and Packaging** — transform, preserve and package food while protecting safety, quality and nutrition.
4. **Food Manufacturing and Artisanal Production** — scale or craft production of baked goods, meat, confectionery, beverages and other food products.

These are provisional, not yet role mappings. A future exact-role crosswalk may merge or split them. Do not keep separate subgroups just because roles are scientists, technicians, artisans or machine operators.

## Computational links

Food analytics, process automation, predictive quality, traceability and computational food microbiology can support several areas. They are methods, not automatic specializations. A computational pathway needs an evidenced food task and a clear downstream relationship.
