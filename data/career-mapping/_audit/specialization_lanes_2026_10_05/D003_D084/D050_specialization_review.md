# D050 — Industrial Automation: specialization review

**Disposition:** source-reviewed; the parent scope mixes control-system engineering with supervision and assembly, and all three stored subgroups are excluded with zero active roles. Do not activate the old ISCO buckets as specializations. No canonical taxonomy was changed.

The domain description combines automated machinery/control design, instrumentation maintenance, production-line supervision and equipment assembly. The defensible parent boundary should center on specifying, designing, integrating, commissioning, operating or maintaining automation/control systems. Supervision or assembly belongs only when the actual tasks include that system responsibility.

## Provisional practice areas

International automation and manufacturing-system sources support four candidates, but they do not prescribe this exact count:

1. **Industrial Control and Instrumentation** — sensing, actuation, measurement and control of processes/equipment.
2. **Robotics and Mechatronic Automation** — integration, programming, commissioning and maintenance of industrial robotic/mechatronic systems.
3. **Industrial Networks and Control-System Integration** — connectivity and interoperability across devices and control platforms in industrial contexts.
4. **Manufacturing Automation and Operations Systems** — automation’s interfaces with manufacturing execution and operations such as production, quality and maintenance.

The four areas are provisional because D050 currently has no active role paths. Validate actual global job families and task boundaries before deciding subgroup count or assigning roles.

## Computational / control-system context

PLCs, DCS, SCADA, industrial networks, MES and OT-security are relevant system contexts. ISA-95 distinguishes process, sensing/actuation, supervisory control, manufacturing operations, and enterprise planning functions. Those layers clarify system relationships; they are not a student learning sequence and do not automatically define separate specializations. Treat OT security and data integration as cross-cutting unless role evidence supports their own route.

## Sources and limitations

- [IEC TC 65 committee structure](https://assets.iec.ch/further_informations/1250/65-SBP-2023_mma.pdf?0502T00=): international scopes include system aspects, measurement/control devices, industrial networks, enterprise integration, OT cybersecurity, digital factory, lifecycle management and safety/security. It is not a job taxonomy.
- [ISA-95 Series of Standards](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-95-standard): official overview of automation/control and manufacturing-operation interfaces; its model organizes activities, not Sifter specialization count or learning order.
- [IEC smart-manufacturing use cases](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/66315): connects automation/control with design, engineering/commissioning, operations and lifecycle work. The cited 2022 report is marked replaced by a 2025 technical specification, so it is contextual rather than current normative evidence.
