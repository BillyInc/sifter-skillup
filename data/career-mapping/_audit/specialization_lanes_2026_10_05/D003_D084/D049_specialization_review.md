The parent description explicitly combines vehicle-system design, testing, upkeep, technician work and mechanic repair. ISO/TC 22 provides a global technical map spanning vehicle lifecycle, software, connected driving, propulsion, chassis/dynamics, safety and testing. This validates several technical scopes, but does **not** prove that every service/repair occupation belongs under an engineering-labelled domain or dictate Sifter’s exact subgroup count.

## Provisional practice areas

These five are an evidence-informed review frame, not a final student taxonomy or count:

1. **Vehicle Systems and Integration** — vehicle-level requirements, architecture, integration and lifecycle engineering.
2. **Propulsion, Powertrain and Electrification** — propulsion systems and components, including vehicle-specific electric drive.
3. **Chassis, Vehicle Dynamics and Safety** — chassis, braking, handling, stability and safety design/validation.
4. **Vehicle Electronics, Software and Connected Systems** — in-vehicle electronics, embedded software and connected-vehicle functions.
5. **Vehicle Testing, Validation and Driving Automation** — system verification/validation and safety evaluation of assisted/automated driving.

Testing may be a cross-cutting task rather than a separate specialization; retain it as a separate subgroup only if exact role and learning-route evidence supports that. Vehicle service and repair should be evaluated as a separate pathway or domain boundary. Do not make a final decision from the old subgroup labels or counts.

## Computational coverage

Vehicle software, connected systems, automated-driving functions, simulation and data analysis are credible computational methods/scopes within vehicle work. ISO’s committee remit explicitly includes hardware/software, automation, connected driving and test equipment. That supports contextual links, not a generic “computational automotive” subgroup or inferred roles. SAE autonomy levels characterize system capability; they are not a learner sequence.

## Sources and limitations

- [ISO/TC 22 — Road vehicles](https://www.iso.org/committee/46706.html): global standards committee remit and subcommittee structure for road-vehicle systems, propulsion, chassis/dynamics, safety, connectivity and electrification. It does not define Sifter’s specialization count or settle the repair-domain boundary.
- [ISO/TC 22/SC 33 catalogue](https://www.iso.org/committee/5383785/x/catalogue/): vehicle-dynamics, chassis, active-safety and automated-driving testing standards. A standards scope is not by itself a job taxonomy.
- [ISO 21782-2:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/72336.html?browse=ics): vehicle-specific electric propulsion motor-system performance testing; does not mean all learners need this specialization.
- [SAE International, Automotive Systems Engineering overview](https://saemobilus.sae.org/books/automotive-systems-engineering-overview-pt-1451): lifecycle links requirements, design, implementation, verification/validation, simulation, testing, manufacturing, operation and maintenance.
- [ASE Certification Pathway](https://ase.com/about/certification-pathway/): US automotive service credentialing, useful only to show service/repair as a distinct practice; label it US-specific, not a global requirement.
