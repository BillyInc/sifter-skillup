# D035 Logistics and Transportation Operations — specialization structure review

**Status:** Source-reviewed candidate set; pending catalogue-wide reconciliation. The proposed count is not a universal standard.

## Finding

D035 currently has four ISCO-derived rows across 143 role mappings: Transport & Supply Chain Managers (80), Freight & Event Coordinators (41), Truck & Vehicle Drivers (13), and Warehouse & Cargo Handlers (9). These mix management level, supply-chain scope, event logistics, driving occupations and cargo execution. They are not evidence that four student specializations are correct.

CILT International distinguishes freight transport, passenger transport, international trade, infrastructure and planning as practice contexts. ICAO/WCO describe air-cargo operations, security, facilitation and documentation; IMO addresses maritime cargo/container safety; UNECE describes international road-transit operations. These sources support bounded specializations and mode contexts, not a fixed taxonomy.

## Proposed student-facing areas

1. Freight, Forwarding and Multimodal Operations
2. Passenger Transport Operations
3. Fleet, Network and Transport Operations
4. Warehousing, Cargo and Terminal Operations

Road, rail, air and maritime should initially appear as contexts/modes within these areas. Make a separate mode branch only if the learning requirements and supported role routes are genuinely distinct. Strategic supply-chain planning and procurement remain in D034; day-to-day movement and operational execution are D035's focus.

## Fix and boundary disposition

- Replace the four current occupation-family labels only after catalogue-wide naming and cross-domain reconciliation.
- Do not infer that all 143 current links belong to D035 or to any candidate branch from ISCO group, title or frequency.
- Separate passenger services, freight/forwarding, fleet/network operations and cargo/terminal work by actual tasks. Driving is a role/task with licensing and safety requirements, not a complete taxonomy of transport operations.
- “Event logistics” is only in D035 when the work is materially about transport, cargo, fleet or terminal operations; general event planning belongs elsewhere.
- Keep strategic end-to-end supply-chain planning/procurement under D034 unless the role's actual work is operational logistics/transport.
- Keep globally recognizable area labels while explicitly localizing customs, licensing, safety, port and transport regulatory requirements.
