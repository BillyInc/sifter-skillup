# D052 — Construction Management: specialization review

**Disposition:** the four stored subgroup records are ISCO occupation classes, not areas of specialization. The parent description blends construction project management with quantity surveying, civil engineering and hands-on trades. All four stored groups have zero active roles. Three practice areas are provisional; no canonical data changed.

## Current structure and scope

| Stored subgroup | Active roles | Historical evidence count | Finding |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Construction & Site Managers | 0 | 4 | Management occupation class, not a practice-area definition. |
| Civil & Infrastructure Engineers | 0 | 10 | Separate technical profession; adjacent to, but not itself, construction management. |
| Site Supervisors & Inspectors | 0 | 52 | Mixed supervisory/inspection class; count does not establish a specialization. |
| Building Trade Workers | 0 | 17 | Trade execution is not construction management by default. |

RICS distinguishes construction project management from quantity surveying/construction commercial management. CIOB education outcomes describe project control across time, cost, procurement, contracts, risk, resources, production, quality, safety and sustainability. ICE separately identifies civil/infrastructure technical areas. These sources support a narrower D052 parent boundary around managing construction delivery—not treating every occupation participating in a project as a specialization under this domain.

## Provisional areas of specialization

1. **Construction Project Management** — project briefs, teams, programme, procurement, contracts, risk, delivery and evaluation.
2. **Construction Commercial and Cost Management** — quantity/cost planning, commercial control, procurement, cash flow, contracts and project accounts.
3. **Construction Site and Production Management** — work sequencing, site resources, subcontractors, progress, quality and safety.

The third area may collapse into Construction Project Management if exact tasks show that “site management” is a role level or setting rather than a distinct learning route. Engineering design and building-trade execution should remain outside D052 unless the role’s core task is actually managing construction delivery.

## Computational relationships

BIM, estimating/cost models, scheduling and project-controls software support particular management tasks. ## Sources and limitations

- [RICS Project Management and Quantity Surveying pathways](https://www.rics.org/join-rics/sector-pathways) distinguish project lifecycle/team coordination from construction cost-management competencies.
- [RICS Quantity Surveying and Construction pathway guide](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/join-rics/qs_and_construction.pdf) provides detailed costing, commercial and project-control competencies.
- [CIOB construction project-management learning outcomes](https://www.ciob.org/media/3294/download) ground project delivery, cost/time, resources, production, safety, quality, contracts and risk.
- [ICE areas of interest](https://ice.org.uk/areas-of-interest) separately classifies technical civil/infrastructure areas such as structures, geotechnical, transport and water.
