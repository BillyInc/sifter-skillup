# D003 — Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

Status: source-reviewed candidate set; parent-scope alignment and later role fit remain open. This is a recommendation, not academic sign-off.

## Finding

The checked-in inventory has no visible D003 specialization cards and one excluded stored subgroup record. That excluded label and its current role memberships were not used to derive the recommendation. The root description currently highlights learning, image recognition, language and prediction, but does not describe several recognized AI areas—knowledge representation, reasoning, planning, agents and robotics. Broaden the root description before publishing the complete set below.

## Proposed student-facing areas

| Area | Plain-language scope | Keep inside / outside |
|---|---|---|
| Machine Learning and Generative Models | Learn patterns from data and build/evaluate predictive, deep, reinforcement and generative models. | Generative models are methods within this area or other branches, not automatically a separate card. |
| Knowledge, Reasoning and Planning | Represent facts and uncertainty, reason from evidence, and search or plan actions. | Keep the related methods together for the student view; do not make every technique its own specialization. |
| Natural Language and Speech AI | Build systems that interpret or generate language and speech. | NLP is a distinct technical direction; language models remain a cross-cutting method. |
| Computer Vision and Perception | Enable systems to interpret images, video and sensor observations. | Image data alone does not make a project an AI specialization. |
| Intelligent Agents and Robotics | Build systems that sense, decide and act, including embodied and multi-agent systems. | The root definition must include this scope. Robotics engineering and autonomous-vehicle systems remain neighboring domains; role links require separate evidence. |

Five is an internal synthesis, not a number prescribed by the sources. It is a compact way to represent AI areas independently recognized by both sources without turning each knowledge unit, model family, application sector or tool into a separate card.

## Primary sources and limits

1. [ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI, *Computer Science Curricula 2023*, AI knowledge area](https://csed.acm.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Version-Gamma.pdf), AI section, printed pp. 23–42 (PDF pp. 21–40). The knowledge units include machine learning, knowledge representation and reasoning, search, planning, agents, natural-language processing, robotics, and perception/computer vision. It supports disciplinary recognition and broad curricular coverage. It does **not** prescribe this five-card product taxonomy or say every unit is a formal degree specialization.
2. [AAAI-26 Keywords and Technical Tracks](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-26/keywords/). Its technical categories include machine learning, NLP, computer vision, intelligent robotics, knowledge representation/reasoning, planning, search, and reasoning under uncertainty. This independently corroborates active AI research/practice areas. A conference submission taxonomy is not a curriculum standard, employment taxonomy, or specialization-count mandate.

## Boundary decisions

- Keep responsible/safe/ethical AI visible across the foundation and all five branches rather than forcing it into a separate specialization card: CS2023 addresses societal impact and ethics across AI; AAAI also treats AI ethics/alignment as a research category.
- Treat generative AI/foundation models as methods spanning multiple areas, not a sixth area by default.
- Treat AI in finance, health, transport or other sectors as application contexts, not technical specializations unless a separate domain-specific route is justified.
- No role-to-domain or role-to-specialization mapping is approved here. The two axes remain separate.
- The five areas make D003 a computational domain, but this single-domain decision does not close the broader computational-coverage audit.
