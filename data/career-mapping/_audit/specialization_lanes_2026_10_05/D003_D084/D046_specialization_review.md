# D046 — Social Work and Community Development: specialization review

**Disposition:** source-reviewed; current subgroups need restructuring. Candidate areas are provisional; no canonical taxonomy changes were made.

## Current catalogue state

D046 has **12 active role mappings** and three visible ISCO-derived subgroup records. Their current counts are 8, 3 and 1, but those counts do not determine the correct number of areas of specialization.

- **Social Workers & Counsellors** combines social work with psychology/counselling work that may have a different professional remit.
- **Care & Support Workers** is a broad occupational bucket, not a clear specialization. Community-based social support, clinical care, and routine personal care need separate task checks.
- **Childcare & Nannying** equates routine childcare with social-work practice. Child/family welfare can be a social-work field when tasks involve assessment, protection, family intervention or social-service access; childcare alone does not establish that fit.

The parent’s global scope is credible, but its current description absorbs routine childcare, generic counselling/psychology and frontline care. IFSW/IASSW define social work across individual, family, group, community and societal levels, including community work, policy and advocacy. Their standards also caution that related categories such as child-care workers, development workers and community workers may be distinct from social workers. This supports a careful boundary—not a blanket inclusion or exclusion. [IFSW Global Definition](https://www.ifsw.org/what-is-social-work/global-definition-of-social-work/), [IFSW/IASSW Global Standards](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards-for-social-work-education-and-training/)

## Provisional practice areas

Three candidate areas are a practical synthesis, not a count prescribed by a professional body:

1. **Direct Social Work Practice** — assess and work with individuals, families and groups to address social challenges and support wellbeing, rights and access to services.
2. **Community and Social Development** — work with communities and organizations on participation, collective problem-solving, inclusion and locally grounded change.
3. **Social Policy, Advocacy and Programme Development** — analyze social conditions, advocate for rights and equitable access, and contribute to social-welfare policy and programmes.

Population/issue fields such as child welfare, health, disability, justice or substance use should initially be cross-cutting practice contexts, not automatic extra subgroups. Promote one to a separate student path only when role coverage and task evidence justify a distinct route. IFSW/IASSW specifically warn that excessive specialization can fragment the profession’s interconnected practice levels.

## Role and computational boundary

The separate D046 role review is **parent-domain fit only**: 5 supported, 5 conditional, 2 unsupported. It does not decide subgroup fit. Psychology, clinical therapy, ordinary childcare and generic care work must be judged by actual task/remit, not title or ISCO code. ILO classification separately groups psychologists (2634), social-work/counselling professionals (2635) and personal-care work; that is a boundary cross-check, not a mapping rule. [ILO ISCO-08](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf)

Social-work/community-development research, digital case-management tools and computational social science are methods or contexts—not automatic specializations. A distinct computational route requires evidence of a defined practice area and task family.

## Evidence and limits

- [IFSW Global Definition of Social Work](https://www.ifsw.org/what-is-social-work/global-definition-of-social-work/) — global profession definition and practice commentary; not a fixed subgroup taxonomy.
- [IFSW/IASSW Global Standards for Social Work Education and Training](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards-for-social-work-education-and-training/) — globally consulted standards describing variation by population, setting and practice level; cautions against fragmentation.
- [IFSW/IASSW Global Standards (2012 edition discussion)](https://www.ifsw.org/global-standards/) — explicitly notes boundary differences among social workers and adjacent welfare-sector categories; older standard, used as boundary evidence only.
- [ILO ISCO-08, Volume 1](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf) — international classification distinction among psychologists, social-work/counselling professionals and personal-care occupations; it does not determine this taxonomy.
