# D007 — User Interface and User Experience Design

Status: source-reviewed candidate set recommended; no role placement or canonical taxonomy implementation in this review.

## Finding

The current lane inventory has no visible specialization cards for D007. That absence should not be read as evidence that the domain has no meaningful areas. International HCI curricula distinguish understanding users, evaluating designs, designing interaction/system behavior and accessibility/inclusive design. The current review synthesizes these into three clear student-facing areas; it does not review individual role fit.

## Proposed student-facing areas

| Area | Plain-language scope | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| User Research and UX Evaluation | Investigate users, contexts, needs and tasks; evaluate designs and usability; synthesize evidence. | Evidence about users/designs, distinct from crafting interface behavior and presentation. |
| Interaction and Interface Design | Design navigation, interaction flows, information presentation, layouts, prototypes and interface patterns. | The designed interaction and its presentation; it should draw on research and evaluation. |
| Accessibility and Inclusive Design | Remove barriers across abilities, contexts and assistive technologies using inclusive methods and accessibility criteria. | A focused area for deeper expertise, while accessible practice remains everyone’s responsibility. WCAG does not cover every medium or need. |

Three is an internal synthesis, not a count mandated by an external source. Research, interaction design and accessibility overlap in real projects but have distinct primary questions and methods.

## Evidence and limits

- [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://committee.iso.org/standard/77520.html?browse=tc), public abstract and status: human-centred design principles and activities across the lifecycle of interactive systems; current edition confirmed in 2025. Supports a lifecycle practice, not a fixed subgroup taxonomy.
- [ACM/IEEE CS2023 HCI Core](https://csed.acm.org/hci-cs-core/): separately identifies understanding users, accessibility/inclusive design, evaluating design and system design/prototyping as knowledge units. Curriculum units support distinctions but do not automatically constitute job families.
- [ACM/IEEE CS2013 HCI knowledge area](https://csed.acm.org/knowledge-areas-human-computer-interaction-hci-cs2013-version/): separately details HCI foundations, designing interaction, and user-centered design/testing; corroborates that evidence-gathering/evaluation differs from interface design.
- [W3C WCAG 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/), abstract and guidance layers: provides technology-independent, testable web-content accessibility criteria for design specifications and evaluation. It supports a specialist accessibility practice but also applies across design work.
- [UXPA Usability Body of Knowledge](https://usabilitybok.org/about/): professional-community initiative describes usability practice as broad and multidisciplinary. It is a living knowledge project, not completed consensus taxonomy or accreditation.

## Boundary decisions

- Accessibility can be a focused specialization but must also be integrated across user research and interface design; it is not an optional silo.
- Prototyping and iterative design are methods used across the areas, not separate cards.
- Service design and product strategy may be adjacent, but evidence reviewed here does not justify expanding this interface/HCI domain into all service design or product management.
- No roles were evaluated or placed. First complete the domain-level role-fit audit; only after specialization structures are settled should role-to-specialization links be assessed.
