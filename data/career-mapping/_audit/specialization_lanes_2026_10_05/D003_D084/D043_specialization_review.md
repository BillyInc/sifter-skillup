# D043 — Journalism and Digital Publishing: specialization review

**Disposition:** the only stored subgroup is excluded and is an unsuitable ISCO-derived occupational bucket, not a student-facing area of specialization. The parent description also needs tightening: it currently blends journalism/publishing with broadcast presenters and generic vloggers. The student taxonomy should distinguish journalistic practice from publishing practice while making digital a format that can span both.

## Current structure

- **62** active role mappings; **1** subgroup record; **0** visible subgroups.
- Stored subgroup: **Legal, Social and Cultural Professionals** (ISCO 26), currently excluded. It includes a broad occupational family, not a coherent journalism or publishing specialization. The count of 62 mapped roles does not validate it.
- Current domain description says correspondents, vloggers, presenters, editors, fact-checkers and translators may be in scope. These labels do not prove fit: check actual tasks. News presenters can be journalism when they research, verify or apply editorial judgment; studio/broadcast-production tasks belong to D042; general entertainment vlogging does not become journalism by using video.

## Scope and candidate areas

UNESCO's cultural/statistical references distinguish publishing in books, newspapers and periodicals—including digital forms—from the editorial work of journalism. IFJ's ethics charter characterizes journalistic activity through research, editing, transmission and dissemination of news/information across media. This supports a clearer parent scope covering journalism and editorial publishing across print and digital, while separating broadcast production/technical operations.

Provisional learning areas:

1. **Reporting and Journalism** — gather, investigate, verify and explain information.
2. **Editorial, Verification and Newsroom Practice** — fact-check, edit and prepare journalism for publication under accuracy and editorial standards.
3. **Publishing and Digital Publication Production** — develop, produce, publish and distribute books, newspapers, periodicals and digital versions.

Three is a source-informed navigation proposal, not a mandated taxonomy. Digital is not automatically a separate area: it is a format/practice context across journalism and publishing. Publishing is broader than journalism, so the interface should label which branch the learner is following.

## Computational interpretation and boundaries

Digital platforms and verification technology are tools or contexts, not automatically computational specializations. Data/computational journalism should appear only when role tasks and external evidence justify the route; no new occupation is invented here.

Crosswalk boundaries requiring role-by-role adjudication:

- Broadcast presentation, camera/audio, studio and transmission work: D042 unless the task is itself journalistic reporting/editing.
- General creator/influencer/entertainment content: D168 or another relevant domain absent journalistic research/verification/editorial duties.
- Libraries, archival work and printing/manufacturing: audit their separate catalogue parents; publishing activity alone does not justify duplicate role links.

## Sources and limits

- [UNESCO UIS, Books and Press](https://uis.unesco.org/en/glossary-term/books-and-press): books, newspapers, periodicals, online newspapers, e-books and digital distribution. A cultural-domain definition, not a career taxonomy.
- [UNESCO recommendation on books, newspapers and periodicals](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/revised-recommendation-concerning-international-standardization-statistics-production-and): distinguishes publishing categories and defines publishing outputs; this does not prescribe the product's subgroup count.
- [IFJ Global Charter of Ethics for Journalists](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists?cHash=f80f679e9447941ec9945439716b4186&hl=en-US): international professional ethics guidance describes research, editing, transmission and dissemination of news/information; it is not universal national law.
- [UNESCO journalism handbook overview](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-empowers-journalists-counter-disinformation-and-misinformation): supports fact-checking and social-media verification as journalism learning/practice topics, not universal job requirements.
- [UNESCO 2025 Framework for Cultural Statistics](https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/methods-and-tools/framework-cultural-statistics): international statistical framework supporting explicit cultural-sector boundaries, not role-level mapping.
