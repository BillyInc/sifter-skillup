# D044 — Graphic Design: specialization review

**Disposition:** the parent needs a tighter visual-communication boundary, and the three current subgroup labels are not a clean specialization set. One merges digital/interaction and industrial product design; another merges illustration with fine art; the third treats photography—sometimes journalistic or commercial—as automatically part of graphic design. There are **12 active role mappings** and **3 visible subgroup records**, but those counts do not establish the right structure.

## Scope correction to consider

Define this domain as intentional visual communication: using image, typography, text, colour, symbols and layout to communicate ideas or information to an audience. UNESCO's 2025 framework gives graphic design this communication purpose across print and digital media. ICoD explicitly distinguishes visual communication/graphic design from industrial design of manufactured objects, while recognizing that design disciplines overlap.

Consider the student-facing name **Visual Communication Design (Graphic Design)** if the broader communication-design scope is accepted. This is a naming recommendation for review—not a canonical change in this artifact.

## Existing subgroup audit

| Current label | Roles | Finding |
|---|---:|---|
| Digital & Product Design | 8 | Mixes visual communication with UI/interaction and industrial product design. Separate only graphic-communication outputs from UI/UX and manufactured-object design. |
| Fine Art & Illustration | 3 | Illustration may serve a communication brief; fine art/painting is not automatically graphic design. The current label combines different primary purposes. |
| Photography | 1 | Photography may contribute imagery to a design brief, but commercial photography or photojournalism is not automatically a graphic-design specialization. Audit actual primary deliverables. |

## Provisional communication-design areas

1. **Brand Identity and Visual Systems**
2. **Editorial and Publication Design**
3. **Campaign and Promotional Communication Design**
4. **Packaging and Point-of-Sale Communication**
5. **Information, Wayfinding and Data Visualization**

These five are a provisional navigation synthesis, not an ICoD/UNESCO-mandated taxonomy or final count. They are intentionally about areas of visual-communication practice, not roles, tools, or software categories. A role must still fit both D044 and a specific area by its actual task.

## Boundaries and computational note

- UI/interaction design: cross-check against D007; a digital screen does not by itself make work graphic design or UX.
- Manufactured product form/ergonomics/materials: industrial/product design, not graphic communication; do not keep it under the current “Digital & Product Design” label.
- Fine art and photography: keep only when the primary task is visual communication for an audience/brief; otherwise route to the relevant separate field.
- Digital software, design systems and production files are tools. Data visualization is a graphic-communication practice when it encodes and communicates information; statistical analysis and software engineering remain separate disciplines.

## Sources and limits

- [International Council of Design, design disciplines](https://www.theicod.org/en/professional-design/what-is-a-designer/design-disciplines): distinguishes visual communication designers (including graphic designers) from industrial/product designers and names example outputs. This is a broad professional description, not a fixed subgroup taxonomy.
- [UNESCO 2025 Framework for Cultural Statistics, Part I](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/in/documentViewer.xhtml?ark=%2Fark%3A%2F48223%2Fpf0000395490%2FPDF%2F395490eng.pdf&file=%2Fin%2Frest%2FannotationSVC%2FDownloadWatermarkedAttachment%2Fattach_import_0edec755-0b78-4b30-ab63-efb9bac610a0%3F_%3D395490eng.pdf&id=p%3A%3Ausmarcdef_0000395490&locale=en&multi=true&v=2.1.196), Section 2.100: graphic design conveys messages through visual communication across print and digital media; the framework distinguishes graphic, fashion and industrial design. It is a cultural-statistics framework, not an occupational classification.
- [AIGA Design Teaching Resource, Brand Identity System Development](https://teachingresource.aiga.org/project/brand-identity-system-development/): gives an example of identity systems and publication typography in design education. A U.S. association resource; useful corroboration, not a global mandate.
