### D048 — Food Science

**Action: restructure the current occupational/job-tier buckets into curriculum-topic areas; treat the areas below as source-grounded, not as a mandatory fixed subgroup count. Confidence: high that the current job-tier labels are wrong; high for the topical coverage; medium for how the product should group it.**

IFT's 2026 Guidelines for Initial Approval of Undergraduate Food Science and Food Technology Programs provide a direct curriculum framework, not merely a general food value-chain source. The document's Table 1 (PDF pp. 5–8) names Food Chemistry, Food Microbiology, Food Safety, Food Engineering and Processing, Sensory Science, Quality Assurance, Food Laws and Regulations, Data and Statistical Analysis, Critical Thinking and Problem Solving, Food Science Communication, and Professionalism and Leadership. It gives measurable outcomes for each. Foundational requirements also include chemistry, biology, nutrition, statistics, physics, and communication (PDF pp. 2–4). This supports replacing the present occupation-based groups with topical learning areas and supports teaching analytical/statistical and communication methods in context. It does **not** require eleven separate subgroups: these may be organized into a smaller, intelligible curriculum map without dropping coverage.

Sources:

- IFT, [2026 Guidelines for Initial IFT Approval of Undergraduate Food Science and Food Technology Programs](https://www.ift.org/link/34cd6e24343048729b50edbecae561d4.aspx), especially Table of Contents and standards/ELOs, PDF pp. 5–8; foundational content, pp. 2–4.
- IFT, [Undergraduate Program Approval](https://www.ift.org/advance-your-career/students/undergraduate-programs/ift-undergraduate-program-approval/), lines 18–27 and 47–55: HERB reviews programs against IFT's program guidelines; the page links the 2026 guidelines.

**Limit:** IFT approval is a professional body's U.S.-based program-review framework. Use it as external discipline evidence alongside the Nigerian CCMAS package, not as Nigerian accreditation or proof that every Nigerian food-science programme covers every item.

### D049 — Automotive Engineering

**Action: replace the mixed engineering/technician/repair occupation groups with technical learning areas, but do not imply autonomous vehicles are a universal required track. Confidence: high on the current category error; medium on the final grouping.**

ABET's 2025–26 Engineering Technology criteria, proposed automotive program criteria (Lead Society: SAE International), apply to automotive engineering technology programmes and define automotive broadly as land, sea, air, or space mobility. They require instruction in operations, maintenance, manufacturing and modern equipment; curriculum areas include computer technologies, probability/statistics, and design, manufacture and maintenance of major subsystems/technologies (associate criteria; baccalaureate criteria additionally cover applied design/management, problem investigation, data analysis, solution implementation and facility operations). This directly supports organizing student learning around vehicle systems/subsystems, engineering design/manufacture, testing/operations/maintenance, and engineering computing/data—not around separate job ranks.

SAE International's automotive systems engineering overview describes lifecycle work across requirements, specification, design, implementation, verification/validation, modeling/simulation, testing, manufacturing, operation and maintenance. SAE's hybrid/electric vehicle engineering academy further identifies vehicle architecture, energy requirements, battery/pack design, BMS, power electronics, electric machines, charging, safety/regulations, diagnostics, thermal management, NVH, and integration as a connected electrification learning cluster. Those are supported candidate pathways/options, not proof that all are core to every automotive curriculum.

Sources:

- ABET, [2025–2026 Criteria for Accrediting Engineering Technology Programs](https://www.abet.org/2025-2026_etac_criteria/), proposed Program Criteria for Automotive Engineering Technology and Similarly Named Programs, associate and baccalaureate curriculum clauses (search extract gives the criteria; the ABET page's interactive rendering blocked line-level page extraction in this pass).
- SAE International, [Automotive Systems Engineering—Overview](https://saemobilus.sae.org/books/automotive-systems-engineering-overview-pt-1451), description: lifecycle and systems-engineering tasks.
- SAE International, [Hybrid and Electric Vehicle Engineering Academy](https://saemobilus.sae.org/courses/hybrid-electric-vehicle-engineering-academy-acad06), course description: named electrified-vehicle systems and their integration.

**Limit:** These sources establish engineering-technology curriculum expectations and professional continuing education, not a universal automotive-engineering taxonomy, Nigerian accreditation requirement, or an autonomous-vehicle learning sequence. Keep AV/ADAS as an optional advanced pathway only where the programme/domain scope and evidence justify it.

### D053 — Hospitality Services and Management

**Action: retain the parent field, and describe student areas by operational learning rather than job titles. Candidate areas are lodging/rooms and guest operations; food, beverage and culinary operations; and events/experience operations only if the local parent scope includes events/tourism. Confidence: medium.**

The prior checkpoint's exact tourism-specialty evidence gap is partly closed by primary institutional curricula, but not by a universal international standard. EHL's current undergraduate course catalogue separately teaches rooms division/housekeeping/front office, food-and-beverage workshops and management, events management and operations, service quality/design, distribution channels, revenue management, and sustainability. Les Roches' bachelor programme describes core operational areas as guest relations, food and beverage, and hotel operations. UN Tourism's education/training strategy explicitly frames sectoral analysis as checking gaps between educational offerings and industry needs; it does not prescribe subgroup names or counts. These sources support the operational categories as real teachable areas, not the claim that every programme must offer each one.

Sources:

- EHL Hospitality Business School, [Undergraduate course catalogue 2025–26](https://info.ehl.edu/hubfs/web/docs/EHL_Course_Catalogue_EN.pdf), contents and course areas (PDF pp. 2 onward): rooms, housekeeping/front office, food & beverage, and events; catalogue's curriculum progression section includes service quality, distribution, revenue management and sustainability.
- Les Roches, [Bachelor of Science in Global Hospitality Management](https://lesroches.edu/programs/bachelor-science-hospitality-management/), Academic Structure / Practical semester: Guest Relations, Food & Beverage, Hotel Operations.
- UN Tourism, [Needs assessment in tourism education and training / human-resource development plans](https://www.unwto.org/es/UNWTO-strategy), sectoral analysis and identifying education-to-sector gaps; evidence for a review method, not a specialty taxonomy.

**Limit:** These are international-school examples, not a Nigerian curriculum rule. Avoid absorbing all tourism, culinary science (D048), or event-management scope into D053 without checking the local domain boundary and CCMAS evidence. The generic hospitality employability competencies in the AHLA/NRAEF toolkit (PDF pp. 2–3) are cross-cutting competencies, not specializations.

### D037 Legal Practice — disposition revised

**Revised primary disposition: restructure, not flatten.** The current set (lawyers/legal advisers; legal records/admin; court/enforcement officers) still mixes legal practice with administrative and enforcement occupations, so the mismatch finding stands. The global IBA Legal Practice Division organizes international practice areas through sections and committees covering, among others, corporate law, criminal law, dispute resolution, energy/environment/infrastructure, financial services, human resources/employment, insolvency, intellectual property/communications/technology, international commerce/trade, real estate, and taxation. This is sufficient to reject “flatten until Nigerian evidence exists” and supports a globally framed, topic-based legal learning structure. It does **not** prescribe a single universal list or count of student subgroups; the candidate areas must still be deduplicated, explained in student language, and kept distinct from local qualification/licensing routes.

Primary source and exact support:

- International Bar Association, [Legal Practice Division](https://www.ibanet.org/LPD), lines 25–29 (Division purpose: practice of law throughout the world and organization through sections/committees), lines 203–264 (Financial Services, Real Estate, Maritime/Aviation, Human Resources/Employment, IP/Communications/Technology, Corporate, and International Commerce sections and committees), plus its additional criminal, dispute-resolution, energy/environment/infrastructure and tax sections listed on the page.
- IBA, [Section Listing](https://www.ibanet.org/committees/divisions/legalPractice/lpdSections?page=0&q=%3Aname-asc), lines 19–21 (sections explicitly group committees into distinct areas of law and describe their international membership/practice-area purpose), lines 29–50 (named sections).

**Claim limit:** the IBA is a global professional association and its sections are practice-area communities, not an international academic curriculum standard. They establish credible global legal-practice areas; they do not establish which specializations every learner must study or how any jurisdiction licenses lawyers. Retain local qualification/required-course differences as localization overlays, not as gates on the global domain taxonomy. The ABA directory remains a U.S. comparison only and is no longer the defining evidence for D037.
