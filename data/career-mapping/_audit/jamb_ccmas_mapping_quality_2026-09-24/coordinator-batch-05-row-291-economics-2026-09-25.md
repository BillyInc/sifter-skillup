## CCMAS component and course/outcome audit

`Social Sciences::Economics` is an exact, full single-component CCMAS match for the genuine Economics offering context. The local CCMAS record contains 38 courses, spanning economic principles; micro/macroeconomics; mathematics and statistics; econometrics; public-sector and fiscal policy; economic planning; project evaluation; research methods and a research project. This is substantial support for the Economics domain and selected narrow bridges; it does not automatically support every finance, insurance, management, marketing, or data occupation in the downstream catalogues.

**Confirmed course-record defect:** the checked-in CCMAS transcription for `ECO 206: Statistics for Economists` has outcome text beginning with the Nigerian economy’s sector profile—the substance of `ECO 205: Structure of the Nigerian Economy`—instead of the statistics outcomes. The official NUC CCMAS PDF places ECO 205 at printed pp. 102–103 and ECO 206 at p. 103; ECO 206’s outcomes and contents explicitly cover descriptive/inferential statistics, probability distributions, sampling, estimation and hypothesis testing. This is not an absence of curriculum evidence; it is a local course-to-outcome alignment error that must be corrected in the source transcription/derived curriculum before any downstream evidence relies on that ECO 206 record.

Other relevant official NUC outcomes include ECO 207 (mathematical formulation and interpretation), ECO 306 (econometric estimation, inference, data processing and interpretation), ECO 308 (government allocation/distribution/stabilization), ECO 405 (economic planning/policy formation), ECO 406 (money/credit and monetary-policy mechanisms), and ECO 407 (taxation, public expenditure, fiscal federalism and debt management). These support economics, quantitative analysis, public finance, and policy work. They do not amount to professional accounting, insurance, actuarial, trading, corporate-finance, or general-management training.

The 38-course CCMAS programme is the right primary component only for the genuine plain-Economics contexts. It must not be propagated to the distinct `Economics and Development Studies` or `Maritime Economics and Finance` offerings solely by shared words. Their course-scope coverage and domain links require separate evidence.
