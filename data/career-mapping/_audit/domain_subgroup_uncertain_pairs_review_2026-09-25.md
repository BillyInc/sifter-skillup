## Evidence and method

The [ILO ISCO-08 overview](https://isco.ilo.org/en/isco-08/) explains the hierarchy: two-digit codes are sub-major groups and each group has scope definitions. I checked the exact titles, definitions, unit-group examples and exclusions in [ILO ISCO-08 Volume I](https://webapps.ilo.org/ilostat-files/ISCO/newdocs-08-2021/ISCO-08/ISCO-08%20EN%20Vol%201.pdf). For group 81, the ILO lists 8151 fibre preparation/spinning/winding, 8152 weaving/knitting, 8153 sewing, 8154 bleaching/dyeing/fabric cleaning, 8157 laundry machine operators, and 8159 textile/fur/leather product machine operators. For military occupations, it defines sub-major groups 01, 02 and 03 and unit groups 0110, 0210 and 0310.

The [European Commission ESCO occupation classification](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/classification/occupation) states that each ESCO occupation maps to exactly one ISCO-08 code. I checked exact role names and mapped codes against the repository’s checked-in `data/career-mapping/_tax_src/esco/occupations_en.csv` and `data/career-mapping/role_library_v3.json`; checkpoint 002 records local file locators and enumerates every observed member. These are source-backed occupation mappings, while domain fit was assessed separately against each domain’s actual scope.

Additional primary or official references used for disputed scope points:

- India’s [Directorate General of Employment NCO portal](https://dge.gov.in/nat) and [NCO Volume II-B](https://www.dge.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/National_Classification_of_Occupations_Vol_II-B-2015.pdf) list Mechanic, Textile Machinery as NCO 7233.1600 / ISCO 7233. [Statistics South Africa’s occupational classification](https://www.statssa.gov.za/classifications/codelists/Occupational.pdf) also indexes textile machinery mechanics under 7233.
- [O*NET’s Life Scientists, All Other](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/19-1099.00) describes a residual category, “all life scientists not listed separately,” with no occupation-specific data. [O*NET’s Physical Science interest area](https://www.onetonline.org/find/descriptor/result/1.B.3.k) treats astronomy and physics as physical-science fields and lists astronomers as a distinct occupation.
- The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics’ [Forensic Science Technicians profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/life-physical-and-social-science/forensic-science-technicians.htm) ties forensic work to collecting and analyzing evidence in criminal investigations and identifies digital-forensics analysts separately. This does not establish that generic astronomers, cosmologists, physicists, or a residual life-science category are forensic-domain occupations.

The checkpoint’s role counts were used only to reconcile extraction, not to infer fit. The exact path membership is taken from the checkpoint’s `observed_role_names_for_scope_review`; a similarly named `audit_add` record elsewhere in the role library is not treated as current path membership. Recommendations are limited to label corrections, subgroup-code corrections, or the single unsupported D143/21 domain path; they do not alter source role names or ownership.
