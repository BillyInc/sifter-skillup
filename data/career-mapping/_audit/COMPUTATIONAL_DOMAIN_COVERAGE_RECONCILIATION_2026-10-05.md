# Computational coverage reconciliation — 2026-10-05

## Scope and rule

This is a catalogue-wide screen of the 168 current domains, their declared domain/specialization text, and role-task evidence. A keyword hit is a search lead—not proof of a specialization, role-domain fit, required skill, or student-facing item. In particular, a generic ISCO subgroup description aggregates occupational competencies and cannot establish that its computational phrase belongs to the parent domain. Methods such as statistics, GIS, programming, simulation, game theory, and machine learning must be linked to the task/use case they support; they are not automatically specializations.

## Source-grounded findings

### D078 — Social Sciences

Computational and quantitative social research is a real interdisciplinary research area with direct relevance to sociology and neighboring social sciences. The American Sociological Association session definition distinguishes computational models of social dynamics, computational data-analysis methods, and research on online/computer-mediated behavior. Academic centers in Singapore, the United States, Japan, and Hong Kong independently describe computational social science as combining social-science questions with computation/data methods. This supports a student-facing specialization label, but does not imply that all social-science students need programming or that every method is needed for every role.

The D078 subgroup label was refined to **Computational and Quantitative Social Research**. Its description presents methods as question-dependent and optional. The other D078 specialization and the complete D078 specialization count still require a full scope review; the two existing ISCO-coded subgroup records do not establish the correct count. No role-to-specialization assignment was made. Role-to-domain fit remains a separate task-based audit.

Sources:

- American Sociological Association, “Section Calls for Submissions,” section on Mathematical Sociology, computational-sociology session description: <https://www.asanet.org/wp-content/uploads/section_calls_for_submissions.pdf>. It identifies computational models, computational data analysis, and online/computer-mediated behavior as distinct approaches. This is a conference-session scope statement, not a universal taxonomy.
- National University of Singapore, Centre for Computational Social Science and Humanities: <https://cssh.nus.edu.sg/>. It describes computing methods and tools applied to social-science/humanities questions and identifies research themes including human behaviour, digital platforms, health, social networks, and text/culture.
- University of California San Diego, Computational Social Science: <https://css.ucsd.edu/>. It documents undergraduate/graduate computational-social-science training combining social-science formal models with computational data analysis. This is a US institutional example, not a global credential standard.
- Kobe University, Center for Computational Social Science: <https://ccss.kobe-u.ac.jp/en/index.html>. It describes a research centre and methods spanning computational science and data science across social-science disciplines.
- University of Hong Kong, Social Sciences Research Centre: <https://www.socsc.hku.hk/ssrc/research/>. It describes computational social science and its application to human behaviour and social phenomena.

### Other findings that must not be promoted automatically

- **D065 Translation, Interpretation and Linguistics:** the candidate phrase “computational linguistics” came only from the generic ISCO science-professionals aggregate in the deterministic scan. That is not enough to add or rename a specialization. A direct discipline source review is still needed; computational-language methods may be a specialization or a toolkit depending on the learner route.
- **D135 Nuclear Engineering and Radiation Science:** “computational physics” also appeared in generic ISCO aggregate text. A physicist role record contains computational-physics task/skill evidence, but the role’s fit to D135 itself must be decided in the independent role-to-domain audit. Do not treat this as evidence for a D135 specialization.
- **D142 Bioinformatics and Computational Biology:** computational biology and bioinformatics are already explicit in the root domain and specialization record. “Computational chemistry” occurs as a method/skill in this context; its presence does not make it a universal bioinformatics requirement or prove it belongs in that subgroup. Keep it optional and context-specific pending a chemistry/biochemistry boundary check.
- **D145 Neuroscience, Cognitive Science and Brain-Computer Interfaces:** the existing computational-method evidence describes computational modelling of neural activity and neural-data analysis as optional specialist methods. These support a focused research route within neuroscience, but they do not make all neuroscience/BCI roles computational.
- **Game theory:** the ESCO definition is mathematical models of strategic decisions; it is not inherently a computational method. Keep it out of automatic computational classification. Any separate economics, finance, or software role association needs role-task evidence rather than a shared ESCO keyword.
- **Computational fluid dynamics and computational chemistry:** deterministic hits identify techniques used in some engineering/science roles. They should be represented as task-linked methods or optional specializations only after direct discipline and role-task evidence; a role’s method list alone does not justify a new subgroup.
