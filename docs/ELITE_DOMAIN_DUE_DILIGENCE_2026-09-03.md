# Elite-domain due diligence register

**Review date:** 3 September 2026  
**Purpose:** record the evidence decision for the domains that were already
marked `elite_domain` in the catalogue. This is an audit register, not a
marketing list and not a claim that the existing flags were correct.

## Decision rule

An elite label may be published only when the specific domain, subgroup, role,
or route has all five of these pieces of evidence:

1. It is a recognisable specialist area, not just a fashionable label.
2. Entry or competent practice has a real barrier, such as advanced training,
   supervised practice, examination, registration, or a demonstrable specialist
   portfolio.
3. The work produces a recognised, scarce, high-consequence, or strategically
   important outcome. A high salary or popularity alone is not enough.
4. A learner can be shown a documented route from a stated starting point to an
   entry or supervised step and then to the specialist destination.
5. The evidence matches the stated scope: Nigeria, global, or Nigeria to global.

The five tests are applied separately at domain, subgroup, role, and
domain-to-role pathway level. A strong role does not automatically make its
whole domain elite. A global signal does not prove Nigerian demand. A Nigerian
regulator may prove a licence route without proving that every job in the
domain is licensed.

## What the source families do

| Source family | What it can support | What it cannot support by itself |
|---|---|---|
| International Standard Classification of Occupations (ISCO-08) | Comparable occupation groups, task similarity, and broad skill level | An elite label or Nigerian demand |
| European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations (ESCO) | Occupation names, alternative titles, skills, and occupation-to-skill links | Nigerian employment volume or a licence requirement |
| O*NET 31.0 | Tasks, work activities, technology skills, and preparation evidence for mapped occupations | Nigerian market size or Nigerian regulation |
| World Economic Forum Future of Jobs 2025 | Employer-reported global growth and transformation signals | A guaranteed job, local vacancy count, or professional licence |
| Nigerian regulators and professional bodies | Local practice boundaries, registration, exams, supervision, and qualifications | A universal claim about every related role |
| Nigerian government, university, and sector sources | Local institutions, training capacity, ecosystem, and sector context | National demand unless the source actually measures it |
| Specialist frameworks and research sources | Specialist vocabulary, competence areas, and research or industry routes | Nigerian recognition or local employment without local evidence |

## Derived result

The current 21 flags do not become 21 elite domains. On this conservative
review, **13 are provisional investigation candidates and 8 are not
established**. **Zero are published as elite domains by this register**, because
the final publication step still requires a completed, named review record for
the exact domain or route and its domain-specific role pathways.

This is intentional. It prevents the platform from telling a learner that a
whole domain is elite when the evidence only supports one specialist role, a
global trend, a Nigerian regulator, or a research ecosystem.

## Domain-to-role pathway rule

Each published route must attach to the specific occurrence of a role inside a
domain, and optionally inside a subgroup:

```text
starting point
→ domain foundation
→ subgroup foundation, where supported
→ earliest credible entry or supervised role
→ specialist role
→ destination role or research outcome
```

If the same role appears in two domains, it receives two pathway assessments.
The title may be the same, but the preparation, tools, evidence, local entry
point, and destination can differ. If a domain has no supported subgroup, the
interface uses domain → role rather than inventing a subgroup.

The platform must show the user:

- what the source evidence establishes;
- what is required first and what is optional;
- which skills, tools, credentials, supervised experience, or portfolio work
  form the bridge;
- whether the route is Nigerian, global, or Nigeria to global;
- what evidence is missing; and
- that an elite label is not a promise of admission, employment, income, or
  prestige.
