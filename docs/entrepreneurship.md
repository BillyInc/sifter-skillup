Honest answer first: everything we've built so far — the archetype database, the seven innovation types, the signal layer, the data sources — is infrastructure. Infrastructure is not a product. A user cannot pay for infrastructure. They pay for what the infrastructure makes possible. So the real question is what does all of this actually do for a human being, and would they pay for that?

Let me work through this properly.

---

**The actual problem your user has**

A final-year pharmacy student who wants to build something sits with a specific set of frustrations. They know their domain deeply. They have no idea what type of business to build from it. They have no framework for evaluating whether their instinct is viable. They have no curriculum that prepares them to build rather than to practice. They have no credential that signals to an investor or partner that they are ready. They have no community of people doing the same thing. And they have no structured path from "I want to build something" to "I am building something."

That is five distinct problems. The entrepreneurship architecture as currently designed solves exactly one of them — showing them what has been built and what might be possible. That is necessary but it is not sufficient. Showing someone a map of restaurants does not feed them.

---

**Where the product actually lives**

The product is not the archetype database. The product is the transformation from domain expert to domain builder — and the evidence that the transformation happened.

Think about what Duolingo actually sells. It does not sell Spanish vocabulary. It sells the feeling of progress toward a version of yourself that speaks Spanish, with daily proof that you are getting closer. The data infrastructure — the spaced repetition algorithm, the mistake pattern analysis, the streak calculation — is what makes that feeling real and earned. But nobody pays for spaced repetition. They pay for the person they are becoming.

Your equivalent is this: nobody will pay for an archetype database. They will pay for becoming the person who built something real in their domain, with a structured path to get there and a credential at the end that proves it.

---

**What the product actually is**

The entrepreneurship path is a second curriculum running parallel to the employment curriculum. The employment curriculum produces a credentialed practitioner. The entrepreneurship curriculum produces a credentialed builder. Same domain knowledge. Different application of it.

Here is what that curriculum looks like in practice, using a pharmacy student building a digital pharmacy platform as the example:

The first stage is discovery. The user sees the full archetype inventory for their domain. They see innovation signals. They apply the Four Innovation Questions. This stage changes their self-concept — from "I am becoming a pharmacist" to "I am a pharmacist who could build this." This stage can be free or included in the base tier. It is the top of your funnel.

The second stage is validation, and this is where payment begins. The user has an archetype in mind — digital pharmacy platform. Now the platform helps them validate whether their specific version of it is viable. This means: how many others are building this, at what formation stage, with what funding, in what geographies. It means: what are the documented pain points in this space that their version would need to solve. It means: what regulatory environment exists for this in the geographies they are targeting. The platform does not do this by pulling external data each time — it does it from the infrastructure we have already built. The user gets a structured validation report on their chosen archetype. This is a product. It saves them months of research they could not do themselves.

The third stage is skill gap analysis. The user knows their domain. The platform knows what skills are required to build the archetype they chose. The gap between what they have and what they need is a personalised curriculum. A pharmacy student building a digital pharmacy platform needs product management, regulatory navigation for digital health, healthtech market understanding, and financial modelling. A pharmacy student building a clinical education platform needs curriculum design, content platform economics, and audience building. These are different curricula derived from the same domain knowledge and different archetype choices. The platform generates this automatically from the archetype-to-skill mapping in the database. This is a product because no career platform anywhere generates a builder curriculum personalised to a specific domain-archetype combination.

The fourth stage is the builder curriculum itself. Structured learning modules — not employment-oriented but builder-oriented. Every lesson connects domain knowledge to business building. A pharmacist learns drug regulatory frameworks not for clinical practice but for understanding what their digital pharmacy needs to comply with to operate. A lawyer learns contract law not for client work but for understanding how to structure co-founder agreements and licensing deals. The curriculum is built once per archetype type but personalised per user based on their existing knowledge gaps.

The fifth stage is execution scaffolding. Templates, tools, and structured exercises that produce real business artefacts. A business model canvas pre-populated with the archetype's standard inputs. A regulatory checklist for the domains relevant to their archetype. A pitch structure built around the innovation signals their archetype addresses. A competitive landscape framework pre-populated with the documented and emerging archetypes in their space. The user is not working in abstract — they are producing real outputs that a real business would need.

The sixth stage is the credential. A Builder's Certification in their domain-archetype combination. Not a course completion certificate. An assessed credential that demonstrates they have validated a real opportunity, built a real business case, mapped a real competitive landscape, identified a real skill path, and produced real artefacts. This credential is what makes the whole thing commercially powerful — because it signals something to the market that a degree alone does not.

**Why someone pays for this**

People pay for three things: saving time, reducing risk, and access to something they cannot get elsewhere.

The validation stage saves months of research. A user who can get a structured market landscape of their chosen archetype in 10 minutes instead of 3 months has received enormous value.

The builder curriculum reduces the risk of building something wrong. The leading cause of startup failure is building something nobody wants. A curriculum that is built around validated archetypes, real market signals, and documented pain points reduces that risk structurally — not by eliminating it but by making the user better informed than they would otherwise be.

The credential is access to something they cannot get elsewhere. No university gives a Builder's Certification in pharmacy entrepreneurship. No accelerator offers this before you apply. The user who has this credential stands out to investors, accelerators, and corporate innovation teams in a way that a degree alone does not.

**The revenue models that follow from this**

The B2C model is a tiered subscription. Discovery is free or base tier. Validation and builder curriculum are premium. The credential is either premium or a one-time purchase. This is the same model as Coursera, LinkedIn Learning, and Duolingo — but for builders, not employees, and domain-specific in a way none of them are.

The B2B model is more interesting. Universities want to tell their students that graduating from their pharmacy programme prepares you to practice or to build. That is a differentiator in student recruitment. Sifter sells the entrepreneurship pathway to universities as a career services add-on — the university pays per student or per cohort, students access it as part of their programme. This is scalable across every Nigerian university because the CCMAS mapping you have already built links the university curriculum to the domain.

The accelerator pipeline model is a B2B2C play. Tony Elumelu Foundation, MEST Africa, CcHUB, Ventures Platform — these accelerators need deal flow. Specifically, they need entrepreneurs who have domain expertise, a validated opportunity, and some evidence of structured thinking. Sifter users who have completed the validation stage and earned a credential are pre-qualified for these programmes. The accelerator pays for referral flow or Sifter users get preferential access. Either way, the entrepreneurship credential has external exchange value — which makes people want to earn it.

The corporate innovation model targets large companies. Access Bank, Dangote Group, MTN, Pfizer Nigeria — large organisations want to know which of their employees or target graduates could found companies in their ecosystem. A pharmaceutical company wants to identify which pharmacy graduates are building in the digital health space so they can offer grants, partnerships, or early customer agreements. Sifter's aggregate data on which archetypes users are pursuing, domain by domain, is valuable intelligence for corporate innovation teams. This is a data product sale.

**What you've actually identified**

You've noticed that the model has a gap between the WHAT and the HOW.

---

**The distinction that's been missing**

There are actually three different strategic positions a person can take relative to any archetype, and the platform currently conflates all three:

**Position 1: Enter an existing space**
The archetype is documented. It works. The question is how you get in and how you differentiate. This is a competitive strategy question, not an innovation question.

**Position 2: Build in a forming space**
The archetype is emerging. The pattern is real but uncrowded. The question is when to enter and what formation stage makes sense for your risk tolerance.

**Position 3: Create something that doesn't exist**
The signal describes a condition. The question is what business concept you synthesise from it and what type of innovation you're performing.

The seven innovation types as currently designed are most relevant to Position 3. For Positions 1 and 2, they tell you historical context — how the category was originally created — but they don't tell you how to enter.

---

**The entry strategy framework that's missing**

For any documented or emerging archetype, there are six ways a new person can approach it. These are not innovation types. They are entry strategies. They are distinct and require different knowledge, capital, and positioning:

**Replicate and compete on execution**
You build the same thing the archetype describes and win through better service, lower price, faster delivery, or superior location. The innovation is zero — you are competing purely on operational excellence. This is how most small businesses in every domain start. A new community pharmacy competes with existing ones by being closer, cleaner, or cheaper. No innovation required. This is legitimate, viable, and the most common path in documented classic archetypes.

**Replicate and adapt to an underserved context**
You take the model and move it somewhere it doesn't exist. A documented archetype exists in Lagos but not in Kano. It exists for large corporations but not for SMEs. It exists in English but not in Yoruba or Hausa. The model is identical. The context is new. This sits at the intersection of the geographic and market innovation type and the documented archetype layer — which is part of why the categories have been blurring.

**Replicate and improve on a known weakness**
Documented archetypes have documented pain points. The HR consulting firm model is known to be slow, expensive, and inaccessible to micro-businesses. Someone who builds an HR consulting firm specifically designed around those weaknesses — faster, cheaper, digitally delivered — is not creating a new category. They are improving an existing one. This is different from replication and different from innovation in the new-category sense.

**Aggregate multiple archetypes into one**
This is what you flagged. Someone who combines an HR consulting firm (documented archetype) with an HR template library (CREATOR documented archetype) and an HR technology platform (SCALABLE documented archetype) into one integrated offering is building something that none of the individual archetypes are. The aggregation itself is the strategic act. No single component is new. The combination creates a new value proposition.

**Decompose one archetype into a sharper focus**
The opposite of aggregation. A large documented archetype — say, a full-service management consultancy — contains within it a specific component that could be extracted and built as a standalone product serving that component better than the full-service firm ever could. Someone who builds exclusively a change management firm, carved out from the general management consultancy archetype, is decomposing and focusing. This is how most B2B SaaS was built — taking one component of an existing enterprise process and building it better as a standalone.

**Build a cross-domain hybrid that incumbents can't replicate**
You combine the archetype with knowledge from a second domain in a way that creates a version existing players cannot match because they don't have the second domain's knowledge. A sociology graduate who builds an HR consulting firm is replicating. A sociology graduate who combines HR knowledge with computational social science to build an HR firm that uses social network analysis to diagnose organisational dysfunction is building something that a standard HR firm cannot replicate — because they don't have the sociological research methodology. This is not creating a new archetype. It is creating a differentiated version of an existing one through domain combination.

**Now the domain architecture question**

You also flagged something that the current model hasn't addressed clearly: some archetypes require more than one domain's knowledge, and the platform doesn't currently specify whether that means you need to acquire the second knowledge yourself, find a partner, or build a team.

This is the domain architecture of a build. Every archetype should carry this specification:

**Single domain — solo viable**
One person with this domain knowledge can build this. No additional domain required. An independent social researcher needs only social research knowledge. A community pharmacy needs only pharmacy knowledge. Solo viable.

**Cross-domain — self-acquirable**
The archetype is stronger with a second domain but the second domain is accessible — meaning a motivated person can develop sufficient working knowledge of it without a full second degree. A sociology graduate who builds a qualitative research analysis tool needs enough software understanding to work with a developer and evaluate what's being built, not a computer science degree. Self-acquirable cross-domain knowledge is skills-level, not degree-level.

**Cross-domain — partnership required**
The archetype genuinely requires deep knowledge in two domains simultaneously. A computational social research firm needs someone who deeply understands sociological methodology AND someone who deeply understands machine learning. Neither can adequately substitute for the other. This is a co-founder requirement, not a skills gap. The platform should tell users: this archetype typically requires a founding team, not a solo founder.

**Cross-domain — ecosystem play**
Some archetypes — particularly SCALABLE and CREATOR archetypes that aggregate multiple value streams — require multiple deep domain knowledges plus capital plus execution capability that no two-person founding team realistically has. These are ecosystem plays: structured partnerships, venture backing, or phased builds where the founder starts with the component they can build alone and acquires the rest over time. The platform should flag these clearly so users don't underestimate the scope.

**The core logic first**

Market intelligence means different things at each layer because the question each layer answers is fundamentally different.

Documented asks: what does this market look like right now?
Emerging asks: how fast is this forming and when is the right moment to enter?

You cannot apply the same intelligence fields to all three. What tells you something useful about a documented archetype tells you nothing useful about an innovation signal, because a signal has no market yet — it has a gap.

Entry modes then cut across all three layers and change which intelligence fields are most relevant depending on how the user intends to approach the archetype or signal.

**The honest categorisation first**

Everything in the market intelligence layer falls into one of three types of work. Not data types. Work types. Because the biggest mistake would be trying to build a data pipeline for things that require human judgment.

**Type 1: Structured data** — exists in a database somewhere, can be downloaded or scraped, does not require interpretation. You build a pipeline or a manual extraction process. This updates on a schedule.

**Type 2: Research content** — requires a human to read sources, extract relevant information, write it in a structured format, and enter it into the platform. This is not data collection. It is domain research. You build a research workflow, not a pipeline.

**Type 3: Editorial judgment** — requires someone who understands both the domain and the archetype to make an assessment. No database contains this. No scraper produces it. A researcher makes a call based on evidence and writes it down.

Most of what I described in the entry modes is Type 2 and Type 3. The reason it felt complex is because it genuinely is — not technically complex, but intellectually complex. Understanding why an archetype has a weakness, which component is worth decomposing, what a cross-domain hybrid cannot be replicated — none of that comes from a database. It comes from research.

Here is the split across everything we discussed.

---

**EMERGING ARCHETYPES — What is realistic**

**What is Type 1 structured data:**

Formation stage and company count comes from YC directory, Techstars portfolio, Briter Bridges, Disrupt Africa, and Ashoka recent cohorts. These are all manually browsable databases. For Nigeria first, you are looking at Briter Bridges Nigeria filter plus Ashoka fellows elected in the last three years plus CcHUB and Ventures Platform portfolio pages. This is not automated scraping — it is a quarterly research task. Someone sits down every three months, checks these four sources, and updates the formation stage and company count for each emerging archetype. One person doing this properly takes two to three days per quarter. That is the realistic data operation for emerging archetypes in Nigeria.

Year first observed is a one-time field you set when you first document the archetype. It does not change.

Funding received is from Briter Bridges and Disrupt Africa for Nigeria specifically. For global, Crunchbase. Again not automated — quarterly manual check.

**What is Type 2 research content:**

Why this is forming now requires someone to read the evidence sources and write a short explanation. "AI tools capable of processing qualitative data at scale became commercially viable in 2022. Researchers using these tools are now founding firms around the capability." That is not in any database. A researcher wrote it based on reading arXiv papers and YC batch descriptions.

Nigeria equivalents documented requires someone to actually check whether any Nigerian company is building in this space. Briter Bridges helps but is incomplete. A researcher also checks Google for Nigerian companies in the category, LinkedIn for Nigerian founders with relevant backgrounds, and professional networks for relevant activity. This takes thirty to sixty minutes per archetype per quarter.

**What is Type 3 editorial judgment:**

Timing signal — "if you enter now you are among the first 15 globally, if you wait two years the category likely has 50+ entrants" — requires someone to assess formation velocity and make a projection. That is a judgment call based on evidence, not a database field.

**The realistic workflow for emerging archetypes:**

You need one domain researcher. Their quarterly job for emerging archetypes is: check four sources, update company counts, note any Nigerian activity, flag any archetype that has moved from PROTO to FORMING or FORMING to CONSOLIDATING, write a short timing signal for each. For twenty domains in Tier 1, this is a two-day task per quarter.

---

**INNOVATION SIGNALS — What is realistic**

Innovation signals are almost entirely Type 2 research content and Type 3 editorial judgment. This is important to understand clearly because it means you are not building a data pipeline for signals. You are building a research publication operation.

**What is Type 1 for signals:**

Gap scale numbers — how many SMEs, how many people affected — come from NBS, SMEDAN, World Bank, and Afrobarometer. These are downloadable datasets with specific figures you extract once and update annually when new surveys publish.

Demand trend signal — growing, stable, declining — comes from Google Trends Nigeria which is free and queryable. Run the domain-relevant keyword searches, look at the five-year trajectory, make the call. This is a fifteen-minute task per signal per quarter.

Source citation is a simple field — name the document, the organisation, and the year.

**What is Type 2 for signals:**

Signal title and body — someone reads the source document and writes the signal in plain language. "73% of Nigerian SMEs have no HR function of any kind" requires someone to have read the World Bank Enterprise Survey Nigeria 2023, found the relevant table, and written a signal around it. That is a research task, not a data pipeline task.

Why it has not been filled yet — this requires someone to understand both the market and the problem well enough to explain the structural reasons the gap persists. That is a judgment call based on domain knowledge plus market reading.

Economic cost of gap — sometimes available from published sources, sometimes requires calculation from proxy data. For the HR signal, the informal labour disputes cost figure comes from NBS informal sector data but the specific calculation linking it to the HR gap requires a researcher to make a reasonable inference and document their reasoning.

**What is Type 3 for signals:**

Signal strength assessment — HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW — requires someone to consider the gap scale, the economic cost, the evidence quality, and the domain applicability simultaneously. That is entirely editorial.

**The realistic workflow for innovation signals:**

You need the same domain researcher doing quarterly signal maintenance. Their job is: check whether each active signal has been commercially addressed (if yes, mark inactive with lifecycle note), update gap scale figures if new survey data has published, check Google Trends quarterly for demand direction. For twenty domains, this is a two-day task per quarter.

The initial signal population — finding and writing the signals for the first time — is a separate research project. For twenty Tier 1 domains at three to six signals per domain, that is sixty to one hundred twenty signals to research and write. Realistically that is four to six weeks of work for one researcher working full time on it, or spread over three months as a part-time project alongside other platform work.

---

**ENTRY MODES — What is realistic and what is not**

This is where you need the most honesty because this is the most complex part.

**Replicate and compete**

What is Type 1: Market density comes from CAC sector data. Geographic concentration comes from CAC registration addresses aggregated by state. These are real structured data fields you can extract and update annually.

What is Type 2: Typical pricing range requires market research. For Nigerian HR consulting retainers, someone needs to either find published pricing data (Jobberman employer surveys, Stears Business sector reports) or conduct primary research — calling or emailing firms to understand their pricing structure. For the first twenty domains this is legitimate research work.

What is Type 3: The "can you undercut" and "differentiation need HIGH" assessment is editorial judgment. A researcher looks at the market density, the pricing, and the typical client base and makes a call. It is documented reasoning, not algorithmic output.

**Replicate and adapt**

What is Type 1: Where it does not exist geographically comes from CAC data — if a business category has few registrations in a state, that state is underserved. NBS regional employment data also helps. This is extractable.

What is Type 2: The specific adaptation required — "same model, different context" versus "model needs significant reworking" — requires someone who understands both the archetype and the target context. This is a researcher making an informed assessment.

What is Type 3: The gap evidence sourcing — connecting the archetype gap to a specific published figure like "73% of SMEs have no HR function" — requires a researcher to have read enough to know that figure exists and to have decided it is the relevant evidence for this specific adaptation signal.

**Replicate and improve**

This is the hardest entry mode to build data for. Known weaknesses of existing businesses are not published anywhere systematically. They come from four sources in practice.

Customer complaints on Google reviews, Nairaland forums, and professional WhatsApp groups. This is qualitative research — someone reads complaints and extracts patterns. Not automated. Not a database. Human reading.

Professional association survey data where associations ask their members what their biggest challenges are. NMA, NBA, COREN and others publish this in annual reports. A researcher extracts the relevant weaknesses.

Direct conversations with practitioners. The most reliable source for understanding what is broken in an existing market is talking to the people who operate in it. For twenty Tier 1 domains, structured interviews with ten to fifteen practitioners per domain gives you enough to identify the two or three most common weaknesses. This is primary research.

Academic literature on domain-specific operational failures. Published research often documents what goes wrong systematically in professional services. A researcher reads relevant papers and extracts weaknesses.

The realistic answer for Replicate and Improve is that this entry mode cannot be populated from databases. It requires primary research for each domain. In the MVP, it is the entry mode you build last, after you have the structured data fields running and after you have capacity for primary research. In the interim, you show the entry mode exists but mark it "Research in progress" with an expected date.

**Aggregate**

What is Type 1: Whether the combination already exists is answerable from CAC and Crunchbase. Search for companies operating across the combined categories. If you find none, the aggregation gap is real.

What is Type 2: Which archetypes can be meaningfully combined requires a researcher who understands enough about each archetype to know whether the combination creates genuine additional value or is just two things sitting next to each other. This is domain judgment.

What is Type 3: "Combined offering creates something none of the parts does alone" is an editorial assessment that a researcher makes and documents.

**Decompose**

What is Type 1: Competition for each component is answerable from CAC data filtered by more specific sector codes. If the parent archetype is HR consulting, the components map to more specific CAC categories — recruitment agencies, labour law advisory, training providers, and so on. Registration counts by component category tell you which components have established competitors and which do not.

What is Type 2: Identifying the components inside the archetype requires someone who knows the domain well enough to name what the actual work streams are inside a full-service firm. An HR researcher knows that HR consulting contains recruitment advisory, conflict resolution, policy writing, performance management, and compliance navigation. A database does not know this.

What is Type 3: Which component has the best standalone value is a market judgment that considers competition, demand, and the user's specific knowledge. This is editorial.

**Cross-domain hybrid**

What is Type 1: Whether the hybrid exists is answerable from Crunchbase and CAC — search for companies that appear to combine both domain knowledge sets. O*NET also has self-employment data for hybrid occupations that gives you a proxy validation.

What is Type 2: What incumbents cannot do requires a researcher who understands both domains well enough to identify what one domain's methodology gives you that the other domain's practitioners do not have. This is the most intellectually demanding research task in the entire platform.

What is Type 3: The "what your equity is in this partnership" assessment is entirely editorial. It requires someone who understands the specific combination and can articulate where irreplaceable value sits.

---

**The skills layer — what is realistic**

The skills fields split clearly by type.

What you have — derived from the user's domain selection. This is entirely automated. The platform already knows what domains the user selected and what knowledge those domains contain.

What you need for self-acquirable cross-domain — this is a research field populated once per domain combination by a researcher who studies what working knowledge of the second domain actually requires, as distinct from full academic mastery. This is Type 2. It is written once and updated when the domain's technology or practice changes significantly.

What a partner needs to bring — this is also a research field populated once per archetype-partnership configuration. A researcher who understands the archetype documents specifically what the non-primary domain partner must contribute. Type 2. Written once, updated occasionally.

Why the partnership works and red flags — Type 3 editorial judgment. A researcher who understands both founding dynamics and the specific domain combination writes these once.

---

**What the realistic MVP actually contains**

Given all of this, here is what you can actually build for Nigeria first without a team of twenty researchers.

**From databases — available immediately:**

Market density per archetype from CAC. Demand trend per archetype from Google Trends Nigeria. Formation stage and company count for emerging archetypes from Briter Bridges plus Ashoka. Gap scale figures for signals from NBS and SMEDAN. Nigerian investment activity from Briter Bridges and Ventures Platform. Source citations for all of the above.

These are the structured fields. They populate the market intelligence layer with real numbers. They are updated by one person quarterly in about four days of work per quarter.

**From research — built over three to six months:**

Signal bodies for Tier 1 domains. Entry mode intelligence for Replicate and Adapt and Aggregate — the two entry modes most supportable from published data. Skills gaps for cross-domain self-acquirable configurations. Known combination gaps for Aggregate mode. Decomposition components for each archetype from O*NET task data.

This is four to six weeks of concentrated research work to build the initial content for twenty domains. After that, quarterly maintenance is light.

**From primary research — built in months four through twelve:**

Known weaknesses for Replicate and Improve mode. This requires practitioner interviews. Ten conversations per domain cluster gives you enough. Twenty domains in five clusters means fifty interviews. At two interviews per week this is six months of ongoing primary research alongside everything else.

**What stays as editorial throughout:**

Timing signals for emerging archetypes. Signal strength assessments. Differentiation need ratings. Partnership equity assessments. These are written by a researcher and reviewed periodically. They do not expire quickly and do not require frequent updates.

---

**The practical structure**

You need one person whose job is domain research. Not a developer. Not a product manager. A researcher who can read NBS surveys, browse CAC data, understand what a Briter Bridges entry means, and write a clear signal body from a World Bank survey table.

That person's first three months: populate the structured data fields for Tier 1 domains, write the initial signals, document the self-acquirable skills gaps, and map the decomposition components using O*NET task data.

Their ongoing work: quarterly structured data refresh, quarterly emerging archetype check, monthly Google Trends pass, annual signal review when new NBS and SMEDAN data publishes.

The Replicate and Improve mode and the cross-domain partnership equity assessments you build as that researcher completes the primary work and has capacity for interviews. Those go into the platform in months six through twelve, not month one.

The platform does not need to be complete to be valuable. Twenty domains with solid structured data and research-backed signals is a better product than one hundred sixty-six domains with thin, speculative content. Build it right for twenty and the model for expanding is proven.

Let me answer each question directly first, then build the admin product properly.

---

**Can AI/LLMs do the judgment calls?**

Yes. Completely. I framed the Type 3 work as "human editorial judgment" because that is what it is intellectually — but LLMs are exactly what you use to do that at scale before you have researchers. The distinction that matters is not human versus AI. It is grounded versus ungrounded.

An LLM making a judgment call with no inputs is speculation. An LLM making a judgment call with CAC registration counts, NBS figures, Google Trends data, and O*NET task descriptions as inputs is synthesis. The output is grounded in real data. The AI is doing what a researcher would do — reading evidence and forming an assessment — just at scale and speed a human researcher cannot match.

This is exactly how you bootstrap. You feed structured data in, prompt the LLM to make the assessment, store the output as platform content, and flag it for researcher review when you eventually have researchers. Users get value immediately. Quality improves over time. That is the right sequence.

---

**Can you show all entry modes?**

Show all six entry modes on every archetype card. But do not show them all equally. The ones you have strong data for show full content. The ones you do not have strong data for show the mode name, a short explanation of what it means, and a "being built" indicator rather than empty fields. Users see the full framework. They just know some depth is coming.

This is honest and it is good product design. It also gives you a roadmap — users can see what is being built and that creates anticipation rather than a sense of incompleteness.

---

**What data can you extract and use with AI without individual verification**

Here is the exact list of what is directly extractable, requires no judgment call, and can be fed to an LLM to generate platform content with genuine confidence.

**From O*NET — download once, update annually:**
Self-employment percentage per occupation code. Task statements for every occupation — these are what you feed an LLM to generate decomposition components. Knowledge and skills requirements per occupation — these are what you feed an LLM to generate skills gap content. Related occupations — this is what builds your domain adjacency map automatically.

The LLM receives: "Here are the task statements for HR Consultants from O*NET. Identify the five most distinct components that could each be a standalone business." That is a grounded synthesis task. The output is reliable.

**From CAC Nigeria — extract quarterly:**
Business registration count by sector code. Geographic distribution by state. Year of registration distribution — this tells you whether a sector is growing (more recent registrations) or mature (most registrations older). You do not need individual company names. Aggregate counts per sector code are publicly available.

The LLM receives: "HR consulting sector has 340 registered firms. 78% are in Lagos and Abuja. Registration rate increased 23% in the last two years. What does this tell us about market density, geographic gaps, and demand trajectory?" Grounded synthesis. Reliable output.

**From Google Trends Nigeria — query monthly, free:**
Five-year search trajectory for domain-relevant keywords filtered to Nigeria. You get a direction signal — rising, stable, declining — and a velocity indicator. No interpretation needed. The trend line is the data.

**From NBS Labour Force Survey — download quarterly:**
Self-employment rate by occupation sector. Earnings by occupation sector. Employment growth rate by sector. These are official Nigerian statistics. No verification needed. They are the evidence.

**From Briter Bridges Nigeria filter — check quarterly:**
Company name, sector, funding stage, year founded, investor name. Count of funded companies per sector. This gives you Nigerian SCALABLE archetype evidence and emerging archetype formation data. The LLM receives the raw entries and generates: "3 funded HR technology companies documented in Nigeria since 2021. Formation stage: FORMING. Evidence: Ventures Platform invested in [category] in 2022 and 2023." Direct extraction, no judgment.

**From YC and Techstars public directories — check per batch:**
Company name, description, batch year, industry tag. Count per industry category. The LLM reads descriptions and categorises them against your archetype taxonomy. This is exactly what LLMs are good at — reading unstructured text and mapping it to a defined structure.

**From Ashoka Fellow database — check annually:**
Fellow name, country, election year, problem area. Filter for Nigeria and for domains relevant to your archetype set. Count per domain. The LLM synthesises: "7 Ashoka Fellows operating in informal labour formalisation space. 2 elected in last three years. Stage: FORMING."

**From O*NET Knowledge and Skills files — download once:**
For every occupation, O*NET lists required knowledge areas and skill categories with importance ratings. Feed these to an LLM with the prompt: "Given that a sociology graduate has these knowledge areas, and this archetype requires these additional knowledge areas, what specifically would they need to develop to bridge the gap?" The output is your skills gap content. Grounded. Reliable.

**What this combination produces with AI synthesis:**

Signal bodies — LLM reads NBS figure plus domain context and writes the signal.
Signal strength — LLM assesses gap scale plus economic cost plus domain applicability and outputs HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW with reasoning.
Decomposition components — LLM reads O*NET task statements and identifies standalone components.
Skills gap for self-acquirable domains — LLM maps O*NET knowledge delta between primary and secondary domain.
Entry mode intelligence for Replicate and Adapt — LLM reads CAC geographic distribution and NBS regional data and identifies underserved geographies.
Entry mode intelligence for Aggregate — LLM reads multiple archetype data records and assesses what combined offering would not yet exist.
Formation stage and timing signal — LLM reads company count data and assigns stage with timing reasoning.
Partnership needs — LLM reads O*NET knowledge requirements for both domains and specifies what each party brings.

The only mode that genuinely cannot be bootstrapped with available data is Replicate and Improve — the known weaknesses — because that requires either customer complaint data or primary research. That mode you build last or mark as "community-sourced" where users contribute what they know about weaknesses in their domain's existing businesses.

**Is the product good enough without market intelligence?**

Yes. Completely. The core product — programme to domain to personalised entrepreneurship paths with documented archetypes, emerging archetypes, and innovation signals, split by scale and innovation type, with cross-domain combinations — exists nowhere else. A user who sees 43 entrepreneurial paths from their sociology degree that they did not know existed has already received enormous value before a single market intelligence field is populated. Market intelligence is the depth layer that makes people stay and return. The core product is the reason they come in the first place.

Build the core first. Add market intelligence as the premium depth layer. Both are valuable independently. Together they are the full product.

**The Admin Product — Product Decisions First**

There are three distinct admin user types and they need completely different things from the same underlying data.

**Secondary school owners** are thinking about student futures at the programme choice level. They want to show parents that their school's academic programmes lead to real options. They do not need domain-level granularity. They need programme-level aggregates and the entrepreneurship path count as a headline number.

**Tutorial centre owners** are thinking about subject combinations and JAMB preparation. Their entire commercial proposition is "study these subjects with us and these doors open." They need the subject-combination-to-programme-to-domain-to-paths chain made visible. The cross-domain multiplier is their most valuable feature because it proves that certain subject combinations unlock dramatically more options.

**Higher institution administrators** — vice chancellors, deans, registrars, career services heads — are thinking about three things: what are their students doing with their degrees, how do their programmes compare to peer institutions, and what curriculum decisions would improve student outcomes. They need the deepest data. They also need it in formats suitable for NUC submissions and board reports.

**The product decisions this surfaces**

The curriculum advisory report is the most commercially powerful thing the admin product does that no other platform does. A university dean can see in one report that adding a single cross-listed data module to their social science programmes would unlock 13 additional entrepreneurial paths for every student in those programmes. That is a data-backed curriculum recommendation the dean could not have generated themselves. It is also directly actionable — they know exactly what to add, why, and what it unlocks.

The NUC report is how you get institutional endorsement without asking for it. Every university needs to demonstrate graduate outcomes to the NUC. You generate that report for them automatically from your data. They submit it. It references Sifter SkillUp. The NUC sees it repeatedly across multiple institutions. That is how you get regulatory visibility without a single government meeting.

The tutorial centre's combination multiplier is how they market to parents. "Students who study our science combination with us have access to 112 entrepreneurial paths versus 67 for those without Physics and Maths" is a concrete, data-backed claim no other tutorial centre can make. That claim is worth paying for — and the tutorial centre pays you for the data that makes it possible.

Let me go through each entry type with complete data requirements first, then rank them, then address innovation signals as a separate architecture.

**ENTRY TYPE 1: REPLICATE AND COMPETE**

The question this entry type answers is: this business exists, I want to build it, what does the market I am entering actually look like?

The data fields required and their sources:

**Market density** — how many businesses of this type currently exist.
Source is CAC sector registration data. CAC classifies every registered Nigerian business under a sector code. You query the count of active registrations under the sector codes that map to this archetype. The challenge is that CAC sector codes are broad. "Management Consultancy Services" includes HR consulting, strategy consulting, PR consulting, and others. You get an approximate count for the category, not an exact count for the archetype. That approximate count is sufficient. You state it as "approximately 300-400 registered HR consulting firms" rather than a precise figure. This is honest and defensible.

How you actually extract it: CAC's public company search at search.cac.gov.ng allows sector-filtered queries. The CAC annual report also publishes aggregate registration data by sector. One person doing this manually can extract counts for all Tier 1 archetype sectors in a single day. You do this quarterly.

**Geographic concentration** — where existing businesses are clustered and where they are absent.
Source is CAC registration address data. Every registered business has a state of registration. CAC aggregate data breaks this down by state. If 78% of HR consulting registrations are Lagos and Abuja, that figure tells you every other state is underserved. You do not need individual addresses. You need state-level distribution percentages. This comes from the same CAC annual report extraction.

**Demand trend** — is demand for this type of business growing, stable, or declining.
Source is Google Trends Nigeria. Free. You search the most relevant keywords for the archetype filtered to Nigeria over five years. An HR consulting firm maps to keywords like "HR consulting Nigeria," "HR services Lagos," "HR consultant." A sustained upward trend is documented as ↑ Growing. Flat is → Stable. Downward is ↓ Declining. You run this query monthly. The LLM reads the trend data and assigns the direction label. This is entirely automatable.

**Typical revenue range** — what businesses of this type charge clients.
This is the hardest field in Replicate and Compete because no Nigerian database publishes professional services pricing. You get at it through three approaches in order of reliability.

First, Jobberman publishes annual salary reports that include employer spend on HR services. This gives you a proxy for what companies are paying for HR support. Not the same as consulting retainer pricing but directionally useful.

Second, federal and state government procurement portals publish contract award data. Any government contract for consulting services in a domain shows a price and scope. NOCOPO (National Oil Company Procurement) and state government procurement bulletins are publicly accessible. These give you real contract values for professional services in specific domains.

Third, professional association rate guides where they exist. The Nigerian Bar Association publishes guideline fees for legal services. COREN publishes guideline fees for engineering services. Where an association has published rate guidance, you use it directly. Where they have not, you note this and use the procurement proxy.

For the LLM: you feed it the Jobberman earnings data plus any procurement figures found plus the CAC market density count and prompt it to synthesise a reasonable revenue range. The output is labelled as estimated and sourced.

**Startup cost** — what it costs to enter this business.
This comes from two sources. NBS MSME Survey publishes average startup capital by business category. World Bank Enterprise Survey Nigeria publishes startup cost data by sector. These are downloadable datasets with specific figures. For MICRO archetypes the startup cost is essentially near zero — skill is the capital. For SMALL archetypes the NBS MSME data gives you a range. For SCALABLE archetypes you use Briter Bridges funding stage data to understand what early funding rounds look like in this category.

**What the LLM synthesises from these inputs:**
Differentiation need assessment — HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW — based on market density plus demand trend. High density plus stable or declining demand means high differentiation need. Low density plus growing demand means low differentiation need. The LLM makes this call from structured inputs. It is documented reasoning, not guesswork.

**ENTRY TYPE 2: REPLICATE AND ADAPT**

The question this entry type answers is: this business works somewhere, where doesn't it exist yet, and what would it take to bring it there?

**Geographic gap** — where the archetype is absent or underserved.
Source is CAC state-level distribution data from the same extraction as Replicate and Compete. If you already have the distribution data you automatically have the geographic gap. States with registrations below their population share are underserved. NBS State Profile data gives you population by state. Divide CAC registrations by state population share to get a penetration index. States below 1.0 are underserved relative to their population. This is entirely calculable from two existing datasets.

**Segment gap** — which customer types are not being served by existing businesses.
Source is NBS MSME Survey. It breaks down businesses by employee count and sector. If you know HR consulting firms exist but serve companies above 50 employees, and 94% of Nigerian businesses have fewer than 10 employees, you have a documented segment gap. SMEDAN National MSME Survey 2023 is the most recent comprehensive source. It is a downloadable dataset.

**Demand in the underserved context** — evidence that the adaptation target market has real demand.
For geographic adaptation, Google Trends by state is the most direct source. If search volume for HR consulting terms in Kano is growing but there are almost no registered firms, demand exists without supply. This is a direct signal.

For segment adaptation, the World Bank Enterprise Survey Nigeria question on whether businesses use external business services gives you stated demand data by business size. The "73% of SMEs have no HR function" figure is exactly this kind of documented segment demand.

**Adaptation complexity** — how much reworking the model requires.
This is a Type 3 judgment call and is where the LLM does synthesis work. You feed it: what the archetype is, what the target adaptation context is, and what differences exist between the origin context and the target context. The LLM assesses whether the model requires minimal change (same model, different location), moderate change (same model, different delivery mechanism), or significant change (same model, fundamentally different operational requirements). The output is documented with reasoning.

This entry type is well-served by existing structured data. The geographic and segment gap data is entirely derivable from CAC distribution data plus NBS MSME figures. The demand evidence comes from Google Trends and World Bank survey data. The adaptation complexity is LLM synthesis from clear inputs.

**ENTRY TYPE 3: REPLICATE AND IMPROVE**

The question this entry type answers is: this business exists and works, but what is broken about it that I can fix?

**Known weaknesses** — documented problems with how existing businesses in this archetype operate.
This has no direct database source. It comes from four places, each requiring different collection methods.

Professional association annual reports are the highest quality source. NMA, NBA, NPhA, COREN, NSE, and ICAN all publish annual reports that document practitioner complaints, sector challenges, and unresolved problems. A researcher downloads these reports annually. The LLM reads them and extracts weakness patterns tagged to specific archetype categories. This is Type 2 research — human-directed, AI-executed.

Customer review platforms are the second source. For Nigerian businesses, Google My Business reviews, Nairaland forum threads, and LinkedIn comment sections contain customer complaints. You can scrape Google reviews for businesses in the archetype category (using the business name plus sector as a search basis), run sentiment analysis, and extract recurring complaint themes. The limitation for Nigeria is that review volume is lower than mature Western markets, so some archetype categories have insufficient reviews to identify reliable patterns. For those categories, you note "insufficient review data" rather than fabricating weakness content.

Jobberman's annual employer survey and candidate survey publish data on what employers find inadequate about the services they use. This covers professional services categories and gives you documented dissatisfaction with specific aspects of existing providers.

Sector-specific academic papers document failure patterns in professional services. A research paper on why Nigerian SMEs do not use HR consulting firms is direct evidence of known weaknesses in how those firms operate. arXiv, Google Scholar, and ResearchGate are searchable for these papers. The LLM reads papers and extracts documented weaknesses.

**The honest challenge with this entry type:**
Review scraping plus NLP processing works technically. The quality degrades for archetype categories where Nigerian businesses have few online reviews. The professional association reports work well for regulated professions (law, medicine, pharmacy, engineering) but are less useful for newer or less-organised professional services categories. The LLM synthesis works when inputs are rich. When inputs are sparse, the LLM either hallucinates or produces generic outputs. For sparse categories, this entry mode shows "Community insights being gathered" rather than low-quality generated content.

**ENTRY TYPE 4: AGGREGATE**

The question this entry type answers is: what happens if I combine multiple documented archetypes into one offering, and does that combination already exist?

**Which archetypes can meaningfully combine** — which combinations create genuine additional value.
Source is the archetype database itself plus domain mapping. You query: which archetypes share the same or adjacent domains? Which archetypes serve the same customer base? Which archetypes are commonly bundled in more mature markets (NAICS industry code combinations)?

NAICS provides parent-child industry structures. An HR Staffing Agency (NAICS 561320) and an HR Training Provider (NAICS 561110) are separate classifications but frequently bundled. Where NAICS documents common bundling in mature markets, that bundling opportunity is available in Nigeria if neither the combined nor the components are adequately served. The LLM reads the NAICS structure and identifies natural aggregation opportunities per archetype.

**Existence check** — whether the combined offering already exists in Nigeria.
Source is CAC plus Briter Bridges plus LinkedIn company search. You search for Nigerian businesses whose descriptions span multiple archetype categories. This is a natural language search problem. An AI agent searches LinkedIn for Nigerian companies describing themselves in terms that span two archetype categories and returns existence evidence. For Briter Bridges, the same approach — company descriptions that span categories.

If no existing company is found spanning the combination, the aggregation gap is documented as "No combined provider found in Nigeria as of [date]." If companies are found, the aggregation opportunity is reduced but may still exist at a different scale or segment.

**Value creation assessment** — what the combination provides that parts do not.
This is LLM synthesis. You feed the LLM the descriptions and data for each component archetype and ask it to articulate what a customer gets from a combined provider that they cannot get from separate providers. This is a service design judgment — does bundling genuinely reduce friction, create new capability, or merely create convenience? The LLM assesses and reasons. The output is documented with the reasoning chain.

**ENTRY TYPE 5: DECOMPOSE**

The question this entry type answers is: what is inside this archetype that could be a more focused standalone business?

**Component identification** — what distinct work streams exist inside this archetype.
Source is O*NET task statements. O*NET lists every task that practitioners in an occupation perform, with frequency and importance ratings. A management consultant's O*NET task list includes strategy development, process analysis, change management, financial modelling, stakeholder communication, and more. Each high-importance task cluster is a potential decomposition component. The LLM reads the task statements and clusters them into distinct service components. This is the most reliable and consistently available data input for decomposition across all archetype categories.

**Competition per component** — how much existing competition exists for each standalone component.
Source is CAC sector codes at the most granular level available. The more specific the CAC sector code, the better the competition data. Some components have specific CAC codes (recruitment agencies are separate from HR consulting). For components without their own CAC code, you use Upwork category data as a global proxy — if a freelance service category exists for this component, the component has standalone commercial validity even without a specific Nigerian registration category. NAICS 6-digit codes also give the most granular US industry breakdown which serves as a developed-market comparison baseline.

**Standalone value assessment** — which components are worth extracting.
This is LLM synthesis from the competition data plus O*NET task importance ratings. High importance tasks with low CAC competition count equal high standalone value. Low importance tasks or tasks with many existing standalone providers equal low standalone value. The LLM makes this assessment from structured inputs and documents the reasoning.

**Time to revenue per component** — how quickly a focused business could generate income.
Source is O*NET self-employment data. Some components of a larger archetype have high self-employment rates on their own (indicating practitioners can quickly go independent in this specific area), while others have very low self-employment rates (indicating they typically only exist inside larger organisations). This is a direct O*NET data field — self-employment percentage per occupation — applied at the component level.

**ENTRY TYPE 6: CROSS-DOMAIN HYBRID**

The question this entry type answers is: if I bring knowledge from a second domain into this archetype, what can I do that existing practitioners cannot replicate?

**Second domain identification** — which second domain creates the most powerful hybrid version.
Source is O*NET Knowledge Crosswalk. O*NET assigns knowledge area requirements to every occupation with importance scores. Two occupations that require overlapping knowledge areas are functionally adjacent. You calculate which second-domain occupations share the fewest knowledge areas with the primary domain (maximising complementarity) while serving the same customer base. This is a calculation from O*NET data — not judgment, just math.

ISCO unit group proximity also helps. Occupations in different ISCO sub-major groups that appear together in emerging job titles (observable from LinkedIn job description data) are forming hybrids. The LLM reads LinkedIn job descriptions for the archetype category, identifies which secondary skill sets appear alongside primary domain requirements, and surfaces the most commonly requested second domain.

**Incumbent limitation** — what existing providers cannot do without the second domain knowledge.
Source is O*NET task statements for the primary domain occupation plus knowledge requirement statements for the second domain. The LLM reads what existing practitioners are trained to do (O*NET tasks) and identifies which high-value tasks would require second-domain knowledge that they do not have. The output is the specific capability gap — what incumbents cannot do — stated precisely.

This is the most AI-dependent of all entry types. The LLM is reading two occupation profiles and reasoning about the gap between them. The quality of this reasoning is high when the two domains are clearly different but complementary. It is lower when the domains are very close (the hybrid value is small) or very distant (the combination is impractical). The LLM should flag low-confidence hybrid assessments rather than generating speculative content.

**Hybrid existence check** — does this hybrid already exist anywhere globally.
Source is Crunchbase company descriptions, LinkedIn company descriptions, and global job posting data. An AI agent searches for companies whose descriptions explicitly span both domain knowledge sets. The search syntax is: [domain A keywords] AND [domain B keywords] filtered to company descriptions. Existence globally reduces but does not eliminate the opportunity — the Nigerian version may still be unbuilt. Existence in Nigeria closes the opportunity for this specific entry mode.

**Partnership skill specification** — what the second domain partner needs to bring.
Source is O*NET knowledge and skill requirements for the second domain occupation. You take the highest-importance knowledge areas and skill categories from the second domain's O*NET profile and translate them into plain language capability requirements. This is a direct extraction and translation task — entirely automatable with structured prompting.

**RANKING: EASIEST TO HARDEST**

**1st — Replicate and Adapt**

The easiest to curate because the core data is entirely structural and comes from two sources you already need for other purposes. CAC state distribution data tells you where the archetype is absent. NBS state profile data plus population figures tells you which absences are significant rather than just sparse. Google Trends by Nigerian state tells you where demand exists without supply. These three inputs together give you a complete, sourced, regularly updatable Replicate and Adapt intelligence set. No primary research. No NLP processing. No ambiguous judgment calls. The LLM synthesis is minimal — assign a direction and explain the geographic gap in plain language from the data inputs. Update quarterly from the same sources you are already querying for other entry types.

Why it is easy: the gap is spatial, and spatial data is always the most structured and consistently available form of market data.

**2nd — Replicate and Compete**

The second easiest because three of its five data fields are directly extractable from structured sources. Market density from CAC. Demand trend from Google Trends Nigeria. Startup cost from NBS MSME Survey. Geographic concentration falls out of the same CAC extraction as Replicate and Adapt. The only genuinely hard field is pricing, which requires secondary research from procurement portals or professional association rate guides. Those are researchable but not automatable. For MVP, you populate pricing for archetypes where structured pricing data exists and mark it as "being gathered" for archetypes where it does not. The LLM synthesis of differentiation need is a clean calculation from density plus trend — no ambiguity.

Why it is easy: most of the required data is aggregate counts and trend directions, both of which come from official statistical sources in standard formats.

**3rd — Decompose**

The third easiest because the component identification step is entirely grounded in O*NET task statements — a structured, downloadable, annually updated dataset. Every archetype that maps to an O*NET occupation code has a task statement list. The LLM reads the task list and identifies components. The competition check per component uses CAC sector codes at the most granular available level plus Upwork category existence. The standalone value assessment is LLM synthesis from importance ratings plus competition counts. No primary research required. No scraping of unstructured text. The bottleneck is that some archetypes map to O*NET occupation codes with broad task lists that contain components of very different types, requiring careful LLM prompting to separate meaningfully rather than arbitrarily. But the data is always there.

Why it is moderately easy: it is fully grounded in O*NET, which is the most richly structured occupational database available, and the LLM task is pattern recognition from explicit text rather than synthesis from sparse signals.

**4th — Aggregate**

The fourth because the most critical step — the existence check — is a natural language search problem rather than a structured data extraction. You are searching company descriptions across CAC, Briter Bridges, and LinkedIn for businesses that span multiple archetype categories. The structured inputs exist. The hard part is that companies do not self-describe using your archetype taxonomy. A company that does HR consulting and HR software reselling might describe itself as "full-service people solutions provider" — you need the LLM to read that description and map it to your archetype categories. This works but requires higher-quality prompting and occasional manual review than the previous three modes. The value creation assessment is also more judgment-dependent than Decompose because you are assessing whether the combination of two different things creates genuinely more than the sum of parts — a harder synthesis than identifying what is inside one thing.

Why it is moderate: one step (existence check) requires NLP over unstructured company descriptions, and one step (value creation) requires more sophisticated synthesis reasoning than the lower-ranked modes.

**5th — Cross-Domain Hybrid**

The fifth because it requires the most multi-source synthesis and the most sophisticated LLM reasoning. You need O*NET knowledge crosswalk data for two domains simultaneously, LinkedIn job description data for hybrid job role evidence, Crunchbase existence checking across combined domains, and a reasoning step about incumbent limitations that requires the LLM to hold two complete domain profiles in context and identify their gap. Each individual data source is accessible. The synthesis challenge is that the LLM must reason about what practitioners trained in Domain A cannot do that practitioners with both Domain A and Domain B knowledge could do — which requires it to hold detailed knowledge of both domain knowledge sets and identify the specific capability gap without drifting into generic statements about the benefits of interdisciplinary work.

The LLM can do this well when given explicit O*NET task and knowledge statements for both domains rather than domain names alone. The quality degrades when the second domain is very large (Data Science is vast) — you need to specify which aspect of the second domain creates the hybrid advantage. This requires more careful prompt design than the lower-ranked modes.

Why it is hard: multi-source synthesis plus nuanced reasoning about domain capability boundaries at the intersection of two professional fields.

**6th — Replicate and Improve**

The hardest by a significant margin because the core data — documented weaknesses of existing businesses — does not exist in any structured database. The closest structured sources are professional association survey data and Jobberman employer satisfaction data. Both exist but cover only a fraction of archetype categories. Beyond those, you are processing Google Reviews (volume limitations in Nigeria), Nairaland threads (unstructured and noisy), and LinkedIn commentary (fragmented). All of these require web scraping plus NLP sentiment analysis plus theme extraction — a full text analytics pipeline rather than a database query. The output quality is directly dependent on review volume, which is the weakest for Nigerian businesses. For many archetype categories in Nigeria, there is simply insufficient public customer complaint data to generate reliable weakness intelligence. This is the one mode where the MVP approach of "show the mode exists but mark it as being built" is not a temporary workaround — it reflects a genuine data infrastructure gap that primary research is the only reliable way to fill.

Why it is hardest: no structured source contains the data, review volume limitations in the Nigerian market degrade NLP quality, and primary research is the reliable alternative but requires human time and access.

**INNOVATION SIGNALS — Accurate Data and Modelling**

Now a completely separate architecture from entry modes. Signals are not about existing archetypes. They are about conditions. The data model and the sourcing logic are fundamentally different.

**What makes a signal accurate versus speculative**

A signal is accurate when four conditions are all met simultaneously. The gap it describes is documented in a named, verifiable, published source. The figure it cites exists in that source exactly as cited. No documented or emerging archetype already fills this gap — meaning the gap is genuinely unaddressed, not just unknown to the platform. The domain knowledge connection is logical — the gap exists in territory where the domain's knowledge is the natural tool for addressing it.

If any of these four conditions fails, the signal is either wrong, redundant, or mislabelled. The modelling process must enforce all four before a signal is published.

**The Signal Validation Pipeline**

This is the process you run for every signal before it enters the platform.

Step 1: Source extraction. A structured data source (NBS, SMEDAN, Afrobarometer, World Bank, Google Trends) produces a specific figure. "73% of Nigerian SMEs have no HR function" is a figure from World Bank Enterprise Survey Nigeria 2023, Table 4.2. The LLM reads the source document and extracts figures relevant to each domain. You store the figure, the source name, the table or page reference, and the year. This is entirely automatable for structured datasets (CSV, Excel tables). For PDF reports, the LLM reads the relevant sections and extracts figures with source references.

Step 2: Gap verification. You check the extracted figure against your archetype database. Is there a documented or emerging archetype that directly addresses this gap? An archetype for "HR Services for Micro-Businesses" in the documented layer would mean the gap is being addressed. If such an archetype exists in your database, the condition is not a signal — it is market intelligence for that archetype. Only gaps with no corresponding archetype proceed to Step 3.

Step 3: Domain alignment check. The LLM reads the gap description and identifies which domain knowledge would be the primary tool for addressing it. An HR function gap connects to Human Resources domain. A medication access gap connects to Pharmacy domain. A qualitative data processing gap connects to Social Research and Data Analysis domains. This alignment must be logical and specific — not "technology could help with this" but "specifically, people with HR domain knowledge have the tools to address this because..."

Step 4: Signal body generation. The LLM writes the signal body from three inputs: the source figure, the domain context (what the domain knowledge enables), and the gap explanation (why this condition persists despite the domain knowledge existing). The signal body must be written as a condition statement, not as a business description. "X% of Nigerian businesses lack Y, which means Z population is underserved despite domain practitioners having the knowledge to serve them" — not "someone should build a business to do Z."

Step 5: Innovation type assignment. The LLM reads the signal and assigns it to one of the seven innovation types based on what kind of approach would address the condition. An access barrier signals a business model innovation opportunity. A manual workflow signals a process innovation opportunity. A cultural practice without commercial infrastructure signals a cultural innovation opportunity. The assignment is a classification task from signal text and is high-accuracy with a well-tuned prompt.

Step 6: Innovation Question linking. The LLM assigns which of Q1-Q4 the signal most directly informs. This is also a classification task. Signals about knowledge asymmetry connect to Q1. Signals about practitioner frustrations connect to Q2. Signals about manual processes connect to Q3. Signals about underserved populations connect to Q4. Some signals connect to multiple questions. The platform shows the primary connection.

Step 7: Strength assessment. The LLM rates signal strength HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW from four factors: gap scale (how many people or businesses affected), economic cost (what the gap costs the affected population), evidence quality (single source or multiple independent sources confirming the same gap), and domain alignment strength (how directly and specifically this domain knowledge addresses this gap). Each factor is scored from the available data and the LLM aggregates to an overall strength rating with documented reasoning.

**Signal type specific data sources and how to extract them:**

**Process Gap Signals**
Primary sources: Professional association annual reports (NMA, NBA, NPhA, COREN, NSE), ISO standards under revision database, APQC process benchmarking data.

Extraction method for association reports: Download the PDF annually. The LLM reads the PDF and extracts all statements that describe persistent practitioner workflow problems, unresolved inefficiencies, or documented failure rates. It returns structured outputs: problem statement, domain, estimated scope, page reference. You review the extractions and accept or reject each as a valid process gap signal.

Extraction method for ISO revision database: Query the ISO development stage database filtered for standards in Committee Draft or Draft International Standard stage. Each standard under revision signals that the current process it governs is acknowledged as inadequate. The LLM maps each in-development standard to the domain it governs and generates a process gap signal from the standard's scope description.

**Business Model Gap Signals**
Primary sources: World Bank Findex (financial access gaps), NBS MSME Survey (service access gaps), Strategyzer pattern analysis (commercial structure gaps), regulatory change databases.

Extraction method for Findex and MSME Survey: These are structured datasets. You query directly for access barrier figures by sector and population segment. The LLM translates statistical gap figures into business model gap signal bodies — "X% of the population needs service Y but cannot access it through current commercial structures because Z."

Extraction method for Strategyzer pattern analysis: This is the one piece of manual work in business model gap signals. The 55 business model patterns need to be applied to each domain. You build a matrix: 55 patterns by 166 domains, marking whether each pattern has been applied to each domain. Gaps in the matrix are business model gap signals. The LLM helps with this by reading archetype descriptions and mapping them to pattern labels — reducing the manual work to review rather than full construction. But the initial matrix build requires a researcher.

**Systems Failure Signals**
Primary sources: NBS Labour Force Survey, Afrobarometer Round 9 data, World Bank Service Delivery Indicators, SMEDAN MSME Survey, UNICEF Nigeria Country Reports.

All of these are downloadable structured datasets or downloadable PDF reports with statistical tables. The LLM extracts figures where a system designed to serve a population is documented as failing to do so. The LLM maps each failure to the domain whose knowledge would address it and writes the signal. The gap verification step is especially important here because systems failures are often large and visible — there may well be documented and emerging archetypes already trying to address them. The platform only surfaces the signal if the gap is genuinely unaddressed at scale.

**Market and Behaviour Shift Signals**
Primary sources: Google Trends Nigeria, Afrobarometer aspirations data, NBS household surveys.

Google Trends is the most current and frequently updated source you have. You run systematic keyword searches for each domain across Nigerian geography on a monthly basis. A keyword with a sustained upward trend over three or more years combined with low existing market density (from CAC data) is a behaviour shift signal. The LLM receives: trend direction, trend velocity, keyword relevance to domain, CAC density for related archetype, and generates a behaviour shift signal when the combination shows demand forming without supply.

Afrobarometer aspirations questions are especially valuable. The survey directly asks Nigerians what services and opportunities they want that they currently cannot access. These stated aspirations, mapped to domain knowledge, are direct behaviour shift signals with uniquely high validity because they come from the affected population themselves.

**Cultural Emergence Signals**
Primary sources: UNCTAD creative economy statistics, Spotify Loud and Clear, YouTube Trends, British Council Nigeria Creative Economy report.

UNCTAD data is structured and downloadable. Trade flow data for creative goods and services, broken down by category and country, shows which cultural products are growing in international trade without Nigerian commercial infrastructure to produce them. The LLM extracts growth categories, checks whether any documented archetype produces them from Nigeria, and generates cultural emergence signals for gaps.

Spotify and YouTube data require API access or web reading. YouTube provides a public Trends platform. Spotify's Loud and Clear is an annual published report in PDF format, readable by LLM. Content categories growing in global consumption without Nigerian production infrastructure are cultural emergence signals.

**Technology Convergence Signals**
Primary sources: WEF Future of Jobs 2025 (already in stack), McKinsey Technology Trends Outlook, Gartner Hype Cycle summaries, arXiv preprint volume trends.

WEF FoJ 2025 is already in your role library. The same document contains convergence signals — sections on technology adoption by industry and fastest-growing roles directly identify where technology is intersecting with domain practice. The LLM reads these sections and extracts domain-technology intersection statements. Where the intersection is described as forming but not yet mainstream, it is a technology convergence signal.

arXiv preprint volume is a leading indicator. A domain-technology intersection where preprint volume is growing rapidly but commercial products are sparse is a convergence signal. You query arXiv by category and keyword combinations. The LLM reads preprint abstracts, identifies domain-technology intersections, and checks whether your archetype database contains commercial products in that space. Gaps are technology convergence signals.

**Knowledge and Pedagogical Gap Signals**
Primary sources: PISA domain data, ILO Skills Demand database, NBS education outcome data, Professional body CPD gap surveys.

PISA data is the most structured and globally authoritative source for documented learning outcome gaps. You extract Nigerian performance data by subject domain where it exists (Nigeria participates selectively in PISA but WAEC and NECO data also provide Nigerian-specific outcome benchmarks). The LLM maps poor outcome domains to platform knowledge domains and generates pedagogical gap signals where persistent failure is documented despite investment.

ILO Skills Demand database documents growing employer demand for specific skills. Where demand is rising for skills with no established Nigerian training infrastructure, the demand-supply gap is a knowledge gap signal. You query the ILO database for Nigeria-relevant skill categories and cross-reference against known training providers per domain.

**How signals stay accurate over time**

A signal is not a static entry. It has a lifecycle. The platform runs four maintenance checks on every active signal.

Quarterly: Re-run Google Trends check. Has the behaviour shift signal flattened or reversed? If demand trend has reversed, signal strength downgrades.

Semi-annually: Re-run archetype database check. Has a new documented or emerging archetype appeared that addresses this gap? If yes, the signal converts — it moves from innovation signal to evidence that the gap is being commercially addressed, and this fact gets added to the emerging archetype's evidence record.

Annually: Re-run source check. Has the source published a new edition? Do the figures still hold? Has a different figure replaced them? The signal updates to the most recent source data.

On regulatory change: Regulatory changes can open or close gaps overnight. When EUR-Lex, the Federal Register, or Nigerian gazette data shows a regulatory change affecting a domain, every active signal in that domain gets flagged for manual review. A regulatory change might create a new business model gap signal (new requirement with no existing compliance infrastructure) or resolve an existing one (previously blocked business model now permitted).

---

**The modelling summary**

Entry modes generate intelligence about existing or forming markets. The data is predominantly quantitative and structural — counts, distributions, trends, costs.

Innovation signals generate intelligence about conditions in unaddressed territory. The data is a combination of quantitative gap figures from official sources and qualitative gap descriptions synthesised by the LLM from those figures. The LLM's role in signals is larger than in entry modes because signals require reasoning about what does not exist, not just measuring what does. Grounding that reasoning in named, verifiable, published figures is what separates the platform's signals from speculation.

The two systems are connected through the gap verification step. Every signal is checked against the archetype database. Every new archetype that enters the database is checked against active signals to see if it resolves one. This connection is what keeps the three-layer architecture coherent over time — signals that get filled become archetypes, archetypes that are no longer novel move up the maturity ladder, and the innovation frontier moves forward as the platform's content evolves.

Let me clear the confusion on innovation signals first because it changes everything else, then build the government dashboard.

**The fundamental thing that's been unclear**

Innovation signal types and archetype layers are not separate systems. They are the same system at different stages of development.

Every documented archetype exists because someone responded to a signal condition. Every emerging archetype exists because early movers are beginning to respond to a signal condition. Every Layer 3 innovation signal exists because nobody has responded yet.

The signal is the origin. The archetype is the response. The layer tells you how far the response has progressed.

This means a process gap signal does not live only in Layer 3. A process gap that was addressed twenty years ago lives in Layer 1 as a documented archetype. A process gap that early movers are starting to address lives in Layer 2 as an emerging archetype. A process gap that nobody has addressed yet lives in Layer 3 as a raw signal.

The same logic applies to every signal type.

**The core value proposition of the government product**

Government agencies do not need another report. They need intelligence they cannot generate themselves. What Sifter SkillUp gives them that they cannot get anywhere else is behavioural intent data — not what graduates say they want in surveys, but what they actively explore, save, and return to.

NBS can tell you employment rates. NUC can tell you how many students are enrolled. SMEDAN can tell you how many businesses are registered. None of them can tell you what 89,000 graduates are actually considering building, which specific archetypes they are saving, which innovation signals are generating the most engagement, and where the geographic gaps between graduate entrepreneurial intent and actual business formation are largest.

That data — generated organically from platform usage — is the government product. The dashboard is just the interface for it.
