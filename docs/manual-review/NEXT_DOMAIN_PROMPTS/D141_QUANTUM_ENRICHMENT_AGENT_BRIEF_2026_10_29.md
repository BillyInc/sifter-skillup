## Research and integration focus

Maintain clear distinctions among physical platforms/devices, control and readout, error characterization/correction, quantum algorithms, software/compiler/runtime stacks, communication/network protocols, sensing/metrology, and classical support infrastructure. Test whether each proposed learning item belongs at domain level across multiple areas or is area-specific. Treat quantum/classical hybrid workflows as combinations with explicit inputs/outputs, not as proof that every learner must study every quantum algorithm or vendor SDK.

For each method or technology record its assumptions, problem scale, hardware/platform dependency, noise/error model, resource requirements, output, and current maturity. Distinguish theoretical results, simulation, small/hardware demonstrations, production services, and roadmap claims. Vendor roadmaps and performance claims require careful date and comparison limits; do not present qubit count or marketing benchmarks as an interchangeable measure of capability. For credentials, record issuer, scope and recognition context without implying a universal license.

Map communications/security claims carefully: identify protocol assumptions, trusted/untrusted nodes, network layer, threat model and experimental versus deployed status. For quantum sensing/metrology, distinguish sensing principles, calibration, estimation, instrumentation and application-specific deployment. For algorithms/software, connect mathematical prerequisites to algorithm tasks, classical baselines and resource estimates where the evidence supports them.
