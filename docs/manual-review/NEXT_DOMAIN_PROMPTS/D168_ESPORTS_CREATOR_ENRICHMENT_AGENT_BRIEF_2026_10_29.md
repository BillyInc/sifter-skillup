## Research focus and important distinctions

Keep esports competition/event operations distinct from team/player development; broadcast/content/audience partnerships; and independent or platform-based creator work. Examine the workflow and relevant evidence for competition rules and integrity, event operations, team/player support and performance, coaching/analysis, health/welfare, broadcast production, audience/community, platform distribution, monetization/partnerships, creator business operations, intellectual property and platform policy. These are research prompts, not an instruction to place all items in all areas.

The domain changes quickly and varies by game, league, platform and country. Date all platform terms, rules and monetization claims; distinguish a platform's own official rules from law, league policy, voluntary practice and creator advice. Do not present platform policies as law. Do not generalize from one game title or league. For health/performance claims, prefer peer-reviewed or recognized sports/health evidence and state population/task limits. For creator work, distinguish audience analytics, editing/production tools, platform algorithms, sponsorship/affiliate workflows, and general business skills. Do not promise earnings or treat a creator's anecdote as a representative career outcome.

For credentials, record the issuing body, recognition scope, actual requirement (if any), jurisdiction and date. Many occupations in this domain have no universal credential; do not invent one to fill the credentials cell. A `not_applicable` or `bounded_no_hit` disposition is valid only under the plan's evidence and boundary rules.
