## Research focus and boundary distinctions

Cover each area across foundations/current practice, established work, emerging practice and carefully labeled research-frontier topics. Explore, where relevant and source-backed:

- Design/narrative: core game systems, rules, player goals/feedback, level/mission design, interaction loops, narrative integration, prototyping, iteration, accessibility and player research.
- Art/animation: visual development, 2D/3D asset pipelines, modeling/rigging/animation, technical art interfaces, art direction and production constraints.
- Engineering: game programming, engine/runtime architecture, graphics/physics/networking/audio interfaces, build/performance/platform deployment, tools and live operations.
- Audio: interactive composition, sound design, implementation, middleware/in-engine integration, spatial/audio accessibility and performance constraints.
- Production: planning, team coordination, pipelines, risk/scope, milestones, version control/release and cross-discipline delivery.
- Quality/research/accessibility: test strategy, bug triage, usability/player research, telemetry interpretation, accessibility practice, compliance/platform requirements and evaluation limits.

These are research prompts, not a preapproved curriculum list. Distinguish game design from software implementation; creative content from tool proficiency; production coordination from product ownership; QA from user research; and platform certification from a general professional credential. Validate tools and engines in context, including open-source, commercial and platform-specific options without treating one vendor as mandatory. Emerging uses of generative AI, procedural content, immersive interaction or user modeling require evidence, human review/rights/safety limits, and clear maturity labels.

## Role-task and relationship work

For every canonical role routed into an assigned area, cite a task source that demonstrates the game-specific activity and map it to the relevant learning items, inputs and outputs. Conditional roles must have the condition stated, such as a game-production, game-audio, game-animation, engine, platform or interactive-media task. The source must establish the task, not merely repeat the title. Identify when an output (design spec, prototype, asset, build, test finding, telemetry insight, release artifact) is consumed downstream. Mark complements and alternatives accurately; do not create blockers from workflow association.

Pay particular attention to cross-area handoffs (design to engineering; art/audio to engine integration; production across disciplines; research/testing feedback into design and release) while keeping item ownership with the area where the learning item belongs. Do not make all roles or all areas compulsory because they collaborate.
