# D078 Area and Role Foundations / Prerequisites Audit

**Date:** 2026-11-08  
**Scope:** The seven canonical Social Sciences areas, their method/design routes and task-family contexts, and the 19 current D078 roles. This audit distinguishes learning foundations from work conditions and formal eligibility. It does not change programme admissions, professional licensing, or the global UI approval gate.

## Decision

The evidence does not support a universal, compulsory “area prerequisite” or a universal role-level course sequence. It does support area-specific concepts learners may review when they enter an area, plus task-specific preparation and project safeguards that should appear only after the relevant work context is selected.

The flow is:

```text
Selected domain method/design and any domain options
  → supported area
  → optional area-wide subject foundation, only where it adds something beyond D078's shared foundations
  → one or more route-supported area task families
  → preparation and project conditions for each selected task family
  → that family's connected role-task context(s)
  → role-owned learning and tools attached to those role tasks
```

An area foundation is a selectable, skippable learning choice—not an admission rule or progression blocker. A task condition (for example access permission, a defined population, an approved ethics plan, or an intended audience) is shown as context and must not be mislabeled as a course prerequisite. Only an exact, reviewed learning dependency may be a conditional prerequisite edge. Existing domain foundations continue into every area and role; do not duplicate them as area cards.

## Task-family preparation and sequence rules

The local task-set ledger has three core area task-family records and two specialist task-family records for each of the seven areas. Each core record says, in substance, that learners need a bounded question/task and understanding of area concepts; it expressly does not infer formal degree or licence prerequisites. Those core notes support the optional area-wide foundations above, not a hard gate.

The specialist records contain task-specific preparation. Examples include basic numeracy/data literacy for economic data work; rates/ratios and source-metadata literacy for population-change work; a construct and population definition for behavioural research design; and data access, unit definitions, permissions, or a suitable audience for other tasks. They do not all describe learnable prerequisites: several are project setup or ethical/legal safeguards. The graph must preserve the exact distinction and surface the note only after the matching task family is selected, before its linked area learning choices. A student may skip optional learning and continue; the app must not waive a real-world permission or ethics safeguard by treating it as optional advice.

The other method-routed area task nodes do not carry a verified, exact prerequisite target in the current graph. Their sources may support their scope and work context, but absent an exact learning dependency they receive an explicit “no additional prerequisite established” disposition—not an invented sequence. Existing approved method and area item edges remain the authority for route membership; they do not imply order by themselves.

## Role decision

Do not add a general role foundation or detached role prerequisite list. The local inventory has 21 role-task records, of which 19 join to current graph roles; every record uses the same project-dependent condition: suitable subject knowledge, evidence access, permissions, and method competence, with no universal entry qualification inferred. The 173 role-item ledger records and their preparation notes do not identify exact, reviewed prior-learning targets. The role graph therefore remains downstream of the selected domain → method/design → area → selected area task context → role-task context. Role items/tools stay under their linked role tasks. A role-item order is added only when an exact source-backed prerequisite or workflow relationship is established.

This is not a claim that jobs have no entry requirements. Employer, jurisdiction, safety, safeguarding, legal authorization, clinical scope, or professional registration requirements must be stated only when an authoritative source establishes them for that exact role and context; they are not inferred from an ESCO/O*NET occupation label or converted into D078 learning blockers.

## External evidence consulted

- **Computational and Digital Social Science:** [University of Amsterdam, Computational Social Science study programme](https://www.uva.nl/en/programmes/bachelors/computational-social-science/study-programme/study-programme.html) integrates social theory, research, ethics, data science, and computational methods across projects; [University of Edinburgh undergraduate course on computational social science](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/26-27/dpt/cxinfr08034.htm) connects question formulation, data/method selection, bias, ethics, and communication. These support subject context, not a universal coding prerequisite.
- **Economics:** [CORE Econ, Doing Economics](https://www.core-econ.org/project/doing-economics/) links economic questions and concepts to data/statistical work and software across distinct projects. It supports question/task-specific preparation, not one compulsory sequence for every economics route.
- **Human Geography and Population:** [National Council for Geographic Education, Geography Standard 1](https://ncge.org/teacher-resources/national-geography-standards/geography-standard-1/) covers spatial representations, geospatial data, purpose, and scale; [United Nations Statistics Division, Population Size and Density](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/demographic-social/sconcerns/popsize/) distinguishes population totals, age/sex, geographic coverage, and census definitions. These are distinct learning contexts, so population measures should not be imposed on a spatial-pattern-only route or vice versa.
- **Politics, Governance and Public Policy:** [American Political Science Association, Majoring in Political Science](https://apsanet.org/careers/careers-for-political-scientists/student-resources/majoring-in-political-science/) describes political concepts, behavior, policy issues, and governmental structures; APSA's [introductory government and comparative-politics syllabus](https://educate.apsanet.org/resource/11-03-2021/introduction-to-government-and-comparative-politics-undergraduate-syllabus) includes institutions, actors, civil society, and policy context. Neither supplies a universal order for every task family.
- **Social Behaviour and Interaction:** [American Psychological Association, Undergraduate Psychology Major Guidelines 3.0](https://www.apa.org/ed/precollege/undergraduate-major) supports scientific inquiry, measurement, ethics, communication, and professional development. The guidelines explicitly do not prescribe fixed course requirements or sequencing; use them for content scope, not D078 gates. The D078 area remains bounded away from clinical practice.
- **Social Research and Evaluation:** [American Evaluation Association, Evaluator Competencies](https://www.eval.org/About/Competencies-Standards/undefined) separates methodology, context, planning/management, and interpersonal competencies, and describes methods as purpose- and context-dependent. This supports task-specific evaluation preparation, not a duplicate universal research foundation.
- **Sociology and Social Anthropology:** [American Sociological Association, Sociological Literacy Framework](https://www.asanet.org/wp-content/uploads/appendix_b_-_prc_manual_march_2020.pdf) covers theory, social structure, interaction, inequality, social change, and research evidence; [UC Berkeley Anthropology student learning goals](https://anthropology.berkeley.edu/undergraduate-program/student-learning-goals) covers sociocultural concepts, theory/method, fieldwork, and analysis. These support the missing area-concepts foundation while leaving fieldwork and method preparation task-specific.
- **Roles:** The [ESCO classification](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/classification), [ESCO handbook](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/Handbook.pdf), and the role-specific [O*NET Sociologists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/19-3041.00) and [O*NET Anthropologists and Archaeologists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/19-3091.00) sources describe occupation scope, tasks, skills, or work context. They do not by themselves establish a D078 teaching sequence or universal prior-learning edge. This is consistent with the existing role-prerequisite reconciliation.

## Implementation acceptance checks

1. The seven-area graph records the area-wide foundation decision for every area, including an explicit no-duplicate-foundation decision for Social Research and Evaluation.
2. Existing area-owned knowledge items are reused where suitable; no duplicate selectable item is created for Human Geography, Politics, or Social Behaviour.
3. The Economics and Sociology foundations add subject knowledge and do not repeat the shared D078 question-design foundation.
4. Common area foundations appear only after a supported area is opened, before task-family choices, remain optional/selectable, and never block progression. A selected foundation is not duplicated in later area-item lists.
5. Task-specific preparation appears only for the selected task family, ahead of that family's linked learning items. Project safeguards are labeled as conditions, not learnable items or prerequisite edges.
6. Role conditions remain inside the selected role-task context; no role is exposed independently of a supported area route. No role foundation/prerequisite edge is added without an exact source-backed target and relationship.
7. Rebuild and test all 207 declared method/design routes, all seven areas, all 19 roles, zero/one/multiple task-family choices, and skip/add/remove/restore behavior. Optional skips preserve the baseline area and role routes.
