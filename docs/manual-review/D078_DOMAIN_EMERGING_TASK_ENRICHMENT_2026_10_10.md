# D078 domain-level emerging-task enrichment — 2026-10-10

**Status:** local evidence-led candidates only; not canonical, not student-approved, and not a completed end-to-end enrichment gate.

This supplement performs the requested domain-level omission scan for newer and less routinely captured social-science work. It is separate from, and does not replace, independent enrichment of the seven areas of specialization/practice or their roles. Product terminology remains invariant: **subgroup = area of specialization = area of practice**.

## What the inventory already contains

The current D078 learning contract has 58 provisional records, including a 29-record existing inventory and candidates for quantitative/qualitative/mixed methods, secondary data, computational social research, agent-based modelling, spatial analysis, and tools/frameworks including Python, pandas, NetworkX, scikit-learn, Mesa and Casevo. I therefore did not add another generic “learn Python/AI” item or duplicate those tools. Instead, I looked for task workflows and implications not represented by the presence of those labels alone.

The search found six bounded candidate workflows or extensions:

1. AI-assisted annotation of social-research text, with human validation.
2. A conditional competency to validate and document AI use, as an extension—not a duplicate—of existing open-science and research-data-care candidates.
3. Experimental use of synthetic responses or LLM agents for exploratory scenario/testing work, explicitly not as replacement participants or population evidence.
4. Research into how AI systems affect people, groups and institutions, including impact assessment and stakeholder evidence.
5. Emerging AI-assisted cross-source data harmonisation, still too weakly validated for display.
6. AI-assisted research-data metadata drafting/standardisation, currently evidenced as an institutional product example, not an independently validated general method.

AAPOR’s 2026 task-force report describes AI use across survey design, interviewing, data processing, analysis and reporting, and gives distinct evaluation, human-oversight, disclosure and human-subject safeguards. The report specifically says to involve people in GenAI-mediated survey work, not assume one task’s performance generalizes, and document decisions. [AAPOR’s report and announcement](https://aapor.org/announcements/task-force-on-responsible-ai-integration-in-survey-research-report/) are professional guidance, not a universal legal mandate. The report’s relevant sections include AI as analyst/colleague/workflow, evaluation criteria, disclosure, privacy, bias and emerging concerns. [Full AAPOR report](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Responsible-AI-Integration-In-Survey-Research.pdf)

The annotation workflow is supported as an emerging, task-bounded option—not as an automatic qualitative-analysis upgrade. An open-access article describes a hybrid LLM/coding workflow while retaining human interpretive leadership; a separate study reports that LLM annotation can introduce systematic bias. Together these sources support a cautious candidate with explicit validation and counter-evidence, not a general claim that AI coding is reliable across constructs or populations. [Dunivin, *EPJ Data Science* (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjds/s13688-025-00548-8), [Ashwin, Chhabra & Rao, *Sociological Methods & Research* (2025)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00491241251338246).

## Emerging and experimental work: carefully bounded

The OECD’s 2025 synthesis identifies possible social-science uses such as hypothesis/experiment/agent design, in-silico testing, LLM-based social simulation and data harmonisation, while also noting risks around bias, diversity, out-of-distribution error, representativeness and synthetic-data validity. This supports tracking the work, but not treating it as settled practice. [OECD, *Is Generative AI a General-Purpose Technology?*, pp. 38–39](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/is-generative-ai-a-general-purpose-technology_6c76e7b2/704e2d12-en.pdf).

That caution is especially important for synthetic respondents. A 2026 PNAS study evaluates LLM-generated social data against real survey data and identifies statistical-realism limitations; a 2025 *Nature Machine Intelligence* study warns that LLMs used as human-participant replacements can flatten or misportray identity groups. The candidate therefore permits only exploratory scenario work, method stress-testing or hypothesis generation under stated scope conditions; it does not support replacing people, samples or empirical evidence. [PNAS study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2538145123), [Nature Machine Intelligence study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-00986-z).

Studying the social effects of AI is a different task from using AI as a research tool. UNESCO’s global Recommendation covers social, economic, behavioural and rights impacts, impact assessment, auditability and stakeholder participation. Its Ethical Impact Assessment offers a practical, multidisciplinary process. Nigeria’s 2025 National AI Strategy adds a Nigerian policy context—human well-being, inclusive/human-centred application and stakeholder co-creation—but is evidence of policy direction, not proof that specific deployments have achieved those outcomes. [UNESCO Recommendation](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence), [UNESCO Ethical Impact Assessment](https://www.unesco.org/ethics-ai/en/eia), [Nigeria National AI Strategy (2025)](https://ncair.nitda.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/National-Artificial-Intelligence-Strategy-19092025.pdf).

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) is included only as a supplementary voluntary U.S. framework, not Nigerian law or a substitute for social-science impact assessment. NIST notes that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised; future student-facing use must check the current version and scope.

## Learning-link assessment

- The University of Edinburgh’s open computational-social-science materials are domain-specific and explicitly cover social-science questions, AI, text analysis, data collection, bias, fairness and ethics. The page says “all rights reserved,” so access is free to view but the licence is not open; verify access and section-level locators before student use. [Course materials](https://opencourse.inf.ed.ac.uk/css).
- Hugging Face provides free, task-specific technical instruction for text classification (fine-tuning DistilBERT on movie reviews), but that example does not teach social-research design, interview coding, privacy or construct validity. It can only complement—not stand in for—those components. [Text-classification guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/tasks/sequence_classification).
- SERISS provides free social-science training on survey-data handling and harmonisation, but it is not an AI-assisted harmonisation tutorial. [SERISS training](https://seriss.eu/training/).
- A dedicated free, hands-on resource for validating AI-assisted social-science coding against human work, or for validating LLM-generated social agents against appropriate empirical data, was not established in this pass. Those candidates remain hidden until the gap is resolved or explicitly retained as research-only.

The official ICPSR materials add two useful checks beyond the research papers. Its methods/workshop index lists generative-AI/LLM analysis for social and political research, text analysis, AI-assisted systematic review and agent-based modelling; offerings vary by season and may require fees, so the index is task-domain evidence, not a free course link. ICPSR also documents TurboCurator, which suggests dataset titles, descriptions and keywords using AI and an ICPSR thesaurus. This supports a distinct emerging metadata-curation candidate, but only as an implementation example: independent accuracy and transferability are not established. The linked product demo is explanatory, not a complete hands-on tutorial. [ICPSR methods/workshop index](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/sumprog/courses/sample-courses-and-workshops), [ICPSR Research Data Ecosystem](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/find-data/working-together/projects/rde). ICPSR's [AI at ICPSR webinar](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/posts/shared/ai-icpsr-webinar-ldw26) is a free orientation video on data-use policy and current experiments, not step-by-step workflow instruction.

The 2025 [ICC/ESOMAR Code](https://standards.esomar.org/assets/documents/icc-esomar-code-2025.pdf) is an additional professional code covering social research/data analytics amid AI and synthetic-data change, with duty-of-care, data minimisation, privacy, fit-for-purpose, transparency and responsibility provisions (PDF pp. 3–4 and 12–17). It is self-regulatory: mandatory for ESOMAR members and adopters, not Nigerian legislation or a universal legal rule.

## Additional domain-level tools: Power BI and Tableau

I added both as separate, optional **domain-level tool candidates** because they have a defensible cross-area use when a social-science project needs to communicate reviewed data through interactive charts, reports or dashboards. They are not shared foundations, research methods, or job-readiness claims. The candidate records are hidden pending a suitable social-science practice dataset and final learning-link check.

- **Power BI** can connect/prepare and model data, then create reports and dashboard views. For a D078 learner, a bounded use might be presenting reviewed survey patterns, service reach or programme-monitoring indicators to an audience. Microsoft's own tutorial distinguishes a report from a dashboard and says creating in a personal workspace may be free while sharing/publishing generally requires Pro/PPU or eligible capacity; check current terms before assigning sharing work. [Power BI overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/fundamentals/power-bi-overview), [dashboard/report explanation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/explore-reports/end-user-dashboards), [free getting-started tutorial](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/fundamentals/service-get-started).
- **Tableau** can prepare data and create visualisations and dashboards. Its official site offers free getting-started videos and foundational training, but the materials teach the product/data-visualisation basics—not social-research design or interpretation. [Tableau getting started](https://www.tableau.com/learn/get-started), [free Tableau learning](https://www.tableau.com/learn). Tableau Public is public, not private: published workbooks and data may be downloadable, and a hidden-profile viz can still be opened by anyone with its URL. Do not use it for identifiable, confidential or restricted research data. [Tableau Public FAQ](https://help.tableau.com/current/pro/desktop/en-us/public_faq.htm), especially its privacy/download and Public Edition sections.

### How they relate to the existing toolkit

These tools belong **after the learner understands the question, measures and data limits**, when the task calls for an interactive report or dashboard. They can follow analysis performed in Excel, SPSS, R or Python; they do not replace SPSS/R/Python statistical analysis, qualitative analysis in NVivo/other suitable software, GIS, or the research method itself. Excel charts or a static report may be sufficient when interactivity is unnecessary. Power BI and Tableau are alternatives for many dashboard/report tasks; learners should choose based on access, audience and workflow rather than being told to learn both.

The relationship is therefore conditional, not a forced sequence: `research question and evidence → suitable analysis → optional dashboard/report tool when useful → interpretation with limits`. A dashboard can display patterns, but it cannot establish that a pattern is causal, representative, or a valid measure. Each area of specialization still needs its own independent review of whether a dashboard task and either tool genuinely fit that area's work.

The official vendor learning links are free to access at the time checked, but they are not social-science-specific tutorials. Before student-facing promotion, attach a safe, openly accessible social-science dataset and a task-specific practice brief; do not use restricted microdata. The candidate records state this gap and remain hidden until that is done.

### Nigerian sources added in the local follow-up

The follow-up found material Nigerian context that can strengthen examples and safeguards without becoming a Nigeria-only eligibility test:

- NOUN's 2024 institutional ethics policy explicitly covers research involving human participants or identifiable human data and includes Social Sciences expertise in its faculty/university review structure. This is an institutional example, not a national rule for all universities. [NOUN Policy on Research Ethics](https://dqa.nou.edu.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Edited_Policy-on-Research-Ethics.pdf)
- UNILAGREC publishes protocol-review and submission procedures. The page also contains incomplete template text, so only its identifiable procedure sections are recorded and no universal requirement is inferred. [UNILAGREC](https://unilagrec.unilag.edu.ng/)
- The University of Ibadan Research Management Office describes a Humanities and Behavioural Studies Review Committee covering interviews, surveys, social/behavioural interventions and related human-participant research. This is another institution-specific pathway, not a national rule. [UI Research Committees](https://rmo.ui.edu.ng/research-committees)
- NBS describes respondent confidentiality, anonymisation and public-use/licensed/enclave categories for its microdata; its terms also restrict redistribution and re-identification. These are NBS-data conditions, not a complete ethics code for all research. [NBS Data Privacy Policy](https://nigerianstat.gov.ng/page/data-privacy-policy), [NBS Terms](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/page/terms)
- The NBS Nigeria Time Use Survey 2024 is a recent applied example with DDI/XML/JSON metadata, defined survey scope and access/confidentiality conditions—not a general methods course or proof that its microdata are unrestricted. [Study description and metadata](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/178/study-description)
- The Nigerian Economic Society documents its role in economics and allied social studies, research publishing and Nigerian economic/social issues. That is useful local professional context, but it must not be generalized to all D078 areas. [NES About Us](https://www.nigerianeconomicsociety.org.ng/about-us)
- NaijaSenti documents human-annotated Hausa, Igbo, Nigerian Pidgin and Yoruba social-media data, including code-mixed examples. Oyewusi et al. describe why standard-English sentiment models can miss Nigerian Pidgin context. These task-specific studies justify local-language and code-switch validation as a concern; they do not establish model performance across languages or constructs. [NaijaSenti](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08277), [Oyewusi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12450)

The Nigerian Anthropological and Sociological Practitioners Association (NASA), Institute of Professional Sociologists Nigeria, and Rural Sociological Association of Nigeria surfaced as potentially relevant professional context. Direct page retrieval for NASA and IPS timed out/failed, and the RUSAN page returned no inspectable body in this pass. Their search-result summaries are **not** treated as verified evidence or professional standards. The association/repository search remains open; no Nigerian analogue is a blocker for a globally relevant task.

## Disposition

All six are still candidates. None is a universal D078 foundation requirement. The first and fourth are potential useful edges for relevant research routes; the second is conditional and should enrich existing open-science/data-care concepts rather than duplicate them; the third is experimental and specialist; the fifth remains blocked from student display by insufficient workflow-specific validation and missing task-specific AI learning material; the sixth is an emerging product example pending independent quality evidence and a genuine hands-on learner resource. Every candidate remains hidden.

The evidence supports adding these eight candidates to the review queue—not claiming that the domain learning path is complete. This includes the six bounded AI workflow candidates above and the two optional dashboard tools. Each must still be reconciled against all seven independently enriched areas, the established task families, the current 58-record contract, and any already-accepted D078 role task sets. Tools are options within the workflow, not proof of competence. No new certification or role is proposed.
