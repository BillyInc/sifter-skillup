## Executive finding

The current D078 inventory already covers the broad research cycle: research
questions and design, evidence limits, responsible research, data care,
quantitative and qualitative approaches, mixed methods, surveys, interviews,
documents, observation/ethnography, secondary data, computational methods,
spatial analysis and a small set of tools.

The exhaustive workflow screen found that this is not yet enough to describe a
complete and defensible domain foundation. The missing pieces are mostly deeper
competencies rather than more named software. They explain how a learner makes
sound choices and checks quality before trusting a result.

## What should be shared at domain level

The strongest cross-route additions are not “learn SPSS,” “learn Python” or
“learn NVivo.” They are the ability to:

1. match a research question to a suitable design;
2. define what is being measured and check whether the measure is suitable;
3. select or assess participants, cases or existing data without pretending the
   result represents more than the design supports;
4. check reliability, validity, uncertainty, bias and limitations;
5. protect people, communities and research data;
6. document sources, decisions, materials, code and deviations well enough for
   another person to inspect the work.

These should be narrated as practical decisions. For example: “Before asking a
large group a question, test whether people understand it in the way you mean;
then record who was included, who was missed and what the answers can support.”
The named methods and software should be presented as branches that help carry
out one of those responsibilities.

## What should not be promoted to the shared foundation

- Causal inference techniques such as difference-in-differences, instrumental
  variables, regression discontinuity and matching require advanced design and
  assumptions; they are not a default skill for every learner.
- Psychometrics, longitudinal/multilevel analysis, systematic reviews,
  discourse analysis and participatory/action research are real methods, but
  each belongs to a route where its research question and responsibilities are
  explained.
- R and Stata are valid alternatives to Python/SPSS for some quantitative
  workflows. They must never become a cumulative software checklist.
- AI-assisted research and digital-trace methods need explicit privacy,
  consent, bias, provenance and disclosure guidance before any named product is
  shown.

## Current inventory comparison

The current D078 labels already mention interviews, surveys, document/content
analysis, observation/ethnography, secondary data, computational methods,
spatial analysis, tool choice and several tools. They do not currently expose
the following as distinct, inspectable candidate records: sampling and
representativeness; measurement/operationalisation; reliability/validity;
cognitive pretesting; focus groups; discourse/conversation analysis;
archival/historical methods; case-study design; participatory/action research;
causal inference; programme evaluation/theory of change; psychometrics;
longitudinal/multilevel analysis; open-science/preregistration; literature
management; R; Stata; and responsible AI/digital-trace research.

“Not explicitly represented” does not mean the domain has never mentioned a
related word in legacy role-derived data. It means the learner-facing domain
candidate inventory does not yet contain a separately defined, source-backed
record with its own use case, limits, relationships and learning link.

   do not require them alongside Python or SPSS.

   be promoted from research candidate to student-facing data.
