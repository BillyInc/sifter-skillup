## Why this pass is not just the old fallback

The prior local fallback listed 15 candidate entries per area, but repeated the same generic set across three task families. Several entries had generic descriptions (“a skill used to carry out this task”), no distinct task-level limits, and no independent area enrichment for modern computational or AI-assisted work. This checkpoint instead defines eleven task families first, then connects typed items to the task, output, prerequisites, complements, alternatives, risks and a learning route. It does not simply duplicate D078’s 58 domain candidates. Where an item overlaps a domain-level capability (for example, research ethics or responsible AI validation), its area function is stated and it is not thereby promoted to a shared foundation.

The selected additions are deliberately conditional. Sociology students are not told they must learn Python, R, qualitative software, AI annotation and agent simulation all together; Economics students are not told to learn every econometrics package or AI tool. The learning path should present a core task and compare justified alternatives. AI-assisted work is a way to support a task, not a replacement for the underlying method.

## Sociology and Social Anthropology

### Task families and sequence

1. **Conduct bounded qualitative field inquiry.** Frame a social question; select interviews, observation or documentary evidence; protect participants and preserve context. Output: a fieldwork plan, consent/context record, de-identified notes and claim–evidence memo. Suitable free study: [OpenStax, Sociological Research](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-sociology-3e/pages/2-introduction). This is an established methods route, not a requirement to use every collection method.
2. **Interpret and compare qualitative material.** Develop/revise codes or analytic categories, compare cases, retain exceptions and explain how context affects interpretation. Output: a traceable codebook or analytic memo with evidence and counterexamples. Manual coding and qualitative-data software are alternatives; software organizes material but does not decide what it means.
3. **Analyse digital text and trace data for a social question.** Assess whether permitted digital text/trace data can address the question, document how the corpus was obtained, prepare it, analyze transparently and contextualize patterns. Output: a documented corpus and reproducible notebook/report. The [SGSSS Practical Computational Methods course](https://github.com/SGSSSonline/text-analysis) gives social-science-specific Python/R materials for collecting and preparing text and trying sentiment/topic methods; [SICSS materials](https://sicss.io/curriculum) include ethics, digital traces, APIs/scraping and automated text analysis. The [UK Data Service](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/learning-hub/computational-social-science/) warns that some Twitter collection demonstrations are outdated—platform access and terms must be rechecked.
4. **Use AI to assist human-led annotation/coding (emerging).** Test a bounded model-labeling task against human-reviewed examples; report error patterns, model/version and uncertainty. Output: validation table, error analysis and workflow note—not an automated “finding.” TUM’s computational social science group documents LLM annotation, qualitative coding and classification as active research and explicitly studies validity, expert comparison, bias and uncertainty. The APA’s *Qualitative Psychology* policy is intentionally treated as a journal-specific caution, not a universal ban: it emphasizes fabrication, bias, confidentiality and researcher responsibility.
5. **Explore social mechanisms using agent-based simulation; optionally inspect LLM-agent prototypes (experimental).** Define agent rules from a theory/mechanism, run scenarios, examine sensitivity and compare implications with evidence. Output: reproducible model, assumptions table and a clear explanation that simulated agents are not observed people. [Mesa](https://mesa.readthedocs.io/) is an open agent-based modelling framework. [Casevo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19498) is a research prototype built on Mesa that adds LLM-enabled agents; its paper uses a U.S. election-debate demonstration. Casevo remains a hidden, read-only research example: the repository LICENSE says Apache-2.0 while package metadata says MIT, release-wheel metadata were not inspected, and the example requires a Baichuan API key. Its installation, runtime, access, cost and privacy terms are not established. It is not proof of Nigerian behavior, a population simulator validated for policy, or a forecast.
6. **Use AI to draft, edit or translate survey questions, then cognitively pretest them (emerging).** This applies only where the sociological question uses a survey. Use AI as a drafting aid, have subject/language-competent people review it, then test how intended respondents understand the questions. Output: versioned wording/translation notes, cognitive-pretest findings and documented human/AI roles. [AAPOR’s 2026 responsible-AI report](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Responsible-AI-Integration-In-Survey-Research.pdf) covers AI question writing/editing/translation (section 3.4, pp. 25–27) and says human oversight, task-specific evaluation and documentation matter. [AAPOR survey best practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) explains cognitive pretesting. This is not a claim that AI translation is valid for Nigerian languages; locally intended speakers must test it. Specialist survey-method ownership may overlap Area 6 and should be reconciled in that lane.

### Area-specific items and relationships

- **Social concepts and contextual interpretation → field question and evidence.** Established knowledge; paired with interviews/observation and interpretive coding. Concepts help frame and interpret evidence; they do not prove a claim.
- **Qualitative interviewing and contextual observation → situated accounts.** Established methods; require a question, consent/confidentiality and note-taking. Interviews, observation and documents are choices, not cumulative steps. Small purposive samples do not establish population prevalence or causal effect.
- **Human-participant ethics/reflexivity → safe fieldwork and transparent interpretation.** Applies where human evidence is involved; ethics review requirements depend on institution/jurisdiction. A checklist does not itself guarantee ethical practice.
- **Transparent qualitative coding → auditable interpretation.** Manual coding or software-supported organization; complements substantive theory and reflexivity. Coding agreement is not proof of interpretive validity.
- **Python or R → repeatable text/data processing.** Optional, alternative environments; code path assumes basic programming and data literacy. Neither is a prerequisite to doing sociology, and neither validates the construct or sample.
- **Text preparation/computational text analysis → patterns in larger corpora.** Established method families, but each method is question-specific. Sentiment, topic, named-entity or classifier output is not automatically a valid social measure.
- **Human-validated AI annotation → first-pass coding support.** Emerging and task-dependent; depends on a human-defined coding frame and validation sample. Human interpretation remains accountable. The [TUM research page](https://www.hfp.tum.de/en/css/forschung/data-methods/) and [APA policy](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/qua/ai-policy) support both research interest and the need to scrutinize validity, bias, fabrication and data confidentiality.
- **Agent-based modelling → explore specified mechanisms.** An established method family, distinct from empirical observation. Mesa is a tool, not a model-quality guarantee.
- **Casevo → experimental LLM-agent example within ABM.** Prototype identity only; learner installation/reuse is closed as not-established because LICENSE and package metadata conflict and runtime/API access/cost were not verified. Keep hidden/read-only. Non-LLM Mesa models, analytic models and empirical study remain alternatives; Mesa Getting Started is the free practical resource.
- **AI-assisted survey drafting/translation → respondent-tested instrument.** Emerging and conditional; human experts and target respondents must check meaning, bias and comprehension. AAPOR’s report covers global survey-method issues but is not Nigerian law or proof of local-language performance. The specialist survey-method task may also be owned by D078 Area 6; do not count it as a second distinct specialization without reconciliation.
- **Cognitive pretesting/translation-equivalence review → evidence about respondent understanding.** An established survey method, conditional on instrument design; a small pretest identifies problems but does not alone establish validity, representativeness or reliability.

## Economics and Applied Economic Analysis

### Task families and sequence

1. **Describe economic conditions with appropriate indicators/data.** Define measure, population, unit and period; appraise source and compare like with like. Output: sourced chart/data brief with caveats. [CORE Econ’s open materials](https://www.core-econ.org/espp/) explain economic ideas and evidence; [Doing Economics](https://www.core-econ.org/doing-economics/) provides free data projects using spreadsheet, R or Python routes. Nigerian context: the [NBS Nigeria Living Standards Survey 2018/19](https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/68) documents welfare, labour, consumption and other household/community topics and explicitly requires weights for representative estimates; it is dated data, not a current indicator. [NISER’s 2026 Economic Intelligence Report](https://niser.gov.ng/v2/niser-economic-intelligence-report-2026/) is a current local research-to-decision example, not a role standard.
2. **Estimate and evaluate an economic relationship/intervention.** Define outcome/comparison, choose a defensible design, examine assumptions and uncertainty, distinguish association from causal effect. Output: reproducible estimate/brief with design assumptions and limits. [The Effect](https://theeffectbook.net/) provides free research-design/causality chapters and R/Stata/Python examples. This is specialist work, not an automatic next step after descriptive statistics.
3. **Build a forecast or nowcast.** Forecast future values or estimate current conditions before final statistics are published; compare with a baseline and explain uncertainty, revisions and scenarios. Output: forecast with horizon, data vintage, baseline and range. It is distinct from causal impact evaluation; forecasts are conditional, not promises. [Forecasting: Principles and Practice](https://otexts.com/fpp3/) is an open, task-specific time-series learning resource.
4. **Use AI/ML-generated economic measures with validation (emerging specialist).** Derive a variable from text/records, validate labels against human-reviewed cases and account for measurement error downstream. The [AEA 2026 guide](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pandp.20261020) specifically notes that ignoring measurement error in AI/ML-generated covariates can bias estimators and invalidate inference. Output: construct definition, validation set, error report and appropriately qualified estimate. This is an emerging specialist edge, not a shared economics foundation.
5. **Use generative AI for bounded economics research microtasks (emerging).** Examples include code scaffolding or a draft search plan, provided factual claims, citations, code and reasoning are checked. The AEA review [Generative AI for Economic Research](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20231736) surveys use cases from ideation and background research to data analysis, coding and derivations, with capability maturity varying by use. Its 2025 [AI Agents update](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles/materials/23826) is horizon-scan material, not evidence that agents can replace economists.

### Area-specific items and relationships

- **Economic concepts/indicators/measurement → indicator choice.** Established knowledge; requires understanding definitions, source, unit and comparability. No single indicator captures all welfare or informal activity.
- **Economic data appraisal/descriptive analysis → sourced data brief.** Established skill; can use spreadsheet, R or Python. Tool choice is alternative, not cumulative; a clear chart can still rest on invalid data.
- **Applied econometrics/causal design → effect estimate where assumptions support it.** Established specialist method; requires statistical/economic foundations. Descriptive analysis is preferable when identification is not defensible.
- **Inference/policy-claim discipline → responsible communication.** Established competency; requires explaining comparison, uncertainty and limitations. Caveats cannot repair a poor design.
- **Forecasting/nowcasting → conditional estimate for planning.** Established specialist family; requires time-series literacy and model evaluation. Simple baseline may be preferable to complex ML.
- **R/Python economic-data workflows → reproducible computation.** Optional alternatives taught through task-specific [Doing Economics projects](https://www.core-econ.org/doing-economics/); do not imply a programme teaches either without curriculum evidence.
- **AI/ML-generated covariates → measurement plus inference.** Emerging specialist method; requires construct definition, validation and error-aware inference. Prediction accuracy is not enough.
- **Validation/reproducibility → check AI-generated measures.** Specialized competency complementing generated measures; validation sample fit and population/language coverage matter; no universal threshold proves fitness for every use.
- **Generative AI research assistance → bounded microtasks only.** Emerging optional workflow aid; requires enough economics literacy to detect wrong output and awareness of confidentiality/publisher rules. Manual work and deterministic scripts remain valid alternatives.
- **AI-agent economic research / LLM-agent simulation.** Emerging-to-experimental horizon scan. AEA’s update discusses possibilities such as research agents and simulation; do not imply those workflows are validated standard practice or necessary for students.

No universal certification was identified as a necessary condition for any of these task families. The ledger therefore does not invent a certification requirement. Any future credential candidate must be separately researched for issuer, scope, eligibility, assessment, jurisdiction and actual relevance.

## Evidence and source boundaries

The JSON stores 23 source records with publisher, direct URL, locator, jurisdiction, evidence role, limitations and access date. It separates task evidence from explanatory material and from free task-specific learning. Representative primary/authoritative sources include O*NET for an occupational task example; OpenStax/SICSS/SGSSS/UK Data Service for methods and instruction; TUM and APA for LLM-method research and cautions; AAPOR for survey AI/pretesting practice; Casevo’s paper and Mesa’s official docs; AEA research for AI-in-economics and generated covariates; CORE Econ and FPP3 for free economic learning; NBS and NISER for Nigeria-specific datasets and context.

Global methods remain globally eligible even without a Nigerian equivalent. Nigeria sources here illustrate local data and research-to-policy context; they do not convert a local report or dataset into a universal task mandate. Conversely, an international source is not treated as proof of Nigerian legal/ethics requirements.
