## Source decisions

## Additional source verification checkpoint — 2026-10-09

The additional links above were checked against the publishers' or institutions' own pages/PDFs. The Open University describes its course as a free, introductory peace-education course, with separate sessions on peace, conflict approaches and peer/community settings. Its educational focus is narrower than university-level peace and conflict studies, so that limitation remains explicit. NOUN's official courseware contains the cited Nigeria-focused conflict-history modules and gives printed page locations; its catalogue identifies related Peace Studies and Conflict Resolution courseware, but NOUN materials remain institution-specific. The AEHN publisher describes its textbook as open access and intended for African university teachers/students; the linked chapter pages identify the exact chapter titles. This improves Africa-wide supporting coverage without claiming exhaustive continental coverage.

These sources are now represented as optional, theme-specific programme resources in the local migration. They remain unseeded/unapplied and therefore are not yet verified as visible in the app. The references link out rather than copy courseware. Their presence does not itself approve all theme narration or pass the programme overview gate.

## Link verification checkpoint — 2026-10-08

The original 20 distinct URLs listed in this matrix were checked against their current
publisher or project destinations. The OpenStax, OpenLearn, Collins and NOUN
destinations opened as the expected web page or PDF. The United Nations link
was also confirmed by the current UNAI search result and remains recorded as a
short orientation resource; if the direct page later fails in the app, replace
it with the verified OpenLearn *Principles and practices of peace education*
course rather than claiming the quiz is a full course. URL reachability does
not validate the academic fit of a resource: the exact scope and limitations
in the table remain part of the student copy and staff provenance.

- OpenStax's publisher-hosted book page identifies it as an openly licensed introductory Sociology text; sections above are linked individually. Its U.S. examples are not presented as Nigerian evidence.
- Collins' official catalogue provides the 8th-edition contents, supporting the specific chapter references above. It is a paid/library reference. Do not imply that a catalogue page provides free access to the full book.
- The NOUN GST 201 course guide directly names its aims and unit structure: Nigerian peoples and culture, Nigerian culture areas, political evolution, and related historical/social topics. It is local contextual support, not evidence of a nationwide prescribed reading list.
- OpenLearn report writing, thinking skills, entrepreneurship, and computing pages are exact Open University learning sections/courses. Each is attached only to the part of a theme it actually teaches.
- The Giddens/Sutton *Sociology* resource row is intentionally not mapped to any theme until an edition-specific contents list and exact relevant locators are verified. A familiar title alone is not evidence of theme fit.
- Some themes have only partial or introductory external resources. Those gaps are recorded above rather than filled with broad keyword matches or unsupported claims.

## Implementation and verification boundary

The local SQL migration writes theme keys, short theme-specific explanations and exact theme links into programme-owned resource provenance. The app resolves the theme-specific link for each card. This file and local migration are not evidence of SQL application, API payload delivery, or a fully rendered student page. Those checks remain part of the programme-overview gate.
