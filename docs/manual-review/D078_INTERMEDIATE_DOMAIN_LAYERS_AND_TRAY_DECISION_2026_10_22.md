## Decision: route order

The checked-in D078 graph already defines the three compulsory foundations, Layers 2 and 3, method choices, named combinations, and method-to-area/role routes. Layers 2 and 3 are not additional universal prerequisites. Since relevance depends on the selected method/task, the learner flow is:

**Display-flow correction (authoritative):** the student sees the domain orientation and three compulsory foundations, then the method grid. Layer 2/3 are not displayed before method selection—not even as a placeholder section. Once a single method or exact named design is selected, the matching Layer 2/3 items appear below that chooser, in the context of that selection, followed by the selected route's downstream content. The conceptual layer numbers remain content classifications; they do not require displaying optional content before the learner can identify a relevant method.

1. Programme overview and programme-owned curriculum context, with the undergraduate route going directly to the programme (no university choice); SS1/SS2/SS3/UTME may carry a selected or compared university context.
2. Domain orientation and the programme-to-domain transition, including only evidence-matched programme themes/outcomes and transferable context.
3. The three approved compulsory domain foundations, in their existing order.
4. **Layer 2 — Field-wide knowledge and useful learning**, followed by **Layer 3 — Research practices and conditional preparation**. These layers occupy their actual place before method choice, but their item lists remain collapsed until the learner selects a method or exact named design. This avoids presenting conditional items as universal requirements while keeping the conceptual sequence honest.
5. **Methods of investigation/research:** select one method. Tell the learner after the first selection that more than one may be selected when the research question needs it. The method cards are a responsive grid. Once selected, reveal only Layer 2/3 records with a checked connection to that method or exact named design in their earlier sections. Keep each record in its Layer 2/3 grouping, preserve its needed/useful/conditional lane and condition, and explain the route connection. Do not show an entire layer as a checklist and do not duplicate a record in the later downstream list.
6. Other method-specific learning, eligible areas, roles, and tools appear in their downstream route screens, using the exact selected method/design evidence.
7. For a multi-method selection, resolve the exact selected set to a graph-backed named design before showing any combined downstream. Show its component-method roles, integration point/order, preparation, limits, and design-specific downstream.
8. Move through separate screens: the field/method screen is the gateway to eligible areas; opening an area moves to its area screen; opening a supported role moves to its role screen. Do not place area/role cards in a side rail or make them appear as already-selected stages before the learner opens them.

Layer numbers classify graph content and the sequence is meaningful: foundations → Layer 2 → Layer 3 → methods → method/design-specific deepening → area → role. Before a method/design choice, Layer 2/3 show a short explanation but not a checklist of all candidate records. After the choice, matched items appear inside the earlier Layer 2/3 sections, and only where an evidence-backed route link exists. The three foundations remain the only universal domain progression gate. A selected method is not, by itself, enough to declare every general research skill relevant.

### Carry programme learning into methods and techniques

Where Sociology curriculum records cover or introduce a method, show that context beside the corresponding method card or expanded technique detail. Use a clearly labeled section such as **“How this relates to your Sociology curriculum”**, followed by the relevant course/outcome and a plain-language note on what the downstream route adds. This is context, not a second curriculum list and not a claim that the student has mastered the method.

For statistics, the reviewed Sociology record identifies SOC 301 as *Methods of Social Research and Statistics* and SSC 401 as expecting students to “apply appropriate statistics in data analysis.” The published Sociology course detail available in this repository does not name chi-square specifically. Therefore the chi-square card should acknowledge the relevant programme foundation and say that the optional item deepens choosing, applying and interpreting the test; it must not assert that the national course outline explicitly taught chi-square. Where a learner's own course did cover it, the card should make clear that they can use this as review/deepening rather than being told they are starting from zero. Preserve the course locator and outcome reference in the graph for audit.

Apply this distinction consistently: curriculum evidence may support a method family or broad capability even when it does not enumerate each downstream technique. Say what is documented, what the route adds, and what prior mastery is not assumed. Never promote an externally sourced technique into a claimed programme outcome without matching course evidence.

### Method-fit decision rule (required before suppressing an item)

“Fits this method” must be demonstrated, not guessed from similar words or broad research relevance. Apply this record-by-record rule:

1. **Inspect local evidence first.** The item must be explicitly attached to a selected method's source-backed method-area route through `task_linked_domain_item_ids`, `domain_downstream_item_ids`, or a reviewed typed relationship with an explanation. Do not use the aggregate `available_item_ids` list by itself as proof; it mixes several sources of membership.
2. **Check what the item actually teaches.** Compare the item's definition, condition, output, limitations, requirement lane, and learning/source links with the selected method's definition, task evidence, and design contribution. A thematic resemblance is insufficient.
3. **Confirm the methodological connection externally when the local record does not make it clear.** Search authoritative method standards, official research-ethics/data-policy guidance, established research-data services, or peer-reviewed methodological sources. Record the URL, exact supporting section/locator, access date, and what claim it supports. Sources validate the general method connection; local route/task records still determine whether it belongs in D078's specific pathway.
4. **Apply conditions literally.** For example, responsible-research guidance can be relevant when a selected method is used with people or identifiable/sensitive information. That does not make ethics training a universal method prerequisite. Data stewardship is relevant when the project creates, receives, analyses, stores, or shares research data. AI/digital-trace ethics is relevant only when that use case is actually present.
5. **For a named combination**, connect the item to the method contribution it supports inside that exact design. Do not union item lists simply because the component methods were selected. If multiple designs match, wait for the learner's design choice.
6. **Only after the local inventory and authoritative external search are completed** may a candidate be dispositioned as “no supported connection found.” That is an evidence-based non-match for this D078 route, not a permanent global claim that the item is useless. An unchecked or unsearched candidate remains unresolved in the staff audit and must not be silently discarded or described to students as disproven.

External method references consulted for this rule include the [CDC Program Evaluation Framework guidance on matching questions, methods, measures, sampling and data sources](https://www.cdc.gov/evaluation/php/evaluation-framework-action-guide/step-4-gather-credible-evidence.html), [AAPOR survey research best practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/), [ESRC/UKRI expectations for proportionate ethics review](https://www.ukri.org/councils/esrc/guidance-for-applicants/research-ethics-guidance/ethics-reviews/our-expectations-of-ethics-review/), [UKRI's current research-data policy](https://www.ukri.org/publications/uk-research-and-innovation-research-data-policy/ukri-research-data-policy/), the [UK Data Service guidance on secondary use of qualitative data](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/learning-hub/qualitative-data/) and [secondary analysis more broadly](https://ukdataservice.ac.uk/learning-hub/new-to-using-data/), and the [Cochrane Handbook chapter on systematic searching and evidence selection](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-04). For ABM-specific links, the audit uses Bruch and Atwell's [review of agent-based models in empirical social research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4430112/) and [CoMSES Net's ODD documentation standard](https://www.comses.net/resources/standards/). These sources justify the recorded conditional use cases; they do not make any optional item compulsory or establish a universal prerequisite.

The generated graph now records every one of the 220 Layer 2/3-item × method combinations instead of dropping missing links: 127 have explicit local route evidence, 71 have externally supported conditional-fit records, and 22 mark the social-world item as domain orientation rather than a method task. No pair is labeled `no_supported_fit`. Conditional cross-cutting guidance is not a requirement to learn it for every method: data care appears when the project handles research data; responsible-research preparation appears when participants, sensitive/identifiable information, risk, restricted access or data linkage are involved; secondary-data learning appears when the design reuses existing evidence. The ABM-specific links are limited to model-grounding, operationalisation, population representation, validity/uncertainty and reproducibility use cases. The graph stores source URLs, locators, access dates, conditions and rationale for these external dispositions. A future negative disposition remains prohibited until exhaustive local and external evidence search is recorded.

## Decision: multi-method matching and downstream

Use **“Methods of investigation/research”** as the student-facing section title. A method is an approach a learner can select. A named combination is a graph-backed design that intentionally integrates/arranges two or more methods; it is not another compulsory course and must not be inferred from a generic multi-select.

- Keep the individual-method route intact when exactly one method is selected. Do not require a mixed or combined design for a single-method learner.
- After the first selected method, explicitly say that another method may be selected where the research question needs it. Do not imply that every pair can be combined.
- With two or more methods selected, match the **whole selected method set** to a named graph design. Never show the union of each selected method’s areas, roles, and items as if it were a combined route.
- If exactly one fixed design matches the whole selection, use that design’s integration contract and downstream routes. If more than one design matches the same methods (for example, explanatory-sequential versus convergent survey/interview), show the alternatives and require the learner to choose the design that fits their purpose before revealing downstream.
- A subset of a larger design may be shown as “this design also needs …”; it is not a completed combination and exposes no combined areas/roles/items yet. A selection with no exact supported design exposes no combined downstream and explains that the selected methods do not currently have a reviewed joint route. The learner can remove a method and continue on a valid single-method route.
- The participatory record is a special, evidence-backed design frame: Participatory and action research is required, and one or more explicitly selected component methods are optional. Show downstream only as the selected components within that frame support it; never auto-select all components. Do not treat this controlled frame as permission to union arbitrary selected methods.
- “Dominant” and “supporting” describe the component methods’ roles in the design, not equal shares. A design may be led by one method while another develops, contextualizes, or explains its evidence. Preserve the dominant method’s appropriate contribution and downstream where the graph has route evidence, but do not expand the design into every area or role reached by that method alone.
- For fixed designs, an area is eligible only when the graph has route evidence for the design’s constituent methods in that area, unless a reviewed design-specific record explicitly establishes a dominant-method exception. Within an eligible area, keep method-specific task/item contributions traceable. A role is eligible only when the combination’s task/design evidence supports that role through the area—not merely because it exists in the area.
- A selected named design is one integrated route. Explain each method’s contribution, how the pieces connect, what foundations/preparation support them, the integration point, the sequence, outputs and limitations. Deduplicate shared items; do not require the learner to add the methods again as independent selections.
- Render individual methods in a responsive card grid: side-by-side on wide screens, fewer columns on tablets, one column on mobile. Do not show an always-visible combination catalogue; reveal only compatible named design choices after at least two relevant selections.

These rules avoid the invalid assumption that selecting methods automatically makes their downstream routes one coherent design. Mixed-methods guidance calls for a justified purpose, sequence/priority, and explicit integration; it also allows one method to be dominant. [NIH Office of Behavioral and Social Sciences Research guidance](https://obssr.od.nih.gov/sites/g/files/mnhszr296/files/Best_Practices_for_Mixed_Methods_Research.pdf) and the foundational integration framework by Fetters et al. describe design-level integration and different contributions of methods ([peer-reviewed framework](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4097839/)). CDC guidance likewise describes purposefully integrating methods rather than simply collecting multiple forms of evidence ([CDC evaluation guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/evaluation/php/evaluation-framework-action-guide/step-4-gather-credible-evidence.html)). Those sources support the matching/integration guardrail; D078 area/role eligibility still requires the local graph’s task and role evidence.

## Required graph fields and route contract

Each combination record must contain:

- exact `required_method_ids` (and, only for the participatory frame, explicit selectable component IDs);
- method contribution/priority for each component (`lead`, `develops`, `explains`, `compares`, `contextualizes`, or another justified role);
- why the methods fit together, how/when they connect, where integration happens, the condition for use, expected output and limitations;
- evidence/source IDs for the design;
- exact eligible areas and roles, with per-method task-family/item/tool contributions and a stated route basis;
- a matching rule (`exact_set`, with a separately represented partial-design state), plus the rule for ambiguous matches.

For each compatible learner choice, downstream projection must be derived from the matched design record’s route set. It must not be calculated as an unqualified union. Each eligible combination-area route contains explicit method-contribution subnodes with that method’s task families, learning items and tools; these subnodes are the source for downstream projection. The combination-area node must not copy those component arrays into one flat task/item/tool checklist. The learner sees the named design’s integration sequence first, then the distinct contribution lanes, then eligible areas/roles. Shared item IDs may be deduplicated in a summary only after preserving which component method/design supports each item. Single-method routes remain independent. Unsupported or ambiguous selections must not quietly reveal areas, roles, or items. When enrichment adds a method-specific item later, it must update that method’s contribution record and graph subnode—not append the item to every combination that happens to contain the method.

The current compact graph has 22 methods and 9 named records (eight fixed designs plus the participatory frame). This is not proof that every possible pair is compatible. The graph audit must enumerate every method pair (and supported larger set), mark it as a named design, a component of a larger design, or “no reviewed D078 combination route”; do not infer new combinations from a method name or generic relationship edge alone. Existing supported examples include exploratory sequential, explanatory sequential, convergent, group discussion informing survey development/follow-up, case study using multiple sources, interview+observation, document-elicitation interviews, and participant-led method choice. Same method pairs may support different designs and must be disambiguated by purpose.

## Tray behavior

**Programme-page mounting is implemented locally:** the shared tray now appears on the programme-detail screen and starts with the selected programme. It carries institution context whenever a selected/comparison institution actually exists, regardless of route type; undergraduate Sociology does not require or invent a university, but may retain one selected earlier. Focused programme-screen tests cover both cases. This is local UI wiring, not a database migration or live-browser acceptance.

The required tray progression is explicit: first show the selected programme (and institution only when that pathway actually selected one); after the learner selects a domain, show that domain before its methods and selected domain items; after an area is selected, add the area and its own selected items; after a role is selected within that area, add the role and its own selected items. Keep each level visibly grouped and ordered, with item requirement lanes and alternatives preserved. The programme screen now mounts the tray at the first stage; the D078 journey continues to group domain/method, area, and role choices and selected items. Generic stack counts now include route context as well as chosen learning items, so the collapsed count does not misleadingly show zero when a route is selected.

The D078 route stack intentionally contains D078 route variants only: each branch preserves its own method/design choices, area, role and selected items through snapshot save/reopen, My World, sharing and report generation. Cross-domain and cross-area selections are handled by the general guided-field flow, which builds separate stacks for each selected domain/area/role route, warns about multiple routes, and persists the learner's route-specific choices. Do not expect one D078 method stack to contain non-D078 nodes; the two route types retain their own evidence and item contracts. The programme-page tray is the programme/institution context entry point; downstream screens reconstruct their route state from the carried programme/institution context and selections, rather than treating the initial context-only tray as a saved learning plan.

- The tray begins on the programme page and identifies the selected programme. It carries institution context whenever the journey has one (including an institution selected earlier in an undergraduate route or selected/compared on SS1/SS2/SS3/UTME paths); an undergraduate Sociology route must not invent or require a university.
- As the learner advances, the same saved route stack adds the selected domain before its methods/items, then each explicitly opened/selected area and role with its own selected items. A selected method/design and its item choices remain attached to that route. Multiple domains, areas, roles, or methods stay distinguishable and are reflected consistently in My World and the final report.
- The collapsed icon shows an accurate count of selected programme/domain/area/role context, methods/designs, and added items (without counting inherited descriptive context as a learner-selected learning item). Selecting/removing a method or item updates it immediately; opening reveals the current valid route or the reason a multi-method route is unresolved.
- **Compact**, **Mid**, and **Full** are visibly distinct: Compact is a short summary; Mid is a bounded working view; Full uses nearly all available sheet/viewport space while respecting safe areas and keeping actions reachable. Full must not reuse Mid's fixed 360px content cap.
- On narrow screens the icon opens the sheet; closing does not clear selections. On wider screens the same controls resize the docked tray. Preserve reduced-motion/accessibility and avoid duplicate visible trays.
- The journey spine/side navigation shows only the current stage and valid backward/forward navigation. The field screen is the gateway to areas; areas and roles are opened on their own screens, not rendered as premature side-rail selections.
