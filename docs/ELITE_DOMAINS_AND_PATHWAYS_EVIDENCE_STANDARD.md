# Elite Domains and Pathways: Evidence Standard

## Purpose

This document defines what Sifter means by **elite** before any domain,
subgroup, role, or pathway is labelled that way. “Elite” is not a synonym for
high salary, popularity, prestige, or a field that sounds impressive. It is a
narrow, evidence-backed label for a destination that demands unusually strong
preparation and has a real, identifiable route into it.

The label is useful only when it helps a learner answer four questions:

1. What makes this destination unusually demanding or selective?
2. What evidence shows that the destination matters in Nigeria, globally, or both?
3. What would I need to learn, prove, register for, or complete from my current position?
4. What is the most realistic first step before the destination role?

If the product cannot answer those questions with named sources, the item must
remain an ordinary domain, subgroup, or role with other labels such as
“growing”, “regulated”, “frontier”, or “specialist”. It must not be called elite.

## The definition

An elite destination is a clearly identified domain, subgroup, role, or route
that satisfies all four mandatory conditions below.

### 1. Specialist identity

The work has a distinct body of knowledge, methods, tools, or responsibilities
that cannot be treated as a generic version of a neighbouring occupation. The
identity must be traceable to recognised occupational, professional, academic,
regulatory, or industry sources.

Examples include quantum information science, investment advisory, dentistry,
and satellite systems engineering. A broad label such as “business” or
“technology” is too wide to be elite by itself.

### 2. A real barrier to competent entry

The destination requires more than a short introductory course. The barrier may
be one or more of the following:

- advanced mathematical, scientific, technical, clinical, or legal preparation;
- a regulated degree, supervised practice, professional examination, or licence;
- a demanding portfolio, research record, technical assessment, or demonstrated
  project history;
- a competitive institutional, employer, fellowship, or research selection process;
- substantial experience before independent practice is permitted.

Cost alone is not a barrier. A qualification is not automatically elite because
it is expensive, and a local route is not automatically inferior because it is
less expensive.

### 3. A recognised high-consequence or scarce outcome

The destination must lead to work that is demonstrably important, scarce,
highly specialised, or responsible for significant decisions, systems, safety,
capital, public services, research, or advanced technology. This condition must
be supported by evidence rather than by marketing language.

### 4. A documented route

There must be an explainable path from at least one Nigerian starting point to
the destination. The route must identify the relevant foundation, next-level
specialisation, entry role or supervised stage, credentials where necessary,
and evidence the learner can use to show readiness.

An interesting field with no researched route is an **emerging area**, not an
elite pathway. It may be shown as “frontier” or “research-led” while its route
is being built.

## Scope is part of the label

There should be one canonical concept — **elite destination** — with an
explicit evidence scope. The product must not use “elite” as if local and
global evidence were interchangeable.

| Scope | Meaning | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Nigeria | Elite within a Nigerian professional, regulatory, institutional, or employer context | Nigerian barrier or professional-body evidence, Nigerian market or institution evidence, and a Nigerian entry route |
| Global | Elite within an international specialist, research, professional, or employer context | International standard or professional evidence, global market or institution evidence, and a global entry route |
| Nigeria → Global | A learner can begin through a documented Nigerian route and progress to a globally recognised elite destination | Both Nigerian and global evidence, plus the bridge between them |

“Nigeria → Global” is not a higher rank. It is a route statement. It tells the
learner that the destination has a credible local starting point and a
documented bridge to international work, study, research, or practice.

An item may have more than one scope. For example, a regulated Nigerian
profession may be elite locally but require a separate licensing or
qualification bridge abroad. The interface must say that plainly rather than
showing one vague global badge.

## Domain, subgroup, and role are assessed separately

An elite role does not automatically make the whole domain elite. A domain is
elite only when the domain itself has a specialist identity, a meaningful
barrier, a recognised outcome, and a documented route. The same rule applies to
subgroups and roles.

For example, a domain may contain one highly selective research role while most
of its roles are broad entry occupations. In that case:

- the role may receive an elite-role label;
- the subgroup may receive a specialist label if its own evidence qualifies;
- the parent domain remains unlabelled unless its defining work qualifies.

This prevents a small number of prestigious roles from making an entire field
look inaccessible or falsely elite.

## Evidence dimensions used by the audit

The audit records the dimensions separately. A source may support one
dimension without proving all the others.

| Dimension | What it proves | Suitable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | What the work is and how it differs from neighbouring work | International Standard Classification of Occupations, European Skills/Competences/Qualifications and Occupations, Occupational Information Network, professional bodies, university or industry definitions |
| Skill complexity | The knowledge and task complexity required for competent performance | International Labour Organization skill levels, Occupational Information Network preparation and task data, official curricula, professional competency standards |
| Regulation or selection | Whether practice, progression, or entry is formally restricted or competitively assessed | Nigerian regulators, professional examination rules, supervised-practice requirements, named fellowship or employer selection rules |
| Market or strategic importance | Whether the work is demanded, growing, scarce, or strategically supported | Nigerian employer and labour evidence, National Bureau of Statistics, official sector strategies, World Economic Forum, international labour or industry data |
| Portability | Whether the capability is recognised beyond one employer or country | International standards, recognised professional bodies, cross-border qualification rules, international employer or research requirements |
| Nigerian route | How a Nigerian learner can begin and progress | Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board and university information, Nigerian regulators, Nigerian institutions, employer programmes, research centres, scholarships, fellowships, and verified training routes |

No single source is allowed to carry the whole argument. The World Economic
Forum can support growth signals, but it does not prove Nigerian licensing.
The Nigerian regulator can prove a registration requirement, but it does not
prove global demand. International occupational data can explain the work, but
it does not prove that the work is currently available locally.

## Publication rule

An elite label is **published** only when all of the following are present:

- a stable identity and human-readable name tied to source terminology or a
  clearly explained source-supported umbrella;
- evidence for specialist identity and a real barrier;
- evidence for a recognised, scarce, or high-consequence outcome;
- at least one current source for the declared scope;
- an explicit route from a named Nigerian starting point, or an honest note that
  the route is global-only or research-led;
- at least one entry-level or supervised step before the pinnacle role;
- source dates and links visible to the reviewer;
- a review status of `published`, `provisional`, or `not established`.

The product must not convert a missing data point into a positive claim. A
missing Nigerian employer signal means “Nigerian market evidence not yet
established”, not “no Nigerian opportunity exists”.

## Current catalogue finding

The current repository contains conflicting elite counts and flags: the domain
metadata marks 21 domains, the application policy names 10, and an older content
migration comment says 17. The elite-pathway table is also designed to remain
empty until real rows are supplied. Therefore no fixed elite count should be
shown to learners and the existing flags must be treated as **unreviewed
candidate signals**, not published truth.

The existing role composite is useful as an input, but it cannot by itself
decide elite status. A composite based on role barriers, compensation,
scarcity, or feedback is incomplete when its demand tables or independent
professional review are missing. Domain and subgroup labels must be reviewed
against the publication rule above.

## What the current sources do — and do not — establish

The sources provide **audit evidence**, not a pre-approved elite list. For
example, the Pharmacy Council of Nigeria establishes accredited education,
indexing, internship, examination, and registration requirements. The Medical
and Dental Council establishes a regulated practice context. The Council for
the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria establishes qualification, experience,
examination, and registration routes. The Securities and Exchange Commission
establishes qualification, experience, certification, and registration rules
for investment-adviser representatives. These facts support investigation of
specialist routes; none of them, by itself, proves that every related domain,
subgroup, or role is elite.

Likewise, international sources identify important or emerging areas such as
artificial intelligence, data, cybersecurity, robotics, quantum information,
bioinformatics, biomedical engineering, space technology, nuclear science,
and advanced materials. Those signals justify a source review of the relevant
records. They do not prove that Nigeria has a large direct employer market or a
complete local route for every role.

No elite domain, subgroup, role, or route is approved by this document alone.
Each one must pass the publication rule after its own identity, barrier,
outcome, scope, and Nigerian/global pathway evidence have been checked.

Broad fields such as software engineering, management, marketing, accounting,
diplomacy, credit analysis, and creative work may contain elite roles or
subgroups. Their broad labels are not enough. The audit must evaluate the
specific destination rather than promoting an entire field because one of its
roles is selective or well known.

The following sources are used as evidence anchors. They do not all make an
“elite” classification; they support different parts of the reasoning.

- [Central Bank of Nigeria Payments System Vision 2028](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/PaymentsSystem/PSV2028.html): supports the Nigerian payments, financial-technology, security, inclusion, and cross-border financial-systems context.
- [Securities and Exchange Commission Nigeria investment-adviser rules](https://sec.gov.ng/for-investors/keep-track-of-circulars/new-rule-and-sundry-amendments-to-the-rules-and-regulations-of-the-commission/): documents qualification, experience, certification, and registration requirements for investment-adviser representatives.

## Product representation

The user-facing label should be:

> **Elite route — Nigeria → Global**

or, where appropriate:

> **Elite route — Nigeria**

> **Elite route — Global / research-led**

The detail view must immediately explain:

- why the item received the label;
- whether the label applies to the domain, subgroup, role, or only the route;
- the local and global evidence separately;
- the learner’s likely starting point;
- the first realistic step;
- the bridge skills, tools, credentials, research, experience, or licensing;
- what is optional and what is required;
- what is not yet known.

The label must never imply guaranteed employment, guaranteed admission,
superiority over ordinary careers, or a promise of wealth.

## Pathway flow

The pathway is attached to the ordinary career graph rather than being a
separate catalogue that competes with it:

```text
starting point
  → domain foundation
  → chosen subgroup (when the evidence supports one)
  → earliest credible role or supervised stage
  → specialist role
  → elite destination role or research outcome
```

When a domain has no supported subgroup, the product must use:

```text
starting point → domain foundation → entry role → specialist destination
```

For each learner, the system should show what is already covered, the next
smallest useful bridge, and the evidence needed to progress. It should not
present a long list of elite credentials as if all are required at once.

## Audit decision

The current elite flags are unverified input data, not selections. The next data
task is therefore not to increase or defend the existing count. It is to review
every flagged domain, subgroup, and role; verify its identity; assign its
evidence scope; attach the Nigerian and global sources; map the route from
Nigerian starting points; test every domain-role relationship; and publish only
the records that satisfy the mandatory conditions.
